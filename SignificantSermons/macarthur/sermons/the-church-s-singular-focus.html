The Church's Singular Focus

1 Corinthians 2:2 John MacArthur
Thesis The church's sole calling in the world is to proclaim the gospel of Jesus Christ and Him crucified, not to pursue political power or impose morality through social activism.

Theological claims· 13
  1. The church throughout history has consistently struggled to maintain focus on Christ and Him crucified. unit #2
  2. The term 'Christian' has lost its original meaning as a label of identification with the rejected Christ and has become a meaningless cultural designation. unit #8
  3. Both Peter and Paul demonstrate the same apostolic pattern of singular focus on the gospel of Christ despite countless social and temporal distractions. unit #15
  4. Contemporary evangelicals are being rejected by the culture not for identification with Christ or gospel proclamation, but for political activism and attempts to impose traditional morality through legislative power. unit #17
  5. Christians should expect rejection and persecution, but the critical question is whether they are being rejected for the right reason—identification with Christ and the gospel. unit #18
  6. Contemporary evangelicals are being resented for the wrong reason—political activism and moral crusading—rather than for gospel proclamation and identification with Christ. unit #22
  7. While Christians legitimately support biblical morality and oppose cultural immorality, moral reform is not the reason the church exists in the world. unit #24
  8. Lasting virtue and genuine righteousness cannot be achieved through political activism, media campaigns, lobbying, and cultural pressure. unit #25
  9. God does not bless morality; Jesus accepted wretched sinners while pronouncing His harshest condemnation on the morally religious Pharisees. unit #26
  10. Morality apart from godliness cannot avert divine judgment; even the religiously moral Jews committed to God's law faced judgment in 70 AD, demonstrating that morality without genuine relationship with God is satanic deception. unit #27
  11. The church's mission is to introduce the world to Christ as Lord, Savior, and Judge—not to impose biblical morality, which properly follows after conversion to Christ. unit #28
  12. Evangelicals should be known for proclaiming the gospel with love, clarity, conviction, and boldness, but are instead being resented for political activism. unit #29
  13. Political and moral activism may produce temporal benefits, but like physical exercise, they have no eternal value; only the gospel can rescue sinners, and hell will be populated with moral people who never came to Christ. unit #30

0 · MacArthur frames the sermon around his recent interviews and introduces Paul's statement in 1 Corinthians 2:2 as the defining text for Christian purpose

As some of you know, I've done a number of recent interviews and people have asked me all kinds of questions. Some of them you no doubt have seen some of those interviews. I've been focusing on the fact that what Paul said in 1 Corinthians 2:2 I am determined to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and Him crucified, is a very clear statement for Christians as to their purpose in the world. It is a statement that narrows everything down.

1 · MacArthur expounds on Paul's use of the word 'determined,' emphasizing that maintaining gospel focus requires intentional effort and resistance against distraction

I am determined, he says. That indicates to us he has to fight to maintain this focus. I am determined to know nothing among you but Christ and Him crucified. It's him saying, I don't want to get off message. It's always about Christ and Him crucified. This is our calling as a church in the world.

2 · MacArthur asserts that the church's failure to maintain gospel focus is not a new problem but a recurring pattern throughout church history

But the church has always struggled to stay on message. Its history is ample evidence of that.

3 · MacArthur illustrates the church's historical struggle to stay on message by pointing to mainline denominations a century ago that abandoned the gospel for the social gospel

You could just go a hundred years ago and you would arrive at a time in the church life in the United States of America when the mainline denominations of this nation, many of them very wealthy and powerful, with thousands and thousands of churches and all kinds of institutions, had basically abandoned Christ and Him crucified. They had been sucked into all kinds of social issues, and Christianity was almost synonymous with what was called the social gospel. They were concerned about social things rather than spiritual things. There was an attack on the inerrancy of Scripture, the inspiration of Scripture, the deity of Christ, the doctrine of the Trinity, salvation by grace through faith alone and Christ alone. These attacks were literally coming from those very denominations that declared themselves once to be Christian denominations, and they were content to fight every temporal social battle in place of the one reason for which they existed in the world, and that was the gospel.

4 · MacArthur continues the historical illustration, showing how the fundamentalist movement arose as a corrective response to the social gospel's abandonment of core doctrine

As a result of that, there was a group of men who got together and they wrote several volumes titled the Fundamentals. This is a group of Christian scholars who said, we've got to go back to the fundamental. They had a great impact, and it led to a movement called fundamentalism. It went on for a while, and then eventually it sort of morphed into what we today call evangelicalism, which is, strangely enough, very hard to define.

5 · MacArthur transitions from historical illustration to biblical exposition, examining the origin of the term 'Christian' in Acts 11:26

But we are looking at a day in the history of the church when Christianity as such is seen for something that it is not. And we need to get back to being seen for what we are. And I think I can make that pretty clear to you by just a couple of words. Let's take Christian. In the Book of Acts, chapter 11 and verse 26, it says, and the disciples were first called Christians. Now, that was not a title of honor. I mean, it was for them, but it wasn't intended that way. When the society identified the disciples of our Lord as Christians, that was full of ridicule, scorn, mockery. It was a term of derision. To be called a Christian, to be so completely identified with Christ was considered by the populace as a mark of severe intellectual inferiority. You were a lowlife. It was not intended to be an honorable term. Christianus, little Christs, what a joke. The fact that it was a term of scorn shows up in the 26th chapter of Acts.

6 · MacArthur continues his exposition by examining Acts 26:28, interpreting Agrippa's statement as mockery rather than genuine interest

You can look at it if you want, but I'll read verse 28. Paul is talking to King Agrippa. And Agrippa says to Paul, in a short time, you will persuade me to become a Christian. Now, you might read that and say, wow, Paul was having success with Agrippa. Maybe he was actually thinking about becoming a Christian. Well, if you thought that, you missed the interpretation. He is mocking Paul. He is saying to Paul, in a short time, you will persuade me to become a Christian. Full of scorn, ridicule, mockery and shame. Are you a. Are you making a joke of me? You think I'm that kind of fool? That you could so easily talk me into being one who has received the scorn and ridicule of this culture?

7 · MacArthur completes his exposition of the term 'Christian' by examining 1 Peter 4:16, where Peter acknowledges that suffering is inherent to the Christian identity and instructs believers not to be ashamed but to glorify God in that name

In First Peter, Chapter 4, Peter makes reference to this use of Christian as well. He says, if anyone suffers as a Christian, may. I mean, it was almost built in that if you were identified as a Christian, you would suffer because the very term was filled with disrespect. If anyone suffers as a Christian, he is not to be ashamed. Don't be ashamed. They say that about you to make you ashamed. Don't be ashamed, but glorify God in this name. Turn it on them. They call you a Christian to shame you. You turn it on them and you Accept that identification and give glory to that name.

8 · MacArthur laments the dilution of the term 'Christian' from a badge of identification with the rejected Christ to a generic cultural label

I wish it was still clear that the term Christian was so totally identified with Jesus Christ, the one whom the world rejects, that it was used as a term of derision so that Christians would understand the hostility against Christ and their responsibility to bring the message of Christ and flip that use and show them the glory of Christ. But Christian has just become another lowercase C word in the dictionary.

9 · MacArthur expounds on the term 'evangelical,' tracing it to the Greek word euangelizo and establishing its definition as one who preaches the gospel

Now, I don't mind being called an evangelical. I've been called that. I've been called worse things. But I don't mind being called an evangelical because that's a really good word, too. It's the word euongalidzo in Greek, and it means to preach the gospel. That's what it means, to preach the gospel. Look at 1 Corinthians, chapter 9 and verse 16. Just as an illustration of the significance of this term. Paul says, for if I preach the gospel, there it is. You, Angelizo. I have nothing to boast of, for I am under compulsion. For woe is me if I do not preach the gospel. So what is an evangelical? Somebody who, what, preaches the gospel. That's an evangelical. And Paul says, that's what I do. That's my calling. And woe is me if I don't do that.

10 · MacArthur pivots from defining 'Christian' and 'evangelical' to demonstrating what gospel preaching looks like in practice

Now, what does that mean? That means that the narrow focus of Christians in the world is to be identified with Christ, and that is the fullness of Christ in the Scriptures as he is revealed, and we are to be identified as those who preach the gospel of Christ. What does that look like? Well, let's go to the 10th chapter of Acts, and we have an illustration of it.

11 · MacArthur expounds Acts 10:33-35, establishing the context of Peter's sermon to Cornelius and focusing on Peter's opening declaration of God's universal salvation

Here is Peter, and he is speaking to a Gentile named Cornelius. Cornelius has been desiring to know what the message is that the apostle has. And in chapter 10 of Acts and verse 33, Cornelius says, I sent for you immediately, and you've been kind enough to come. Now then, we're all here, Cornelius and his friends, and we're present before God to hear all that you have been commanded by the Lord. Tell us what God wants us to know. We're gathered together. Give us the message from heaven. What is it? Verse 34. You have a response. Opening his mouth, Peter said, I most certainly understand now that God is not one to show partiality, but in every nation, the man who fears him and does what is right is welcome to him. Now remember, this is a Gentile. And Peter is saying, okay, I get it. Here's the message from heaven. God is not limited to the Jews. His salvation is not limited to them. God is not one to show partiality. But in every nation, the man who fears him and does what is right is welcome to him. There's no such thing as national conversion. It's all personal. It's in every nation the man who fears him.

12 · MacArthur continues his exposition of Acts 10, focusing on verse 36, where Peter declares that peace with God comes exclusively through Jesus Christ, who is Lord of all

God is offering a universal salvation to everyone in every nation. And what is the method of that? What is the message that leads to that salvation? Verse 36. The word which he sent to the sons of Israel, that is the original coming of Christ and the opening of the Gospel. The original message which came first to Israel and now is extended to the Gentiles, is that peace comes through Jesus Christ. That's the message. And who is He? He's Lord of all. This is the message. This is so direct. Cornelius, a Gentile, says, what does heaven want me to know? He wants you to know that salvation is not limited to Israel, but that any person from any nation who fears God does what is right is welcome to him. But the way to come to him is through Christ, because peace with God is through Jesus Christ, and Jesus Christ is Lord of all.

You know the story of Jesus. Peter reminds them who came with Holy Spirit power, did good, healed, overpowered demons, was crucified, rose again, appeared to many after his death.

14 · MacArthur expounds Acts 10:42-43, where Peter declares Christ as appointed judge and proclaims that everyone who believes in Him receives forgiveness of sins

And then in verse 42, and he ordered us to preach to the people and solemnly to testify that this is the one who has been appointed by God as judge of the living and the dead. He is not only Lord of all, he is not only the one who alone can make peace With God. He is the judge of the living and the dead. It is of him all the prophets bear witness that through his name, everyone who believes in him receives forgiveness of sins. That is the marvelous conclusion that you have with Peter's restatement of the gospel concerning Christ. Believe in him and have your sins forgiven. That's the message of the gospel. The gospel means good news. The good news is he is Lord of all, he is judge. And if you're going to be right with God, you have to make peace with God through him. And if you don't make peace with God through Him, you will be judged. But if you believe in him, you will receive the forgiveness of sins.

15 · MacArthur brings Peter's gospel proclamation full circle back to Paul's statement in 1 Corinthians 2:2, arguing that both apostles demonstrate the same singular focus on the gospel despite countless potential distractions

That's the message from heaven. Colonel Nilius says, what did God tell you to tell us? What's the message? It's the gospel. I love the simplicity of that. That's like Paul. I've been thinking about it in the endless cacophony of political harangue going on in our country, which seems to have sucked up so many, quote, unquote, evangelicals to the degree that when I do these interviews, people ask me things like, what do you think of Christian nationalism? 17 · MacArthur diagnoses the contemporary problem: evangelicals are perceived and resented not for their identification with Christ or gospel proclamation, but for political activism and moral crusading

Sadly, evangelicals are seen as a conservative political movement, sort of set against the established power of liberalism and progressivism. We are being accused of being a kind of revolutionary group that has political objectives, objectives for power, so that we can take control of. We're accused of trying to take over the American government by political means to install morality as the traditional Judeo Christian morality, as the dominant position in society. So we are being basically rejected, not for our identification with Christ, not for preaching the gospel, but for traditional morality and political ambition.

18 · MacArthur acknowledges that Christians should expect rejection and persecution, but pivots to the critical question: for what reason should Christians be rejected? This claim sets up his argument that Christians are currently being rejected for the wrong reasons

Now, look, we're not surprised to be rejected. Yes, our Lord said we would be hated. He said we would be persecuted. He said we would be rejected. He said we would be jailed looking at Christian history, he said, and we would even be killed. The question is, for what? For what?

20 · MacArthur continues his exposition of John 15, emphasizing that persecution should result from identification with Christ's name and gospel message

When you're persecuted, it's going to be because the name of Christ means something. You're being persecuted for my name, for you're identifying with me and my message of the gospel. 21 · MacArthur completes his exposition of John 15, identifying the root cause of the world's hatred: Christ exposed their sin through His message and works

Because, as he says earlier in the Gospel of John, because I told them their deeds were evil. Really summing it up, verse 25. They hated me without a cause. They hated me without a cause. 22 · MacArthur applies his exposition of John 15 to the contemporary situation, asserting that evangelicals are being resented for political activism rather than gospel proclamation

Evangelicals today are being resented for the wrong reason. We're being resented for our efforts in the political arena, efforts at societal morality, political power. This is why they accuse us of Christian nationalism, like trying to take over the government.

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the church's apostolic pattern of singular focus on Christ crucified—from the foundation of the gospel's power to transform sinners, through the cost of faithful proclamation, to the mission field before us.

Monday 1 Corinthians 9:16

Paul's declaration—'Woe to me if I do not preach the gospel'—reveals that apostolic urgency was not optional zeal but compulsion born of divine calling. When the church loses this singular focus, it does not drift into neutrality but into competing missions; Paul's fierce words remind us that any substitute for gospel proclamation, however noble, represents a departure from what makes us faithful witnesses.

Tuesday Matthew 23

In Matthew 23, Jesus reserves His most devastating rebuke not for the openly sinful but for the scrupulous moral guardians of His day—those who tithed mint and cummin yet neglected justice and mercy. This passage shatters the assumption that moral correctness and religious observance constitute righteousness; only identification with Christ through repentance and faith restores us to God, while even exemplary morality apart from Him incurs judgment.

Wednesday Acts 10:36-43

Peter's proclamation to Cornelius—declaring Jesus as 'Lord of all,' announcing peace through the gospel, and testifying that all the prophets bear witness to Christ's power to forgive sins—shows no divided attention toward political influence or moral legislation. His message centers entirely on Christ as Savior and Judge; we are called to embody that same undivided allegiance in our witness, recognizing that the culture's transformation follows conversion, never precedes it.

Thursday John 15:18-21

Jesus prepares His followers for hatred from the world, yet His comfort rests not on avoiding persecution but on clarity about its source: we are hated because we belong to Him and do not belong to the world. The shame we bear must be for Christ's sake and the gospel's sake, not for political ambition; this passage calls us to examine whether our rejection flows from faithful proclamation or from worldly entanglement.

Friday Acts 10:42-43

Peter declares that Christ commanded the apostles to proclaim that He 'is the one ordained by God as judge of the living and the dead' and that through His name everyone who believes receives forgiveness—a message of judgment and mercy offered universally. We must approach those who disagree with us not as foes to be defeated politically but as sinners whom Christ came to save; our enemy is not the unbeliever but the spiritual darkness that blinds them, and the gospel alone possesses power to open their eyes.

Memory verse this week

1 Corinthians 2:2

For I decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified.

Why this verse: This verse is Paul's crystalline statement of the church's singular focus and becomes the sermon's foundational text for all that follows. MacArthur uses it to establish that the church's sole calling is gospel proclamation, not political activism or moral reform—making it the essential anchor for remembering that Christ crucified, not cultural power, is our message.

Sunday-evening family table

What Are We Known For?

For the parent

This prompt invites your family to think about reputation and identity—what MacArthur emphasized as the difference between being known *for Christ* versus being known *for other causes*. Listen for whether your kids distinguish between doing good things and being identified with Jesus Himself.

If someone who doesn't know Jesus watched our church or our family for a week, what would they think we care about most? What do you think Jesus would want them to notice about us?
works for ages 7+ — younger children may need help articulating their thoughts, but the question invites honest observation and reflection
Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. What does Paul mean when he declares in 1 Corinthians 2:2 that he resolved to know nothing except Jesus Christ and Him crucified? What would it look like for him to know 'something else' instead?
    1 Corinthians 2:2
    → How might the Corinthian church have been tempted to dilute or supplement this singular focus with other pursuits or priorities?
  2. The sermon argues that the church throughout history has repeatedly abandoned its focus on proclaiming Christ in favor of social reform and political activism. Where do you see this pattern playing out in the evangelical church today, and what makes this shift so tempting?
  3. Jesus pronounced His harshest condemnation on the morally religious Pharisees while welcoming wretched sinners (Matthew 9:13). What does this pattern reveal about God's posture toward morality apart from faith in Christ?
    Matthew 9:13
    → Why might the church be so prone to assume that moral reform is the same thing as gospel advancement?
  4. The sermon contends that morality without genuine relationship with God cannot avert divine judgment. What's the difference between a person who lives righteously because they have been transformed by the gospel and a person who lives righteously through cultural pressure or political enforcement?
  5. According to Acts 10:42-43, Peter's proclamation moves from Christ as Judge to Christ as the one through whom forgiveness of sins is given. How does understanding Christ's role as both Judge and Savior reshape the way we engage people who oppose our political or moral positions?
    Acts 10:42-43
    → What would change in our conversations with unbelievers if we genuinely saw them as the mission field rather than the enemy?
  6. The sermon suggests that evangelicals today are being rejected not for identifying with Christ and the gospel, but for political activism. If that's true, what would it mean for our church to reorient ourselves so that people primarily know us for proclaiming Christ with love, clarity, conviction, and boldness?
Couples · three questions over coffee

Christ Crucified: Our Singular Witness Together

  1. What conviction did the sermon surface in your heart about where we may have drifted from simply proclaiming Christ—and how might that conviction shape what you need from me this week?
  2. As a couple, are we known more for our political convictions or our gospel proclamation? Where might we need to repent together of substituting activism for the boldness of simply naming Jesus as Lord and Savior?
  3. How can we pray for one another to have courage this week to speak Christ crucified to someone in our circle—and to see people around us not as enemies but as the mission field God has placed before us?
