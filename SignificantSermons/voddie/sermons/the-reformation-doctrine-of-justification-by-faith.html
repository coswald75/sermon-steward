The Reformation Doctrine of Justification by Faith

Romans 3:21-26 Voddie Baucham
Thesis Justification is by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone, to the glory of God alone, as taught by Scripture alone — the five solas of the Reformation that stand against any system requiring human works for salvation.

Theological claims· 11
  1. The practice of selling indulgences rests on the grotesque, unbiblical doctrine of purgatory, which denies that believers can be fully justified. unit #5
  2. Righteousness required for salvation means perfect law-keeping in word, thought, and deed, both inwardly and outwardly. unit #8
  3. Human beings cannot achieve the perfect righteousness God requires because they are born in sin and incapable of perfect law-keeping in word, thought, and deed. unit #9
  4. Scripture teaches justification by faith alone, not by doing your best in this life and suffering in purgatory to complete what is lacking. unit #10
  5. Justification is by grace alone, meaning it is a gift that cannot be earned — if you earn it, it is wages, not grace, and there is nothing to boast about when receiving a gift. unit #15
  6. Justification is by the grace of God alone with no human contribution, and the Roman Catholic Church preaches another gospel by requiring temporal punishment in purgatory even after justification, condemning those who believe grace alone is sufficient. unit #16
  7. Justification is through Christ alone because only the atoning work of Christ can satisfy the righteous wrath of God against sin. unit #17
  8. Propitiation from God's wrath is necessary, and we must discuss it because Scripture does. unit #20
  9. Understanding God's mercy requires understanding the wrath we deserve, and facing our guilt and incapacity without hope drives us to the only answer: grace alone, faith alone, in Christ alone. unit #22
  10. Justification comes through the double imputation of Christ's active obedience (His perfect righteousness imputed to us) and passive obedience (our sin laid upon Him and nailed to the cross). unit #25
  11. Christ purchased us by His blood, paying the price we owed and could never pay ourselves, accomplishing justification by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone. unit #26

25 · Introduces the doctrine of double imputation through Christ's active and passive obedience: Christ's virgin birth freed Him from Adamic sin, His active obedience (perfect law-keeping) provides righteousness imputed to us, and His passive obedience (bearing our sin on the cross) removes our guilt — together accomplishing justification

26 · Synthesizes the three solas covered so far into a single declaration and introduces the language of purchase and payment, framing the atonement as a transaction where Christ pays a debt we could not pay

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through the Reformation doctrine of justification by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone — beginning with Abraham's exemplary faith, moving through God's wrath and our incapacity, and culminating in Christ's atoning work that accomplishes what we cannot.

Monday Genesis 15:6

Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness — not because of his deeds, but because of his trust in God's promise. In this single verse, the entire Reformation doctrine finds its Old Testament anchor: justification comes through faith, not through human achievement or the accumulation of works. We, like Abraham, are justified not by what we do but by what we trust God to do.

Tuesday Romans 1:18

God's wrath is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness — and this is not a side doctrine but the essential context for grasping grace. Until we feel the weight of divine wrath against sin, mercy remains cheap sentiment rather than scandalous gift. The gospel humbles us as we grasp that we are the targets of this wrath, and only in that terror do we run to Christ.

Wednesday 2 Corinthians 5:21

He made Him who knew no sin to be sin on our behalf, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him — the cosmic exchange at the heart of justification. Christ takes our guilt and death; we receive His perfect obedience and standing before the Father. In this single verse, the double imputation stands complete: our sin flows to Him, His righteousness flows to us, and the justice of God is perfectly satisfied.

Thursday Mark 10:45

The Son of Man came to give His life a ransom for many — a price paid, a debt satisfied, a purchase completed. We could never accumulate the merit or endure the punishment our sin demands; Christ alone could and did. Grace means this ransom was given freely on our behalf, not because we earned it but because He chose to pay what we owed.

Friday Romans 9:30-32

Israel pursued righteousness as if by works, but stumbled over the stone — the tragic irony of trying to earn what God offers as a gift. We, like Israel, stand incapable of the perfect law-keeping God requires; yet faith alone unites us to Christ, who fulfilled the law in our place. The five solas stand together: by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone, for God's glory alone, as Scripture alone declares.

Sunday-evening family table

Can We Ever Be Good Enough?

For the parent

This prompt anchors in the sermon's central claim: that perfect law-keeping is impossible for human beings, which is why grace through faith is our only hope. Listen for how your children understand their own inability and God's provision — this opens the door to gratitude for what Christ has done.

Pastor Voddie talked about how God requires perfect righteousness — perfect in our words, our thoughts, and our actions. Do you think anyone in our family, or anyone you know, has ever been perfect in all three of those things, even for one whole day? What happens when we realize we can't do it?
works for ages 7+ — younger children can share observations about imperfection; older kids and teens can wrestle with the weight of the standard and why grace becomes necessary
Couples · three questions over coffee

Grace Alone, Faith Alone—Together

  1. When you heard that justification is a gift we cannot earn, what stirred in your heart—relief, conviction, wonder, or something else?
  2. How do we subtly slip into earning God's favor in our marriage—through performance, conditional love, or unspoken debts—rather than resting in the grace that justifies us both?
  3. What is one area where your spouse needs to hear and believe that they are fully accepted in Christ, not because of what they do but because of what He has done—and how can you pray that truth over them this week?
Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. In Romans 3:21-26, Paul says righteousness comes apart from the law, yet the law and the prophets testify to it. What do you think Paul means by this apparent paradox, and why does he emphasize that this righteousness is revealed now?
    Romans 3:21
    → How does this help us understand why the Roman Catholic system of earning righteousness through works actually misses what Scripture itself has been pointing toward all along?
  2. The sermon emphasizes that the righteousness God requires means perfect law-keeping in word, thought, and deed—both inwardly and outwardly. When you honestly examine your own life, what does this standard reveal about your condition before God?
    Romans 3:22-23
    → Rather than trying to improve your record, what happens in your heart when you realize you cannot achieve this perfection on your own?
  3. Paul describes justification as a gift of grace (Romans 3:24). The sermon notes that if something is earned, it becomes wages rather than grace—there is nothing to boast about in receiving a gift. Why is this distinction so important for understanding what it means to be justified?
    Romans 3:24
    → Can you think of a time when you were tempted to take credit for your own spiritual progress, and what shifted your perspective back to grace?
  4. The sermon teaches that propitiation—God's wrath being satisfied—is central to justification. Looking at Romans 3:25-26 and the cross-reference in Isaiah 53:6, how does understanding that Christ bore God's wrath on our behalf change the way you see your own standing before God?
    Isaiah 53:6; Romans 3:25-26
  5. The doctrine of double imputation means Christ's perfect righteousness is credited to us, and our sin is imputed to Him on the cross. How does grasping this exchange—His active obedience for our failure, His passive suffering for our guilt—reshape your gratitude and your willingness to follow Him this week?
    2 Corinthians 5:21
    → What would it look like in a specific situation you're facing to respond to someone out of joy at this exchange rather than out of obligation?
  6. The sermon presents the five solas—grace alone, faith alone, Christ alone, Scripture alone, glory to God alone—as a unified whole, each protecting the others. Which of these solas do you find yourself most tempted to compromise, and what does that reveal about where you're still seeking righteousness apart from Christ?
