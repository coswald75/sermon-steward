The Sufficiency of Scripture

2 Timothy 3:1-5, 10-17 September 1, 1992 James Boice
Thesis The God-breathed Scriptures are sufficient for all ministry and life, requiring no supplementation from contemporary methodologies, and cultural transformation occurs not through political power or entertainment strategies but through the faithful proclamation of the Word that changes hearts from the bottom up.

Theological claims· 11
  1. The Inerrancy Council's work defending biblical inerrancy from 1978-1988 was important and necessary, producing creedal documents and conducting nationwide conferences to recall evangelicals to the historic high view of Scripture. unit #5
  2. The urgent issue facing evangelicals today is not the inerrancy of Scripture but its sufficiency—while evangelicals affirm the Bible's truthfulness, they functionally deny its adequacy for evangelism, sanctification, and cultural transformation, seeking instead sociological techniques, special methods, and political power. unit #6
  3. The worst condition Paul describes in 2 Timothy 3:5 is not found in the pagan world but in the visible church—people who maintain the outward form of godliness (worship, creeds, religious activity) while denying its power because they lack the Holy Spirit. unit #10
  4. Scripture is sufficient for ministry in the last days because it is God-breathed—possessing the same creative power by which God spoke the cosmos into existence—and therefore accomplishes the miracle of bringing people from death to life and from ignorance to truth. unit #14
  5. Contemporary evangelism methods that rely on demographic surveys, entertainment-focused services, and theatrical effects represent a functional denial of Scripture's sufficiency, treating evangelism as a marketing problem requiring sociological techniques rather than as a work accomplished through the proclaimed Word. unit #17
  6. Entertainment-driven church growth produces large gatherings but not genuine conversions, because while the world will come for what it wants, authentic gospel work happens only through God's converting power operating through the proclaimed Word. unit #18
  7. Contemporary churches treat sanctification as requiring psychological analysis and therapeutic intervention rather than biblical categories of sin and faith, adopting culture's language and functionally denying Scripture's sufficiency for growth in godliness. unit #32
  8. Contemporary churches approach sanctification through either legalistic formulas (Bible reading, prayer, witnessing without understanding) or charismatic experientialism (second blessing, tongues, miracles), but neither approach produces genuine holiness because both bypass biblical doctrine. unit #33
  9. Sanctification is fundamentally a matter of doctrine—poor behavior in the contemporary church results from failure to understand and mentally grasp what God has accomplished, with holy action flowing from doctrinal comprehension rather than from formulas or experiences. unit #36
  10. Political engagement seeking to elect Christians to power and impose Christian laws mistakes the function of civil government—which requires competence, not Christianity—for the church's spiritual work, confusing two distinct spheres of authority. unit #41
  11. Cultural transformation occurs from the bottom up through gospel conversion of individuals who then create laws reflecting God's law, not through top-down political power—seeking change through government makes the state an idolatrous savior, displacing God's saving work through the proclaimed Word. unit #42

Monday Romans 1

Paul opens Romans by anchoring the gospel's power not in human persuasion but in God's self-revelation through the Word—the same Word that accomplishes salvation, from death to life and ignorance to truth. We need not supplement Scripture with techniques or strategies; the gospel itself carries the efficacious power of the all-glorious God who spoke creation into being.

Tuesday Mark 1:21-22

Notice the contrast: Jesus taught with authority because His words flowed from union with the Father and the Spirit's power, while the scribes—religious professionals maintaining the forms of piety—lacked that transforming authority. The contemporary church often mirrors the scribes' condition, preserving creeds and worship while functionally denying the Spirit's power to change hearts through the proclaimed Word.

Wednesday Mark 1:37-38

When the crowds pressed Jesus to stay and capitalize on His popularity through more healings and signs, He redirected toward His true calling: proclamation of the gospel in the neighboring towns. We face the same temptation—to abandon the hard, slow work of preaching God's Word for methods that draw crowds and feel more immediately successful. But genuine conversion happens only through the proclaimed Word, not through entertainment or therapeutic appeal.

Thursday Romans 6:11

Paul exhorts us to reckon ourselves dead to sin and alive to Christ—a call grounded in doctrinal understanding of our union with Christ in His death and resurrection. Contemporary churches often replace this mental grasping of doctrine with legalistic disciplines or charismatic experiences, but genuine growth in godliness requires that we grasp with our minds what Christ has done and live accordingly.

Friday John 6

When the crowds wanted to make Jesus king and impose His rule through political means, He withdrew and redirected attention to the true bread from heaven—conversion through faith in His Person and work. We must recover this vision: real cultural change flows from individual hearts transformed by the gospel, not from Christians wielding political power or adopting the world's methodologies to advance the kingdom.

Memory verse this week

2 Timothy 3:16-17

All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work.

Why this verse: This verse is the theological capstone of Boice's entire sermon—it establishes Scripture's sufficiency by grounding it in God's very breath and demonstrates that the Word alone equips believers for every ministry and life task, requiring no supplementation from cultural methodologies or contemporary techniques. Memorizing this verse anchors the congregation in the Reformation principle of sola scriptura and guards against the functional denial of Scripture's adequacy that Boice identifies as the urgent evangelical problem.
