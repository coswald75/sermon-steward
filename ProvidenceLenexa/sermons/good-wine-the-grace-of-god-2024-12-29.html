Good Wine & the Grace of God

December 29, 2024 Pastor Dov Cohen
Thesis Jesus's first sign at Cana—transforming water into wine—reveals that his New Covenant salvation is a finished, complete, effectual work of grace that replaces the barren, repetitive purification rites of the Old Covenant, calling us to live in settled security in God's acceptance and serve this good wine of salvation to others.

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the arc of grace from Christ's sovereign transformation of the old covenant into the new, through the finished sufficiency of his salvation, to our grateful response of worship and obedience born from freedom rather than self-effort.

Monday Genesis 1

Creation declares God's dominion over all things—He spoke and matter obeyed. In Cana, Jesus exercises that same creative power, revealing that He who commanded the heavens and earth to exist now stands present in history as the eternal Word made flesh. We behold in this sign the authentication of his deity and his authority over the physical order.

Tuesday Hebrews 9:26

The old covenant required endless ritual—water poured again and again, never fully cleansing the conscience. But Christ has appeared once, at the culmination of the ages, to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself. The sign at Cana points to this glorious reality: where the law offered repetition and incompleteness, grace offers finality and full efficacy through Christ's singular, perfect work.

Wednesday Galatians 2:16

No one is justified by works of the law—this is the beating heart of Paul's gospel declaration. Yet many of us, though saved by grace, drift into justifying ourselves through obedience and spiritual performance. When we grasp that Christ's finished work alone secures our standing before God, the motive for obedience shifts radically: we no longer obey to earn righteousness, but from the overflow of hearts already made righteous in Him.

Thursday Ephesians 1:3-8

We have been blessed with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ—redemption through his blood, forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace. The wine of Cana points to this overwhelming abundance: not meager provision, not rationed mercy, but grace poured out in extravagant measure. This is the character of our Father's covenant with us, sealed in Christ.

Friday Psalm 4:7

You have put more joy in my heart than they have when their grain and wine abound. The psalmist testifies that the gladness Christ gives surpasses earthly abundance—and that joy is meant to overflow in gratitude. As we drink the wine of Christ's finished salvation, we are compelled to rejoice and give thanks, not grimly, but as those who have tasted the goodness of the Lord and find it sufficient for every season of life.

Prayer for Grace Over Self-Effort

Father, we come before you in awe of Jesus, who transformed the ordinary into the extraordinary and replaced our weary striving with the wine of his finished salvation. We marvel that he alone holds dominion over nature and time, that he accomplished once for all what we could never achieve through endless repetition and self-effort (Hebrews 9:26). Yet we confess how easily we slip back into the old way—laboring to justify ourselves, trying to earn your favor through our obedience, as though the water of our works could make us righteous. We admit our total depravity and our deep inclination to trust in ourselves rather than in Christ's free grace. Forgive us for this fundamental unbelief.

In the gospel, we have been given something infinitely better than what our hands could ever produce. Christ drank the cup of God's wrath on our behalf, and in exchange he offers us the full, rich, effectual grace of his life, death, and resurrection—grace that is complete and requires nothing from us but faith (Galatians 2:16). The barren, repetitive rites have given way to his vibrant, living covenant. We are loved not because of what we do, but because of what he has done.

Grant us, O Lord, the freedom to rest in your loving acceptance in Christ, so that our obedience flows not from fear or self-justification, but from worship and love (John 1:14). Teach us to examine our hearts: are we obeying to earn your favor, or are we obeying because we have already been lavishly accepted? Fill us with such deep gratitude for your kindness that our rejoicing becomes the anchor of our souls even in difficulty. Make us a people marked by grace-enabled gladness, living in the freedom that only the gospel brings.

To you, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, be all glory and honor. We commit ourselves to drink deeply of Christ's free grace and to love one another out of the overflow of souls set free.
