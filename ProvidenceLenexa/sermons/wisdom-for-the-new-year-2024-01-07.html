Wisdom for the New Year

January 7, 2024 Pastor Dov Cohen
Thesis In light of God's eternality and our brevity, we must pray for and pursue lives that number our days with wisdom, satisfy our souls with God's steadfast love, and invite God to establish the work of our hands for His kingdom.

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how God's sovereign work in history shapes our present sanctification—from creation's foundation through the wilderness pattern to our call for intentional growth in Christ.

Monday Genesis 1:1

The opening declaration—'In the beginning, God'—establishes what Psalm 90 assumes: that before the mountains were born and before time itself existed, the Lord reigned in eternal power. This is not abstract theology but the bedrock that makes our prayer to 'number our days' meaningful; we petition a God whose creative word sustains every moment we live.

Tuesday Exodus 14

At the Red Sea, Israel stands between death and deliverance, helpless to save themselves yet compelled to move forward in faith. This is the wilderness pattern Psalm 90 describes—not chaos, but purposeful trekking under God's hand. We too walk between the old self and our final inheritance, learning in the crossing that our days have meaning precisely because they are steps toward the Lord's promised rest.

Wednesday 2 Peter 3:8

Peter's reminder awakens us to the fact that our frantic numbering of days reflects a fear that time is slipping away, yet the Lord's patient sovereignty operates on a wholly different scale. This does not excuse laziness but liberates us from the tyranny of mere chronology; our sanctification unfolds in God's time, not our panic, and each intentional step—whether visible progress or hidden faithfulness—counts in His reckoning.

Thursday Exodus 19-20

At Mount Sinai, the Lord gives Israel the law not as arbitrary burden but as the pattern for a people set apart to Him. Similarly, to 'number our days' in wisdom means identifying specific areas where God is calling us to grow—prayer, generosity, patience, witness—and then seeking the relationships and resources that transform intention into action. The covenant God makes with us in Christ demands our whole heart and soul.

Friday Exodus 2-40

Moses's forty-year leadership shows a man who prayed for Israel's deliverance yet labored tirelessly to lead them, administer justice, and build a nation. His life answers the question: how do we number our days? Not by passively hoping God will change us while we coast, but by naming our growth-goals, seeking mentors and resources, refusing compromise—all while knowing that genuine transformation flows from grace, not mere effort. The new year invites us into that same earnest partnership with God.

Numbering Our Days with Intentional Grace

Father, we come before you in awe of your eternal nature—you who dwell in unapproachable light, before whom a thousand years are like a day that has just passed (Psalm 90:4). You are our dwelling place in all generations, sovereign over all things, and we rest in your unchanging character even as our days are swift and our strength fades. We praise you that in Christ we are not wanderers without hope, but your people being led through the wilderness toward the promised land of your eternal kingdom.

Yet we confess, O Lord, that we often drift through our days without intention or vigilance. We coast, we waste hours in pursuits that do not advance your kingdom or our sanctification, and we fail to number our days with the fear and wisdom you call us to embrace. We ask for forgiveness for the times we treat our fleeting years as though we have endless time, neglecting the specific areas where you are calling us to grow in Christlikeness. Help us to see the preciousness of each day as a gift held in your sovereign hand.

In the gospel we have been given not only the assurance of your presence but the promise that you will complete the good work you have begun in us (Philippians 1:6). Through Christ's finished work, we are freed from the tyranny of sin and death, and empowered by your Spirit to pursue genuine transformation. The gospel humbles us as we grasp that our growth is always dependent on your grace, yet it also ignites in us the glad desire to cooperate with what you are doing—to pray earnestly for change while working with all our heart and soul to align our lives with your Word.

Grant us, we pray, the grace to identify with specificity the areas where you are calling us to grow this year. Give us wisdom to seek the resources, counsel, and community we need to support that growth, and give us the courage to refuse the comfort of coasting. Help us to saturate our plans with prayer, knowing that our genuine intercession should move us to action done in reliance upon you. As we number our days, may our hearts be satisfied with your steadfast love each morning, and may the favor of the Lord our God be upon us to establish the work of our hands (Psalm 90:14, 17). To you alone be the glory, now and forever.
