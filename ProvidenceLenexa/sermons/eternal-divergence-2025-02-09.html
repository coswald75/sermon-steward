Thesis Jesus draws an absolute line between light and darkness, freedom and slavery, children of God and children of the devil — and the health of your soul is revealed by how eagerly you long for his return.

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through the spiritual tests that confirm our salvation, deepen our grasp of Christ's sufficiency, guard us against the enemy's schemes, and compel us toward holiness and eager expectation of Christ's return.

Monday 1 John 1:9

John writes that confession of sin is inseparable from our claim to know Christ — it is one of the tests by which we verify our salvation is real. When we confess sin to God, we are not earning His favor but exercising the very faith that proves His grace has transformed us. This simple act of honest acknowledgment becomes evidence that the Spirit dwells within us, working repentance and restoration.

Tuesday Isaiah 9:2

Isaiah prophesied of a great light breaking into darkness, and Jesus claimed to be that light (John 8:12). To walk in His light is not merely to possess information about Him but to be transferred from the dominion of darkness into the kingdom of His beloved Son — a decisive spiritual repositioning. Those who reject this light remain in darkness by their own choice, unable to see or follow the way of life.

Wednesday Psalm 139:23-24

David's prayer invites God to search his inner life and expose anything in him that leads away from God's way — a posture of humble cooperation with the Holy Spirit's sanctifying work. We, too, must cultivate this openness, allowing God to search our thoughts, motives, and attitudes, knowing that His examination is not punitive but redemptive. This willing vulnerability becomes our protection against self-deception and slow apostasy.

Thursday 1 Peter 5:8

Peter warns that our adversary the devil prowls about like a roaring lion, seeking someone to devour — and he exploits every unguarded thought, unchecked impulse, and tolerated sin as an opening. The gospel does not offer us careless grace but vigilant grace, empowering us to resist at every point where darkness seeks entrance. Our pursuit of holiness, then, is not burdensome striving but warfare — the glad defense of the kingdom Christ has won for us.

Friday Philippians 3:20

Paul calls our citizenship heavenly and reminds us that we eagerly await the Savior's coming from heaven — this longing is the posture of a transformed heart. If our affections remain fixed on this age, if we feel no ache for Christ's appearing, something has drifted in our faith. The gospel calls us to examine ourselves: Do we truly belong to Christ? Let the Spirit awaken in us a fresh hunger for His return.

Pray together this week

Prayer for Spiritual Discernment and Glad Pursuit

Father, we come before you in awe of your character as the God who calls us out of darkness into your marvelous light (Isaiah 9:2). You alone discern the hearts of all people, and you have given us your Spirit to bear witness with our spirits that we are truly your children. We confess that we are prone to spiritual drift, content to claim the name of Christ while our affections wander from him. We acknowledge our weakness in pursuing holiness—how easily we compromise in thought and deed, advancing the enemy's agenda rather than your kingdom. We confess that some of us have grown weary in our expectation of Christ's return, our hearts grown cold to the promise of his appearing.

Yet in the gospel we are reminded that our salvation is entirely the work of Christ and the Spirit, not our own doing. Jesus has conquered the darkness through his death and resurrection; he alone is the true light by which we see clearly. The gospel humbles us as we grasp that every spiritual grace—every capacity to believe, to grow, to persevere—flows from his efficacious work on our behalf (Romans 8:1). We have nothing to boast of except his name.

We ask that you grant us discernment to test ourselves by your Word, that we might celebrate the work of grace you have done and guard against spiritual complacency. Strengthen our desire for Christ's return; kindle in us that blessed hope that marks us as your children. Give us grace to pursue holiness in every dimension—in thought, in attitude, in word and deed—not from fear of judgment but from gratitude for salvation freely given. Grant us courage to endure persecution for righteousness' sake, knowing that all who follow Jesus will face opposition (2 Timothy 3:12, Matthew 5:10-12). Make us a people who examine our souls regularly in light of Scripture, confirming afresh that we are truly saved and loved by you.

Father, receive our praise for the immeasurable grace displayed in Jesus Christ. We commit ourselves together to this glad pursuit of Christlikeness, trusting in your faithfulness to complete the good work you have begun in us.
