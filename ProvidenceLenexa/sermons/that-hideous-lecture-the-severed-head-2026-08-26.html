Honor your father and mother—the fifth commandment assumes a created order where some are placed above others, yet demands that those below give honor upward. This is not oppression; it is the grammar of love itself. When we reject hierarchy categorically, we sever the possibility of honor flowing upward and care flowing downward.

Tuesday 1 Corinthians 12:12-27

Paul does not say the eye and the hand are identical in function; he says they are one body precisely because they are different. Insisting on ontological equality while denying functional distinction does not liberate the body—it paralyzes it. We are equal in worth, unequal in calling, and our flourishing depends on accepting both.

Wednesday Colossians 1:15-20

The elites in our age promote a spiritually empty materialism not by accident but by design—to monopolize power unopposed while Christians abandon the spiritual battle. When Christ is not exalted as head, we lose sight of the demonic opposition to God's order and become susceptible to forces we cannot name. Reclaiming Christ's headship means reclaiming our capacity to see.

Thursday Philippians 2:5-8

Christ did not refuse his place at the right hand of God, nor did he cling to it when the Father called him to empty himself unto death. He is simultaneously the one who holds all authority and the one who washes feet. This paradox—supreme strength united with radical self-giving—is the only model that can heal a broken marriage, a broken church, a broken society.

Friday Revelation 5:11-14

The vision of heaven shows not the absence of order but its perfection: every creature worshipping the Lamb at the center, every angel and elder bowing, every tongue confessing his lordship. This is not the tyranny we fear; it is the deepest freedom. When we submit to Christ's headship, we discover that we have been restored to the order for which we were made.

Sunday-evening family table

Who's the Head of Your Home?

For the parent

This prompt invites kids to think about authority as *care* rather than *control*—and to notice when leaders (at home, school, sports) are actually taking care of those under them. Listen for whether your kids see leadership as something that *costs* the leader, not just something that gives the leader power.

In our house, who takes care of us the most? What do they do that shows they're taking care of us—not just telling us what to do, but actually making sure we have what we need? What would change if they stopped doing those things?
works for ages 6+; younger kids will name a parent and describe concrete care (making meals, helping with homework); older kids can articulate the pattern and notice when leaders fail to care
Couples · three questions over coffee

Hierarchy, Honor, and the Head

  1. When you heard that hierarchy is the shape love takes, not a disease to cure, what shifted in how you think about authority in our marriage?
  2. Where in our relationship do you see me either abdicating headship or resisting submission—and how is Christ calling us both to repent and trust him as the true Head?
  3. What is one way I can honor the calling Christ has given you this week, and how can we pray together that you'll see his authority holding our marriage together?
Memory verse this week

Philippians 2:5-8

Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus, who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross.

Why this verse: This passage embodies the sermon's central claim that Jesus Christ heals broken hierarchies by holding together supreme authority and total submission. Christ's dual posture—refusing to exploit equality while obediently submitting—is the model that makes human hierarchy functional and kind rather than exploitative or usurped.
