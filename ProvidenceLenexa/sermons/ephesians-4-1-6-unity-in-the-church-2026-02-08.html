Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the arc of unity from its theological foundation in Christ's work and God's grace, through the virtues that embody it, to the repentance and remembrance that sustain it in our life together.

Monday Ephesians 1-3

Paul opens his letter by establishing the bedrock on which unity alone can stand: our election in Christ, redemption through His blood, and the sealing of the Holy Spirit (Eph. 1:3–14). Before he calls us to live humbly together, he reminds us that every spiritual blessing is ours because Christ paid our debt. As we grasp the immensity of what we've been forgiven, the natural response is to lay down our grievances with one another.

Tuesday John 13:34-35

Jesus washed the disciples' feet—the work of a servant—and commanded them to love one another as He had loved them. This is no abstract virtue; it is the love of One who bent low to wash the very feet of the man who would betray Him. When we comprehend that our Lord demonstrated humility and patience at such a cost, our reluctance to bear with one another in love reveals how little we've let the gospel reshape our hearts.

Wednesday Colossians 3:12-13

Paul lists the garments we are to wear as God's chosen ones: compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience, and forbearance in love. These are not optional adornments; they are the very fabric of corporate life in Christ. When we see another believer struggle, our first instinct should be to cover them with these virtues—not to expose their weakness or nurse a grievance, but to bear with them as Christ has borne with us.

Thursday Philippians 2

Paul holds up Christ's humiliation—His descent from glory to a criminal's cross—as the paradigm for how we are to regard one another. He calls us to consider others more significant than ourselves and to look to their interests as well as our own. This is the posture that shatters pride and makes true unity possible; it is not something we muster on our own, but the grace-enabled fruit of beholding Christ's humility.

Friday Matthew 18

Jesus teaches that when a brother sins against us, we go to him privately, and if he repents, we forgive him—not seven times, but seventy times seven. The passage invites us to ruthless honesty: are we harboring any ill feeling, nursing any grievance, restraining any affection from someone in our church? Repentance here is not shame-driven but grace-compelled; it always leads to freedom. This week, let the Spirit search your heart and invite you into the reconciliation that unity in Christ makes possible.

Pray together this week

Prayer for Unity Rooted in Grace

Father, we stand amazed at the beauty of Your church — a unity more glorious than any human achievement, a body bound together by the Spirit and the gospel of Your Son. Yet we confess that we often fall short of this calling. We harbor grudges, withhold affection, and struggle to extend the humility, gentleness, and patience that Paul exhorts us to display (Ephesians 4:2–3). We know that our impatience and our failure to bear with one another in love betray a forgetfulness of the overwhelming grace poured out on us through Christ.

Remind us, O God, of the tremendous debt from which we have been freed. Every spiritual blessing — chosen before the foundation of the world, redeemed by the blood of Christ, sealed by the Holy Spirit — has been lavished upon us who were once Your enemies (Ephesians 1:3–14). This grace is not a onetime gift but the present power by which we live. As we remember what Christ has accomplished for us, grant us the grace to extend the same forbearance toward one another that He has shown to us.

We ask that You would work in us the virtues of humility and patience that flow from the gospel. Where we harbor ill feeling or restraint of affection, grant us repentance that leads to freedom (Matthew 18). Help us to walk eagerly in the unity of the Spirit, bearing with one another in love, so that our corporate worship and witness might display the immeasurable greatness of Your grace in Christ. To Your glory alone, we commit ourselves to this glad pursuit of unity in the body of Christ.

Walking Worthy Together

For the parent

This prompt invites your family to explore what Paul means by 'walking worthy' — living in a way that matches the grace we've received. Listen for moments when family members recognize how God's forgiveness of us frees us to forgive and bear with one another.

In the sermon, Pastor Dov talked about how Paul calls us to 'walk worthy' of the calling we've received — to live in a way that matches the amazing grace God has given us through Jesus. Think about someone in our church family (or our own family) who has been patient with you, or gentle with you, when they could have been harsh. What did that person's kindness feel like?
