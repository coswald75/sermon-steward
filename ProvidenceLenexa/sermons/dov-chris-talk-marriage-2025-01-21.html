January 21, 2025 Pastor Dov Cohen
Thesis Christian husbands are called to steward their marriages with the same sacrificial, sanctifying love Christ shows the church, making their wives the clearest human priority while orienting the marriage toward a mission that glorifies God and serves His kingdom across generations.

Theological claims· 11
  1. The call for husbands to love like Christ is not generic spiritual language but a specific, weighty, awe-inspiring responsibility to sacrifice for the eternal good of their wives. unit #5
  2. Husbands are called to sacrifice for their wives according to God's will and plan, not merely according to their wives' wishes—sacrificing for them, not to them. unit #6
  3. Husbands should invest sustained, detailed attention in their wives' sanctification—like a restoration project—ultimately presenting them to God as better than they found them. unit #8
  4. A husband cannot properly lead his wife in sanctification without first experiencing and understanding God's patient, gracious work in his own life. unit #9
  5. Understanding God's patient, varied, layered approach to our own sanctification equips us to lead our wives with the same patience, faith, and skillful variety rather than simplistic methods. unit #10
  6. Marriage is designed to reflect the relationship between Christ and the church, making visible the selfless love and sacrifice Jesus shows His bride. unit #11
  7. Marriage must be oriented toward a mission beyond itself—rooted in the creation mandate and fulfilled in Christ and the church—which provides the proper framework for prioritizing sanctification efforts. unit #12
  8. Spousal love must exceed all other human loves in intensity and scope—spouses must be each other's clear number one priority, above children, work, and other legitimate pursuits. unit #14
  9. Husbands are the primary stewards and initiators in marriage, responsible for consistently attending to and maintaining the relationship so their wives can trust their leadership. unit #15
  10. Historically great marriages are among the most impactful and world-changing works average Christians can accomplish, creating security for children and blessing many generations. unit #22
  11. A clear mission beyond self-fulfillment is essential to a great marriage—without it, all practical advice will fail and spouses' weaknesses will feel personal rather than obstacles to the mission. unit #24

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how Christ's sacrificial love for the church becomes the pattern husbands follow in loving their wives, and how that love shapes both personal sanctification and a marriage oriented toward God's eternal purposes.

Monday Ephesians 5:25

Christ's love for the church is not sentimental but redemptive: He gave Himself up for her, surrendering everything to secure her eternal good. When we grasp this as the model for husbands, we see that spousal love demands the same weighty intentionality—not mere feeling but deliberate sacrifice for our wives' sanctification and flourishing in God.

Tuesday 2 Corinthians 3:18

We are all being transformed into Christ's image 'from glory to glory,' a progression that requires time, grace, and the Spirit's patient work. A husband who recognizes this slow, gracious process in his own sanctification becomes equipped to lead his wife with faith and patience rather than demanding instant change, modeling the very transformation he invites her into.

Wednesday John 5:1-9

Jesus does not instantly heal the invalid at the pool; He first asks penetrating questions about the man's will and condition, then provides what he specifically needs. This attentiveness to one particular person's unique struggle mirrors the husband's call to know his wife deeply, attend to her specific growth areas, and shepherd her transformation with skillful, patient care rather than formulaic methods.

Thursday Genesis 1:28 and Genesis 2:24

From the beginning, marriage has been framed not as an end in itself but as a partnership for God's purposes: to multiply and steward His creation. When husbands and wives align their marriage around this transcendent mission—not merely happiness or self-fulfillment—their individual weaknesses become obstacles to overcome together rather than personal affronts, and their sanctification becomes a shared, purposeful work.

Friday 1 Peter 3

Peter exhorts husbands to live with their wives 'in an understanding way,' giving honor and dwelling with them as co-heirs of grace—a call to consistent, attentive presence that surpasses the claims of work, children, or other pursuits. When we embrace this priority, we demonstrate that marriage is not a secondary good squeezed around life's 'real' demands, but the foundational human relationship through which Christ's love becomes visible and our sanctification deepens.

Pray together this week

For Husbands: Grace to Love Like Christ

Father, we adore You for the immeasurable love You show Your bride, the church—a love so costly and patient that You gave Your Son to make us holy and without blemish (Ephesians 5:25-27). We marvel at how Christ presents His church to Himself in all her glory, having worked with sustained, gracious attention to transform her into His image. We confess that many of us husbands have not grasped the weight of this calling. We have treated the command to love like Christ as generic spiritual language rather than the specific, awe-inspiring responsibility to sacrifice for our wives' eternal good. We have loved according to our wishes and whims, not according to God's will and plan. We have failed to invest the detailed, patient attention that true leadership requires, and we have not first allowed God's transforming work in our own hearts to shape how we lead.

In the gospel, we have been reconciled to the Father through Christ's substitutionary sacrifice, and we continue to experience His patient, varied, layered approach to our sanctification (2 Corinthians 3:18). The Spirit does not rush or simplify; He knows us thoroughly and works with skillful care to conform us to Christ's image. We come now asking that this same grace would overflow from us toward our wives. Grant us eyes to see our marriages not as pursuits of personal happiness but as opportunities to reflect Christ's covenant love and to raise up the next generation in security and blessing. Give us the patience to attend to our wives' sanctification as Christ attends to His church—with sacrifice, with constancy, with hope that we are presenting them to God more radiant than we found them (Ephesians 5:27). Strengthen us to lead with the same grace we have received, making our wives our clear first priority and our greatest earthly treasure. May our marriages become visible demonstrations of the gospel, declaring to our children and our generation the selfless, world-changing love of Jesus Christ.

Sunday-evening family table

Loving Like Christ Loves

For the parent

This prompt invites kids to think concretely about what it means for a husband to love his wife the way Christ loves the church—not as abstract sentiment, but as real, costly attention and care. Listen for whether they grasp that love is an action, not just a feeling, and help them see how this reflects what they've witnessed in their own parents' marriage.

In the sermon, Pastor Dov talked about husbands loving their wives the way Jesus loves the church—giving everything up, paying attention to help them become better, not giving up on them even when it's hard. Can you think of a time you saw a parent or grown-up give something up or spend a lot of time helping someone they love become better at something? What did that look like?
Works for ages 7+ — younger children may need a specific family example to anchor their thinking; teens can engage with the theological weight of self-sacrifice
