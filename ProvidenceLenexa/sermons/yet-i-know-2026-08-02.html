Yet I Know

Ecclesiastes 8:10-15 August 2, 2026 Pastor Chris Oswald
Thesis Wisdom — rooted in knowing God rather than merely observing circumstances — insulates believers from the seductive lie that the prosperity of the wicked proves there is no God or that fearing God is futile.

Illustrations· 5
  1. historical example · unit #11 — Illustrates the problem with a stark historical contrast: Jim Elliott (missionary martyr at 32) vs. Hugh Hefner (died at 91 of old age). The disparity between righteous suffering and wicked prosperity is not theoretical but empirically verifiable.
  2. analogy · unit #15 — Personal anecdote explaining 'deceitful brook' through frontier homesteading: a settler builds his entire future next to a water source that turns out to be unreliable. The image captures the fear that following God might prove similarly futile.
  3. analogy · unit #28 — Extends the illustration: intelligence without wisdom leads to spectacular failure (Titanic metaphor). If forced to choose, wisdom (knowing the Maker) is more valuable than intelligence (horsepower). History is full of intelligent people who crashed because they lacked wisdom.
  4. analogy · unit #35 — Introduces illustration of pontoon bridges: military engineers build temporary bridges by connecting floating sections to cross water quickly. The image will become a metaphor for how people build their lives.
  5. analogy · unit #36 — Applies the pontoon bridge image: many people are faithful at connecting life's sections (career, marriage, parenting, finances) but may be building a bridge without a tether. The pastor observes this pattern pastorally without indictment.
Theological claims· 13
  1. To fear God is to live with acute awareness that every thought, word, deed, and omission is visible to God and therefore to be genuinely concerned with pleasing Him. unit #4
  2. The prosperity of the wicked appears to prove that their godless way of living is correct. unit #7
  3. The preacher was not seduced by the prosperity of the wicked because he possessed wisdom — the capacity to see beyond immediate appearances to ultimate realities. unit #20
  4. Wisdom is the capacity to recognize that what seems right to human perception may lead to death, and what seems wrong may lead to life — the cross of Christ being the ultimate proof. unit #22
  5. Wisdom is the capacity to see beyond immediate appearances and trust God's hidden purposes — preventing seduction by the world's false prosperity. unit #23
  6. Wisdom is the capacity to hold 'I saw' and 'I know' in tension — trusting God's revealed character above observable circumstances. unit #25
  7. All spiritual trouble exists in the gap between what you see and what God says — wisdom closes that gap by trusting God's revealed truth above observable reality. unit #26
  8. Intelligence is horsepower — raw computational power — but wisdom is knowing the Maker of the track, His purposes, and the meaning of each curve in the race. unit #27
  9. Wisdom comes from the fear and knowledge of God — it is not innate but derived from relationship with the Maker. unit #29
  10. Wisdom is not knowing the 'what' of a thousand details but knowing the 'who' behind all of them — God's character is the foundation of all wisdom. unit #32
  11. Knowing God produces certainty about ultimate outcomes — God will judge, His work endures, and it will be well with those who fear Him — even when present circumstances contradict this. unit #33
  12. Deconstruction is the inversion of wisdom — reading God's character through circumstances rather than trusting God's revealed character to interpret circumstances. unit #39
  13. God is most clearly known at the cross of Jesus Christ, where His justice and mercy meet — hating sin yet loving sinners enough to bear their punishment Himself. unit #41

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the spine of wisdom: from the fear of God as its root, through the tension between what we see and what we know, to the cross as the place where God's character is most clearly revealed.

Monday Ecclesiastes 1:13, 16-17

The Preacher set his mind to seek wisdom about all that happens under heaven, yet discovered that the more he accumulated knowledge and intelligence, the more he encountered grief. Wisdom is not the same as raw intellectual horsepower. True wisdom begins when we stop trusting only what our minds can compute and start trusting what God has revealed about Himself — His character, His purposes, His mercy.

Tuesday Psalm 73

Asaph watched the wicked prosper while he suffered, and his feet nearly slipped into the lie that fearing God was futile. But when he entered God's sanctuary — when he remembered God's presence and purpose — his perspective inverted entirely. What looked like proof of God's absence was actually the preacher's opportunity to prove that his confidence rests not on circumstances but on the God who sees all and judges justly.

Wednesday Proverbs 14:12

There is a way that appears right to us — it makes sense, it feels good, it delivers pleasure — yet its end is the way of death. We cannot trust our perceptions alone. The cross of Christ is the ultimate proof: execution looked like defeat and abandonment, yet it was the purchase of eternal life. Wisdom teaches us to hold our observations loosely and God's revealed character tightly.

Thursday Jeremiah 15:15-18

Jeremiah cried out that God had become treacherous to him, that his wound was incurable — all because what he saw (persecution, opposition, suffering) contradicted what God had promised (vindication, purpose). Yet God did not rebuke Jeremiah for observing reality; He called him to return and trust that his words had weight even when his circumstances screamed otherwise. This is wisdom: holding fast to what God says when what we see tempts us to doubt.

Friday Ecclesiastes 8:11-13

Because sentence against an evil deed is not executed speedily, the human heart is emboldened to do evil — we mistake God's patience for God's indifference. But the Preacher knows otherwise: it will be well with those who fear God, because He sees everything and judges justly. Our certainty does not rest on what we observe today but on what we know of God's character and His promised return. That knowledge is our anchor when the wicked prosper and the righteous suffer.

What You See vs. What You Know

For the parent

This prompt invites kids to notice the difference between observation and trust — a distinction the sermon makes central to wisdom. Listen for whether they can hold both 'I see this happening' and 'I know God is good' at the same time without one canceling out the other.

The preacher talked about seeing wicked people do really well — get rich, get famous, seem happy — while good people sometimes struggle. He said the difference between being confused by that and not being confused is wisdom. Can you think of a time when something *looked* like it was going to turn out bad, but it actually turned out good? Or when something looked good but turned out to hurt you? What helped you trust what was actually true, even when what you saw didn't match?

Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. The preacher describes seeing wicked people buried with honor while righteous people are forgotten in the city where they did right. What is he observing about the visible world, and why does that observation matter enough to name it explicitly?
    Ecclesiastes 8:10
    → When have you personally witnessed something similar — someone living against God's ways who seemed to prosper, while someone faithful seemed to suffer? What feeling did that create in you?
  2. The preacher says 'Yet I know' — holding tension between what he saw and what he knows. What is the difference between these two ways of knowing, and why can't observation alone settle the question of whether fearing God matters?
    Ecclesiastes 8:12-13
  3. Proverbs 14:12 says 'There is a way that appears to be right, but in the end it leads to death.' How does the prosperity of the wicked make that proverb harder to believe, and what would wisdom need to do to trust it anyway?
    Proverbs 14:12
    → Can you think of a 'way that appears right' in our current moment that the world is treating as obviously correct?
  4. The sermon distinguishes intelligence from wisdom — saying intelligence is 'horsepower' but wisdom is 'knowing the Maker of the track.' What does that mean practically? How would a wise person and an intelligent person respond differently to the same set of circumstances?

    → What would it look like to close that gap this week — not by ignoring what you see, but by deliberately trusting what God has said?
  6. The sermon ends by saying God is most clearly known at the cross, where His justice and mercy meet — where He hates sin yet loves sinners enough to bear their punishment. How does that knowledge of God reshape what you do with the prosperity of the wicked you observe around you?
