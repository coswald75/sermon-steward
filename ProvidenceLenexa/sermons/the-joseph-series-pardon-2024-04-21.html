5-day reading plan

This week we meditate on forgiveness as the gospel grace that humbles us as sinners, compels us to pardon others, and fills us with joy—tracing the arc from our universal need, through Christ's example, to the transformed life we live together.

Monday Romans 12

Paul opens Romans 12 by calling us to present our bodies as living sacrifices—a posture of humility that begins with grasping our own sinfulness and God's mercy toward us. As we recognize ourselves as forgiven sinners, the natural response is to extend that same pardon to others, becoming living displays of Christ's forgiving character to the world around us.

Tuesday Matthew 18

Christ's teaching on forgiveness in Matthew 18 reveals that pardoning another is not mere sentiment—it is a deliberate act of the will to release an offender from judgment and restore relationship. We see in this passage that forgiveness is both a command and a gift, binding us together in the body of Christ with the same mercy our Father has shown us in the gospel.

Wednesday Ephesians 4:32

Paul anchors forgiveness in the finished work of Christ, reminding us that we are tenderhearted and forgiving *because* God in Christ forgave us. This is not burdensome duty but the joyful response of redeemed sinners who have tasted immeasurable grace and cannot help but extend it to one another.

Thursday Luke 23:34

Christ's cry from the cross—"Father, forgive them"—reveals forgiveness not as weakness but as the deepest strength and obedience. When we forgive as He did, we are freed from the poison of bitterness, reconciled to one another, and aligned with the heart of our Father, experiencing the profound joy that only comes from imitating our Savior's love.

Friday 1 Corinthians 11

As we gather at the table to remember Christ's broken body and shed blood, we are reminded that our debt is paid and our sins are forgiven—a reality that fills us with power to forgive others. We come to communion as those who have received grace, and we leave as ambassadors of reconciliation, proclaiming through our forgiveness the gospel that transforms all relationships until Christ returns.

Pray together this week

A Prayer for Pardon and Reconciliation

Father, we come before you in awe of your character as the God of all grace, who through Christ the Great Forgiver has pardoned us completely and calls us to imitate his mercy toward one another (Ephesians 4:32). We confess that we are all sinners—both those who have deeply wounded others and those who carry the weight of bitterness from wounds received. We acknowledge our tendency to nurse grievance, to rehearse old hurts, and to withhold forgiveness even when repentance has been offered; we are quick to demand justice and slow to grant pardon.

Yet the gospel humbles us as we grasp what Christ has accomplished: he paid our entire debt on the cross, absorbed our offense in his own body, and rose victorious so that we might be reconciled to God and to one another (Romans 12). Because we are forgiven much, we are compelled by grace to forgive those who have sinned against us—not by denying the hurt or erasing all consequences, but by committing ourselves to genuine reconciliation (Genesis 50:19-21).

We ask you to grant us the courage to forgive the repentant, to turn from bitterness that would defile us and rob us of joy, and to display the beauty of Christ the Great Forgiver to a watching world (Hebrews 12:14-15). Give us wisdom to speak truth in love, to seek deep and functioning relationships within your body, and to approach your table this week aware that our own sins are forgiven and our debt is paid—empowering us to release others just as you have released us (Matthew 18). May the joy of Joseph fill our hearts as we embrace the sacred work of reconciliation, until Christ returns and completes what he has begun.
