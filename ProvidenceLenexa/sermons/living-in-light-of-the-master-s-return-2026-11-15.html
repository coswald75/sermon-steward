And now second, a word for anyone here who recognizes that maybe, maybe in just a small degree, that you are coasting. Maybe you've taken your foot off the gas. Maybe you've served the Lord faithfully for years and you've grown a little comfortable. Or maybe you're younger and you've hit a wearying stretch. The baby cries at night, work is demanding, life feels heavy, and little by little your zeal has begun to fade. Well, wherever you find yourself, I'd encourage you to keep your eyes on Christ. If you've gotten a little comfortable, get back in the race. If you're in a wearying season, ask the Lord for the strength to keep going, but don't coast. What if by God's grace, this next season in your life could be your most faithful yet? What if, if you're in your 40s or your 50s or even in your 70s, What if that were marked by a deeper love for Christ, a stronger faith in him, and renewed zeal, committed zeal to serve him? So get back up, run hard, run well, not to earn your master's approval, but in dependence on his grace, because you belong to him and he is worthy of your life. Never retire from following Christ. Never retire from being his disciple. And until your master returns, keep seeking to be a good and faithful servant.

38 · The conclusion synthesizes the sermon's movements and reiterates the main thesis

Alright, so let's bring it home. Jesus entrusted you with a fortune— your time, your money, your gifts, your relationships, that is grace, and it came before he asked you for anything. He's coming to settle accounts, and for those who belong to him, there is joy, his joy. Well done, good and faithful servant, enter into the joy of your master. As you walk out of here today, be encouraged where you see evidence of grace. Confess where confession is necessary. Reflect and plan, and then obey our good, gracious, generous, holy Master. And if you need help, ask for help. Talk to a community group leader, talk to a friend in community group, talk to someone in your huddle, just talk to, talk to a pastor, elder. Do this in community and hold on to the promise we started with. Therefore, my beloved brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that in the Lord your labor is not in vain. Because our master will return, we must faithfully put to work what he's entrusted to us, looking forward to entering his joy, the joy of being with him.

39 · Closing prayer addressing Jesus as the good master

Let's pray. Dear Jesus, you are such a good master. Thank you that you sacrificed yourself for us so that we could be in you, so that we could follow you in your approval. Lord, I thank you for your word. I pray you'd help us to steward it well, to think deeply on it, to apply it to our lives, and with a tender heart to obey it. We thank you, we praise you. In Jesus' name we pray, amen.

    Matthew 25:16-18, Matthew 25:24-25

    Matthew 25:26
  4. The parable teaches us that 'the servants did the work, but the master gave the growth.'     1 Corinthians 15:58
    → Where in your life right now do you need to be reminded that fruitfulness depends on the Master's generosity, not merely your effort?

    Matthew 25:21, Matthew 25:23

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we meditate on how Christ's return calls us to faithful stewardship—tracing from the foundation that our labor matters, through the grace that multiplies our work, to the call to fix our eyes on Jesus rather than comparing ourselves with others.

Monday 1 Corinthians 15:58

Paul grounds all our work in the resurrection of Christ, declaring that nothing we do in the Lord is wasted or futile. When we steward our talents, time, and gifts faithfully, we are not laboring in a cosmos destined for ruin—we labor in light of Christ's triumph, and our faithful work will endure and bear fruit in eternity.

Tuesday Matthew 6:21

Jesus teaches that investment and affection are inseparable: we love what we pour ourselves into. When we faithfully deploy the Master's talents rather than bury them, our hearts are drawn toward his kingdom and his return; we grow to delight in what he delights in, and our stewardship becomes an expression of genuine love for him.

Wednesday Psalm 127

The psalmist reminds us that all fruitfulness flows from the Lord's hand—unless he builds the house, we labor in vain. This echoes the parable's logic: the servants invested their talents with diligence, but the increase was the Master's gift. We are freed from the burden of guaranteeing results; our calling is faithful action, and Christ's generous kingship ensures that our work, offered to him, will bear fruit we cannot manufacture alone.

Thursday Matthew 24-25

Matthew 24-25 frames the entire discourse on Christ's return and judgment, positioning the parable of the talents within a narrative of accountability and preparedness. We do not drift passively through history; we live with the sober reality that the Master is coming to settle accounts, and this knowledge is meant to awaken us from apathy and kindle active, faithful stewardship in every season.

Friday 1 Corinthians 15:58

Paul's exhortation to 'be steadfast' and 'abound in the work of the Lord' stands in a context where comparison and anxiety could paralyze us. Our task is not to measure our talents against another's, but to hear the Master's voice calling us to faithful stewardship of *what we have received*—rooted in the unshakeable truth that in Christ, our labor is never wasted and will be vindicated on the day of judgment.

Sunday-evening family table

What Are You Doing With What You've Been Given?

For the parent

This prompt invites your family to think concretely about the gifts and opportunities each person has right now—not in the abstract, but in real life. Listen for where kids see themselves as active stewards rather than passive receivers, and gently help them connect faithful use of what they have to Jesus's return and his pleasure.

In the sermon, the Master gave each servant a different amount of money and then left on a journey. When he came back, two servants showed him how they had put their gifts to work and made them grow. The third servant buried his and did nothing. If Jesus came back today and asked you, 'What did you do with the time, the abilities, the people, and the opportunities I gave you?'—what would you tell him? (You don't have to answer right away; think about your week.)
Works for ages 8+; younger children can listen and contribute with parental help
