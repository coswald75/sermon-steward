6 questions for your group this week

  1. What does it mean that the church enters into a covenant—a binding agreement—with God and with one another? How is this different from simply attending a church service as a consumer?
    → Can you think of a time when you've experienced the difference between casual membership in a group versus committed covenant partnership?
  2. The sermon identifies three steps toward church membership: reviewing covenant materials, attending services regularly, and joining a community group. Why do you think the pastor structures membership this way rather than making it a single-step decision?
    → Which of these three steps feels most important to you for truly belonging to a local church, and why?
  3. What does regular, faithful attendance at Sunday worship communicate about our view of Christ's headship over the church and our commitment to the body?
    → What patterns in your own life—whether toward or away from regular gathering—reveal what you actually believe about the church's importance?
  4. The sermon emphasizes that community groups create the relational space where covenant truly lives. What are we admitting about ourselves if we try to live out church membership in isolation?
    → What fears or hesitations might keep someone from pursuing these kinds of close relationships within the church, and how does the gospel speak to those?
  5. How does understanding the church as a covenant community—rather than merely a voluntary association—reshape what it means to submit to leadership, serve sacrificially, or speak truth in love to one another?
    → What is one concrete way this covenant perspective should change how you relate to another member this week?
  6. What gospel truth—that Christ has made us members of His body, bound together by His blood and Spirit—should motivate us to take these three steps toward formal membership rather than remain uncommitted?
    → If you're not yet a member, what question or obstacle is keeping you from moving forward, and how can our group help you process that?

5-day reading plan

Monday Ephesians 1:22-23

Paul's vision of the church transcends organizational structure—it is the living, corporate embodiment of Christ's presence in the world. As we consider church membership and covenant, we are not joining an institution but entering into vibrant union with Christ's body, where He supplies all authority, direction, and power for our corporate witness and growth together.

Tuesday 1 Corinthians 12:12-27

The covenant we enter is not solitary but symphonic—each of us has received grace for a specific role, and the health of our congregation depends on the interdependence of diverse members functioning in their gifts. When we commit to church membership and community, we are pledging to steward our particular calling for the mutual edification of the whole body.

Wednesday Hebrews 10:24-25

The writer of Hebrews presents church gathering not as optional piety but as essential for our perseverance and holiness—we are called to provoke one another toward love and good deeds through intentional presence. Accessing covenant materials, attending services, and joining community groups become concrete expressions of our pledge to bear one another's burdens and encourage one another toward Christ.

Thursday 1 Peter 2:9-10

Our covenant relationship with Providence Community Church roots us in the grand narrative of God's redemptive purpose—we are not simply members of a local congregation but part of a people set apart by God's sovereign grace for gospel witness. This identity compels us to approach membership with seriousness, recognizing that our covenant commitment participates in God's eternal plan to gather a people for Himself.

Friday Matthew 28:19-20

Our pathway into covenant—reviewing materials, gathering weekly, and building relationships in community groups—is ultimately preparation for the mission Christ has given us. The gospel humbles us as we recognize that our membership is not about receiving a spiritual commodity but about joining Christ's redemptive work; every step of covenant commitment equips us to fulfill our calling as witnesses and disciple-makers in our neighborhoods and spheres of influence.

A Prayer for Covenant Faithfulness

Father, we come before You in awe of Your sovereign design for the church—that You would gather us, a redeemed people, into covenant community bound together by the gospel of Christ. We marvel at Your wisdom in calling us not merely as isolated believers but as members of one another, accountable, interdependent, and compelled by grace to walk together in holiness and love.

Yet we confess that we often treat the local church with casual indifference, viewing membership as optional rather than essential, or approaching it as consumers rather than as covenant keepers. We struggle to prioritize the disciplines that deepen our belonging—regular worship, intentional relationship, honest submission to one another's encouragement and correction. We have not always grasped that our commitment to this particular body is a commitment to Christ Himself.

Thanks be to You that in the gospel we have been brought near through the blood of Christ, made members of His body and heirs of His promises. The Holy Spirit dwells in us and binds us together as His temple, making our covenant with one another not a burden we bear but a privilege we cherish—a visible expression of the love that Christ poured out for us. In Him, every hesitation toward covenant faithfulness is met with enabling grace.

We ask You now to grant us courage to step deeper into the life of this church community. Give us wisdom as we review our membership materials, attentiveness in worship to hear Your voice through preaching, and hearts prepared to build genuine friendships within our community groups. Make us a people who prize our covenant vows, who show up for one another, and who delight in the interdependence that marks the body of Christ. Let every member—present and yet to come—know that they belong here, and that their presence matters to our corporate witness.

To You, our sovereign Lord and the Head of the church, be glory forever. We commit ourselves afresh to this covenant community, trusting that You will perfect what concerns us and complete the good work You have begun in us together.
