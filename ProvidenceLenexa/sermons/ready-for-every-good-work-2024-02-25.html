Pray together this week

Ready for Every Good Work

Father, we come before You in awe of Your sovereign grace that has made us vessels in Your great house, chosen and set apart for honorable use. We marvel at Jesus, who demonstrated perfect readiness for every good work, supremely in His willingness to suffer and die for the church (2 Timothy 2:20–21). Yet we confess that we often shrink from the labor of self-cleansing, neglecting the daily disciplines of Your Word that would align our hearts with His. We know that readiness does not come naturally to our flesh; we need grace to resist the futile quarrels and passions that distract us from Your purposes (2 Timothy 2:22–23).

We thank You that in the gospel, Christ has secured for us both the desire and the power to become vessels prepared for honorable use. His finished work humbles us, strips away our pride, and fills us with grateful desire to honor Him in return. We ask that You would kindle in us a fierce love for His church, so that high regard for Jesus and His bride compels us toward readiness for every good work (2 Timothy 2:20–21). Grant us grace to engage Your Word daily, both in the quiet of our own hearts and together in community, and give us obedience to apply what we hear. Make us a people who run together with intensity, encouraging one another, exhorting the wandering, correcting the stubborn, and spurring one another toward the goal of Christlikeness (2 Timothy 2:24–26).

We commit ourselves anew to the pursuit of readiness, knowing that as we do, we honor You, experience the deep joy of Your pleasure, and love our neighbors by promoting their flourishing. To You, the all-glorious God who empowers us by grace, be glory forever.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how readiness for good works flows from Christ's example, is cultivated through Scripture and community, and becomes our joyful response to His sovereign grace.

Monday Philippians 2:5-11

Paul's hymn unveils the mind of Christ—His willing descent from glory to servant's form to death itself. In His readiness to empty Himself for our redemption, we see the pattern of all true readiness for good works: not reluctant obligation, but joyful submission to the Father's will for the church's sake. As we meditate on His humiliation and exaltation, our own resistance to service dissolves in grateful wonder.

Tuesday 1 Corinthians 11:23-26

In remembering Christ's death through the Lord's Supper, we participate in a corporate act of meditation on Scripture—the gospel story embodied and consumed. This rhythmic, communal return to the cross purifies us from competing loyalties and refocuses our hearts on Christ's self-giving love. When we gather to remember, we are cleansed and prepared afresh for the good works He calls us to.

Wednesday Matthew 8

From the centurion's recognition of Christ's authority to the demoniac's restoration to the healing of many in Peter's house, Matthew 8 shows us a Christ worthy of absolute trust and a God whose power extends to every corner of human need. When we behold His authority and compassion in Scripture, we are reoriented toward the urgent work of His kingdom in our own neighborhoods and communities.

Thursday 2 Timothy 2:20-21

We are not passive objects shaped by circumstance; we cooperate with God's sanctifying work through deliberate self-cleansing—turning from the vain disputes and hollow pursuits that diminish us as vessels. The great house of God's people contains vessels of many kinds, yet each is called to that exacting standard: fitness for the Master's hand. Our readiness is both His gift and our responsibility.

Friday 2 Timothy 2:22

Paul's command—pursue righteousness, faith, love, and peace—is not grim duty but an invitation to run together with brothers and sisters who call on the Lord with pure hearts. We are ready for every good work not because we fear judgment, but because we long to honor the Christ who honored us, to taste the deep joy of His pleasure, and to see those we love flourish in truth. This pursuit binds us together and propels us outward in love.
