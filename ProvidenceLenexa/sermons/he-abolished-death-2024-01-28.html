He Abolished Death

Thesis Jesus Christ has abolished death, transforming it from the King of Terrors into a doorway to eternal life for all who trust in him.

Theological claims· 6
  1. Calling Jesus 'Lord' and 'Savior' was an explicitly political act challenging Caesar's exclusive claim to those titles. unit #3
  2. The greatest gospel comforts come only through facing uncomfortable truths about sin and death — refusing to think about these realities withholds the most profound comforts from us. unit #7
  3. Death is universal and often surprising — presuming you will have time later to respond to the gospel is to presume on God with no evidence that future opportunity will come. unit #9
  4. Death is a one-way door to divine judgment — there are no second chances, and every human being will stand before God's judgment seat. unit #11
  5. Those who die in Christ are not driven out but escorted into eternal life — carried in a triumphal procession as royalty into the presence of God. unit #15
  6. Jesus is a military hero — the strongman who abolished death, defeated the ultimate enemy, and brought a kind of peace that exceeds any other human experience. unit #18

Whatever you would say about this change, it is the most marvelous and glorious change of all time. And that's what Jesus did in abolishing death. He did something utterly goes beyond our ability to talk about it.

18 · Corrects a common misunderstanding — Jesus was a military hero, just not the kind expected

You'll sometimes hear that the Jews missed Jesus's messiahship because they were looking for a military hero. And he wasn't that wrong. He was a military hero. He is a military hero. He is the strongman who abolished death. He has defeated the enemies and brought a kind of peace to us that exceeds any other human experience.

19 · Orwell quote applied to Christian death — just as rough men stand ready to protect sleeping citizens, the strong man Jesus has removed death's sting

I was reminded this week of a quote from George Orwell, who once said, we sleep soundly in our beds because rough men stand ready in the night to do violence on those who would harm us. When the time comes, dear Christian, whether it is expected or unexpected, if you are in Christ, you may close your eyes and sleep soundly in peace. You may sleep soundly on your deathbeds because the strong man Jesus Christ has removed the sting of death for you. It is as if God walked into that hospice or that nursing home or over that crashed car and put a banner on the day of your death, put a banner over that tragic scene saying, o death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory? As we've said at the beginning, this whole book of two Timothy is about giving us courage. And as John Flavell writes On this verse, 2 Timothy 1:10, if our souls be immortal, certainly our enemies are not so formidable as we are apt by our sinful fears to represent them.

20 · Direct application — to build courage, believers must reframe death as upgrade, graduation, or advancement

One incredibly important ingredient for courage, for building courage, is to look at death and say, upgrade, graduation day, advancement. Or as Paul says, to be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord.

21 · Pivots from exposition and application to the Lord's table

So now we turn our attention to this table. Celebrate communion every week. I want to start by asking you, how can you be sure that you are on the right side of death?

22 · Direct pastoral defense of the sermon's tone

You know, if anyone here thinks that I have embraced scare tactics with this talk, it's like, I just want to let you know that you're thinking that because you're afraid. But I haven't embraced scare tactics any more than if I told you that if you jumped off of a mountain, gravity would have its effect. I have giving you facts. And if you're afraid as a consequence of me sharing those facts, friends, that's not, that's not me, that's not Christianity. That's not an attempt to manipulate. This is simply. You've just read the brochure that's describing how this life works. You will die. You don't have any choice over how quickly. It will probably come quicker than you think it will. Death is a one way door into eternal judgment. If you've not placed your faith in Jesus Christ, you will go to hell. If you have placed your faith in Jesus Christ, you will go to heaven.

23 · Application centered on the Lord's table as a self-diagnostic test

And as we are preparing to partake in the Lord's table, one way that you can discern which side of death you are on is to understand this. Jesus says in introducing the table, he says, this is my body which is for you. Do this in remembrance of me. And in the same way he took the cup after supper and said, this cup is the new covenant in my blood. Do this as often as you drink it in remembrance of me. Now I think the way to discern, one way to discern what side of death you're on is to ask simply this. Why did Jesus have to offer his body up to be crucified? And why did Jesus have to spill his blood, die? Why? Now the Christian knows the answer to that question. The Christian knows not simply abstractly or theoretically or from a theological point of intelligence, but the Christian knows the answer to that question. And the Christian would say, Jesus had to die because I had piled up such a massive record of sin debt against God, a debt I could never repay. Jesus had to die in order to secure with his perfect righteousness my forgiveness. When the Christian hears, this is my body which is for you, and this is my blood which is poured out for you, the Christian knows that's my only plan. When the Christian is on his or her deathbed, this Very fact presented to us today on this table will be the difference maker. This very fact will be the thing that affects the way in which they pass from this life to next. This is what they will be thinking of. So what about you? Are you a follower of Jesus Christ? Is his death your hope? Do you believe that there was no other way and that Jesus had to buy you out of this incredible slavery to which you submitted your own self? Do you believe that this is your only hope? Well, if so, Jesus as your only hope is all the hope you need on your deathbed, friends. And there really isn't a lot more you could hope in at that point.

24 · Invitation for believers to partake — the table celebrates that death will be marked by God with the banner of Christ's victory

If so, why don't you come and partake of this table today and celebrate the fact that for you, whenever it is that you do pass, the Lord will send a couple angels down there to hang a banner over that painful scene. And that banner says, O death, where is thy victory? O grave, where is thy sting? To be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord. Precious in the eyes of the Lord are the death of his saints. Why don't you come and partake of this table?

25 · Instruction for unbelievers not to partake of the table

But if you don't know Christ, if, if you are unpersuaded by the gospel message, well, I would let you know not to take this table. Because this table is essentially the individual saying, I need what Jesus has offered. And at this stage you don't believe that you do need what Jesus has offered. And that's fine, that's your choice. But what would it hurt you if you listen to this sermon again? What would it hurt you? Seems prudent to me if you're not persuaded today. It seems prudent to me to take another chance, take another swing at it and say, well, let me make sure I'm right. And I really think I'm right. Well, what harm would it cause to think a little longer on these uncomfortable things? I would say not a lot. Seems pretty smart to me. Seems like a good idea.

26 · Final invitation — believers are encouraged to partake

So for those of you who are in Christ, I encourage you to come and partake of this table. And if during this sermon, the Lord awakens your heart and said, hey, we've got a problem. This isn't going well for you, friend. All you have to do while everyone's walking up here is just close your eyes and call out to the Lord and say, I don't want to have any other plan but this, that you died for my sins, secured my forgiveness, and have made a way for death to be completely a different thing for me than it would have been without you.

27 · Closing prayer confessing human inability to see truth apart from God, asking for the Spirit's grace to connect to the truths proclaimed

So let's pray for one another and then come oh Father God we confess we have absolutely no capacity to see the truth, to say the truth, to believe the truth apart from you and so we pray God that your spirit would give us grace to connect to these vital truths and that that would lead ultimately to rejoicing. Lord if there's anyone here who doesn't doesn't know where they will go when they die or help them to hear the gospel which has been proclaimed and help them to be honest with even someone in this church today to say I don't really know, not sure Lord, may your peace that passes all understanding rest upon them as they hear the good news. We thank you Lord for what you've done. It's really such a strain to come up with words to describe the glory of it but maybe right now we just should taste and see that the Lord is good in Jesus name.

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how Christ's abolition of death reframes our entire hope—from the political courage to name Him Lord, through the uncomfortable but necessary confrontation with mortality, to our final confidence that death itself has become our graduation into His presence.

Monday Daniel 3:16-18

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego refused to bow to Nebuchadnezzar's image, declaring allegiance to the true King even unto death. When we confess Jesus as Lord, we make the same radical political statement—we dethrone every other authority and crown Christ alone as sovereign over our lives and our deaths. This is the courage that flows from understanding whose we truly are.

Tuesday Hebrews 9:27

Scripture's blunt declaration—'people are destined to die once, and after that to face judgment'—offers no loopholes, no second chances, no hidden exit. The gospel comfort comes not from denying this reality but from facing it honestly and then discovering that Christ has already walked through that judgment door on our behalf. Delaying our response to His lordship is to bet our eternities on a tomorrow we do not own.

Wednesday Hebrews 2:14

Christ's incarnation was not a pastoral visit but a military campaign: He 'shared in our humanity so that by his death he might break the power of him who holds the power of death.' Death held dominion over all humanity as death's master; Jesus conquered that stronghold from within. The peace He brings is not the absence of struggle but the triumph of the victor who has already won the war that matters most.

Thursday 2 Corinthians 5:8

Paul's eager declaration—'we would prefer to be away from the body and at home with the Lord'—reveals that death for the Christian is not an ending but a promotion, not exile but homecoming. We reframe death not as the final enemy but as the doorway through which we pass into the immediate presence of our King. This transforms our posture toward mortality from dread into longing.

Friday Psalm 116:15

'Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his faithful servants.' Our death, when it comes in Christ, is not a tragedy mourned in heaven but a treasured homecoming, a moment of inestimable worth in God's eyes. As we gather at the Lord's table, we celebrate that our own deaths—whenever they come—have been purchased, marked, and rendered eternally precious by Christ's victory. This truth gives us courage to live faithfully now and to die hopefully when that day arrives.

Pray together this week

Prayer: Christ the Conqueror of Death

O God, we come before you in awe of your Son, Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior—titles that declare His exclusive reign over us and over all creation. We confess that we often avoid the hard truths about death and judgment, preferring comfort to reality. Yet in our refusal to face these realities honestly, we rob ourselves of the deepest gospel consolations. We admit that we sometimes presume on tomorrow as though we control our days, when in truth every breath is a gift held in Your sovereign hand (Hebrews 9:27).

But we rejoice that Christ has abolished death through His own death and resurrection (2 Timothy 1:10). He is the strongman who defeated the ultimate enemy, and He carries us—His beloved—in triumphal procession into eternal life (Psalm 116:15). The gospel humbles us as we grasp that His death is our only hope, and it is a hope that is entirely sufficient, even on the deathbed (2 Corinthians 5:8).

Grant us, O Father, the grace to reframe death not as terror but as graduation into Your presence. Give us courage to speak of these things openly, and to live in light of the reality that to be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord. As we gather at Your table, remind us that we feast in the company of the crucified and risen King, celebrating His victory that is also our victory. Work in us a glad readiness to follow wherever He leads, knowing that our death—like all things—is held in His victorious hands.

To You, through Christ, in the power of Your Spirit, be glory and dominion forever. Amen.
