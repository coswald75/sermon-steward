The Earthy Piety of Providence

Ecclesiastes 9:7-10 August 9, 2026 Pastor Chris Oswald
Thesis In a world marked by uncertainty and unfairness, we are called to do Eden anyway—faithfully discharging our God-given duties in work, celebration, relationships, and worship, trusting that our creaturely limitations and the brokenness around us do not change what we are or what we are for.

Illustrations· 3
  1. cultural reference · unit #18 — The pastor introduces a 2019 psychological study (Waits, Haight) that gave participants 100 credits of care to allocate across concentric circles from family to universal concerns. The study revealed that conservatives allocated credits centrally (family, community, city) while liberals allocated them outward (universal rights, global concerns).
  2. personal story · unit #30 — The pastor shares a personal story from October 2001, a month after 9/11, when he walked through lower Manhattan and observed the pervasive layer of dust covering everything far from ground zero and the dazed look in people's eyes. The illustration sets up the metaphor he is about to apply.
  3. historical example · unit #36 — The pastor shares the founding story of Providence Community Church: the original families were all gripped by the sovereignty of God in its fullest biblical sense during the same period and named the church Providence because this doctrine was glorious and undeniable to them and demanded to be acted upon. The congregation sits in the fruit of that doctrinal conviction.
Theological claims· 14
  1. Ecclesiastes answers the question of how to live a good life with four categories: work (productive toil), wine (daily celebration of lawful pleasures), wife (committed relationships), and worship (the fear of God as the atmosphere in which we do everything else). unit #8
  2. Despite having even less certainty about the afterlife than we do, the writer of Ecclesiastes confidently prescribes work, celebration, relationships, and worship as the content of a good life. unit #13
  3. Providence Community Church embodies the vision of Ecclesiastes: an earthy piety of simple, ordinary faithfulness in daily things linked together over years. unit #14
  4. The writer of Ecclesiastes acknowledges that life feels uncertain from our creaturely perspective, but he does not believe the universe is fundamentally random—God is in control, and the four categories of life (work, wine, wife, worship) are prescriptions for living in uncertainty, not things to wait on until certainty arrives. unit #16
  5. Christianity is a religion that prioritizes facts over feelings—we acknowledge that life feels random, but because God is actually in control, we can process uncertainty in a fundamentally different way. unit #17
  6. Ecclesiastes endorses the pattern where we focus our care on those nearest to us—family, community, city—because we are creatures with limited capacity and must allocate love according to providential proximity, as Augustine taught. unit #19
  7. The preacher of Ecclesiastes answers perceived uncertainty by continually redirecting attention back to immediate, proximate responsibilities: house, meals, worship, spouse, committed relationships, church. unit #20
  8. The root of busybody behavior is often pride and avoidance—people whose own households have fallen into disorder escape the pain by critiquing others' households, which is easier than fixing their own. unit #26
  9. The metaphysical busybody tries to audit God's work, demanding explanations for suffering and prosperity—but the answer is the same: go home, work, celebrate, love your spouse, and worship God. unit #27
  10. The writer of Ecclesiastes, after surveying a broken world, rediscovers that the answer to 'what should a human do' is simply what humans were created to do in the first place: worship God, work, celebrate daily gifts, and be faithful to the people God has given us. unit #29
  11. The fall was like a bomb that covered everything in dust and made all of life harder, but the preacher of Ecclesiastes concludes: do Eden anyway—our fundamental identity and calling have not changed, so we are to work, worship, love the people God gave us, and celebrate daily gifts. unit #31
  12. Earthly is a New Testament word for sinful worldliness, and while the church has rightly preached against earthliness, it has sometimes neglected to commend earthiness, which Scripture affirms. unit #33
  13. Earthy means embracing our creaturely existence—bread, wine, oil, clean shirts, faithful relationships—and piety means godliness that faithfully discharges ordinary duties, not abstract spirituality disconnected from real life. unit #34
  14. The good works God prepared beforehand are the ordinary duties catalogued in Ephesians 5-6 and Ecclesiastes 9: love your spouse, raise children, work hard, be kind—this is what it means to be a human living in the will of God, worshiping him through work, celebration, and relationships. unit #42

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through Ecclesiastes' answer to the question of how to live: work, celebration, relationships, and worship—the earthy piety of ordinary faithfulness that rediscovers what we were made to do.

Monday Genesis 1:26-28, 2:15

Before the fall, before sin, before uncertainty entered the world, God embedded this pattern into us: tend the garden, name the animals, multiply, subdue. The preacher of Ecclesiastes doesn't invent a new answer to the question 'what should a human do?'—he rediscovers what was written into us at creation. Work, celebration, relationships, worship: this is not plan B. This is Eden, and we are to do it still.

Tuesday Ecclesiastes 5:18

Notice the permission and the confidence: it is good to eat, drink, and enjoy the fruit of our labor. Not 'if you can,' not 'when things settle down.' Now. The writer has just confessed the futility and uncertainty that covers all our days, and his answer is not to withdraw or postpone living—it is to embrace the ordinary gifts that surround us. This is not escapism. This is the courage to live while we wait.

Wednesday Ephesians 2:10

Paul echoes Ecclesiastes. We are God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works—not for abstract spirituality, not for solving the world's problems, but for the works he prepared beforehand. Those works are your marriage. Your labor. Your church. Your table. Your faithfulness to those nearest you. The New Testament does not contradict Ecclesiastes; it confirms it. This is what fulfills your design.

Thursday 1 Thessalonians 4:11-12

Paul's command is earthy and specific: aspire to live quietly, mind your own affairs, work with your hands. Not world-changer language. Not influencer language. Just: tend what is yours. Love those God gave you. Work faithfully. Build trust in your neighborhood by being trustworthy there. The preacher of Ecclesiastes would nod at every word. Our job is not to audit the cosmos or fix what is far from us—our job is to do Eden in the place we are, with the people beside us.

Friday Ecclesiastes 12:13-14

After everything—after the vanity, the uncertainty, the broken world—the preacher returns to the beginning and the end: fear God and keep his commandments. This is the whole duty of man. Not as escape from work or pleasure, but as the foundation of them. When we worship God, when we tremble at his word and trust his sovereignty, our ordinary days—our meals, our labor, our marriages—become worship. We do Eden not because we can fix the world, but because God is God, and we are his.

  3. What is one specific way your spouse has faithfully discharged a duty this week that you want to thank God for and pray he or she would continue to grow in?
Memory verse this week

Ecclesiastes 9:7-10

Go, eat your bread with joy, and drink your wine with a glad heart, for God has already approved what you do. Let your garments be always white, and let not oil be lacking on your head. Enjoy life with the wife whom you love, all the days of your vain life that he has given you under the sun, because that is your portion in life and in your toil at which you toil under the sun. Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might, for there is no work or device or knowledge or wisdom in Sheol, to which you are going.

Why this verse: This passage is the answer Ecclesiastes gives to the question of how to live well in an uncertain world—not through escape or abstract spirituality, but through four concrete categories of earthy piety: work, celebration, relationships, and worship. It anchors the sermon's central claim that we are called to do Eden anyway, faithfully discharging our creaturely duties regardless of the brokenness around us.
