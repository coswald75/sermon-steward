35 · Illustrates the sermon's vision of marriage through Jonathan Edwards's deathbed message to his wife: our marriage has been deeply spiritual, and I trust that spiritual part will continue after this life is over

    Ecclesiastes 9:7–9
    → How does that picture compare to what you see modeled in your church, your family of origin, or the culture around you?
  2. Chris said that most of us approach marriage with a consumeristic mindset—looking for someone who will make us happy. Where do you see that mindset at work in our culture? And if you're honest, where do you feel that pull in your own heart?
  3. Using the metaphor of a garden: what does it look like to 'tend' your spouse? What are the specific practices of care, attention, watering, and protection that show up in a marriage that's being tended well?

  4. Chris emphasized that 'the word of God applied to your spouse on a regular basis is the thing that will make them grow into the person that you can enjoy for your whole life.' What does it mean to apply the word of God to your spouse? How would you actually do that in a conversation?
    2 Timothy 3:16–17; Ephesians 5:25–26
    → What stops most of us from doing this consistently?

    Ephesians 5:25–26

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we walk from the foundation—that marriage is God's design for mutual sanctification—through the means by which a spouse becomes enjoyable, closing with the joy of shared praise.

Monday Genesis 2:18

The Lord said, 'It is not good that the man should be alone.' Before sin, before any work needed doing, God looked at His image-bearer and saw that solitude itself was incomplete. Marriage is not a solution to a problem; it is God's design for celebration. We are made for partnership in praise.

Tuesday Song of Solomon 4:12

A garden locked, a spring sealed, a fountain closed—these words describe intimacy reserved, depth protected, beauty preserved. But a garden is not beautiful because it is locked. It flourishes because someone tends it. Our spouse becomes the source of ongoing delight only through consistent investment of attention, words, and care.

Wednesday Ephesians 5:25-26

Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her, that he might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of water with the word. Christ's love is active, purposeful, and oriented toward our growth. Our marriage mirrors this: we sanctify one another through the word—speaking gospel truth, God's promises, and words of love that wash away shame and plant hope.

Thursday Proverbs 31

The woman of noble character is praised not for her individual achievement but for her investments: she tends her household, she rises while it is still night, her hands are busy, her lamp does not go out. Proverbs sees the home as the arena where God's order, His provision, and His faithfulness are displayed in daily rhythms. Our marriage is not a refuge from the kingdom; it is where the kingdom is built.

Friday 1 Peter 3:7

Husbands, live with your wives in an understanding way, showing honor to the woman as the weaker vessel, since you are heirs together of the grace of life. Co-heirs. Not competitors, not consumers of one another, but partners in receiving grace together. The ultimate goal of marriage is not individual happiness but shared worship—two people who have tended the garden together now standing before God, praising His faithfulness, celebrating what only the two of you have walked through.

Sunday-evening family table

The Garden You Tend

For the parent

This prompt invites kids to think about what happens when we care for something over time. The goal is to help them see that marriage, like a garden, grows beautiful only through regular attention and watering—and to set up a conversation about how parents speak love and gospel to each other.

Think about a plant or garden that gets watered and cared for every day versus one that doesn't get watered. What's the difference? Now, the preacher said your spouse is like a garden—what do you think he meant by that? What would it look like to 'water' your wife or husband with words?
Works for ages 8+. Younger kids can listen to the conversation; teens will engage with the metaphor more directly.
