Theological claims· 17
  1. Wisdom helps you choose well when you have choices; wisdom helps you trust well when you have no choices. unit #2
  2. Wisdom is an attribute of God, and its presence in our lives is a direct indicator of how godly we are. unit #3
  3. Because wisdom is presented in Scripture with superlative language as the supreme prize, it inevitably produces counterfeits. unit #4
  4. Counterfeit wisdom has been present since the fall, when the serpent tempted Eve with fruit that promised to make her wise. unit #6
  5. The pursuit of wisdom contains a frustrating circularity: distinguishing true wisdom from counterfeit wisdom requires wisdom itself. unit #7
  6. Counterfeit wisdom is true wisdom fused with pride and self-centeredness. unit #9
  7. The essence of wisdom is 'You are God and I am not,' which makes worship the most wise activity and covenant marriage the second most wise—both feel like death but lead to life. unit #13
  8. Unlike salvation, there is no 'once wise, always wise'—wisdom can be lost after acquisition and does not automatically persist with age. unit #14
  9. Humility is the preservative for wisdom—it prevents wisdom from spoiling into foolishness through contamination by pride. unit #16
  10. Wisdom includes the discernment to know when action is futile and when stillness, contentment, and trust are the wise response to circumstances beyond your control. unit #24
  11. Anxiety, worry, and obsessive control are counterfeit wisdoms—foolish responses to uncertainty. True wisdom trusts in God's goodness and sovereignty. unit #26
  12. God made the unchangeable hardship in your life—he did not merely permit it at arm's length. Biblical wisdom requires affirming God's absolute sovereignty, not diminishing it to make theology more comfortable. unit #29
  13. The pastoral comfort of divine sovereignty is that hardship is carefully measured and sent by God, not random chaos outside his control. unit #30
  14. The unchangeable hardship God sends is the great engine of providence for revealing what is actually inside us—it shows us who we really are. unit #31
  15. Humility is the supreme human virtue because it is the destination for both halves of wisdom: when you have choices, choose humility; when you have no choices, be humble and accept God's work. unit #33
  16. Wisdom grants status, status breeds pride, and pride destroys wisdom—Solomon's life is the archetypal warning that wisdom without humility ends in ruin. unit #40
  17. God brings humbling circumstances (mourning, rebuke, hardship) into the lives of the wise as mercy—these things preserve wisdom by preventing the pride that would destroy it. unit #41

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week traces wisdom's arc: from its supreme value as God's attribute, through the peril of pride that spoils it, to the humility that preserves it—and the hard mercies God sends to keep us humble.

Monday Proverbs 14:12

Solomon warns that 'there is a way that appears to be right, but in the end it leads to death.' This is why wisdom matters at the moment of choice—our instincts deceive us, our desires mislead us, and the culture around us packages death as righteousness. Wisdom is the God-given faculty that stops us at the fork and says: not that way.

Tuesday Genesis 3

The serpent's first lie was 'Did God really say...?'—casting doubt on God's word—and the second was 'you will be like God, knowing good and evil.' Eve was tempted not by foolishness but by the *appearance* of wisdom: the fruit promised knowledge, discernment, and equality with the divine. The counterfeits we face today wear the same mask—they always promise us godlike power over our circumstances, our bodies, our outcomes.

Wednesday 1 Kings 11:1-8

Solomon was granted unparalleled wisdom—more than any man before or after him—yet he ended his reign enslaved to his own appetites, his heart divided among a thousand women and a thousand false gods. His wisdom did not protect him from pride; instead, his wisdom *enabled* his pride by giving him the power to justify whatever he desired. Status and success are the soil where pride grows, and pride is wisdom's poison.

Thursday Ecclesiastes 7:13-14

Solomon tells us to 'consider what God has done: who can straighten what he has made crooked?' The 'crook in the lot'—the hardship you cannot fix, cannot escape, cannot negotiate away—is *God's making*. And in the prosperity he also made, you forget to be humble; in the hardship he also made, you are forced to see who you actually are. This is mercy, not punishment. Wisdom sees it as such.

Friday 2 Corinthians 12

Paul was granted extraordinary visions—a form of wisdom and knowledge—but God sent him a 'thorn in the flesh' to keep him humble, 'so that I will not become conceited.' This is the pattern: God gives wisdom and blessing, then sends the hardship that will keep us from pride. We spend our prayers asking God to remove the thorn; wisdom teaches us to pray instead that the thorn keeps us humble, because humility is what allows us to use wisdom at all.
