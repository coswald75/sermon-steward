God Sees

Isaiah 1:1-20 November 1, 2026 Pastor Chris Oswald
Thesis God's vision reveals that true salvation through his servant includes both justifying pardon and transforming repentance as gifts of grace, not earned merit or cheap grace, leading to eternal fellowship with him.

  2. hypothetical · unit #9 — Uses the illustration of a Roomba spreading dog excrement to demonstrate what it means for something to not merely fail at its purpose but to invert its purpose—doing the opposite of what it was designed to do. Sets up the teleological understanding of sin.

Theological claims· 15
  1. God's vision in Isaiah reveals reality as it actually is, unfiltered by the veils and shadows that limit human perception. unit #3
  2. Sin fundamentally violates the purpose for which a person was created. unit #8
  3. Sin is teleological violation—it doesn't just fail to fulfill purpose but actively inverts and perverts the purpose for which we were made. unit #10
  4. Human purpose is to image God by loving God and loving people, which is why Jesus summarizes all the law in the command to love God and neighbor. unit #11
  5. Sin is not merely the failure to love God and others but the active inversion of our God-given purpose. unit #12
  6. God's people have not merely failed passively but have descended into open rebellion and hatred against him. unit #14
  7. Sin does invisible violence to the soul that we cannot see and are often too deceived to recognize, believing repentance would cause more harm than the sin itself. unit #17
  8. The worshipers in Isaiah 1:11-15 are not knowing hypocrites but self-deceived people whose sin has blinded them to the falseness of their repentance. unit #20
  9. True repentance requires six biblical ingredients, and the worshipers in Isaiah 1 are missing at least one, which is why God rejects their worship as false repentance. unit #21
  10. Some sins become so normalized by habit, family, or culture that we cannot see them, and we engage in religious activities without actually surrendering the sins themselves. unit #22
  11. God can offer pardon because he sees his servant, who makes forgiveness possible even when sin deserves judgment. unit #27
  12. Both isolating verse 18 (cheap grace) and reading verses 16-17 as conditions for verse 18 (works righteousness) misunderstand the salvation God offers. unit #31
  13. The two ways to misread Isaiah 1:16-20 are cheap grace (ignoring the call to obedience) and legalism (making obedience the cause of salvation). unit #32
  14. God's salvation through his servant includes both the grace to pardon and the grace to repent—both are gifts, and repentance is never absent from saving grace. unit #33
  15. Repentance that leads to life is a gift from God, empowered by his grace, and true salvation always includes genuine transformation. unit #34

There's another wrong way to get this passage, and that would be to see the washing that takes place in verses 16 and 17 that contains the six ingredients of biblical repentance, that that washing, that repentance, is what cleans us and saves us so that we see salvation coming from a sincere effort to change, putting in a lot of work, making sincere effort. Those are the two ways to get it wrong.

33 · Resolves the tension by asserting that both pardon and repentance are gifts of grace from the servant

This passage is provided to us to show us what I think is the basic truth of the salvation that God sees and to show us the gaps between the salvation that God sees and the salvations that we see. I think that most people see one of those two errant salvations. They see a cheap grace salvation that has no expectation of change at all, or they see a no grace salvation that has expectation of you earning your way into God's good pleasure. Here's the deal. Everything that is happening in verses 16 through 19 is happening because the servant is extending his grace to a soul in different ways. To put it differently, God does not provide the grace to pardon you without also providing the grace to let you repent truly. true repentance is a gift from God. True pardon is a gift from God. It's all of grace. It's all part of salvation. And it's all from the servant. The actions called for in verses 16 and 17 are not requirements to be saved. I'm just basically giving you the crisp version of Westminster Confession right now. It's not requirements for you to be saved, but your repentance will always be present when God exercises an act of changing, transformative, saving grace on you. You will repent. Really, it can be no other way because we see here people who are fundamentally corrupted and broken, people who cannot even see their sin, people who think they are repenting when they're not. How is a person like that ever to crawl out of that kind of pit of self-deception and delusion and futility of mind? How can they ever possibly repent on their own?

34 · Uses Augustine's maxim ('God empowers what he commands') and the healing miracles as analogies to explain how God grants repentance

Listen, guys, when God calls these people to repent, in verse 16 and 17, it is the exact same vibe, the exact same theological vibes, as when Jesus tells a lame man to rise and walk. He is, as Augustine would say, empowering what he commands. Repentance is part of God's gracious saving work. It does not cause God's gracious saving work, but it is never absent from God's gracious saving work. There's a reason why the apostles, as they were evaluating this really crazy development, Gentiles started getting saved in Acts 11. There's a reason they frame it the way they do in Acts 11, 18. They're watching all this happen. And listen to what they say. Repentance that leads to life is a gift from God. It is not an earning of salvation or forgiveness, but it is never absent from those things. And so we can conclude that true salvation that comes from God's servant always involves true repentance, not false repentance. And at the offer of come now, let us reason together, I will make you new. And the call to learn obedience, learn to change your ways, stop sinning, those are both calls made and empowered by the grace of the Lord Jesus. Because God sees his servant, a true salvation is possible for you and me. Not a cheap grace one, but a transformative one where we actually do change. Not a false signal, not a false repentance, but an actual transformation.

35 · Moves to the fourth and final point—'God sees the supper

And in verses 19 through 20, we see that final S, the one that I crammed in there. God sees sin, God sees his servant, God sees salvation, and God sees the supper. Look at verse 19. If you are willing and obedient, you shall eat the good of the land. But if you refuse and rebel, you shall be eaten by the sword, for the mouth of the Lord has spoken. God promises that those he transforms and saves, he will also bring into his presence, his fellowship, those whom he justifies, he also glorifies. If you are transformed, you will eat of the good of the land.

36 · Transitions from exposition to application by acknowledging the density of the material and deferring fuller application to the following week

Now, what do we do with all this? Really quickly, we can't do too much. That was just a lot that we had to get taken in to start acting on it. But I want you to consider this as we move into the next section next week. These folks were most likely blind to their sins because their sins were so normal either to them or to their culture. And so we can do that work together next week and continue to do that work. But we need to do that work also with the Lord this week.

37 · Closing prayer asking God to reveal hidden sin, directing the congregation to look to Jesus the servant, and requesting that the sermon would create souls hungry for fellowship with God

Let's pray. Create in me a clean heart, O Lord. Renew in me a steadfast spirit. Search me and know me, O Lord, and see if there is any offensive way in me. Let's pray. Lord God, we come before you and we would say, if you don't show us what you see, we'll never see. We're too limited and we're too broken. So we pray that through your holy word you would help us to see these things well, Lord. But God, we so praise your name that today in this moment, it's always the right time to look at Jesus. And so we do look to the servant, the one in whom your soul delights. We look to the servant and say, Lord Jesus, you have healed blind men before. If anyone here is misunderstanding their heart posture before you, would you as an act of kindness and grace reveal that to them? Not so that they could be less bad or something like that, but so that they could enjoy you and they could enjoy what you promised, the Laodiceans in Revelation 3, that behold, you stand at the door and knock and if anyone hears your voice and opens the door, you'll go in and you will eat with them and they with you. And Lord, we just pray that you would use this sermon to create souls that are hungry to eat with you, to be with you, to enjoy you, to know you, to fellowship with you. And that we would ask God that you would, through your servant, make all that possible. In Jesus' precious name, we pray. Amen.

38 · Closes the sermon by framing communion as a participation in the 'supper' mentioned in the sermon—both a present fellowship made possible by Jesus' sacrifice and a foretaste of the eschatological feast

Well, communion is our opportunity to look forward to this supper that Jesus has promised for us while also acknowledging we have fellowship with God right now because he has offered himself for our sins. And so if you're here today and you're a follower of Jesus, I would just encourage you to come and grab the elements, turn to your seat, and we'll partake of them together. Come.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we see four things as God sees them: sin as violation of purpose, judgment as consequence, his servant as provision, and salvation as gift that transforms. Monday through Friday walk us through God's vision of reality and his redemptive answer.

Monday Matthew 22:37-40

Jesus distills all of Scripture into two commands: love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, and mind, and love your neighbor as yourself. This is not added instruction—it is the original design written into our humanity. When we fail to love God and neighbor, we don't merely miss a goal; we violate the deepest purpose for which we were made.

Tuesday Psalm 139:23-24

David asks God to search his heart and know his thoughts, to reveal any wicked way in him. He recognizes that sin can hide itself from our own awareness—that we can be wrong about our own hearts. The habits and blindnesses we inherit from our families and culture run so deep that only God's vision can expose them. Do you ask him to search you?

Wednesday Isaiah 53:5-6

The servant was pierced for our transgressions; the punishment that brings us peace fell on him. God does not ignore our rebellion or offer cheap forgiveness. He offers real pardon—purchased at infinite cost by the One who bears the weight of our sin. Judgment fell on the servant so that we might receive mercy without God compromising his justice.

Thursday Acts 11:18

When Peter reports the salvation of Gentiles to the Jerusalem church, they recognize what God has done: 'Then to the Gentiles also God has granted repentance that leads to life.' Repentance is not something we manufacture or earn—it is granted. God gives both the power to turn and the direction toward life itself. Salvation without this gift is not salvation at all.

Friday Revelation 3:20

Jesus stands at the door and knocks; if anyone hears his voice and opens the door, he will come in and eat with that person. Salvation culminates not in escaping judgment but in intimate fellowship—the ancient image of the covenant meal restored. God's vision of salvation is not evacuation but reconciliation, not distance but table fellowship with him forever.

Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. Isaiah begins by saying 'Hear, O heavens, and give ear, O earth; for the Lord has spoken' (Isa 1:2). What does it mean that God is calling creation itself as a witness to what he's about to say? What does that tell us about the seriousness of what follows?
    Isaiah 1:2
    → When you think about God calling heaven and earth as witnesses, does that change how you read the rest of Isaiah 1—or how you think about God's judgment in general?
  2. In verses 5-6, Isaiah describes Israel's condition using the image of a body 'bruised from head to foot' with 'no soundness in it.' What is Isaiah saying about sin when he uses this language of sickness and injury rather than just legal guilt?
    Isaiah 1:5-6
  3. God rejects the worship in Isaiah 1:11-15—not because the people aren't showing up, but because their repentance isn't real. What do you think it means to have 'false repentance'? Can you think of a time when you've gone through the motions religiously without actually surrendering something?
    Isaiah 1:11-15
    → What made the difference between that false moment and a time when your repentance was genuine?
  4. Jesus says in Matthew 22:37-40 that all the law hangs on two commands: love God and love your neighbor. How does that help us understand what Isaiah means when he says sin is a violation of our created purpose? What are we actually violating when we sin?
    Matthew 22:37-40
  5. In verses 16-18, God calls Israel to 'cease to do evil, learn to do good' and promises 'though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow.' But some people read verse 18 alone and think it means God forgives without requiring any change. What does it mean that salvation includes both pardon and genuine transformation—that they're inseparable?
    Isaiah 1:16-18
    → Is there an area of your life where you've been hoping for pardon without the transformation? What would it look like to ask God for both?
  6. The sermon says that some sins become so normalized—by habit, family, or culture—that we can't see them anymore, and we end up engaging in spiritual activities without actually surrendering those sins. What are sins in your own life or in our culture that have become so normal that people barely notice them? How do we recover the ability to 'see' what God sees?
    Psalm 139:23-24
    → What role might the Holy Spirit play in showing us things we've become blind to? (See Psalm 139:23-24.)
