The first one is that I would say that all these other kings in this whole chain of events took. But Jesus gave the vast majority of Israel's kings hoarded earthly wealth. When Samuel warned the people about the kind of king they were going to get back in chapter eight of First Samuel, he listed all of these consequences with the word take. He says, the king will take your sons for his chariots and horsemen. He will take your daughters as perfumers and cooks and Bakers, he'll take the best of your fields, he'll take the 10th of your grain and your flocks. And in the end, you will be his slaves. That's the warning. How does Jesus shine in that moment? Because Jesus, the king of kings, they, all the other kings take from you. Jesus gives.

31 · Evangelistic application: stop worrying about what you give up to follow Jesus

I want to be clear, if some of you are kind of flirting around the edges of finally giving your life to Jesus, you are just totally doing the math wrong if you think that means giving up. He is saying, come here. And you're like, but what about. You have no idea what I'm going to give you. If you just follow me, I'm going to give you myself. I'm going to give you things that can't be gotten any other way. And you're like, I don't know if I want to follow him or not. I don't know. I have to give this up. I'm worried about stop. Every other system in this world, including the one you've cooked up in your own head, is going to take from you. It's going to leave you poorer. It's going to leave you worse off. But there's one king who gives. Every other king got wealthier when he took under the commission of the Lord. But the Bible says that while Jesus was rich in heaven, he became poor for your sake so that by his poverty, you might become rich. Philippians 2 says that while every other king is out there trying to make much of himself, Jesus took the form of a servant. Every other king in Samuel's warning will turn you into slaves. Jesus turned himself into a slave.

32 · Second Christological contrast: the kings ignored Scripture to their hurt; Jesus obeyed Scripture to His own hurt — all the way to the cross

That's one of the ways we see Jesus in this text. The second one is that all these other kings ignored the scriptures to their own hurt. Like there was always that dusty Bible in my dad's lost and found that if they had just grabbed it and read it, they would have been blessed. They would have received good things. They would have avoided terrible mistakes. Do you realize that Jesus followed God's law all the way to the cross? That he obeyed God's word to his own hurt. Not simply to his own help, but to his own hurt.

33 · Third Christological contrast: God's sovereignty works through all the chaos to deliver Jesus

Number three. The sovereignty of God displays Jesus. We see when we think about the revelation of Jesus Christ over the next thousand years as it unfolds from this moment, we see that God has worked through, through countless variables to bring the God man to earth, to be a person, to live a perfect life, to die a substitutionary death for you and for me. And then he worked through countless families, countless Jerry Springer moments in the Old Testament, countless genocidal kings, countless agendas and failures and so forth. And he did all of that to deliver you the treasure of the ages. Jesus Christ.

34 · Applies the sovereignty of God to anxiety: yes, complex systems are overwhelming, but God navigates all variables to accomplish His will

Friends, if you are a worrier, if you have anxious things, I want to tell you that fundamentally it actually makes sense to worry in some sense. And let me just argue for that for a moment. If you're worrying, you're probably in what is known as a complex system. And that system has so many variables that you can't control. And so you're just observing the reality. There's a thousand things that could happen that I can't influence or affect. And it's like, yeah, that's exactly right, man. Welcome. I have an ulcer named named Variables. It's my pet ulcer named Variables. No, it's exactly right. But look at what we see in this text and in the Bible in general, and that is that those complex variables are checkers, not even checkers to the Lord God. And he has navigated through incalculable variables to deliver his perfect will on time, every time, and ultimately in Christ.

35 · Cites Piper and Romans 8:28 to comfort believers: all the perplexing turns in your life are going somewhere good

John Piper writes in his really good book on Ruth, he says, all the perplexing turns in our lives are going somewhere good. And if you're in Christ, this is what Romans 8:28 means. All the perplexing turns in our lives are going somewhere good. They do not lead off a cliff. In all of the setbacks of our lives as believers, God is plotting our joy.

36 · Fourth Christological contrast: Solomon receives mercy 'for the sake of David

And finally, let me leave you one final reason that this text points us to Jesus. And that is we see some evidence, some light evidence, what you might call gospel headship on display. Look back at this sentence, this statement that God said to Solomon, he's judging Solomon. He's like, I'm going to really take away your legacy. But look at the kindness too. Look at verse 11. Therefore the Lord said to Solomon, since this has been your practice and you have not kept my covenant and my statutes that I commanded you, I will surely tear the kingdom from you and will give it to your servant. Look at verse 12. Yet for the sake of David, your father, I will not do it. In your days, I will tear it out of your hand, but I will tear it out of the hand of your son. Verse 13. However, I will not tear away all the kingdom, but I will give one tribe to your son. For the sake of David, my servant, for the sake of Jerusalem that I have chosen.

37 · Explicates the typology: if David's merit bought Solomon one generation of mercy, Christ's merit secures eternal mercy for you

So Solomon's just absolutely a shipwreck. He is just an absolute mess. He is using God's creation. He's trotting over people. He's using God's gift of wisdom. He's just a train wreck, but he still gets a little bit of mercy because of who he's related to. David's his dad, so at least he's got that going for him, which is nice. Do you see the little hint of your entire hope in life and death? And that is, is that you are connected to the one in whom God finds full favor. Jesus Christ. Listen, friends, if David had some merit that postponed the tearing of his kingdom by one generation, Christ's merit will keep you from being torn from God and from everything else. Like if David bought Solomon a little mercy, how much more mercy does Christ buy you?

38 · Reiterates the thesis and closes with doxological application: sin wrecks happiness but cannot stop God's plan to glorify Jesus

So as we've started in the beginning, sin will surely wreck our happiness. And not just our sin, sometimes other people's sin. And sometimes just the effects of sin in the world, the curse. But none of those things will ruin God's plan. And God's plan is to make much of Jesus. And I want to submit to you that if all he gave you in this life was Christ, he would have given you everything. You would be wealthy as a result of what he gave you. So as we partake of the table today, if you're a follower of Jesus Christ, come and feel blessed to know Jesus Christ and to have been given his righteousness through his perfect life, death and resurrection.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week, we trace God's sovereignty through a kingdom's collapse — from the seeds of grumbling in Samuel, through Solomon's refusal of warning, to the division that serves God's plan to display Jesus.

Monday 1 Samuel 8

Israel's demand for a king in 1 Samuel 8 was not a neutral request — it was grumbling against God's rule, a refusal to be satisfied with what He had provided. Samuel warned them plainly: the king you demand will take, exploit, and enslave you. Yet they insisted. Watch how their discontent echoes forward into Solomon's reign and explodes in Rehoboam's cruelty. The rotten fruit does not appear in one generation; it compounds across generations, each refusal to trust God making the next generation's idolatry easier to swallow.

Tuesday 1 Kings 11:1-8

Solomon was given more wisdom than any man who ever lived — yet he used that wisdom to multiply wives and accumulate gold, directly contradicting the law God had given him. The tragedy is not that Solomon lacked knowledge; it is that he had knowledge and refused it. He knew the commandment. He chose the counterfeit. His wives turned his heart from the living God, and the man whom God had crowned with unprecedented blessing became the instrument of his own family's ruin. Refused warnings do not disappear — they compound into catastrophe.

Wednesday Deuteronomy 17:18-20

God had given Israel's kings one simple command: keep a copy of the law and read it all the days of their life, so their hearts would not turn from God's way. This was not mysterious or hidden — it was inscribed, available, and meant to guard the king's affections. Rehoboam had access to this very passage. He had wise counsel from old men who would have pointed him to it. Yet he chose instead the counsel of his peers, who knew nothing of God's law and everything of human pride. When you face conflicting counsel, Scripture is not a luxury — it is your path forward, and your ignorance of where to find it is a choice, not an excuse.

Thursday 1 Kings 11:9-14

God's anger burned against Solomon because he had turned away from the covenant. The kingdom would be torn from his hand — yet not completely, and not at once. God would preserve one tribe for the sake of David, His beloved. This is the pattern of Scripture: human sin is always catastrophic, always consequential, always bearing bitter fruit in the present. But God's sovereignty is never threatened by human rebellion; it moves through it, over it, and beyond it, always bending history toward the display of Christ. Solomon's sin wrecked his dynasty, but it could not wreck God's purpose to bring forth a King who would obey perfectly and give everything away.

Friday Philippians 2

Every king of Israel took from the people — they demanded tribute, seized property, conscripted labor. They operated on the logic of power: extract, accumulate, rule. Jesus inverted that logic entirely. Though He was God, He emptied Himself and took the form of a servant. He obeyed the law of God perfectly, all the way to the cross, paying the price Solomon and every other king refused to pay. The taking kings could not save themselves or secure their thrones; the giving King secured eternal mercy for all who believe in Him. God's sovereignty was driving toward this reversal the whole time — every collapse of human kingship pointing toward the One King who gives Himself away and makes us rich forever.

Sunday-evening family table

What Are You Listening To?

For the parent

This prompt anchors in Rehoboam's failure to consult Scripture when he had conflicting counsel. The goal is to help your family see that when we're confused about what to do, we have something Rehoboam didn't fully use — the Bible itself. Listen for where your kids naturally turn when they're unsure, and gently point them toward God's Word as their anchor.

Rehoboam had to make a big decision, and he got advice from two different groups of people — the older men and his friends. He listened to the wrong group and it broke the whole kingdom in half. Right now, when you're confused about something — like whether to tell the truth when it might get you in trouble, or how to treat someone who's being mean to you — who or what do you listen to? And where could you look in the Bible to get God's answer instead?
works for ages 7+; teens can engage with the harder question of why we tend to listen to our friends instead of Scripture
