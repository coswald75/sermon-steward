5-day reading plan

This week we explore how the eldership qualifications in 1 Timothy 3 reveal the character of godly manhood that all Christian men are called to pursue, not merely those aspiring to formal office.

Monday 1 Timothy 3:1-7

Paul opens by affirming that desiring the office of elder is noble—yet the qualifications that follow are not peculiar to leadership alone. The qualities listed (temperate, respectable, hospitable, able to teach, not given to drunkenness, not violent) form the portrait of Christian maturity. We are reminded that these virtues are the goal of sanctification for every man in the congregation, not a rarified standard reserved for a few.

Tuesday 1 Timothy 3:8-13

The deacons' qualifications mirror the elders', underscoring that character is not situational—a man of integrity brings the same faithfulness to the table whether he serves formally or in his family and work. The emphasis on managing one's household well (verse 12) shows that godly manhood is lived first and foremost in the relationships nearest to us, where pretense cannot hide what we truly are.

Wednesday 1 Timothy 3:14-16

Paul grounds these character requirements in the reality that the church is 'the pillar and foundation of the truth' (verse 15). The body of Christ stands or falls on the spiritual maturity and integrity of its members, particularly its men. When we grasp that our character—or its deficiency—affects the corporate witness of God's people, the call to pursue these virtues becomes not burdensome duty but eager stewardship of our role in the church's testimony.

Thursday 1 Timothy 3:2-3

In a culture that often associates manhood with aggression and dominance, Paul's portrait challenges us: the mature man is temperate, gentle, not quarrelsome—qualities that reflect Christ's own character and the fruit of the Spirit. These virtues don't diminish a man's strength; they redirect it toward the building up of others, showing that true masculine leadership is expressed through restraint, wisdom, and a servant's heart.

Friday 1 Timothy 3:4-5

The requirement that an overseer 'must manage his own family well' is not incidental—it is the crucible where character is forged and tested. We are compelled by grace to take seriously our roles as husbands and fathers, understanding that how we love and lead at home is the truest measure of our fitness for any wider ministry. The gospel calls us to pursue this not as burden but as the privilege of reflecting Christ's love in the relationships where we have the most daily opportunity.

Pray together this week

Prayer for Godly Character in Our Men

Father, we come before you in awe of your design for manhood and leadership in your church. You have given us a portrait of character in 1 Timothy 3 that calls us to something far greater than the world's hollow ideals—a vision of men marked by self-control, gentleness, hospitable hearts, and faithful devotion to Christ. We adore your wisdom in establishing these standards not merely for those who would lead, but as the measure of godly manhood that all our men are called to pursue.

We confess that many of us fall short of this vision. We struggle with self-mastery, we grow defensive rather than gentle, we are careless with our families and neglectful of one another, we chase significance in the eyes of the world rather than the eyes of our Savior. None of us perfectly demonstrates the character you have set before us, and we grieve the gap between what you call us to be and what we often are.

Yet the gospel humbles us with immeasurable grace. In Christ we have forgiveness for every failure, and through His Spirit we have the power to put off the old self and put on new character (Ephesians 4:22–24). He lived the self-controlled, gentle, faithful manhood we cannot conjure on our own, and in Him we are being remade. Every man in our fellowship is called and equipped to grow in these virtues not by striving, but by the grace that flows from knowing we are loved beyond measure in Christ.

We petition you now: strengthen our men to pursue godly character with glad hearts. Give them the courage to name their struggles to one another, the humility to seek help, and the faith to believe that you are faithful to complete the good work you began in them. Help us, as a church, to encourage these men in the hard, joyful work of becoming like Christ. Grant us all the vision to see that this pursuit of manhood is not burdensome duty but the natural response of hearts overwhelmed by grace.
