  1. Faithful witness consists in doing all the Lord commands, regardless of whether the listener responds positively. unit #8

All Right now for communion this morning, I want to draw our attention to an even greater witness than Moses and Aaron. An even greater sign than a staff turning into a snake. God, in sovereignly ordaining for Jesus to be crucified, he he has given us a sign of his sending authority, his sovereignty, his supremacy. An accomplishment that like the snake in Egypt, has swallowed up sin and death forever. And for those who would bow their knees to Jesus, repent and believe on his salvation. Jesus was pierced for our transgressions. He was crushed for our iniquities. Upon him was a chastisement that brought us peace. And with his wounds we are healed. Ultimately, that God would deliver a people from slavery, not in Egypt, but as we saw last week, deliver us from from slavery to sin and free us to worship and serve him wherever we are. A sight of his suffering can soften the hardest of hearts. A sight of his cross can bend the stiffest of knees. And a celebration of his resurrection can give hope to the farest off people. May we see the sign today, his cross and celebrated God who is witnessed to us of his great justice and love. Knowing that because of this Satan has been defeated, the power of sin broken, and our acceptance with God as our Father, God as our Father secured, and ultimately our ability empowered to testify, to proclaim and to declare that the Lord is God to the ends of Johnson county and the ends of the earth.

5-day reading plan

This week we follow the arc of God's sovereign grace from the foundation of His authority to witness, through the paradox of divine hardening and human responsibility, to the supremacy of Christ as the ultimate sign that swallows up all lesser witnesses.

Monday Romans 9:15

Paul anchors God's electing mercy not in human worthiness or effort, but in God's free will alone—"I will have mercy on whom I have mercy." This is the bedrock of the sermon's claim: God's sovereignty in salvation is not conditioned by our response, but flows from His own gracious determination. We begin here because every other truth about witness, hardening, and supremacy rests on this foundation of God's unfettered, glorious freedom.

Tuesday Romans 9:17

Paul quotes Exodus to show that God raised up Pharaoh precisely so His name might be declared throughout the earth—hardening became the instrument of God's universal glory. The sermon's claim finds its theological justification here: God's sovereignty includes not just mercy but also the hardening of hearts, and this hardening serves His ultimate purpose of making Himself known. Our witness, like God's judgment of Pharaoh, is always caught up in something far larger than an individual's decision.

Wednesday Romans 9:14-18

Paul does not resolve this tension by choosing one side—he holds both truths: God has mercy on whom He wills, yet Pharaoh's heart was hardened by Pharaoh's own choice and God's decree working together. This paradox guards us from two errors: neither fatalism that excuses human rebellion, nor human autonomy that denies God's absolute control. We live faithfully in this mystery, knowing that our obedience matters even though God's purposes are already secured.

Thursday Genesis 3

The serpent in Genesis 3 brought death and exile through rebellion against God's word; in Exodus 7, God's rod becomes a serpent that swallows Egypt's serpents whole, signaling that the ancient enemy's dominion is being undone. The sermon's claim reveals that Exodus is not merely political deliverance—it is God's public victory over the cosmic serpent, with Pharaoh as the serpent's earthly agent. When we witness to Christ today, we participate in this same cosmic war, announcing the supremacy of the One who has already defeated Satan's final weapon.

Friday Isaiah 53:5

The Exodus delivered God's people from Egypt through judgment and blood; Christ's death delivers all peoples from sin and death through His own blood, swallowing up the final enemy forever. The sermon shows that every sign God gave Pharaoh pointed to this: the cross is the definitive witness to God's sending authority, His sovereign power to save, and His supremacy over all forces—sin, death, and Satan included. Our faithful witness this week, whether heard or rejected, participates in the proclamation of this greater sign that has already won the cosmos.

Pray together this week

That We May Know God's Supremacy

Father, we come before You in awe of Your absolute sovereignty and supremacy over all things. You alone determine the hearts of men, and You alone possess the right to show mercy and to harden whom You will (Romans 9:18). We marvel that even the hardness of human hearts serves Your purpose to proclaim Your great name throughout the earth, and that nothing—no earthly power, no rebellion, no darkness—can thwart Your eternal counsel.

We confess that we often shrink from faithful witness, hesitating to proclaim Your word because we fear rejection or because we measure our faithfulness by the response of others. We admit that we sometimes doubt Your power, looking for signs and wonders rather than trusting that the gospel itself is Your power unto salvation. Forgive us for our small faith and our desire to control outcomes that belong to You alone.

We thank You that in the gospel we behold a sign far greater than any staff turning to serpent—the crucifixion and resurrection of Jesus Christ, where You swallowed up sin and death forever and demonstrated Your ultimate sovereignty over Satan and all His works (Genesis 3). In Christ's finished work, You have shown us the supreme sign of Your authority, Your mercy, and Your power to save. This is the gospel that saves, not by our eloquence or by miraculous display, but by the Spirit's work in hearts You have chosen.

Grant us courage to proclaim Your word faithfully, regardless of whether we see the response we hope for. Give us wisdom to trust that You are at work even when hearts seem hardened, and help us rest in Your sovereignty as we witness. Make us ambassadors of reconciliation who speak the gospel of Christ knowing that salvation belongs to You alone, and that our obedience is the only outcome we can control. We commit ourselves afresh to Your glory and the exaltation of Your name among all peoples.

To You, O God, belongs all honor, power, and dominion, now and forever. Amen.
