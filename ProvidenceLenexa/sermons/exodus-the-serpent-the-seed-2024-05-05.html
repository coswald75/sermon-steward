Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the promise of Genesis 3:15 through Scripture's unfolding witness—from the serpent's ancient deception to Christ's final victory—seeing how Jesus alone fulfills what all other candidates failed to accomplish.

Monday Genesis 3

In the account of humanity's first fall, God does not leave us to our ruin. The promise embedded in the curse reveals the gospel's skeleton: a coming champion will undo what the serpent accomplished through deception, yet the victory will cost him a wound. This first proclamation of redemption teaches us that Christ's triumph was always God's intention, written into the very moment of our rebellion.

Tuesday John 8:44

Jesus identifies the serpent not merely as a creature but as a personal enemy—the devil himself—who wages war through falsehood and seeks our death. This clarifies what Genesis 3:15 promises: not victory over a mere animal, but conquest over the spiritual rebel whose lies enslaved the human race. Our Savior's warfare is not merely physical but cosmic, engaging the ancient adversary at the root of all deception.

Wednesday Matthew 1:18-23

Matthew's genealogy and annunciation show us the seed arriving without the stain of human sin or the envy and impatience that ruined every predecessor. Jesus is Emmanuel—God himself entering our flesh—which no other candidate could claim. In his incarnation, we see the fulfillment of the ancient promise: one who is truly and fully qualified, untainted by the serpent's corruption, able to stand where Adam fell and where Israel stumbled.

Thursday Matthew 3:7-12

John the Baptist's testimony reveals Christ as the judge who separates the seed of the woman from the serpent's brood. The winnowing fork in his hand is not mere judgment; it is the vindication of the promise—the seed has come with power to execute what was spoken in Genesis 3:15. We who are in Christ participate in his victory, knowing that his Spirit empowers us to bear the fruit that testifies to the serpent's defeat.

Friday Revelation 20:1-3, 7-10

The promise made in Genesis finds its completion in Revelation: the serpent who deceived the nations is seized, bound, and ultimately cast into eternal judgment. Yet this vision reminds us that the battle continues until Christ returns; we live between the wound inflicted at the cross and the final crushing at his appearing. Until that day, the Lord's Supper nourishes us in the wilderness, proclaiming Christ's death and reminding us that we are invited to feast with the one who has conquered death itself.

Pray together this week

The Seed Who Crushes the Serpent

Father, we come before you in awe of your mercy and your perfect plan. You spoke into the darkness of our rebellion a promise that would echo through the ages—that a Seed would come, born of a woman, to crush the serpent's head and undo the ruin sin has wrought (Genesis 3:15). We adore you that in your sovereign grace, you did not leave us orphaned in our enemy's domain, but set in motion a redemption that would cost your own Son his blood.

We confess that we are prone to wander from this gospel truth. We trust in our own strength, our own righteousness, our own leadership—forgetting that every human candidate for salvation has failed, wounded by envy, impatience, and lust. We live as though the serpent still reigns, rather than as those ransomed by the Seed who bore the wound to crush our enemy (Romans 16:20). Forgive us for our unbelief, and grant us eyes to see Jesus in every promise and every type.

In the gospel, we have received the One who combines all virtue without vice—who holds firstborn status, divine authority, and infinite strength, yet remained sinless and obedient unto death. Through his resurrection and exaltation, the serpent's head is crushed, his authority is broken, and we stand secure in Christ's victory. Every time we come to his table in remembrance, we proclaim his death and our deliverance until he returns in triumph (Matthew 26:3-5; Revelation 20:1-3).

Give us grace, O Lord, to feast with joy at the Lord's Supper, sustained as your people in the wilderness of this age. Strengthen our faith to recognize Jesus as our true Seed, our rightful King, and our perfect Savior. Make us bold to proclaim his name to a world still enslaved to the serpent's lies, confident that his kingdom cannot be shaken. To you, the Father almighty, through Jesus Christ our Lord, in the power of the Holy Spirit, be all glory, honor, and dominion forever.
