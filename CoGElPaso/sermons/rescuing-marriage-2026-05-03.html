Rescuing Marriage

May 3, 2026 Pastor Sal Valenzuela
Thesis Marriage must be rescued by recovering its God-designed purpose as an image of Christ's covenant love for the church, which requires repentance from cultural accommodation and renewed submission to biblical authority.

0 · The pastor frames the sermon's central problem by acknowledging the cultural crisis around marriage but redirecting attention to the church's failure to model biblical marriage, thus establishing the need for internal correction rather than external critique alone

Turn with me if you would in your Bibles to the book of Genesis chapter 2. Genesis chapter 2. We're going to be looking at verses 18 through 25 this morning. Genesis 2:18-25. We're going to be talking about marriage this morning. And I want to begin by acknowledging something that I think all of us understand, which is that marriage is under attack in our culture. That's not news to anybody. Marriage is struggling. The institution of marriage, the biblical idea of marriage, is being questioned, is being redefined, is being set aside by many people in our culture. And that should concern us. That should concern us as Christians. But I want to suggest to you this morning that the real crisis with marriage is not primarily what's happening out there in the culture, as troubling as that is. The real crisis with marriage is what's happening in here, in the church. Because the truth is, the church has not done a very good job of rescuing marriage, of presenting marriage as God designed it, of living out marriage as God intended it.

1 · The pastor reads the primary text aloud, providing the biblical foundation for the sermon's argument about God's design for marriage as recorded in the creation narrative

So let's look at God's Word together. Genesis 2, beginning in verse 18: 'Then the LORD God said, "It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him a helper fit for him." Now out of the ground the LORD God had formed every beast of the field and every bird of the heavens and brought them to the man to see what he would call them. And whatever the man called every living creature, that was its name. The man gave names to all livestock and to the birds of the heavens and to every beast of the field. But for Adam there was not found a helper fit for him. So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the man. Then the man said, "This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man." Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh. And the man and his wife were both naked and were not ashamed.'

2 · The pastor establishes the foundational theological claim that marriage is a divine institution rather than human invention, thereby grounding the authority for marriage definition in God rather than human preference, which directly counters cultural assumptions

Now, the first thing I want you to see in this text is that marriage is God's idea. Marriage is God's design. Marriage is God's institution. That seems obvious when you read this text, but it's important to say it clearly because in our culture today, marriage is treated as if it's a human invention, as if it's something that we can define however we want, as if it's something that we can change and adapt and modify to fit our preferences and our desires. But that's not what the Bible teaches. The Bible teaches that marriage is God's idea. God designed marriage. God instituted marriage. And that means that God gets to define what marriage is and how marriage works and what the purpose of marriage is. We don't get to make up our own definitions. We don't get to redefine marriage according to our own preferences. God created marriage, and therefore God defines marriage.

3 · The pastor exposits the text's declaration that solitude is 'not good' and unpacks the Hebrew term 'ezer' (helper), correcting misunderstanding of the term by showing it carries no connotation of inferiority since it's used of God Himself, thereby establishing complementarity rather than hierarchy as the text's emphasis

Now let's look at what God says about marriage in this text. First, notice that God says, 'It is not good that the man should be alone.' That's a remarkable statement because everything else that God has made in creation, He's looked at and said, 'It is good.' But now He looks at the man by himself and says, 'It is not good.' Why? Because God designed human beings for relationship. God designed human beings for companionship. God designed human beings for community. And so God says, 'I will make him a helper fit for him.' Now that word 'helper' is sometimes misunderstood. Some people think that word 'helper' means that the woman is inferior to the man, that the woman is just there to serve the man, that the woman is somehow second-class. But that's not what the Bible teaches. That word 'helper' in Hebrew is the word 'ezer,' and it's used most often in the Old Testament to describe God Himself as our helper. So when God says He's going to make a helper for Adam, He's saying He's going to make someone who comes alongside Adam, someone who complements Adam, someone who is suitable for Adam, someone who corresponds to Adam.

4 · The pastor traces the narrative's progression from Adam naming the animals to his awareness of his unique aloneness, arguing this was divinely orchestrated pedagogy to make Adam conscious of his need before God provided the solution, thus showing marriage as God's purposeful answer to designed human incompleteness

So God brings all the animals to Adam, and Adam names them. And as Adam is naming all these animals, he's seeing pairs. He's seeing male and female. He's seeing that every creature has a companion, has a mate. But for Adam, there's no one. Verse 20 says, 'But for Adam there was not found a helper fit for him.' And I think as Adam is going through this process of naming the animals, there's a growing awareness, there's a growing sense of his aloneness, there's a growing sense of his incompleteness. And that's exactly what God wanted Adam to feel. Because God wanted Adam to understand that he needed someone, that he was designed for relationship, that he was made for companionship. And then, having established that need, having established that sense of incompleteness, God acts. Verse 21: 'So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh. And the rib that the LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the man.'

5 · The pastor derives theological meaning from the text's physical description of woman's creation from man's rib, arguing this method establishes fundamental unity, shared nature, and equality, explicitly rejecting hierarchical interpretations by emphasizing positional symbolism (side rather than head or foot) and Adam's joyful recognition

Now, there's something really important we need to see here. God doesn't create the woman from the dust of the ground like He created the man. God creates the woman from the man. He takes a rib from Adam's side and forms the woman from that rib. Why does God do it that way? Because God is establishing the fundamental unity of man and woman. God is establishing that man and woman are of the same substance, they share the same nature, they are equal in dignity and worth and value. The woman is not created from Adam's head, so that she would be over him. The woman is not created from Adam's foot, so that she would be under him. The woman is created from Adam's side, from his rib, so that she would be beside him, so that she would be equal with him, so that she would be his companion and his partner. And this is why Adam responds the way he does. Look at verse 23. When God brings the woman to Adam, Adam says, 'This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.' There's joy in that statement. There's delight in that statement. There's recognition in that statement.

6 · The pastor exposits Genesis 2:24 as God's definitive commentary on marriage, identifying three essential components—leaving (establishing a new primary relationship), cleaving (permanent covenant commitment), and becoming one flesh (comprehensive union beyond merely physical)—that together constitute God's definition of marriage

And then we come to verse 24, which is absolutely crucial for understanding what marriage is. Verse 24 says, 'Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.' Now, this is God's commentary on what He has just done. This is God's interpretation of the creation of marriage. This is God's definition of what marriage is. And notice three things that God says here. First, marriage involves leaving. 'A man shall leave his father and his mother.' Marriage involves a new primary relationship. When you get married, your relationship with your spouse becomes your primary human relationship. It takes priority over your relationship with your parents, it takes priority over your relationship with your friends, it takes priority over every other human relationship. That doesn't mean you stop honoring your parents, but it does mean that your spouse now comes first. Second, marriage involves cleaving. 'Hold fast to his wife.' That word 'hold fast' means to cling to, to be joined to, to be glued to. It speaks of permanent commitment. It speaks of covenant faithfulness. It speaks of loyalty and devotion. And third, marriage involves becoming one flesh. 'They shall become one flesh.' That speaks of the sexual union in marriage, yes. But it speaks of more than just the sexual union. It speaks of the total union of two lives—physically, emotionally, spiritually. Two separate individuals becoming one united whole.

7 · The pastor signals a structural shift from describing marriage's nature to explaining marriage's purpose, setting up the sermon's movement toward deeper theological meaning

Now, so far we've been looking at what marriage is according to God's design in creation. But if we stop there, we haven't yet answered the most important question, which is: Why did God create marriage? What's the purpose of marriage? What's marriage for?

8 · The pastor introduces Ephesians 5 to provide the New Testament's redemptive-historical interpretation of Genesis 2:24, revealing that marriage was designed from creation to image Christ's covenant relationship with the church, thus establishing marriage's ultimate purpose as gospel display rather than human fulfillment

And to answer that question, we need to turn to the New Testament. Turn with me to Ephesians chapter 5. Ephesians chapter 5, beginning in verse 25. Paul is talking about marriage, and he writes this: 'Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her, that he might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of water with the word, so that he might present the church to himself in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or any such thing, that she might be holy and without blemish. In the same way husbands should love their wives as their own bodies. He who loves his wife loves himself. For no one ever hated his own flesh, but nourishes and cherishes it, just as Christ does the church, because we are members of his body. "Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and hold fast to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh." This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church.' Now, this is absolutely stunning. Paul quotes Genesis 2:24, the verse we just looked at, and then he says, 'This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church.' What is Paul saying? He's saying that marriage, from the very beginning, was designed to be a picture of the relationship between Christ and the church. Marriage is not ultimately about us. Marriage is ultimately about displaying the gospel. Marriage is ultimately about imaging God's covenant love.

9 · The pastor draws out the theological implications of marriage as gospel image, showing how each element of marriage (husband's love, wife's submission, permanence, exclusivity, intimacy) corresponds to specific aspects of Christ's relationship with the church, thereby arguing that marriage's importance derives from its function as testimony to Christ's glory

This changes everything. This means that when we understand marriage rightly, we understand it as a living picture of the gospel. The husband's love for his wife is meant to reflect Christ's sacrificial love for the church. The wife's submission to her husband is meant to reflect the church's joyful submission to Christ. The permanence of marriage is meant to reflect the unbreakable covenant that Christ has made with His people. The exclusivity of marriage—one man, one woman—is meant to reflect Christ's exclusive claim on His church. The intimacy of marriage is meant to reflect the intimacy that Christ has with His people. Everything about marriage is meant to point beyond itself to the greater reality of Christ and the church. And this is why marriage is so important. This is why marriage is worth fighting for. This is why we need to rescue marriage and restore marriage and recover God's vision for marriage. Not just because marriage is good for us, though it is. Not just because marriage is good for society, though it is. But ultimately because marriage is meant to put the glory of Christ on display.

10 · The pastor applies the gospel-imaging purpose of marriage directly to married couples through specific, concrete questions that operationalize how husbands should love sacrificially (like Christ) and wives should submit joyfully (like the church), while emphasizing covenant permanence and calling both to see their marriage as witness

So let me bring this home. If you're married this morning, I want to ask you: Is your marriage displaying the gospel? Is your marriage putting Christ on display? Husbands, are you loving your wives the way Christ loved the church? That means sacrificially. That means laying down your life for her. That means putting her needs above your own. That means leading her in a way that serves her and nourishes her and cherishes her. Wives, are you submitting to your husbands the way the church submits to Christ? That means joyfully, not grudgingly. That means respecting him and honoring him and supporting him. That means trusting God's design even when it's hard. And both husbands and wives, are you committed to the permanence of your covenant? Are you fighting for your marriage when it's hard? Are you pursuing reconciliation when there's conflict? Are you extending forgiveness when there's sin? Are you doing whatever it takes to preserve and protect and strengthen your marriage? Because your marriage is not just about you. Your marriage is meant to be a witness to the watching world of the covenant love of Christ.

11 · The pastor extends application to single people by showing how the gospel-imaging purpose of marriage should shape their preparation for potential marriage (through discernment based on gospel compatibility) and their embrace of singleness (by demonstrating Christ's sufficiency), thus connecting singles to the sermon's theological vision

If you're single this morning, this matters for you too. Because this vision of marriage should inform how you think about marriage, how you prepare for marriage if God calls you to it, and how you live in singleness if God doesn't. If you understand that marriage is meant to display the gospel, then you'll be discerning about who you marry. You won't just marry someone because you're attracted to them or because they make you happy. You'll marry someone who shares your commitment to Christ and who can help you display the gospel in your marriage. And if you remain single, you can display the gospel in your singleness by showing that Christ is sufficient, that Christ satisfies, that your ultimate identity and fulfillment is found in Him, not in a spouse.

12 · The pastor steps outside the expositional flow to address those in struggling marriages directly with pastoral comfort, pivoting from the ideal vision to realistic hope by asserting that the gospel which marriage images is also the power that can restore broken marriages, while issuing specific action steps toward reconciliation

Now, I know some of you are sitting here this morning and your marriage is hurting. Your marriage is struggling. Maybe you're thinking, 'I wish my marriage looked like what you're describing, but it doesn't. My marriage is a mess. We're barely holding it together.' If that's you, I want you to hear this: There is hope. There is hope because the gospel that your marriage is meant to display is also the gospel that can heal your marriage. The same grace that saved you is the grace that can transform your marriage. The same forgiveness that you've received from Christ is the forgiveness you can extend to your spouse. The same power that raised Jesus from the dead is the power that can resurrect a dying marriage. So don't give up. Fight for your marriage. Pursue help. Get biblical counseling. Humble yourself. Confess your sin. Extend forgiveness. Do whatever it takes. Because God is in the business of rescuing marriages.

13 · The pastor concludes by returning to the sermon's opening tension (marriage under attack) but now reframing the primary threat as the church's internal failure rather than external opposition, then issues a prayer-form charge that God would give the congregation a theologically grounded vision for marriage that transcends self-interest and serves gospel witness

Let me close with this. Marriage is under attack in our culture. But the greatest threat to marriage is not what's happening out there. The greatest threat to marriage is our failure in here—in the church—to understand marriage, to honor marriage, to live out marriage according to God's design. So my prayer is that God would use His Word today to rescue marriage in this church. That He would give us a vision for marriage that's bigger than our own happiness, bigger than our own fulfillment, bigger than our own preferences. A vision for marriage that sees marriage as God intended it—as a picture of Christ and the church, as a display of the gospel, as a testimony to God's covenant faithfulness. May God rescue marriage in our day. And may He start with us.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we recover marriage's rescuing purpose by grounding it in God's sovereign design, the equal dignity of man and woman, Christ's covenant love, and the gospel witness of our unions.

Monday Ephesians 5:25-32

Paul anchors the entire portrait of Christian marriage in Christ's own covenant love—the pattern that reveals why marriage exists at all. When we recover God's authority over marriage rather than accepting culture's redefinition, we repent of autonomy and submit ourselves to the One who created it. This passage shows us that marriage's meaning is not ours to invent; it is revealed in and through the mystery of Christ and the church.

Tuesday Ephesians 5:25-32

Notice how Paul describes the husband and wife as 'one flesh'—the same language used in Genesis 2:24 to capture the profound union established when woman was formed from man's rib. This passage deepens our understanding that equality in dignity and partnership is not a modern invention but the very design embedded in creation itself. We, as married couples and as the church, are called to honor this complementary partnership as God's good design, not as an afterthought or concession.

Wednesday Ephesians 5:25-32

The apostle presents the husband's sacrificial love and the wife's joyful submission not as power dynamics but as gospel choreography—a living display of how Christ gave Himself for the church and how the church responds in trusting devotion. When we grasp that our marriages are meant to *image* this covenant reality, we see that the details of how we love, submit, and cleave to one another are not merely personal preferences but gospel witness to a watching world. The mystery Paul celebrates is that in our ordinary unions, the glory of Christ's redemptive love becomes visible.

Thursday Ephesians 5:25-32

Paul's vision compels us to honest self-examination: Are our marriages portraying Christ's sacrificial devotion, or are we withholding, demanding, using one another? Are we pursuing permanence as covenant oath or treating marriage as a revocable arrangement? As we sit with this passage, the Spirit invites us to repent where we have dimmed the gospel's shine through our selfishness, and to recommit to the sacrificial, submissive, permanent love that testifies to Christ's faithfulness.

Friday Ephesians 5:25-32

Though this passage addresses married couples, Paul's grounding of marriage in Christ and the church establishes the standard for those considering marriage: a partner who shares your gospel commitment and vision to image Christ together. For the unmarried among us, this passage liberates you to singleness as a fully valid calling—one that witnesses to the church's complete sufficiency in Christ alone, needing no earthly union to be whole. Whether married or single, we are called to testify to Christ's all-sufficient love.
