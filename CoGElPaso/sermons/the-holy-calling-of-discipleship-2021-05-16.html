The Holy Calling of Discipleship

Mark 9:42-50 May 16, 2021 Pastor Tom Wilkins
Thesis Jesus calls his disciples to radical holiness and humble mission, warning them that causing others to stumble or persisting in unrepentant sin brings God's wrath, while promising that suffering for his sake will purify them into preserving, peaceful witnesses who exalt Christ.

Theological claims· 12
  1. The Word of God actively examines and changes us rather than passively waiting for our evaluation. unit #2
  2. The believer's propensity to wander from God, confessed in song, is confirmed and intensified by the hard truths of today's passage. unit #5
  3. Jesus warns that causing new or humble believers to stumble warrants judgment so severe that drowning would be preferable. unit #10
  4. The church must continually preach the foundational gospel truths to new believers, not relegating them as "basic" but recognizing them as essential and central. unit #12
  5. The text commands radical holiness without resolving how grace enables obedience; Jesus simply says, "Take sin seriously and turn from it." unit #18
  6. The text establishes four realities: sin is horrific, hell is real, heaven is real, and repentance is the call — all without yet explaining Christ's atoning work. unit #19
  7. Repentance is the universal call: believers must turn from sin completely, and unbelievers must turn to Christ or face eternal, inescapable judgment. unit #20
  8. Jesus calls both believers and unbelievers to radical holiness, refuting presumption on grace, while offering himself as the only means of salvation for those who cannot save themselves. unit #21
  9. Jesus promises suffering to his disciples, both the common suffering of fallen humanity and the specific suffering that comes from following him. unit #25
  10. Suffering will persist until Jesus returns in glory to vindicate his people, and this suffering serves a purpose in Jesus' refining work. unit #26
  11. Suffering's purpose is to drive believers to Christ himself, where peace is found not in answers but in the character of God revealed in his Word. unit #28
  12. Jesus is sanctifying the church through holy demands and suffering to shape believers into a preserving, peaceful influence in the world. unit #31

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the arc of Christ's call to radical holiness: from God's Word as active examiner, through the reality of sin and judgment, to suffering as sanctifying grace that shapes us into witnesses of peace.

Monday Romans 6:1

Paul's rhetorical question — 'Shall we go on sinning that grace may increase?' — exposes the human heart's tendency to presume upon grace rather than flee from sin. The sermon's warning against this exact presumption shows us that Christ's radical call to holiness directly confronts our drift toward spiritual complacency. We cannot hear the severity of his demands without being forced to examine whether we, too, harbor this dangerous assumption.

Tuesday 1 Timothy 3:3-5

Paul's instruction to Timothy on the character requirements for church leaders emphasizes that spiritual maturity and stability cannot be rushed or presumed. These standards reflect the same concern Jesus expresses: that leaders and mature believers bear responsibility for the spiritual formation of those newer in faith. When we recognize how critical this formation is, we understand why the sermon calls us to continually reinforce gospel truth — it is the bedrock on which all faithful living and leadership rest.

Wednesday Revelation 19

In Revelation's vision of Christ's return and final judgment, we see the consummation of all things — the vindication of the righteous and the finality of separation from God for those who refuse him. This panoramic view of eternity illuminates why Jesus spoke with such intensity about sin and repentance in Mark 9: the stakes are absolute and irrevocable. The seriousness of his warnings flows from the seriousness of reality itself — heaven and hell are not metaphors but eternal destinations that demand our whole-hearted turn to Christ.

Thursday Revelation 19

The same passage that displays Christ's ultimate triumph also portrays the opposition and affliction his followers endure before his return. Suffering is woven into the Christian story not as accident but as part of our discipleship — a reality promised, not explained away. Understanding that Jesus himself was 'perfected through suffering' (Hebrews 5:8-9) reframes our trials not as failures of faith but as the very means by which we are refined into his likeness and shaped into witnesses who point others to him.

Friday 1 Timothy 3:3-5

Paul's charge to leaders — that they be 'self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach' and manage their own families — describes people whose holiness is visible and whose peace is infectious. These are the marks of disciples refined by suffering, those who have learned that their words and presence either draw others toward Christ or lead them astray. As we examine ourselves this week, we ask: Are we the kind of disciples whose lives, having been sanctified by grace and suffering alike, leave others with genuine peace and a clearer vision of Jesus?
