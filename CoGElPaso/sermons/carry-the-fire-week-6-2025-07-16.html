Illustrations· 5
  1. personal story · unit #1 — The pastor narrates his personal discovery of hidden idolatry—placing his wife and children above God—which surfaced during a crisis involving his wife's health and following the deaths of two infant children. The story demonstrates how God reveals hidden sin in believers' hearts through the removal of His felt presence, leading to confession and restoration.
  2. cultural reference · unit #9 — Uses the well-known Footprints poem as a cultural illustration of God's nearness during suffering. The illustration makes the abstract doctrine of divine presence emotionally accessible by depicting God carrying believers through their hardest seasons when they feel most alone.
  3. personal story · unit #17 — Extended personal narrative of adopting John during the same hospital stay when the pastor and his wife lost their daughter Hannah. The story demonstrates the providential orchestration of multiple factors (the timing, the nurse from Michael's NICU, Dr. Mendoza's presence, Judy's surprising clarity) leading to their decision to adopt.
  4. historical example · unit #20 — Briefly references the parable of the prodigal son to illustrate the father's active love and watchfulness for the returning son, emphasizing the father's initiative in seeing and running to meet the son.
  5. personal story · unit #25 — Illustrates the reality of dramatic experiential encounters with the Holy Spirit using Angelica's recent filling as a contemporary example and the pastor's own first filling as a historical example. The Grand Canyon analogy makes the memorability of such moments accessible.
Theological claims· 7
  1. God faithfully uses difficult circumstances to expose and remove hidden idols in believers' hearts as part of the sanctification process. unit #2
  2. In genuine adoption, adopted children are fully and equally legitimate children, indistinguishable in status and affection from natural-born children. unit #18
  3. God sees believers as fully legitimate sons and daughters—joint heirs with Christ—despite our having been His enemies, just as adopted children are fully legitimate members of their new families. unit #19
  4. God the Father initiates salvation by His Spirit—causing enemies to stop running, turn back, and receive the grace and faith necessary to believe. unit #21
  5. The parable of the prodigal son is fundamentally about the father's love and character, not the son's sin and repentance. unit #22
  6. The regular, ordinary means by which the Holy Spirit fills believers is through the study of God's Word, which the Spirit makes living and personally applicable. unit #26
  7. Believers experience the extraordinary presence of the Holy Spirit through three ordinary means: Spirit-enlivened Scripture, simple honest prayer to our Heavenly Father, and the Spirit-created unity of the gathered church worshiping together. unit #27

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace God's faithfulness through five ordinary means of grace: His Word, His adoption of us, His presence in prayer, His character revealed through Scripture, and His gathered church testifying to His nearness.

Monday Galatians 4:4-7

Paul anchors our freedom not in our own effort but in God's sending of His Son to redeem us and grant us adoption as His children. Notice that adoption here is not a lesser status—it is the fullness of sonship. We cry 'Abba, Father' with the same intimacy and claim as Christ Himself, heirs of God because we are His children by grace.

Tuesday Joshua 1:5-9

Joshua faces the terrifying task of leading Israel into a hostile land. God's answer is not to remove the difficulty but to promise His presence through it: 'I will not leave you or forsake you.' The hard obedience—the battles ahead—becomes the means by which Joshua learns to trust God completely. Our own difficulties are similarly designed by God to strip away false refuges and deepen our reliance on Him alone.

Wednesday Psalm 34:15-18

The psalmist promises that the Lord's eyes are on the righteous and His ears open to their cry—God is near, listening, responding. This nearness is not mystical or distant; it is the intimacy of a Father who hears the prayer of His broken children and draws near to comfort them. When we cry out to God in honest prayer, we are not reaching into a void but speaking to One already attending to us.

Thursday Luke 15

The father's scandalous love—running to embrace the returning son, throwing a celebration before the son can even confess—reveals God's character. This is not conditional adoption. The father saw his son while he was still far off and ran toward him, not because the son had earned restoration but because the father is a father. This is the God who initiates salvation and receives us as His beloved children.

Friday Revelation 3:20

Jesus stands at the door and knocks, inviting us to open it so that He might come in and eat with us. This intimate, personal fellowship—Him speaking to us, us responding—happens when we open the Word and let the Spirit speak to our specific circumstances today. His nearness is not reserved for the extraordinary; it comes through the ordinary rhythm of reading, listening, and responding to His voice in Scripture.

Prayer of Adoption and Nearness

Father, we come before you in awe of your nearness. You have not left us as orphans, but you have sent your Spirit to dwell in us, to confirm that we are your beloved children, fully legitimate members of your family. We marvel that you call us 'Abba'—that intimate name that speaks of your fatherly tenderness toward us. We adore you for the character that pursues us, that exposes the hidden idols in our hearts not in anger but in love, that you might free us to love you more fully.

We confess, Father, that we often live as though you are distant. We carry on spiritual warfare without calling on your nearness. We harbor secret idolatries that we do not want to face, yet your Spirit faithfully reveals them—not to shame us, but to sanctify us. We acknowledge our weakness: we forget that you are here. We forget that the Holy Spirit indwells us. We settle for distant faith when you offer intimate communion. Forgive us for the ways we have turned from your voice and run after lesser loves.

Thank you, Lord Jesus, that through your finished work we have been adopted as co-heirs with you. Thank you that your Spirit now lives in us, making the extraordinary ordinary through your Word, through honest prayer, through the gathered church. You have given us the means of grace—Scripture alive with your Spirit, access to the Father through prayer, and the fellowship of believers who confirm your faithfulness to one another. In these simple disciplines, you meet us. In these ordinary channels, your Spirit flows.

We ask, Father, that you would make us people who carry the fire of your presence into our weeks. Grant us eyes to see your faithfulness in crisis and in the dailiness of ordinary life. Give us courage to testify to what you have done in us, knowing that our witness to your nearness strengthens the whole body of Christ. Help us to study your Word with expectation that your Spirit will make it live. Teach us to pray with the confidence of children calling out to their Father. And knit us together as a church family so that in our unity, we experience the extraordinary presence of your Spirit. We commit ourselves to you—to know you, to be known by you, to walk in the light of your presence all our days. To you be all glory and honor, now and forever.
