Theological claims· 10
  1. The Palm Sunday crowd's celebration of Jesus as King is strange to modern Americans because they are rejoicing in someone having absolute authority over them. unit #4
  2. Jesus reverses the pattern of earthly rulers—he appears less impressive than he is, not more. unit #6
  3. Jesus is the King who brings both rule and restoration wherever he steps, reversing the curse of Genesis 3. unit #14
  4. Jesus must go to Jerusalem and the cross to transform his localized restoration work into universal restoration. unit #17
  5. Jesus was not crushed by history but sovereignly chose to go to the cross, possessing authority to lay down and take up his life. unit #18
  6. Jesus goes to the cross to take on all the consequences of sin that his people might be restored, thereby extending his restorative rule into the hearts of any who welcome him. unit #19
  7. We wait in hope because Jesus' rule and restoration are progressively extending now and will be fully realized at his return. unit #20
  8. The Pharisees repeat Adam and Eve's sin by placing themselves in authority over Jesus, and we face the same temptation to evaluate Jesus rather than bow to him. unit #24
  9. When we see who Jesus is and what he does, we must bow the knee to him in every area of life, trusting that where he rules, he also restores. unit #27
  10. The Christian life is fundamentally characterized by the posture of bowing the knee—both in initial conversion and in ongoing progressive repentance across all life areas. unit #30
