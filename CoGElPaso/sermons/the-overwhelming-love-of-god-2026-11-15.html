The Overwhelming Love of God

Ephesians 3:14-21 November 15, 2026 Pastor Jonathan Vogan
Thesis The power for the believer comes from God's inestimable love for his own, and the proper response to comprehending this love is worship that engages the whole person—head, heart, and body.

Illustrations· 5
  1. historical example · unit #10 — The preacher illustrates the paradox of Paul's kneeling by drawing on King David in Psalm 3: hunted by his son's army, David was able to sleep because he trusted in God's protection. David's posture of vulnerability was actually a posture of confidence in God's power—just as Paul's kneeling is both humble and bold.
  2. personal story · unit #19 — The preacher recounts a personal experience hearing John Piper, who taught that the posture of the body can helpfully reflect the posture of the heart. This serves as an illustration of the principle that physical posture and spiritual posture are related.
  3. analogy · unit #46 — The preacher illustrates the variety of emotional ranges in the congregation, using the analogy of the Franklin Mountains (a wide range) versus a parking lot (a narrow range). Some believers are exuberant; others are steady. Both are legitimate.
  4. personal story · unit #48 — The preacher recounts a personal story from the pastor's conference, where he was emotionally drained and unable to sing but was ministered to by the worship of 1,500 voices around him. His tears turned from weariness to gratefulness. The illustration demonstrates that corporate worship builds up the downcast and that emotional vulnerability is appropriate in worship.
  5. personal story · unit #49 — The preacher returns to the Letchworth State Park illustration, adding Niagara Falls as another wonder of the world near his hometown. He observes the danger of familiarity: for those who grew up around these wonders, they became routine and taken for granted.
Theological claims· 13
  1. The power for the believer comes from God's inestimable love, and without an overwhelming affection for God, there will be no power to defeat sin. unit #7
  2. Believers were dead in sins and far from God, but God so loved the world that he gave his only Son, Jesus, who willingly bore our sins and died to make us right with God. unit #13
  3. God's effectual calling is not based on human works, morality, or avoidance of sin—believers contribute nothing to salvation except the sin that made it necessary. unit #15
  4. Christ had each believer specifically in mind on the cross, and through his blood, believers are adopted into God's family by grace through faith—not by works. unit #16
  5. Every salvation is a miracle, no matter the circumstances or age at which it occurred. unit #17
  6. Because Christ dwells in believers, their identity is no longer their own, and Christ's inheritance becomes their inheritance. unit #25
  7. God's love is the foundation of all his attributes and actions—his wrath, justice, and truth are all expressions of his great love for us. unit #28
  8. The preacher rehearses the gospel using a simple four-part framework from Trillia Newbell's children's book: God made it, people ruined it, God rescued it, he will finish it. This is a compressed narrative of creation, fall, redemption, and consummation. unit #35
  9. God rescued us through Christ, who came, lived, died, and rose according to the Scriptures, and God will finish his work by bringing the new heavens and new earth where believers will worship him forever. unit #36
  10. Nothing will heal more than the love of Christ, because even if nothing else is fixed, knowing you will be with Jesus is what matters most. unit #39
  11. Our knowledge of God's love pales in comparison to the reality of God's love, and the proper response is worship—doxology is the overflow of the heart in light of what we know in our heads. unit #43
  12. It is not only okay but vital for the gospel to affect emotions—understanding God's love will affect your emotions. unit #45
  13. Worshipers should know richly, feel deeply, and express passionately, and should savor the supremacy of Scripture as God's gift of love. unit #54

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through Paul's prayer in light of God's overwhelming love—from the miracle of salvation itself, through Christ's specific work for us, into the foundation of all God's attributes, and finally into the doxological response that alone befits such grace.

Monday John 3:16

In this most familiar promise, we encounter the very heart of the gospel: God's initiative in love toward the helpless and dead. The Father's willingness to give his own Son is not the response to human merit or striving, but the expression of an affection so vast that it bridges the chasm between sinners and the holy God. As we begin this week meditating on overwhelming love, let this verse anchor us: everything flows from the Father's heart toward us in Christ.

Tuesday Isaiah 53

Isaiah's prophecy paints Christ as the suffering servant who bears not generic sin, but *our* transgressions, *our* iniquities. When we read that the Lord laid upon him the iniquity of us all, we grasp that his death was not a distant transaction but a personal act of substitution—he thought of you, by name, in that moment of agony. The specificity of Christ's work means his love was not abstract; it was concrete, deliberate, and aimed at your redemption and mine.

Wednesday Ephesians 1

In Ephesians 1, Paul opens by declaring that we are blessed in Christ with every spiritual blessing and chosen before the foundation of the world. This election flows not from random decree but from love; God's sovereignty over our salvation, his justice in redemption, his wrath against sin—all of these are manifestations of one comprehensive love. When we understand that even God's holy attributes serve his loving purposes for his people, we begin to see that nothing in his character is contrary to his affection for us.

Thursday Ephesians 2:6-10

Paul declares that God has raised us up with Christ and seated us in the heavenly places—not as a future promise alone, but as a present reality through union with him. Our identity is now hidden with Christ in God; we are no longer defined by our sin, our struggle, or our past, but by his perfect status and inexhaustible resources. When we grasp that his inheritance is ours, we understand that the overwhelming love we have been contemplating all week has already claimed us as sons and daughters with full rights to the Father's house.

Friday Psalm 3

In Psalm 3, David cries out from affliction, surrounded by enemies who say his God cannot save him; yet he declares that the Lord is a shield about him, his glory, and the lifter of his head. This is the prayer of the suffering believer whose only comfort is the presence of God. When we have grasped across this week that we are loved with the Father's love, adopted in Christ, and seated in heavenly places, we respond as David did—with the cry of worship and trust that transcends circumstance. Our doxology is not the overflow of an easy life but the response of a soul convinced that nothing matters more than Jesus himself.

What does Paul seem to be saying about the relationship between what we can intellectually understand about God's love and what we actually experience of it?
    Ephesians 3:17-19
    → How have you personally encountered a gap between knowing something about God's love intellectually and actually feeling or experiencing that love in a moment of struggle?
  2. The sermon emphasizes that in salvation, 'believers contribute nothing except the sin that made it necessary.' How does this claim sit with you, and what does it mean for how we understand our own conversion story?
  3. Paul says he prays that we would be 'filled with all the fullness of God.' What does it mean practically—not theologically, but in your daily life—to be filled with God's fullness rather than filled with anxiety, ambition, or self-protection?
    Ephesians 3:19
    → What would need to shift in your prayers, your thoughts, or your choices this week if you truly believed that God's fullness was available to you right now?
  4. The sermon argues that 'nothing will heal more than the love of Christ, because even if nothing else is fixed, knowing you will be with Jesus is what matters most.' Do you believe that? Where do you find yourself resisting or doubting that claim?
  5. According to the sermon, the proper response to comprehending God's overwhelming love is doxology—worship that engages head, heart, and body. When you gather with us on Sunday or pray alone, how are you currently expressing worship with more than just your head? What would change if you intentionally added your emotions and your physical posture?
    Ephesians 3:20-21
    → What feels risky or unfamiliar about worshiping with your whole person rather than just your mind?
  6. The sermon traces a line from grasping God's love → being filled with his fullness → overflowing in doxology. How would you describe what happens in your own heart and life when you truly encounter—not just know about, but encounter—the fact that Christ died specifically for you and that you are adopted into God's family by grace?
    Ephesians 2:6-10

Jesus Had You in Mind

For the parent

This prompt invites kids to grasp the personal nature of Christ's sacrifice—that Jesus didn't die for a faceless crowd, but for each of us specifically. Listen for wonder and emotion, not correct theology; the goal is to help them feel the weight of being loved by name.

Pastor Jonathan said that when Jesus hung on the cross, he had each one of us specifically in mind—like he was thinking of you by name. If Jesus was thinking about you when he died for you, what does that make you feel? And how might that change the way you think about him?

Memory verse this week

Ephesians 3:17-19

So that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith—that you, being rooted and grounded in love, may have strength to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length and height and depth, and to know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge, that you may be filled with all the fullness of God.

Why this verse: This passage captures the sermon's central movement: from Christ's indwelling presence to the believer's rooting in God's love to the doxological response of comprehending His overwhelming affection. It is the theological heart of Paul's prayer and the foundation for the sermon's claim that grasping God's immeasurable love produces worship that engages head, heart, and body.
