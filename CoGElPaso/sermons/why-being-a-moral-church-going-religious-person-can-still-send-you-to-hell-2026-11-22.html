Even that outward sign isn't enough. Because 3 times in the Old Testament we are told that outward circumcision really is meant to be an outward symbol of an inward reality. Meaning that the outward symbol was meant to be sort of marking us, marking God's people on the outside, and then the inward symbol was meant to match that marking. The outward symbol would say we are God's people. The inward life would say we are God's people. The two would work together. That's what was intended. But when God's people with the outward sign did not have the inward reality, what Paul is saying is it's as if they never had the outward sign to begin with because they didn't just fail on the physical level, they failed on the heart and soul level.

30 · Exegetes Romans 2:28-29 to establish the theological principle that membership in God's people is determined by inward spiritual reality, not outward religious markers

Paul is saying, listen, you can have all of the outward external markers of being quote unquote one of God's people. Here is the danger. As long as you are clinging or half clinging to those things as if they can save you, Jesus cannot save you. It's one or the other. You can't have both.

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through the unraveling of self-righteousness—both irreligious and religious—to discover that only Christ's righteousness, received by faith after all self-justification burns away, can save.

Monday Romans 1:16-17

Paul declares the gospel as the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes. Notice: not to everyone who tries hard enough, or knows enough doctrine, or attends church. The gospel's power is released when we stop defending our own righteousness and receive Christ's instead. This is the foundation of everything Paul is about to dismantle in Romans 2.

Tuesday Matthew 5:27-28

Jesus pushes the law inward: lust in the heart is adultery before God, not just the outward act. The religious person who keeps the commandment outwardly but harbors pride, contempt, or coveting inwardly is exposed as lawless. Religion becomes a path to hell when it teaches us to manage our image while leaving our hearts unchanged. This is the trap Paul is warning against.

Wednesday Deuteronomy 10:16

God calls Israel to circumcise their hearts, not merely their flesh. The law itself points beyond outward obedience to an inward work only God can do. When Moses speaks these words, he's already diagnosing the impossibility: we cannot circumcise our own hearts. Both the irreligious person who ignores the law and the religious person who trusts the law's outward markers stand equally condemned before the God who sees all.

Thursday Jeremiah 4:4

Jeremiah echoes Deuteronomy's call: circumcise yourselves to the Lord, circumcise your hearts. Yet Israel possessed the law, the temple, the covenant signs—and remained spiritually uncircumcised. We can gather every outward marker of faith—baptism, confirmation, years in the pew—while our hearts remain sealed off from the Spirit's work. The gospel demands we ask: am I trusting the sign, or the Savior?

Friday Romans 3:21

Now, apart from law, the righteousness of God has been manifested. After stripping away both irreligious excuse and religious pride, Paul opens the door: there is another righteousness—not ours, but God's—revealed and given as a gift. We enter the gospel not by defending ourselves but by abandoning ourselves and receiving what Christ alone has accomplished. This is the pivot from destruction to rescue.
