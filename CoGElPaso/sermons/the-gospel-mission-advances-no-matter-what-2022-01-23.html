Theological claims· 24
  1. The church is commissioned in the gospel of Jesus that will advance in power no matter what. unit #8
  2. The gospel mission advances in power because it is God's work. unit #11
  3. The gospel advances in God's power in such a way that it is unmistakably His work, not ours, which moves us to worship Him. unit #16
  4. God advances the mission in power to make clear that He is the one advancing it, which gives believers hope. unit #17
  5. The apostles were ordinary men chosen and empowered by God to declare the gospel of repentance and forgiveness in Jesus Christ alone. unit #19
  6. The church has been given the same call as the apostles, and the Holy Spirit superintends the preaching that Jesus is the Christ. unit #20
  7. The mission will advance in power no matter what because it is empowered by God himself. unit #21
  8. God works through His church — through ordinary stewards and messengers — to accomplish the gospel mission. unit #22
  9. The gospel message itself has the power to save — the power resides in the message, not in the messenger. unit #23
  10. God's power is revealed in the advance of the mission in such a way that it is clear He is the one doing the work. unit #25
  11. The gospel mission advances in power, but it also advances in opposition. unit #31
  12. Knowing that the mission advances in God's power grounds us when we face opposition. unit #32
  13. Standing firm is not bravado but a firm and humble resolve of the will submitted to God's will. unit #36
  14. Opposition to God is rooted in the fall — Adam's first response after sin was to accuse God. unit #39
  15. God is not surprised by opposition to the gospel, which is why He commands the church to stand firm. unit #40
  16. Opposition to the Lord and His Christ has always been part of God's redemptive plan — God is familiar with it and calls us to stand firm. unit #41
  17. The gospel will advance in power no matter what evil opposes it. unit #50
  18. Opposition to the gospel provides further paths for the advance of the message and clarifies the message itself. unit #53
  19. The suffering the church faces is real, overwhelming, and more than we can handle on our own. unit #63
  20. Suffering will not stop the mission; Christ uses suffering to do a powerful work in believers that He accomplishes by no other means. unit #64
  21. Suffering connects believers to Christ's own suffering — the apostles tasted what Jesus drank fully in His passion. unit #67
  22. Suffering does a profound personal work to connect believers to the suffering of Jesus, producing genuine rejoicing in being counted worthy. unit #68
  23. Suffering galvanizes the church's identity in Jesus and empowers believers to see Him more clearly. unit #69
  24. Suffering is worth it in the end, and those who have suffered have a unique message to deliver to others. unit #82

Monday Acts 4:29-30

The early church's prayer reveals the foundation of all boldness: they do not ask for courage in themselves, but for God to stretch out His hand and perform signs and wonders through the name of Jesus. This is the spine of the sermon—the mission succeeds not because the apostles are strong, eloquent, or strategically brilliant, but because God Himself is the one advancing it. We face the same reality: our weakness becomes the stage for His unmistakable power.

Tuesday 1 Corinthians 2:3-5

Paul's transparency about his own frailty—his fear, weakness, and trembling as he preached—underscores a liberating truth: the apostle is not the vehicle of power; the Spirit's demonstration through the message is. When we grasp that the gospel's saving efficacy flows from Christ's work and the Spirit's witness, not our eloquence or personality, we are freed from the burden of human persuasion. The message carries its own weight; we are simply stewards who declare it.

Wednesday Acts 4:25-26

The psalm quoted here reveals that the rage of rulers against the Lord and His anointed is not a surprise to God; it is woven into the fabric of redemptive history. The nations rage, yet God sits enthroned, unmoved. Our passage shows that opposition today—increasingly legal, physical, and fierce—follows the same pattern. When we see antagonism rising, we are not witnessing the failure of God's plan but the confirmation of it; our call is the apostles' call: to obey God rather than men and keep pressing forward.

Thursday 2 Corinthians 1:8-9

Paul's language—despair of life itself, death sentence—mirrors the overwhelming weight many of us carry now. Yet his testimony moves us to the heart of the sermon: suffering stripped him of self-reliance and taught him to rely on God who raises the dead. The mission advances not despite the apostles' affliction but through it; their suffering became proof that Jesus is worth everything, a sermon preached wordlessly to a watching world. We too, in our deepest pain, participate in a work God performs through suffering that cannot happen any other way.

Friday Romans 10:14

This verse strips away excuses: preachers must be sent; the gospel must be proclaimed; faith comes by hearing. The chain of dependence—God sends, messengers go, people hear, faith awakens—reminds us that our obedience, even in opposition and suffering, is not insignificant. We are part of an unbroken apostolic mission. When we stand firm, rejoice in suffering, and keep declaring Christ despite cost, we are not heroic—we are simply answering the same commission, trusting the same God, and knowing that He accomplishes His purpose through us.

But Peter and the apostles answered, 'We must obey God rather than men.'
