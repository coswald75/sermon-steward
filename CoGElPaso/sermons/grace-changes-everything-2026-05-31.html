Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. The sermon is titled 'Grace Changes Everything.' What does it mean for grace to actually change something? Can you think of a concrete way you've seen grace alter the trajectory of someone's life—or your own?
    → When you say grace 'changed' things, what specifically shifted—their behavior, their motivation, their self-perception, their relationships?
  2. Many of us live as though grace is primarily about what happens at conversion—the moment we're saved. How might the sermon be pressing us to see grace as something far more expansive than that single moment?
    → If grace is not just for the beginning of the Christian life but for all of it, what difference should that make in how we approach a struggle we're facing right now?
  3. What is the human condition that makes grace necessary in the first place? What are we unable to do for ourselves that requires God's gracious intervention?
    → Why is it so hard to admit that we genuinely *need* grace—that we cannot produce the change in ourselves that we most deeply want?
  4. The sermon emphasizes that grace is not earned and cannot be merited. What does this mean for how we approach our own spiritual growth and maturity in Christ?
    → If grace cannot be earned, what are we still tempted to do—what effortful, self-reliant patterns persist in how we relate to God?
  5. How does the gospel of Jesus Christ—His substitutionary death and resurrection—address the deepest problem that makes grace necessary? What did Christ accomplish that grace now applies to our lives?
    → When you face a specific area of spiritual weakness this week, how should the reality of Christ's finished work reshape what you ask of God?
  6. If grace has truly changed everything—if it is the operative power in our redemption and sanctification—how should that reshape the way we live together as a church community this week?
    → Where might we be treating one another as though grace has *not* changed everything? Where are we still operating under judgment, striving, or performance?

Pray together this week

Prayer: Grace Changes Everything

Father, we come before you in awe of your sovereign grace—that unmerited favor which has saved us, sustains us, and is even now reshaping us into the image of your Son. You are the God who delights to give good gifts to those who deserve nothing, and we marvel at the truth that in Christ we have been chosen, redeemed, and adopted into your family. We confess that we often live as though grace were a one-time transaction rather than the fountain from which all of life flows. We struggle to believe that your grace is sufficient for our daily sins, our persistent weaknesses, and the wounds we carry. We default to striving, to shame, to the false comfort of self-reliance, forgetting that you have already accomplished what we could never earn.

Yet the gospel announces what our hearts need to hear: through the finished work of Christ, we are forgiven completely, accepted fully, and empowered by the Spirit to live in the glad reality of your grace. Every motivation and every grace we need to obey you, to serve one another, and to pursue Christ-likeness flows from this single source—that Jesus lived the perfect life we could not live, died the death we deserved, and rose to seat us with him in heavenly places. We are not left to our own devices; you have given us the Holy Spirit as the very power of our transformation.

Grant us grace this week to live as those who truly believe that grace changes everything. Help us to extend to one another the same immeasurable kindness you have shown us in the gospel. Give us courage to confess our sins without shame, knowing that confession leads not to condemnation but to deeper fellowship with you and with our brothers and sisters. Empower us to make decisions—in our families, our work, our witness—that flow from gratitude for grace rather than from fear or the burden of earning your favor. And as we gather again together, open our eyes afresh to the beauty of your sovereign grace poured out for us in Christ. All glory to the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, whose grace changes everything.

For the parent
