Theological claims· 17
  1. As the people of God, we can trust God the deliverer. unit #1
  2. Daniel's response demonstrates that the safest course of action is radical obedience to God, and true faith remains consistent regardless of circumstances. unit #8
  3. Daniel's consistency in prayer remained unchanged whether in rest or difficulty, demonstrating faith independent of circumstances. unit #10
  4. Spiritual maturity is evidenced by a quicker and more consistent turn to prayer when trials arise. unit #12
  5. Prayer is the most powerful resource available to believers and functions as the weapon needed for spiritual warfare. unit #13
  6. Prayer recenters the believer by making God big in the heart, which proportionally makes problems and worries smaller. unit #15
  7. The central challenge of the Christian life is keeping God central in the heart by resisting competing allegiances, accomplished through prayer, God's Word, and the Holy Spirit. unit #16
  8. Daniel exemplifies the pattern believers should follow: having the Spirit, shaping prayer by God's Word, and turning immediately to the Lord in difficulty. unit #17
  9. Darius's frantic, sleepless response reveals that he is operating out of self-preservation and self-reliance rather than trust in God. unit #21
  10. Daniel 6 reminds believers that the proper response to difficulty is trusting God the deliverer, not self-reliant effort. unit #24
  11. Daniel 6 gives a resounding yes to the question of whether God can be trusted, revealing that this is not merely a feel-good story but a historical account demonstrating God's character and faithfulness. unit #30
  12. Daniel's habit of prayer flowed from his conviction that God delivers his people either in this life or in eternity, and this account became a repeated source of encouragement for Israel. unit #32
  13. Daniel 6 points typologically to Jesus Christ, the greater Daniel, whose death and resurrection accomplished ultimate deliverance for sinners who trust in him. unit #33
  14. Daniel's faithfulness resulted in God's name being proclaimed to the nations and demonstrates the hope and victory God gives his people even under pagan rule. unit #35
  15. God sovereignly uses the trials of his people to accomplish his purposes and to provide testimony that proclaims the hope and victory found in him. unit #36
  16. The tested faith of believers is precious to Christ, and he will personally commend their faithfulness in trials on the day they stand before him. unit #38
  17. Believers live between the cross (past deliverance) and the final revelation (future vindication), when all God's purposes in their trials will be made clear. unit #39

And here's the cool thing about Daniel 6 is the people of Israel were able to look back at the example of Daniel time and time again when they needed those reminders.

33 · Establishes the typological connection between Daniel and Jesus, tracing multiple parallels: both plotted against, both prayed, both placed in tombs, both delivered, both resulted in the defeat of enemies

Here's the good news for us today. We get to look back to Jesus and the cross. The story of Daniel 6 is pointing to the truer and greater Daniel. Jesus Christ. Guys, look at the parallels just all over this text here. Daniel was plotted against by the high officials and satraps. Jesus was plotted against by the chief priests and scribes. Daniel prayed as their plan to kill him was unfolding. Jesus prayed in Gethsemane on their way to arrest and put him on trial. Daniel was thrown into a tomb of lions to die. Jesus hung on the cross to die and was placed in a tomb. Daniel escaped death unharmed and was brought up up out of the tomb. Death could not hold Jesus down. He rose out of the tomb three days later. Daniel's enemies were defeated in the end. Death, Satan and sin were defeated once and for all by Jesus Christ. Notice not anything Daniel did that produced these results. This was completely the work of God. Daniel was delivered from the wrath of lions and death. Our text tells us why because he trusted God. Today sinners are saved from death, from the wrath of God's holy justice, from eternal hell by simply placing their faith in Jesus, who made faith even possible.

34 · Applies the gospel evangelistically by warning those outside Christ that they will face God's justice, then proclaiming the good news that God has provided deliverance through Jesus

Here's the thing. If you are not in Christ like these officials and satraps, you will face God's justice one day because of your sin. But this is the good news of the Gospel. God did something to prepare and provide a way of deliverance. Amen. Do you have that assurance today that God will deliver you at the end of your life and that you will be with him forever? Jesus died so that all who believe in him and trust in him will have eternal life and will have their sins forgiven.

35 · Expounds Daniel 6:25-28 to show the missional fruit of Daniel's faithfulness—Darius issues a decree proclaiming the sovereignty, eternality, and deliverance of the God of Daniel

And this chapter reminds us of the great hope and victory we have as the people people of God. Look at how Daniel's faithfulness in the midst of opposition impacted Darius in verse 25. Then King Darius wrote to all the people, nations and languages that dwell in all the earth, peace be multiplied to you. I make a decree that in all my royal dominion people are to tremble and fear before the God of Daniel, for he is the living God enduring forever. His kingdom shall never be destroyed and his dominion shall be to the end. He delivers and rescues, he works signs and wonders in heaven and on earth. He who has saved Daniel from the power of the lions. So this Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius and the reign of Cyrus, the Persian church.

36 · Asserts the doctrine of God's sovereign purposes in suffering: God never wastes trials but uses them to display his glory and allow believers to proclaim hope

God never ever wastes the bad things that happen to his people. In fact he uses them according to his purposes so that through us we can then proclaim the hope and victory found in only Him. There's hope and purposes in our trials and our suffering that others need to hear. And you never know what other people what the Lord will do in their lives by hearing your faithfulness in trials. Look what this did in Darius life. And now we have this pagan king who went from self preservation to I need to proclaim this kingdom

Last Comments as we close, while we can look back to the cross to shape our responses to the bad things in life, we also have the privilege of looking forward turn to first Peter Chapter one Just to give some context here, Peter is talking to exiled Christians who are going through trials and he is reminding them of the living hope that they have in Christ and how God has an inheritance waiting for them in heaven when we get there is what he says in verse 6 in this in this living hope we have in this inheritance that's waiting for us in this you rejoice, though now for a little while if necessary, you have been grieved by various trials so that the tested genuineness of your faith, more precious than gold that perishes though it is tested by fire, may be found to result in praise and glory and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ.

38 · Unpacks the stunning claim that our tested faith is precious to Christ himself

Look what he says here. Our faith is precious to who? Verse 7. He's talking about Our faith is precious to Christ. One day we will all stand face to face with the Lord, and the lifter of our heads is going to look at us and he's going to say, man, when you when you walked through the loss of your child and you still had faith and you still trusted me, that pleased me, he will say. When you walked through those difficult medical situations with your families and you stood firm in your faith and trusted me, that pleased me. To Daniel, that was hard. That was scary. But you still had faith as you were thrown into the den of lions and you trusted me and that pleases me. Well done, good and faithful servant.

39 · Summarizes the believer's eschatological hope: we look back to the cross for deliverance accomplished and forward to the day when all God's purposes in our trials are revealed and we stand rejoicing before Jesus

Church we get to look at the cross, and then we get to look to that day when all of God's purposes are revealed. In all the trials that we walked through, in all the opposition we faced, we will stand rejoicing at the face with Jesus.

40 · Concludes by synthesizing the sermon's major moves: don't panic when trials come (God is sovereign), remember the testimony of prior saints and of Christ himself, take a breath and remember God's past deliverance, remain faithful, pray like you believe, and trust God the deliverer

So to wrap things up, Church when bad things happen in this life, let's not panic. God is not surprised by this. We do not need to be surprised either. Many of our brothers and sisters have walked down this path of difficulty, of trials, of suffering and opposition. Our own Savior faced injustice and opposition in his life and ministry as well. And when bad things happen, take a deep breath. Remember, God has delivered his people before. God will do it again in this life or the next.
