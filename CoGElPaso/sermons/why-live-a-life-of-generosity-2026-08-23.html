Why Live a Life of Generosity

Genesis 12:1-4 August 23, 2026 Pastor Ricky Alcantar
Thesis Christians should live lives of radical generosity because they have been blessed by God through Christ in order to be a blessing to others, stewarding God's gifts for the purposes of building His kingdom rather than their own.

Theological claims· 10
  1. God's promise of a new land to Abram is the first post-Fall promise of restoration to God's place, echoing the Garden of Eden. unit #6
  2. God's call redefines Abram's identity from being determined by horizontal relationships (place, family, work) to being determined by his vertical relationship to God. unit #8
  3. God's call to Abram was all-encompassing, demanding total rather than partial surrender—there was no option to retain the old identity while receiving the new. unit #12
  4. Genesis 12 represents the reversal of the Tower of Babel: instead of gathering to exalt oneself, Abram is sent out to bless others. unit #15
  5. Christians are stewards, not owners, of God's blessing—the key question is not what we want to do with God's blessing but what God wants to do with it. unit #18
  6. Genesis 12 is ultimately about Jesus Christ, the true and greater Abraham, who perfectly fulfilled God's promise by bearing the curse so that we could receive blessing. unit #25
  7. Receiving Christ as King means Christians no longer need what the world offers because they've received Christ's blessing, and they receive Christ's authority over all areas of life including finances. unit #27
  8. The prosperity gospel is damnable heresy because it attempts to make money and Christ co-equal sources of blessing, when Jesus declares no one can serve both God and money—only Christ offers true life and blessing. unit #28
  9. Christians remain the biggest givers across all groups, religions, and demographics, maintaining a pattern of radical countercultural generosity that has persisted since Acts 2. unit #35
  10. Christians are invited to build Jesus' eternal, unfading kingdom by fulfilling the trajectory of Genesis 12 and Matthew 28—using their one brief life to share God's blessing so more people can be gathered into God's people. unit #36

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we follow the arc of Genesis 12: from identity redeemed, to purpose redirected, to blessing stewarded—and discover that every step belongs to Christ and His kingdom.

Monday Genesis 3-11

For ten chapters, Genesis shows us the aftermath of the Fall—exile from the garden, the multiplication of sin, the scattering at Babel. Then Genesis 12 arrives: God calls Abram toward a land. This is not random geography. It is God saying, after all that brokenness, *I am still a God who restores.* When Abram leaves Ur and heads toward the land, he walks toward the first genuine promise of home since Adam lost the garden.

Tuesday Genesis 11

At Babel, humanity gathered—*Come, let us build a city*—and in building, exalted themselves. God scattered them. Now Abram receives the opposite call: *Go from your country.* He scatters, not in judgment, but in blessing. Where Babel said, *We will make a name for ourselves,* Abram moves toward a different name—one God gives him, one that will be great precisely because it becomes a channel of blessing *to all the families of the earth.* One builds for self; the other leaves self behind to bless.

Wednesday 2 Corinthians 5:21

Abram was called to leave everything and receive a blessing that was never meant for him alone—it was meant to flow through him to the nations. Christ did the same, but infinitely deeper. He left the glory of heaven, bore the curse we deserved on the cross, and rose so that His blessing could flow to us and through us to all people. When you receive Christ, you receive the very blessing Abraham pointed toward—and you inherit his calling too.

Thursday Matthew 28

The Great Commission is Genesis 12 in full bloom. *Go to all nations.* *Make disciples.* Jesus is sending His people out—as Abram was sent—not to build our own kingdoms but to extend His. We have received immeasurable blessing in Christ. Now the question is the same one that faced Abram: Will we steward that blessing only for ourselves, or will we allow it to flow outward so others can know Him too?

Friday Acts 2

Acts 2 shows the church doing what Abram did: giving up comfort and security to be a channel of blessing. *All the believers were together and had everything in common. Selling their possessions and goods, they gave to anyone as he had need.* This is not the exception; this is the trajectory of the blessed. When you know who you are in Christ, your relationship to money changes. It becomes not *What can I keep for myself?* but *What does God want to do with this?* Your checkbook becomes an instrument of the kingdom.
