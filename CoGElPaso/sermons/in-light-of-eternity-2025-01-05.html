Illustrations· 8
  1. personal story · unit #1 — Extended personal narrative about the pastor's trip to Alcantara, Spain—the town from which his surname derives. The story builds emotional intensity around homecoming desire and the joy of reaching a long-anticipated destination. Functions to establish the sermon's central metaphor: the longing for home.
  2. analogy · unit #9 — Brief analogy comparing home remodeling excitement to Jesus's preparation of heaven. Functions to make the theological claim emotionally accessible by connecting it to a common life experience.
  3. personal story · unit #14 — Personal illustration comparing weekly family night gatherings to eternal feasting in heaven. The pastor uses his own practice of gathering for meals, laughter, and affection to make the theological claim accessible, then pivots to the eschatological payoff: in heaven, the gathering never ends.
  4. · unit #24 — Brief quotation from John Bunyan establishing the principle that future hope motivates present faithfulness. Functions as transition from theological claim to extended quotation in the following unit.
  5. · unit #25 — Extended quotation from John Bunyan establishing that faithful deeds are stored as treasure to be revealed and rewarded at the final day. The pastor contextualizes Bunyan's credibility (pastor, author of Pilgrim's Progress, wrote in prison) to strengthen the ethos of the citation.
  6. historical example · unit #27 — Anecdote about John D. Rockefeller's wealth used to illustrate the futility of earthly treasure accumulation. The punchline ('All of it') drives home the contrast between earthly wealth (which stays behind) and heavenly treasure (which can be 'sent on ahead'). The illustration sets up the Matthew 6 citation that follows.
  7. · unit #31 — Extended quotation from C.S. Lewis's 'The Weight of Glory' establishing that our problem is not that we desire reward too much but that we desire it too little. The mud pies metaphor makes the claim vivid: we settle for trivial pleasures when infinite joy is offered. The quotation serves as climactic support for the sermon's call to live for eternal reward.
  8. cultural reference · unit #33 — The pastor extends the Narnia illustration by referencing the refrain 'further up and further in' from the same book, using it to capture the endless progression and escalating beauty of heaven. The repetition of the phrase mimics the book's own rhetoric and drives home the sermon's vision of eternal increase in joy. The unit pivots from illustration to direct address ('This is what awaits us').
Theological claims· 11
  1. The most amazing aspect of heaven is that we will see Jesus face to face and bear his name forever, which is made possible only because Jesus came from heaven and secured our entrance. unit #6
  2. Jesus himself is personally preparing our eternal home, which guarantees it will exceed anything we could design or imagine. unit #8
  3. At the resurrection, believers will receive new, imperishable, immortal bodies, never again subject to sickness, weakness, or death. unit #10
  4. Heaven will be a place of incredible joy because God will remove every source of sorrow—death, mourning, crying, and pain will no longer exist. unit #12
  5. Heaven will include literal eating and drinking—feasting at Jesus's table on rich food and well-aged wine, fulfilling the promises of Scripture. unit #13
  6. Heaven will be a place of eternal reunion with all believers—those who have died will be raised, and we will all be caught up together to be with the Lord forever. unit #15
  7. Salvation is not earned by good works, church attendance, or giving—it is a free gift of God's grace through faith in Christ Jesus alone. unit #17
  8. The only way into heaven is to believe in Jesus and accept his death and resurrection in our place—no sin is too great for God's grace, but no other path exists. unit #19
  9. Good works do not save us, but those who are saved are created in Christ for good works, and we should live in light of the day we stand before Jesus. unit #23
  10. Scripture teaches that there are rewards in heaven for faithful service, and this doctrine should motivate present obedience. unit #26
  11. God will reward every act of faithfulness, no matter how small or unnoticed, and this promise should motivate sacrificial giving and service in the present. unit #30

34 · Closing charge directly tying the sermon's vision of heaven to present living as the congregation enters a new year

So as we begin a new year, let us remember what is truly important and where our ultimate destination is. Let's live for Jesus and his kingdom that will never end and will bring us joy and happiness that is beyond our imagination.

35 · Closing prayer of thanksgiving and petition

Let's pray. Father, thank you. Thank you. That what you have prepared for us is beyond our imagination. It's better than we can think of. It's better than anything we deserve. And it's all made possible because of Jesus. I pray, Lord, that you would help each one of us keep that in our hearts and in our minds as we live each moment of each day and each decision that we make. Lord, let us make it in light of eternity. In Jesus name, Amen.
