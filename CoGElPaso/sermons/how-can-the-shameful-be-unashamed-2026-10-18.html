How Can the Shameful be Unashamed?

Romans 1:16-17 October 18, 2026 Pastor Ricky Alcantar
Thesis The gospel transforms what the world calls shameful—a crucified King, empty-handed faith, and confession of moral failure—into the very power of God for salvation, available to all who believe.

23 · Expounds the final phrase of Romans 1:17—'from faith for faith'—as faith from beginning to end, and connects this to Habakkuk 2:4 to show justification by faith is not a New Testament innovation but the consistent Old Testament pattern (Abraham, David, Habakkuk)

So then, this entrance into the Gospel, this entrance, your entrance, is that is not shame but glory. How then do we get in on this? Okay, how then do we become the unrighteous made righteous by the gospel? Well, the way we get it is not the way we would expect, because what we would expect is for such a great gift we must offer something in return. We must prove ourselves worthy. We, we must trade money or enough spiritual power or enough of a good life or a renewed commitment to do better to receive that gift. What does it say? For the righteousness of God is revealed from faith for faith, as it is written, the righteous shall live by faith. Look, that phrase, from faith for faith, is probably best translated— it's kind of a play on words— it's probably best translated as from faith, or faith from first to last, or faith from beginning to end. What it means is that God's gift of righteousness is revealed and offered to us, and the way that we receive it is by faith from the beginning to the end. In fact, this quotation from Habakkuk 2 reinforces that this is not a new thing. Sometimes Christians get the idea that, okay, in the New Testament we come to God by faith, but in the Old Testament they came to God by the law. No, Habakkuk 2 shows us that it actually was always the path. The path of Abraham, the path of David, the path of Habakkuk was this. The righteous shall live by faith, by belief, by open empty hands grasping on to what they could not earn and do not deserve. That, friends, is how you enter the world of the Gospel. But few do.

24 · Introduces Wilt Chamberlain's granny shot—an objectively superior technique he abandoned because it felt undignified

Why do so few do? Well, there is a technique I want to tell you about in basketball. That is assuredly one of the most effective techniques in sports that nobody uses. In the 1960s, Wilt Chamberlain was a dominant basketball player. Wilt the Stilt Chamberlain, with one glaring weakness, as so many big men in the NBA, his free throws. Abysmal. Absolutely abysmal. Worse than Shaq, in fact. If anybody remembers that. So somebody convinced him in the middle of the season to try a new technique, to throw the ball underhanded up at the bat. Now, what do we call that? Granny style. Not the most flattering of terms. But Wilt the Stilt Chamberlain, master of all the game except for for free throws, tried it. He threw underhanded and his free throw percentage improved dramatically, somewhere in the range of 20 to 30% at minimum. But at the end of the season, Wilt Chamberlain abandoned it and did not use it again, saying that it felt silly. And that he felt like a sissy doing it. Now, look, physicists can give objective data for why the underhanded free throw, lovingly known as the granny shot, is objectively better than shooting overhand. Objectively. Softer angles, more forgiving, higher percentage of conversion, yet no one does it. No one.

25 · Applies the granny shot illustration to the gospel: people reject it not because it doesn't work, but because it requires surrendering dignity

And that, friends, is often why so few come to Christ. Because to come to Christ means to say, I am an achievement failure. I am an identity failure. I am a moral failure. And almost as if to add insult to injury to our pride, the way that we receive this gift of righteousness is with the empty hands of faith. And like so many of us, we say, well, that feels silly. I can't keep— look, here's the reality. You cannot come to Christ and keep your human prideful dignity, quote unquote, intact. You cannot hold on to Christ and hold on to your pride. You just can't. That's the reality. And Paul is going to lay out for the next many chapters, the righteous shall live by faith. Period. That's it. There is no other way to Christ, right? It requires us to lay down our dignity, our power, our self-importance, and simply humble ourselves and ask and open our hands, right? That, that means that you're not trading something for it. You're not offering something in return. You're not going, well, you might give me this, but I can offer— no, it's faith, and that's it.

26 · Begins the closing invitation by tying back to Paul's defensive opening and urging those outside Christ to lay down power, identity, and comparative moral superiority

So friends, as we close, I want to point out 4 things we must lay down. And this is— now do you see why Paul is defensive from the beginning? He's like, I'm coming to preach the gospel, but I'm not ashamed, he says from the beginning. And the whole rest of Romans is his argument for why this thing that the rest of the Roman world says is a shame and a humiliation is in fact the glory and power of salvation for all who believe. Right? He is absolutely— he is going to tear into theological kind of explosion after explosion after explosion to lay out for this Roman church and friends, for us in 21st century America, why we should cling to the gospel that looks like a granny shot to the rest of the world. Because we believe that in it is power for salvation to all who believe. So friend, today here is the invitation. If you are not in Christ, will you come to him today? Maybe this is the day, friend, that you finally will stop trying to use your power and to cover your shame, your identity to cover your shame, your moral superiority— at least I'm better than my sister— to cover your shame. And instead, admit the truth about yourself and come to Him.

27 · Addresses believers with the pastoral reality that the Christian life is lived the way it began—by faith alone—and the temptation to reclaim a little pride or achievement

And friends, for those of us who are in Christ, here is the difficulty. In the Christian life, we continue as we began. For faith, from faith, by faith. We can't— here's the thing. At some point, we're like, I did surrender. I did the surrender. I laid myself down. But if I could just get a little pride back, that would be great. If I could just hold on to a little bit of this identity, a little bit of this power, a little bit of this achievement. If I could just— it's like Jesus really did save me, but I'm not that bad. There are some respectable things about me. No, friend. This is a call to just go, we got to lay it back down. Lay down the achievement, lay down the identity, lay down the success, lay down the power.

28 · Returns to the Fosbury illustration with a final twist: elite athletes rejected the technique even after witnessing gold-medal success, but powerless kids with nothing to lose adopted it immediately

And let me end with this. After 1968, Dick Fosbury said in an interview that even after he won a gold medal flopping his way to victory, The other athletes refused to adopt the technique from that Olympics. All the people, they all saw him win the gold medal and they all went, nope. But do you know who did adopt the technique? All the kids who ran track and who were watching the Olympics. You know why they did? Because they had no power. They had nothing to lose. They had no pride. And so they said, you know what? That guy is standing on the podium. What do I have to lose? And they flopped their way to victory.

29 · Ties the baptism service from the beginning of the sermon to the Fosbury illustration, framing baptism as the ultimate 'flop'—going limp and powerless as a symbol the world sees as humiliation but believers experience as victory

Friends, we just witnessed in baptism, one of the most humbling moments that you could ever participate in. We witness person after person go limp and powerless and declare they are being buried in the likeness of Christ and then raised to newness of life. Friends, that should be a symbol to the rest of the world of humiliation, but for those of us in Christ, it is a proclamation of victory. It is a proclamation that the power of God for salvation comes to the unrighteous as the righteous Christ offers us what we cannot earn and do not deserve, and it is only by faith as we flop our way into his arms that we are raised to newness of life. Amen. Amen.

30 · Closes in prayer for two groups: the unconverted struggling with shame and searching for identity/power/achievement, and the church as a community willing to be 'losers and failures in the eyes of the world' and unashamed to tell their testimony of coming to Christ empty-handed

Would you stand? Would you stand and let's pray. Now, Heavenly Father, I first pray for anyone in this room that Lord, I just sense that there are some you are calling to yourself today. Lord, I specifically want to pray for those that may be dealing with just a subjective sense of shame about themselves and who are looking everywhere and anywhere to try to cover it. Maybe it's a failure in their past. Maybe it's a failure they feel when they look in the mirror. Maybe it's a failure that they feel when they talk to their parents. Like, whatever it is, God, whatever it is, and they've been trying to find an identity, find a power, find an achievement, find something to give them a place to stand, to make them secure, to give them life. And Lord, I just sense that you are inviting them. You're calling them to yourself right now and you are inviting them to lay down everything and come to Jesus Christ today. And Lord, I pray that they would— Lord, I pray that you would give them the grace to lay it all down and to open their hands and like a child run to you, for that is the only way. That is the only way. And Lord, I just pray also, in addition to that, I just pray for our church, God. I just pray we would never be afraid to be a humbled church, unafraid of being losers and failures in the eyes of the world. Lord, may we never as a church long for power, long for achievement, long for success. Lord, may we be a happy band of people who said, we laid it all down because none of us could make it on our own. And I pray that we would be a church that is unashamed, unashamed to tell our testimony, unashamed to speak of our failures, unashamed ashamed to come to Jesus Christ that we might find divine power for salvation to all who believe. In Jesus' name, amen.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week, we follow Paul's thesis in Romans 1:16-17: the gospel transforms what the world calls shameful into God's power for salvation, available to all who believe.

Monday Romans 1:16

Notice Paul doesn't begin with apologetics; he begins with a confession: 'I am not ashamed of the gospel.' The very fact that he must say this tells us something—the gospel was viewed as foolishness, weakness, failure. Yet Paul plants his flag exactly where the world says shame lives. This week we learn why that reversal changes everything.

Tuesday Habakkuk 2:4

Habakkuk asked how a holy God could remain righteous while dealing with injustice. Paul's answer: the righteous shall live by faith. God does not lower His standard; He imputes His own righteousness to those who believe. The shameful sinner receives the verdict 'righteous'—not because the shame disappears, but because another's righteousness is credited to our account. That is the power.

Wednesday Romans 1:17

Paul does not call the gospel 'moral philosophy' or 'good advice.' He calls it dynamis—the power of God. This power operates on a different register than political influence, wealth, or social status. It saves from the inside out, transforms the soul, and applies across every nation and culture. The world's power fades; God's power through the gospel accomplishes salvation itself.

Thursday Romans 1:16-17

In Paul's day, many Jews believed salvation belonged to them by birthright. Paul demolishes that assumption: the gospel is 'to everyone who believes'—Jew and Gentile, slave and free, male and female. No identity, no background, no ethnic credential unlocks the door. Only belief does. We live in a culture obsessed with identity categories; the gospel makes all of them secondary to faith in Christ.

Friday Romans 1:16

What do we reach for when we feel ashamed? More money, more status, more control, more affirmation. Yet Paul insists the only power that addresses shame—the shame of our sin, our failure, our emptiness—is the gospel itself. This Friday, ask yourself: where am I still trying to hide my shame instead of bringing it to the foot of the cross? The gospel offers what no earthly achievement ever could: justification freely given.
