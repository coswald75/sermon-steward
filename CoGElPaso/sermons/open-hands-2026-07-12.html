Theological claims· 20
  1. Most people assume that striving will eventually produce lasting gain, but the Preacher tested that assumption deliberately so we could learn from his findings instead of repeating his futility. unit #4
  2. The word 'gain' in Ecclesiastes means what lasts beyond death—and the Preacher tested whether anything under the sun can provide it. unit #5
  3. There is no gain under the sun, but there are gifts from God's hand—and the posture that receives them is open hands, not closed fists. unit #6
  4. The Preacher's experiment matters because life is short, and we need to know what's actually worth living for before it's too late. unit #9
  5. The Preacher tested the theory that accumulating pleasurable moments produces a meaningful life—and found that pleasure does not carry over into lasting gain. unit #10
  6. 'Under the sun' refers to the closed horizontal system of earthly life viewed without God's eternal perspective—and within that system, no lasting gain can be found. unit #14
  7. The first experiment (pleasure) failed to produce gain, so the Preacher turns to the next candidate: wisdom. unit #16
  8. Wisdom is the best thing this world offers for navigating life, but it cannot overcome death—and therefore it cannot produce lasting gain. unit #18
  9. If wisdom, the strongest thing this world offers, cannot produce lasting gain, then nothing under the sun can—and the result is existential despair. unit #19
  10. The problem with toil is not the work itself but the fact that it must be left to someone else over whom you have no control—and the payoff is sleepless anxiety, not lasting gain. unit #23
  11. Ecclesiastes 2 is not in the Bible to shame us but to kindly tell us the truth: there is no gain under the sun—so we can stop chasing it and turn to what God offers instead. unit #25
  12. The passage's turn is not from grasping to releasing, but from grasping to receiving—from the preacher's closed fist to God's open hand, which gives rather than takes. unit #27
  13. God gives the same realities the Preacher tested (food, drink, work, wisdom), but he also gives the capacity to enjoy them as gifts—which changes everything. unit #28
  14. The problem is not the gifts (pleasure, wisdom, work) but what we demand of them—we ask created things to do what only the Creator can do, and a gift makes a lousy God. unit #29
  15. The one who pleases God is not the one who earns favor through impressive works but the one who lives in humble dependence, fearing God rather than trying to control life. unit #31
  16. Ecclesiastes creates a hunger for a gain that death cannot take away—and that hunger is resolved only in the gospel. unit #32
  17. The grasping life diagnosed in Ecclesiastes 2 originates in Genesis 3, where Adam and Eve reached to take what God had not given—the first act of unbelief. unit #33
  18. Christ, the second Adam, reversed the grasping posture of the first Adam—he did not grasp but gave himself, and through his pierced hands God gives the gift of salvation by grace, not wages. unit #34
  19. The gain the Preacher could not find under the sun came down from above it in Christ—and it cannot be grasped, only received. unit #35
  20. Christ is the ultimate gain, and unlike the world that continues grasping vapor, believers are called to live backwards—with open hands. unit #39

The chapter ends with a line being drawn between 2 lives. To the one who pleases him, God gives wisdom, knowledge, and joy. And of course, to the one who pleases him— I don't want there to be any confusion, so a quick note— it's not talking about salvation for the impressive, or the one who pleases God is not the one who finally builds enough like the preacher. You're gonna see that throughout Ecclesiastes, fearing God rather than trying to control life is the posture. That's the posture of true wisdom. The one who pleases God is not earning favor but living in humble dependence on him. That's what it means to please him. To the sinner, by contrast, he gives a different assignment. Gathering and collecting sound familiar? That's the grasping life. Verse 26 says God governs every gift and he distributes it as he sees fit.

32 · Sal identifies the passage's role in the biblical-theological arc: Ecclesiastes creates a hunger for a gain that death cannot take away—a gain that the Old Testament cannot provide but that points forward to Christ

And so as we look at our passage this afternoon, even the best our text has to offer today, the received life, the received life, It's truly a grace from God. It's like a daily bread from the hand of God, but it still creates a hunger, still leaves us with the hunger for a gain that death cannot take away. I think that's what Ecclesiastes is doing in the redemptive arc. It's creating a hunger for a gain that death cannot take away.

33 · Sal traces the grasping posture back to Genesis 3

So to address that lingering hunger that's been created, we got to go back further than the time of Ecclesiastes, all the way back to the garden in Genesis 3. A tree and a hand reaching out to take. The serpent's promise was gain. You will be like God. And humanity grabbed when Adam and Eve ate of it. That's where the grasping life was born. Every grasping impulse in this chapter and every grasping impulse in our lives traces back to the first act of unbelief— taking what God had not given.

34 · Sal presents the gospel as the resolution to the grasping posture

But here's the good news. Here is the good news. Philippians chapter 2 says that Christ Jesus, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped. What Adam reached to steal, Christ already had, and he didn't grip it. He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant. The second Adam did not come grasping. He came giving himself, stretched out on a cross, and there he His hands, his hands were pierced. And through those pierced hands, God handed over the gift, not wages, because scripture says in Romans 6 that our wages is death. That's what we deserve. But instead, a gift was given. Ephesians 2 also says, by grace you have been saved, and that is not your own doing, it is The gift of God, not a result of works.

35 · Sal summarizes the gospel resolution: the gain the Preacher sought under the sun came from above it in Christ

Church, that's the gain the preacher couldn't find. It was not under the sun. It was never meant to be found under the sun. The gain that the preacher was looking for came down from above it. It cannot be grasped, it can only be received.

36 · Sal applies the gospel to unbelievers

If you've never trusted in Christ, you've probably spent your life looking for gain, and the preacher already searched for it everywhere in the world. It isn't there. But there is a gift held out to you in Christ. John 1:12 says, but to all who did receive him Him. He's the ultimate. To all who did receive him, who believed in his name— that's what it means to receive Christ, to believe in his name— he gave the right to become children of God. You can't earn it. You can only receive him by faith. So today, turn from the life of grasping for what this world can't give and put your trust in Jesus.

37 · Sal applies the gospel to believers

And church, for those of us who have trusted him, do you see how free you are? I see the world enslaved at— enslaved in their gifts. Do you see how free you are? The question that drove the preacher: was it all worth it? Is already answered for you, and it was answered at the cross, not at your job, not at your accomplishments, not at your wisdom, not in the things you experience or the places you visited or the comfort you had or the respect everyone gave you. It was found already in the cross. So this week, I want to encourage you, receive your life The dinner, the work, the people at your table— none of it has to carry the weight of your great gain. Christ already carried that. And you won't open your hands by trying harder. We're— our sinful posture is this: clenching. You won't open it by yourself. It's too old and too deep within us. Today, ponder asking the Holy Spirit to do it. The same God who gives the gifts also gives the power to enjoy them. The gifts can finally just be gifts.

38 · Sal returns to the Brady illustration, now reframing it as a question for the congregation

Let's go back to the Tom Brady interview for a minute. Do you remember what his answer was to his own question, what else is there for me? He said, I wish I knew. Now imagine with me for one minute that at the end of your life or the next greatest accomplishment, you all are the ones being interviewed. What would you answer? What else is there for you? What would you say? Do you have an answer, friend?

39 · Sal answers the hypothetical question with the gospel

Thanks to the Word of God, you do know and you can know Christ is the ultimate gain. Paul says, I considered all things lost that I would gain Christ through Christ, we are brought into right relationship with God. The world around you will continue grasping and climbing and closing his fists on vapor. But as for you and me, let's live this life backwards. Let's live with open hands.

40 · Sal invites the congregation to embody the open-handed posture physically during the closing prayer

I want you to do something with me today. Let's stand as we go to the Lord in prayer. This is not going to be a mystical thing, or— but if you want to do it, you can, to paint a picture in your own mind if you would like to, right? It's just to paint a picture in your mind. Like when we raise our hands and we say, you know, I surrender, it's just like a posture of surrender. I'm raising my hands to surrender to the Lord. The Lord is already working through his word. We don't need any postures to do anything else. But if you want to with me, if the Lord has brought something to your mind that you need to release, or maybe you just— the things that you have, they are from the Lord, and you're, you're thinking that you need to take it for yourself, you're not even truly enjoying it anymore— I want you to put your hands in an open posture as we go before the Lord in prayer.

41 · Sal closes with a pastoral prayer asking God to open the congregation's hands, grant them the ability to enjoy his gifts, and make Christ their treasure

Father, we come before you this afternoon, and God, we have received from your eternal, all-wise, all-knowing word that you are the one that gives the ability to enjoy the gift. This afternoon, may we let it go and instead receive every day that we get as a gift from you. Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the Preacher's experiment from closed fists grasping at emptiness to open hands receiving gifts from God—and discover that the gain he could not find under the sun comes down to us in Christ.

Monday Ecclesiastes 1:3

The Preacher's opening question frames his entire investigation: "What does a man gain from all his labor?" This is not a question about comfort or temporary satisfaction, but about lasting, permanent gain—what survives death itself. As we face our own mortality, we must ask what we are laboring for and whether it can endure beyond our final breath.

Tuesday Genesis 3

In the garden, Adam and Eve closed their fists around forbidden fruit, refusing the open-handed trust that God's provision was enough. That first act of reaching for what was not given us echoes through every generation—we inherit their posture of grasping rather than receiving. The Preacher's closed fist in his experiments traces back to the serpent's whisper: God is holding out on you; take what you want instead.

Wednesday Philippians 2:6-7

Where the first Adam reached for power and divinity he did not possess, Christ, though he was God, did not grasp at his rights but emptied himself in obedience unto death. His pierced hands, outstretched in crucifixion, are the reverse image of Adam's grasping fists—and through them flows the gift of salvation that cannot be earned, only received. In Christ we see the posture God has always desired: vulnerable openness, not controlling grasp.

Thursday Ephesians 2:8-9

Paul's declaration cuts to the heart of our dilemma: we are saved by grace through faith, not by works—and yet we live as though works could save us, as though our striving could produce what only God's hand can give. The Preacher tested pleasure, wisdom, and toil looking for ultimate gain, but each failed because he was asking them to be his god. When we receive the gifts of work and pleasure as gifts alone—not as grounds for our security—we find the contentment the Preacher could never grasp.

Friday Psalm 23:1

"The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want." This is the answer to the Preacher's despair—not because we will never lack earthly comfort, but because we have been given what truly satisfies: the Lord himself, the ultimate gain that no death can take away. Our open hands, stripped of the illusion that we can secure ourselves, are now free to receive what Christ offers and to hold all earthly gifts loosely, knowing they come from his generous hand.

Open Hands, Closed Fists

For the parent

The sermon contrasted two postures toward the gifts in our lives: grasping (closed fists) and receiving (open hands). This prompt invites your family to notice where they're still trying to squeeze something for lasting gain. Listen for where kids are striving, and gently help them see what they're really asking that thing to do.

Pastor Sal talked about how the Preacher kept his fists closed, trying to squeeze everything he had into something that would last forever—pleasure, success, wisdom, money. What's something you're working really hard for right now, thinking it will make you happy or feel good about yourself? Is that something you're holding with open hands (like a gift from God), or are you squeezing it with closed fists (like it has to prove something about you)?
works for ages 8+; younger children can listen and share with gentle parent help
