Pastoral correction · unit #24
"Concrete application calling the congregation to intentionality in Sunday gatherings. Sal affirms that normal life conversations (work, family, sports) matter but urges believers to also pursue faith-strengthening conversations — asking real questions, listening to real answers, asking about what God is teaching, praying for one another. He diagnoses a common pattern of getting halfway to gospel encouragement (talking about need and suffering) but failing to counterbalance with reminders of hope in Christ."

  1. personal story · unit #3 — Extended personal story of Sal's experience working at Wilson's Leather in his mid-20s, where he violated company policy by giving unauthorized discounts and was confronted by his district manager. After taking full responsibility, he received a final warning but later was promoted to store manager when the manager tore up the warning document and told him she believed in second chances.   2. personal story · unit #22 — Personal testimony illustrating the principle of mutual encouragement established in Romans 1:12. Sal recounts how he first encountered Cross of Grace through Ricky's radio preaching in 2012-2013, noticed not only faithful teaching but the life of the church reflected in the sermons, longed to visit, and a decade later (2022) found the church to be as vibrant and healthy as he had heard. He testifies that he has received strengthening from the people of the church, embodying Paul's expectation of mutual encouragement.
  3. personal story · unit #25 — Personal example of Mike Greenland as a living illustration of mutual encouragement. Sal commends Mike for faithfully teaching How People Change and for embodying the principle in his own conversations — his responses strengthen Sal's faith, and his questions draw out real answers. The illustration provides a concrete, contemporary model of what Romans 1:12 looks like in the life of the church.
Theological claims· 15
  1. The gospel is that our sin was so serious that Christ had to die for it, and so great was God's mercy that he gave his Son for us. unit #4
  2. When gospel grace grips your heart by God's miraculous work, it transforms everything — how you see people, relate to God's people, and what you're willing to give to others. unit #5
  3. God's grace grips our hearts, drawing us into the life of his people and sending us out to make Christ known. unit #6
  4. We are called to embrace grace not only as individual recipients of salvation but as members of God's people who gladly strengthen one another and make Christ known. unit #7
  5. Faith is evidence of God's grace at work, and when somebody has faith, it is a gift from God that we cannot discount. unit #12
  6. Maturity doesn't mean we outgrow our need for encouragement, and what matters is not the strength of our faith but the object of our faith — Christ and the gospel. unit #20
  7. When we are not in community, we are cutting ourselves off from the grace that God intends to deliver to us through others. unit #21
  8. The local church is a gift from God because he uses the faith of other believers to strengthen our own. unit #23
  9. Paul's obligation and eagerness go together — understanding the grace we've received produces not only a sense of duty but eager desire for others to know the gospel. unit #32
  10. The gospel places us under a glad obligation to make Christ known, and understanding grace produces care for those who have yet to hear the good news. unit #33
  11. We were sinners who could not reconcile ourselves to God, but God sent his Son to live the righteous life we couldn't live, die the death we deserved, and rise again, so that through faith in Christ we are forgiven, reconciled to God, and brought into his family. unit #35
  12. Grace changes the way we see our lives — we are brought into a family whose faith strengthens ours, and the gospel that saved us is now a message we gladly make known to others. unit #36
  13. Paul's vision is far bigger than 'my relationship with Jesus' — the grace that reconciles us to Christ also binds us to his people and sends us on mission to those who need to hear the gospel. unit #37
  14. What grips our hearts shapes our lives — Paul's life was gripped by grace, and the same grace is meant to shape our lives as we understand what we've received in Jesus. unit #38
  15. We have been loved, forgiven, and brought near to God through Christ, and that grace begins to reshape how we live. unit #39

Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. Paul expresses thanksgiving for the faith of believers in Rome whom he has never met. What does this reveal about how Paul viewed the faith of other Christians, and what might this suggest about his understanding of the gospel's work beyond his own life?
    Romans 1:8
    → How does Paul's posture here differ from what we might expect if someone viewed their own spiritual life as entirely separate from others' faith?
  2. In verses 11-12, Paul describes his desire to visit Rome to 'impart some spiritual gift' but then immediately says he wants to be 'mutually encouraged by each other's faith.' Why does Paul frame his visit not as a one-directional ministry but as mutual encouragement?
    Romans 1:11-12
  3. The sermon emphasizes that 'maturity doesn't mean we outgrow our need for encouragement.' How does this challenge the way our culture—and sometimes our churches—think about spiritual growth or Christian leadership?
    → Can you think of a time when you received encouragement from another believer's faith? What did that reveal about your own need?
  4. Paul says he is 'under obligation' to preach the gospel to both Greeks and barbarians, yet the sermon also describes this obligation as 'glad.' How can obligation and joy coexist, and what has to be true about Paul's heart for both to be present at the same time?
    Romans 1:14-15
    → What does this tell us about the difference between duty done out of compulsion versus duty done out of grace?
  5. The fallen condition the sermon surfaces is that without community, we cut ourselves off from the grace God intends to deliver through others. What does this passage suggest about the spiritual danger of isolation, and how have you experienced the need for the body of Christ in your own Christian life?
    Romans 1:11-12
  6. According to the sermon, the gospel that reconciles us to Christ also binds us to his people and sends us on mission. How should understanding that grace works in these three directions—toward God, toward the church, and toward the lost—change the way we think about what it means to be 'gripped by the gospel'?
    → Of these three directions, which one do you find yourself most neglecting, and what might that reveal about where your heart has been gripped by grace and where it hasn't?

Paul reminds us that we have been saved by grace, not by our own effort—this is the foundation of everything that follows in Romans 1. Tuesday James 2

James calls us to regard the faith of others without partiality or favoritism—and this flows from understanding what grace has done for us. As Paul does with the Romans' faith, we are called to celebrate God's work in others' lives and to honor the gift of their faith in our midst, not diminish it.

Wednesday John 15

Jesus teaches that we remain in Him by remaining in His love and in relationship with one another—just as Paul emphasizes that believers strengthen one another through mutual encouragement. When we cut ourselves off from the community of God's people, we cut ourselves off from the grace God intends to deliver to us through their faith and witness.

Thursday Romans 10:17

Paul shows us that faith comes by hearing, and hearing comes through the word of Christ—this is why Paul is eager and obligated to preach in Rome. Our understanding of the grace that saved us naturally produces in us a desire to see that same transforming grace at work in the hearts of those who have not yet heard the good news.

Friday Matthew 5:16

We are called to let our light shine before others so that they may see our good deeds and glorify our Father—this is the outward expression of hearts gripped by gospel grace. The grace that reconciles us to Christ binds us to His people and compels us to bear witness to His redemption, not from grim duty but from eager desire to see others experience the mercy we have known.
