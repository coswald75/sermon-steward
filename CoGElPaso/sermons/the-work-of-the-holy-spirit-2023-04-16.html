The Work of the Holy Spirit

1 Corinthians 12:1-11 April 16, 2023 Pastor Mark Prater
Thesis Christians live aware of the Holy Spirit's presence and work by celebrating his broad regenerating, illuminating, sanctifying, and persevering work in their lives and by using their Spirit-empowered gifts to serve others for the common good and the glory of the Triune God.

Serves as evidence that God speaks through prophecy to build up his people.
  4. personal story · unit #28 — Testimony of healing: a woman with a decade-long blood disorder was healed after prayer. The healing was verified by medical tests over time. Emphasizes that she doesn't remember who prayed but knows God healed her—reinforcing that the gift of healing is God's work, not human power.
Theological claims· 7
  1. Christians should cultivate a profound awareness that the Holy Spirit is always present as the most real and powerful factor in their daily environment. unit #4
  2. When a familiar verse suddenly comes to life in a believer's heart and mind, the Spirit is at work illuminating Scripture—a charismatic and spectacular work. unit #8
  3. When believers use their diverse Spirit-empowered gifts, they reflect the diversity and unity of the Trinitarian Godhead, grounding the exercise of gifts in the very nature of God. unit #15
  4. The gifts of the Spirit enable believers to understand and apply the gospel, serve others, and be empowered for gospel mission—making the whole experiential dimension of Christian life essentially charismatic and gospel-centered. unit #20
  5. The structure of 1 Corinthians establishes that the gospel—Christ and him crucified—is of first importance and that gifts are given to continue the work of the gospel in believers' lives, not to replace it or move it to the periphery. unit #21
  6. Gifts are given to strengthen and edify the work of the gospel in believers' lives and at times can lead non-Christians to Christ by revealing the reality of God. unit #22
  7. The primary way God speaks is through Scripture illuminated by the Spirit, but at times God makes believers aware of his presence through the gift of prophecy. unit #25

5-day reading plan

This week, we celebrate the Holy Spirit's regenerating, illuminating, and sanctifying work in our lives, then learn how our Spirit-empowered gifts reflect God's Trinitarian nature and serve the gospel's advance.

Monday John 3:6-7

Jesus speaks of spiritual birth as the Spirit's sovereign work—we cannot manufacture it ourselves, nor can we understand it apart from the Spirit's action. This foundational reality means we are not merely moral beings striving for improvement, but spiritually alive people indwelt by the most powerful Person in the universe, which is the bedrock of all our awareness of His presence.

Tuesday 1 Corinthians 2:12-13

Paul shows us that the Spirit both gives us the gift of understanding divine truth and enables us to communicate it to others with words the Spirit Himself teaches. When we experience a verse suddenly piercing our hearts or answering a question we didn't know how to ask, we are witnessing the Spirit's illuminating work—not a distant theological concept, but His present, active power reshaping how we see ourselves and God.

Wednesday Philippians 2:12-13

Paul weaves together our responsibility to work out our salvation and God's work in us to will and to act—the Spirit's persevering grace is not passive, but actively energizing our pursuit of Christlikeness. As we grow aware of His presence, we recognize that every victory over sin, every act of sacrificial love, every step toward holiness flows from His relentless, gracious empowerment, not from our moral bootstraps.

Thursday 1 Corinthians 14:24-25

The Spirit's gifts, exercised in the gathered church, possess the power to convict and draw unbelievers into encounter with the living God—prophecy reveals the secrets of human hearts, exposing the reality that God is truly among us. This reminds us that our gifts are never ornamental; they are instruments of God's missionary presence, making the gospel tangible and undeniable to those who have yet to believe.

Friday 1 Corinthians 2:2

Paul's declaration anchors the entire charismatic life of the church to the cross; every gift, no matter how spectacular, exists to magnify the finished work of Christ rather than draw attention to itself or the gifted. As we step into our Spirit-empowered roles this week—whether teaching, serving, leading, or interceding—we ask: Does this gift point others to Jesus? Does it strengthen the gospel's hold on our hearts and the hearts of those we serve?
