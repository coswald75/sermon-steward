Illustrations· 4
  1. personal story · unit #3 — Personal story establishing the sermon's existential problem: identity shaped by accumulated human judgments (positive and negative) is inherently unstable. The illustration lands the listener in the psychological experience of self-definition by résumé.
  2. personal story · unit #10 — Personal story illustrating how deeply the two-tier religious hierarchy (ordinary believers vs. saints specially called to God) is embedded even in Protestant minds shaped by border Catholic culture.   3. personal story · unit #13 — Personal story of financial anxiety before pastor's college. The obsessive recounting of meager assets illustrates the visceral experience of identity-by-inventory—how having nothing material can produce a 'hole in the heart' and profound inadequacy.   4. personal story · unit #18 — Personal story of bureaucratic healthcare nightmare resolved by knowing a well-connected orthopedic surgeon. The narrative vivifies the principle that access and care are radically transformed when you know someone with authority and willingness to intervene on your behalf.
Theological claims· 7
  1. Romans 1:7 is aimed directly at the instability of self-definition and will reorder the believer's identity from the ground up. unit #4
  2. Paul can reorder our identities because he writes God's Word to address the one thing he knows about all Christians: they are in Christ. unit #5
  3. Union with Christ grounds Christian identity in three stable realities that replace the instability of self-definition by accumulation. unit #6
  4. Christians are loved by God not because of their lovability but because God has set his love upon them in Christ. unit #8
  5. There is no higher calling than being set apart to be near God, which means Christians need not chase secular achievements to secure identity or worth. unit #11
  6. Believers know the Lord Jesus Christ—Creator, Savior, Messiah, and sovereign over all things—which is the ultimate relational asset and identity anchor. unit #20
  7. Paul's greeting is not syncretistic addition but exclusive claim—Jesus alone is Lord, Savior, and King, demolishing Caesar's competing titles and the Roman pantheon. unit #21

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through Romans 1:1-7 by asking the deepest question: Who am I? Paul anchors Christian identity not in achievement or approval, but in three stable realities—loved by God, called to be saints, and known by Christ—that reorder everything.

Monday Romans 3

Paul's argument in Romans 3 strips away every human claim to righteousness and proves that no one earns God's favor by their own work. This is the foundation: if we cannot earn identity through performance, then our identity must come from outside us, given as a gift. When you woke up this morning, you did not earn your standing before God—it was already settled in Christ.

Tuesday John 15:9

Jesus tells his disciples that the Father's love for them is permanent—not conditional on their performance or their worthiness. Notice the direction: God's love comes toward us, not earned from us. This is countercultural; the world measures love by achievement and desirability. But in Christ, we are loved simply because God chose to love us.

Wednesday Romans 4

Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness—not because he accumulated virtue or status, but because he was reckoned righteous in union with God's promise. Romans 4 shows us that Christian identity has always worked this way: we are declared and secured in a relationship we receive, not a resume we build. The believer's identity is as stable as God's reckoning.

Thursday Romans 5

In Romans 5, Paul moves from justification to reconciliation—we are not only declared righteous, but brought near to God as friends and family. The culture tells us that identity comes from title, salary, and social media standing. But Paul says the highest possible achievement is access to God himself. When you know you are near to God and loved by him, lesser pursuits lose their grip.

Friday Romans 1:7

Paul's opening greeting claims that Christians are called to belong to Jesus Christ, and in that belonging, we know grace and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus. This is not a religious add-on to an identity formed elsewhere; it is the foundation that rewrites everything. Who are you? You are someone who knows Jesus Christ. That is the whole story—and it is enough.
