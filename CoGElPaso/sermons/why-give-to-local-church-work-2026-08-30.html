5-day reading plan

This week we deepen our understanding of why the church matters so much that Jesus built it on the rock of His own person—by walking through five cross-references that show us the church's irreplaceable role in God's economy.

Monday Ephesians 4:11-12

Paul reminds us that Christ gave the church apostles, prophets, evangelists, and pastor-teachers—not to display perfection, but to equip the saints for the work of ministry. Notice: the leaders themselves are gifts *to* a broken body, not judges *of* it. We gather as broken people who have met Jesus, and He has given us these gifts precisely because we need help growing up into His image together.

Tuesday 1 Peter 5

Peter writes to shepherds scattered across different nations and cultures, yet they share one flock, one Chief Shepherd. The unity isn't ethnic, social, or cultural—it's covenantal. We belong to one another because we all belong to Christ. This is why giving to the local church is not an optional charity; it's an investment in the only organization on earth held together by the blood of Jesus.

Wednesday Galatians 6

Paul tells the Galatians to bear one another's burdens and so fulfill the law of Christ—because the church is not a collection of isolated individuals but a family bound by shared grace. When we give to the local church, we're funding the only place on earth where strangers become family, where the lonely find home, and where rebels are reconciled both to God and to one another through the cross.

Thursday Ephesians 4:11-12

The gifts Christ gave—pastors, teachers, apostles—work together to build up the body of Christ toward maturity in Him. No single leader or strategy makes the church powerful; rather, it is the Spirit's work through the *assembled people* who have been equipped by these gifts. This is why the church's power cannot be replicated by a podcast, a book, or a parachurch ministry; it requires the actual gathering of God's people around God's Word.

Friday Galatians 6

Paul calls the Galatian church to sow generously into God's work, knowing that those who sow to the Spirit will reap eternal life. When we give to the local church, we're not writing a check for a temporary program—we're investing in eternal proclamation, eternal transformation, and eternal reconciliation. No other organization has these stakes; no other organization works on the scale of heaven and hell. This is the most important work in the world.
