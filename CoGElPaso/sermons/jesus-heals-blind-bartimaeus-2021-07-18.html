Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we follow Bartimaeus's journey from desperate outcry to devoted discipleship, learning how Jesus's servant Messiah stops for the overlooked and calls us to both cry out boldly in our need and see the forgotten around us.

Monday 2 Samuel 7:11-16

When Bartimaeus cried out, 'Son of David, have mercy on me,' he was invoking the deepest messianic hope of Israel—the promise that God would raise up a king from David's line who would reign forever. This passage shows us the ancient covenant Jesus fulfills: He is that eternal King, and His stopping to heal one blind beggar reveals how His kingship manifests not in distant majesty but in intimate, personal mercy. We encounter the Messiah not in some far-off throne room but in the one who notices us when we cry out.

Tuesday Matthew 12:22-23

When Jesus healed the blind and mute demoniac, the crowds marveled and asked, 'Could this be the Son of David?'—the very title Bartimaeus uses. Yet even as healing stirs faith in desperate hearts, it simultaneously hardens those who refuse to see Him rightly. Bartimaeus's sight—both physical and spiritual—places him in the company of all who have ever truly recognized Jesus for who He is: the Messiah whose works prove His mercy extends to the forgotten. Our question today is which crowd we join: those who marvel at His grace or those who turn away?

Wednesday Matthew 15:21-22

The Canaanite woman's persistence—crying out despite the disciples' rebuke, pressing past cultural barriers—mirrors Bartimaeus's own bold cry when the crowd tried to silence him. Both reveal that Jesus's mercy answers not the clamor of the privileged but the desperate call of those society overlooks. When we examine our own lives and communities, we must ask: whose cries are we silencing, and whose desperation might be calling us to become more like the Jesus who stops to listen?

Thursday Luke 9:57-62

Bartimaeus's response—he 'jumped up' and 'followed Jesus on the way'—embodies the radical commitment Jesus demands of His followers in this passage. Unlike those who hesitate or negotiate conditions with Jesus, Bartimaeus surrenders everything in that moment, his newfound sight immediately redirected to the face of his Savior and Master. We are called to that same undivided allegiance, not paralyzed by what we must leave behind, but moved by the singular beauty of following Jesus.

Friday Acts 12:5-17

The praying church's bold cry for Peter's deliverance mirrors Bartimaeus's persistent cry for mercy—and both reveal believers who know Jesus hears and answers the desperate prayers of His people. Yet notice the church's stunned response when Peter actually appears: they had prayed but hardly believed He would come. We too must learn Bartimaeus's faith: to cry out boldly for what only Jesus can provide, and then to recognize and follow Him when He answers, trusting that the Messiah who stops for one overlooked beggar is active in our own corporate need and deliverance.
