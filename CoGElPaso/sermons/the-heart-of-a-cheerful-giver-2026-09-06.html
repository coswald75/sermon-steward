5-day reading plan

This week we trace how the gospel of grace transforms our relationship with money—from ownership anxiety to joyful stewardship—and compels us toward generosity that glorifies God and builds his kingdom.

Monday 2 Corinthians 8:2-4

The Macedonian churches gave generously not because their circumstances were abundant but because grace had transformed their hearts. Their poverty became the soil for extravagant generosity—a living proof that the gospel reshapes our capacity to give. We, too, are invited into this same grace-driven abundance, where our willingness to sow overflows from hearts remade by Christ.

Tuesday Hebrews 12:2

Jesus endured the cross 'for the joy set before him'—his eyes fixed on the eternal purposes of God rather than the temporal cost. As we fix our gaze on Christ, his joy-filled obedience becomes our model for generosity toward God's kingdom work. When we look to Jesus as the supreme Giver, our own giving is reoriented from self-preservation to gospel advancement.

Wednesday Romans 5

The grace of God revealed in Christ's death for us while we were still sinners establishes that our security rests not in hoarded wealth but in God's infinite mercy. When we grasp how completely God has given himself for us, the grip of material anxiety loosens, and our hands open freely. Stewardship becomes an act of worship, not a white-knuckled defense against loss.

Thursday Jeremiah 29:7

God calls his people to seek the welfare of the city where they dwell, recognizing that kingdom blessing flows outward from God's covenantal people. Our generosity toward the church's mission equips us and strengthens us to bless our neighbors and communities with gospel hope. When we sow abundantly into God's work, we become agents of his blessing to all those around us.

Friday 2 Corinthians 8:15

Paul's citation of the manna principle—'the one who gathered much did not have too much, and the one who gathered little did not have too little'—reveals that God's purpose is not equality of poverty but equality of provision and peace. Our eager, cheerful generosity is the concrete expression of trusting that God supplies what we need and that our open hands honor his ownership. In giving joyfully, we declare to ourselves and our community that grace, not greed, now governs our lives.

6 questions for your group this week

  1. In 2 Corinthians 9:6-7, Paul presents two contrasting pictures of the giver—one who sows sparingly and one who sows generously. What does Paul mean by 'sowing' in this context, and how does his language challenge the way we typically think about money?
    2 Corinthians 9:6-7
    → What would it look like in your own life to 'sow generously' rather than sparingly in the areas where God has entrusted resources to you?
  2. The sermon emphasizes that God owns everything and we are stewards, not owners. How does this theological conviction differ from the world's message about money, and what evidence of that worldly message do you see in your own thinking about your possessions?
    2 Corinthians 9:10
  3. Paul writes that 'God loves a cheerful giver' (2 Corinthians 9:7). What does cheerfulness in giving reveal about a person's relationship with God, and how is it possible to be cheerful when giving costs us something real?
    → When have you experienced joy in giving, and what was different about that moment compared to times when giving felt more obligatory?
  4. According to 2 Corinthians 9:8-11, God supplies seed to the sower and increases the harvest so that the giver can be generous in every circumstance. How does understanding God as both the source and the multiplier of our resources reshape our anxiety about money?
    2 Corinthians 9:8-11
    → What specific area of financial worry might change if you truly believed that God supplies both your needs and your capacity to give?
  5. The sermon argues that the purpose of any increase in wealth is increased capacity for kingdom work and increased thanksgiving to God—not increased consumption. What does 'lifestyle creep' look like in practice, and how can we guard against it as our means increase?
  6. In 2 Corinthians 9:12-15, Paul connects the Corinthians' generosity to the gospel itself—their gift becomes an overflow of gratitude for God's grace.
