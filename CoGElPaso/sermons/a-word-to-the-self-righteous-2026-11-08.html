Theological claims· 9
  1. Paul's strategy is to eliminate the us-versus-them divide in righteousness by forcing everyone to see they are equally guilty. unit #9
  2. True repentance requires seeing both the gravity of our sin (what it deserves) and the magnitude of God's mercy (what we receive instead). unit #15
  3. Christians must never think their sins make them less deserving of God's judgment than others—all sin equally deserves wrath. unit #17
  4. God is fair, merciful, and righteous—His mercy does not violate His justice. unit #24
  5. Paul's confrontation of sin is not condemnation but a necessary step to reveal the depth of mercy in Christ. unit #26
  6. God's kindness leads to repentance, but repentance requires awareness of our sin—mercy and conviction are not opposites but partners. unit #29
  7. The gospel is God declaring us guilty but taking our guilt upon Himself in Christ—mercy and grace displayed by an impartial judge. unit #34
  8. Self-righteousness is the opposite of the gospel—the gospel says we are far worse than we think but far more loved than we dare imagine. unit #35
  9. The cross eliminates all self-righteousness—the ground is level, and no one deserves mercy, yet all receive it through Christ. unit #36

God will not act in a way that is counter to His character. Paul is just laying out the reasonable and realistic end for all unrighteousness. One more quote from Sproul that I think is helpful here. God does not always act with justice. Sometimes he acts with mercy. Mercy is not justice, but it is also not injustice. Injustice violates righteousness. Mercy manifests kindness and grace and does no violence to righteousness. We may see non-justice in God, which is mercy, but we never see injustice in God. Verses 6 through 11 help us see God for who he is: a fair, merciful, and righteous judge.

The Apostle Paul calls himself the chief of sinners. He himself talks about the war between his flesh and the things of the Lord. 35 · Vogan delivers a series of contrasts between self-righteousness and the righteousness of God

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace Paul's demolition of self-righteousness through five cross-references, moving from the exposure of universal guilt, through the sufficiency of God's justice and mercy, to the gospel's power to break our self-protective comparisons and remake us in Christ.

Monday Romans 3:10

Paul's citation—'There is none righteous, no not one'—echoes the Old Testament's comprehensive verdict on humanity. This is not a word about 'those people over there,' but about every one of us. We cannot exempt ourselves from this judgment, nor can we rank ourselves above others; all stand equally condemned before the God who sees truly.

Tuesday Romans 3:23-26

Here Paul reveals the paradox that demolishes self-righteousness: we are justified freely by grace through the redemption in Christ, and God's justice is upheld precisely in that very act. The cross is not mercy overriding justice; it is mercy and justice meeting in Christ's death. This moves us from fear of an impartial judge to awe before a righteous judge who is also our advocate.

Wednesday Romans 5:8

Paul shows us the timing that breaks self-righteousness: Christ died for us 'while we were still sinners.' We do not earn or prepare ourselves for mercy by moral improvement; we receive it while still actively rebelling. This measure of God's love—given to the undeserving—is what transforms us from self-protective judges of others into grateful, repenting sinners.

Thursday Romans 7

Even as Paul describes his own ongoing struggle with sin as a believer, he refuses the comfort of comparison. The 'wretched man' he depicts is not worse than others, nor is he excused; he is simply honest about the war within every Christian heart. This honesty—not self-exoneration—is the path to freedom and gratitude for the mercy we do not deserve.

Friday Ephesians 2:4-5

Because of God's mercy and love, He made us alive in Christ, not as a reward for our worthiness, but as an act of grace toward the dead. This is how we move from self-righteous judgment to genuine repentance: not by harsh condemnation, but by seeing the staggering kindness that reaches us in our guilt. The ground is level at the cross, and on that level, we all bow together.
