Corpus Note

Haddon W. Robinson served as Harold John Ockenga Distinguished Professor of Preaching at Gordon-Conwell Theological Seminary. His textbook Biblical Preaching (1980) has shaped a generation of seminary-trained preachers. The "Big Idea" method — every sermon has one controlling idea that everything else serves — is the dominant homiletical paradigm in evangelical preaching education.

The Robinson Signature

Robinson's signature is structural clarity built around a single, well-crafted subject-complement proposition. His sermons are case studies in what he taught: the big idea governs every paragraph, every illustration, every application. No wandering, no rabbit trails. He is the Hall's preaching teacher — the one whose influence is felt most in how other preachers think about sermon architecture.

The Empty Tomb – Faith's Firm Foundation
Cultural Reference
H.G. wells was a critic of the church, and in his own way, I think, was one of our good friends. In one of the stories that he wrote for New Yorker magazine several years ago, he imagined that an Episcopalian bishop in a church in New York, he picked on the Episcopalians, but he could have picked on Presbyterians or Methodists or people in Believers chapel. But he imagined this Episcopalian bishop. He was a man who had folks come to him for counsel, and he had some platitudes he used that he thought were especially effective. If you said them in just the right way, they could sound very pious and religious. And one that he liked especially was to say to somebody who had come to him with a problem, have you prayed about it? He found that if he said it in just the right tone, it seemed like spiritual counsel. The trouble was that the bishop never prayed very much himself. Life was in a nice neat package for him, all wrapped up in his back pocket, and he didn't have much need of prayer. And then one day, Wells imagined that life tumbled in on this good bishop, and he decided that maybe it would be good to try his own advice. And one Saturday afternoon when the cathedral was empty, he walked down to the front and knelt on the crimson carpet and looked up and folded his hands and thought how childlike and simple he was. Then he said, oh, God. And there was a voice, crisp and businesslike, and the voice said, well, what is it? The next day when the worshippers came to church, they found the bishop sprawled face down in the front of the cathedral. When they turned them over, they saw that there were lines of terror etched upon his face. What H.G. wells was saying was simply this, that there are a great many folks talking about God, and perhaps in some way praying to him would Be scared to death if they discovered that they were really in his presence.

How to Give Meaning to Monday Morning
Analogy
Frankly, I think that one of the reasons that a great many marriages falter and fail is because young couples come to the marriage altar and have never thought through the cost of that committal that they make to each other. The minister says that marriage ought not be entered into lightly or unadvisedly, but reverently and thoughtfully and in the fear of the Lord. But it is often there that couples make their mistake in the glow of marriage. They get caught up in a committal that they have not thought about. While they cannot weigh all of the costs of that relationship, they certainly can weigh some of them. And the failure to do so often causes a shattered marriage. And that hurts both the couple and their children. There can be disgrace and embarrassment and even a kind of disaster.

Personal Story
Early this summer, I came in my own life to an illustration of this verse. I was invited to a dinner at $50 a plate. I assure you I don't go to dinners at $50 a plate when I'm paying for the plate. As I look at our meat bill at home, however, I think I'm getting dangerously close to that. But in this case, I was invited to go as someone else's guest. Someone else was paying the tab. In fact, when I was in the office of the man who was sponsoring the dinner, one of the ladies in Dallas who had obviously contributed generously to the cause, was saying to him something like, I wish you would do me a favor. I know you're going to have roast beef and potatoes with sour cream and a rich dessert, but I can't eat it, so could you see that I would have a bowl of bread with some milk on it. That's what I'm able to digest. That's what he says. When you're young and lean and hungry, you eat hamburger and hamburger Helper. And when you're old and successful and have a bank account and you can afford steak, your digestion just won't stand it. There's a mystery there you don't quite understand why God has put the world together that way, why the hungry people don't have the big banquets. But you can't figure it out. That's the way life is.

Historical Example
William Carey was a brilliant missionary. He was a cobbler, a repairer of shoes. But he learned 34 different languages. And India has the Bible today because of the work of William Carey. When William Carey went to India, he was not accepted by the British citizens that were serving there. Many of them came from nobility. One evening, we are told, William Carey went to a dinner party and one of the men sitting next to him, in a rather loud voice, decided to put him down. He said, Mr. Carey, I understand that you spent a large part of your life as a shoemaker. William Carey said, no, sir, that's not accurate. I did not have enough ability to make shoes. I simply repaired them. That response of that gracious man indicated something about his own view of himself. He was not interested in impressing British nobility. William Carey lived before God and had an enchanting humility.
