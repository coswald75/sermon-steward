And then when Moses blesses all the tribes of Israel and he speaks of Levi, the tribe of the priests, he specifically says, they shall teach Jacob your rules and Israel your law. So, The prophets, they were like God's lawyers. When God's people violated his covenant, the prophets came and charged them, reminded them, you have violated God's word and here are the consequences. Turn, repent, believe, trust, and obey God. The priests, they were the ones responsible for teaching God's people what God required.

24 · The pastor explains the necessity of enforcing God's law through civil authorities (magistrates and judges) authorized by the Persian king

But what good are God's laws, what good are any laws if they're not enforced. As someone once said, the law is but words and paper without the hands and swords of men. And so one of the first things Ezra did was he appointed magistrates and judges in verse 25. And the king of Persia authorized them to enforce the law of God with civil penalties. It lists death, deportation, confiscation of goods, imprisonment, Ezra's mission to teach and to enforce God's word in God's place with God's people is a major step in reformation and revival there in Jerusalem. The whole reason that they had been taken into captivity to begin with was because they disobeyed God's law. That if they returned to the land and rebuilt the temple and continued to live in willful disobedience against God, that they would bring God's judgment on themselves again.

25 · The pastor makes a propositional theological claim about the universal application of God's moral law to all people and nations, refuting the notion that God's law only applies to those who accept it

Listen, God governs the world, all people in all nations, according to his moral law. That's still true today. Some people think God's law only applies to those who want it to apply and everybody else is free to choose whatever way they want to live without any consequence in this life. But God deals with all people according to his moral law. Any nation that violates his moral requirements devolves into chaos and invites God's just judgment.

26 · The pastor grounds the preceding theological claim in Leviticus 18, demonstrating that God's moral law governed even the pagan nations before Israel entered the land

When Israel first entered the land, God gave them clear instructions and warnings. Let me read from Leviticus 18. I think this is important to understand. God's law applied not just to God's people. God's law reveals God's moral ways for all people. Everywhere God told his people, the land became unclean so that I punished its iniquity and the land vomited out its inhabitants. Who were those? Those were the pagan inhabitants who lived there prior to Israel. They didn't have God's law. They didn't have God's covenant. And the land spit them out because of their wickedness and their abominations, because God governs the whole world by his moral law. There's a moral fabric to the world like the laws of physics, the laws of gravity that govern our physical operations. There are moral laws that govern humanity. God goes on to say to his people, but you shall keep my statutes and my rules and do none of these abominations, either the native or the stranger who sojourns among you. For the people of the land who were before you did all of these abominations so that the land became unclean. Lest the land vomit you out when you make it unclean, as it vomited out the nation that was before you. That gives us some sense of the importance of recovering, not just having God's law, but having people who trust God's word and respond to his word and apply his word in their own lives.

27 · The pastor draws the lesson from Ezra 7 and applies it universally: spiritual renewal always requires moral renewal grounded in God's Word

Reviving the ruined people of Israel required a moral renewal. Which meant teaching and believing God's word. The same is true today.

28 · The pastor addresses a contemporary debate about government acknowledgment of God by pointing to Ezra's response to the Persian king's authorization

Today there's an ongoing debate between Christians about whether it would be a net good thing or bad thing for the government to acknowledge God and his word. I would just say, consider how Ezra responded when the pagan king of Persia sent him home with official authorization to fund the temple and teach God's word. He broke out in tears. In praise to God in verses 27 and 28. Blessed be the Lord, the God of our fathers, who put such a thing as this into the heart of the king, to beautify the house of the Lord that is in Jerusalem, and who extended to me his steadfast love before the king and his counselors and before all the king's mighty officials. I took courage, for the hand of the Lord my God was on me, and I gathered leading men from Israel to go up with me. Ezra thanked God. He gave all glory to God, and then he took courage and steeled his nerves, and he got to work.

29 · The pastor pivots from exposition to Christological fulfillment, transitioning from Ezra's mission to Christ as the ultimate Word made flesh

So what does Ezra, this man and his mission, have to do with your life today? Your problems, your challenges? Here's what it means. When God acted to restore and revive his ruined people after their captivity, He sent them His Word through a man named Ezra. But when God acted to save the world, the Word became flesh and dwelt among us. And we have seen His glory, glory as of the only Father, the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth.

30 · The pastor articulates the superiority of the new covenant over the old, emphasizing that while Ezra could only teach God's Word externally, Christ has written it on believers' hearts by His Spirit

And by dying for your sins, Jesus established with His own blood a new covenant A covenant better than the one that Ezra was operating under. He could go and he could teach God's word. And guess what? The people still had hard hearts, but there was a new covenant promise. There will be a day, God said, when I will pour out my spirit on you and I'm going to take out your heart of stone and I'm going to give you a heart of flesh. And I'm going to write my word on your heart. And it's not just going to be outside of you, someone like Ezra teaching you, trying to convince you God's ways are better and you should trust him and obey him. It's going to be inside of you. It's going to be inside of you. I'm going to put my Spirit in you and I'm going to cause you to be careful to walk in my ways, Ezekiel 36 says. We live in that new covenant age because Jesus shed his blood to inaugurate that new covenant. So we live in a better day than Ezra. We have the same word and more, the living word made flesh. And we have his Spirit in us.

31 · The pastor applies the sermon's central thesis to specific life situations, asserting the sufficiency of God's Word through Christ for every problem—personal sin, marriage struggles, parenting challenges, and global concerns

So Ezra is pointing us to Christ and to the supremacy of the Word of God and assuring you, are there any problems in your life? Are there challenges that you're facing right now? Your life personally, your family, you look at the world and the problems around us, the Word of God is light and truth for everyone who will turn to Him and trust in Jesus. Everything that you need for every problem that you face, is in God's word. God's word is sufficient. Are you plagued by sin and guilt and shame? The answer is Jesus. Is your marriage miserable? Mine the depths of God's word for everything that he says to you. It's full of wisdom for how to live as a husband or a wife and experience God's favor and his goodness and his blessing. Are you challenged and struggling in parenting and at your wit's end? God's word is full of truth and wisdom, and it's available to you by his Spirit. Are you worried about what's going on in the world right now? After darkness comes light, and God always works to restore ruined people by his word.

32 · The pastor issues a comprehensive call to action: cling to Scripture, trust in Christ for salvation, and obey God's Word in every part of life

So may we be people who cling to this book. Brothers and sisters, we, we have a book where God speaks to us, and we have a Savior who died for sinners. So that our greatest problem, we are fully assured, has been fully dealt with by Jesus who loved us and gave himself for us. Devote yourself to this word, and the very first thing you'll find this word commands you to do is repent of your sins and trust in Jesus. And it doesn't stop there. From there, it calls you to give yourself to trusting and obeying every word that comes from, every word that comes from the mouth of God. We don't live by bread alone, but by his word in every part of our life. And his word is sufficient. May you be built up and strengthened and empowered by God's word to live for his glory in this dark world. Whatever darkness you face this week, may you leave here with renewed faith in Jesus.

33 · The closing prayer asks God to make the congregation like Ezra—devoted to learning, living, and teaching God's Word—and concludes with a petition for global witness

Let's pray. Father, thank you. Thank you for your word. Thank you for the example of men like Ezra who loved your word and set their heart fully to, to learn it and to live it and to teach it. May we be like that in our own lives, in our homes, earnestly teaching our children, in our places of work, in our neighborhoods. Joyfully bearing witness to those around us who don't know, who don't know Jesus and who don't know the hope of the gospel. God, would You stir in our hearts a love and affection for Your Word and a conviction that Your Word is sufficient for all the problems we face? We love You, Lord. We pray that You would build Your church by Your Word and that You would make us witnesses To the ends of the earth, till this world is filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord like the waters that cover the sea. Amen.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how God restores His ruined people through His Word—from the foundation of Scripture's authority, through the renewal it demands in our lives, to its ultimate fulfillment in Christ's new covenant.

Monday Psalm 119

The psalmist's sustained meditation on God's law reveals what Ezra embodied: a soul entirely shaped by Scripture. As we see in Ezra 7:10, the scribe 'had set his heart to study the Law of the Lord'—and Psalm 119 shows us the fruit of such devotion: wisdom, guidance, and transformation from within. When we commit ourselves to know God's Word deeply, we position ourselves to be restored by it.

Tuesday Leviticus 10:11

Ezra's mission was rooted in the ancient priestly charge: to teach Israel God's statutes and judgments. This passage shows that spiritual leadership has always required not just right ritual but right instruction—priests were to make distinctions and teach discernment. Ezra's work in Jerusalem (Ezra 7:25-26) followed this exact pattern, demonstrating that true spiritual restoration requires those equipped to teach God's Word with clarity and authority.

Wednesday 2 Chronicles 34

King Josiah's reformation mirrors Ezra's mission: both leaders encountered God's Word, were transformed by it, and then led their nation to obedience and renewal. When Josiah discovered the Book of the Law, it sparked corporate repentance and moral transformation (2 Chronicles 34:21-23). Just as Josiah's reforms followed from hearing God's Word, so Ezra's work in Jerusalem shows that nations are renewed when their people encounter, believe, and obey Scripture.

Thursday Ezekiel 36

Ezekiel prophesies what Jeremiah proclaimed: God would make a new covenant in which His Spirit indwells His people and causes them to obey His law (Ezekiel 36:26-27). This is the trajectory that reaches its fulfillment in Christ—the old covenant taught God's Word externally; the new covenant writes it internally through the Spirit. We who live in the new covenant age possess what Ezra could only point toward: the Spirit's transforming power working within us to make us obedient from the heart.

Friday John 1:14

All of Ezra's work pointed forward to this reality: that God's Word would become flesh and dwell among us, full of grace and truth (John 1:14). Where Ezra taught God's written Word to restore a nation, Jesus is God's Word incarnate, offering us not merely instruction but transformation—His Spirit writes God's law on our hearts and grants us the power to obey. In Christ we have the fulfillment of all God promised: restoration that goes to the deepest places of our souls.

    → How have you seen the difference between someone who teaches God's Word without living it, versus someone whose teaching flows from a life of obedience?

    Ezra 7:25-26
  3. According to the sermon, spiritual revival requires not only right worship but renewed lives shaped by obedience to God's Word. Where do you see evidence of this principle in Ezra's commissioning, and what would incomplete revival look like if it emphasized one without the other?
  4. Lamentations 2:9 describes a time when 'the Law is no more,' and the sermon connects this to spiritual ruin. What does our current cultural moment reveal about what happens to a people when God's Word is no longer central to their formation and thinking?
    Lamentations 2:9
    → How does this help you understand why Christians gathering around God's Word in the local church is not optional but essential?

    Ezekiel 36
