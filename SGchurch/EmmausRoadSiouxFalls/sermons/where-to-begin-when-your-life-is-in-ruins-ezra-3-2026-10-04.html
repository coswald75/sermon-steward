Theological claims· 5
  1. Ezra-Nehemiah reveals the way God works in renewing ruined lives so that we can be renewed today through the same patterns. unit #5
  2. Atonement for sin is every human being's most urgent need, and while Old Testament sacrifices pointed to this need, only Jesus Christ—the propitiation God Himself provided—can actually take away sin and secure God's favor forever. unit #13
  3. Restoration moves from internal to external—reconciliation with God first, then worship expressed through work in the world—because worship never stays in the heart but comes out through our hands. unit #18
  4. True piety produces action in the world, while pietism creates a false dichotomy between private devotion and public obedience—the kingdom of God consists in power, not talk. unit #19
  5. Christian optimism—grounded in the resurrection and God's promises—produces confident hope that the best is yet to come, and since God ordinarily works through small beginnings, we must not despise humble starts. unit #28

I think that's true in our own lives. You know that your sins are forgiven, you've been reconciled to God, and yet there's still some problem in your life to address. What do you do? Ask God to show you what is the next step of obedient faith. What's the next right thing to do here? And if you're not sure, ask someone in your gospel community, reach out to one of the pastors for counseling. That's the power of worship. It begins to radiate from hearts that are reconciled to God outward into the world, creating change.

The final scene in these last 4 verses, the people celebrate the laying of the temple foundation with praise and thanksgiving to God. 23 · Exposition of verse 13 and application about worship posture

The people, they sing and they shout with a great shout of praise. So verse 13 says that the sound was heard far away. What a model of this eager, exuberant, fully engaged, expressive, thanks and praise to God. That's how it should be from the people of God in every generation, hearts fully engaged, expressing gratitude to God. That's how we want to engage with the Lord when we gather week after week like this, no matter how we feel, because it's not about how we feel. It's about who he is and what he's doing in our lives. You might be naturally more reserved and less expressive than others, but joy and delight always overcomes our own natural reservations. What's striking here is this worship celebration, it happens before the completion of the temple. They have not yet rebuilt the temple. And I think they model for us, it is always right for us to be looking for every hint and every evidence of the grace of God. And then to be thanking God, not just waiting to the end.

24 · Personal testimony about failing to thank God for answered prayer, leading to conviction

I don't know if you're like me and you realize usually long after the fact that you had prayed and asked God for something and then come to think of it, actually he did fulfill, he did answer, he helped, he provided, he came through. 25 · Concrete application flowing from the personal testimony: actively look for evidence of God's grace in your own life and in others (spouse, children, gospel community), and when you see any progress, celebrate it and thank God

If you're trusting God and relying on God to work in your life, then keep your eyes out for all the ways that he is restoring and renewing you. Look for evidence of his grace. And when you see any progress at all, you do this for yourself, do this for your spouse, do this for your kids, do this for those in your gospel community. If you see any progress at all, celebrate that and thank God for that.

But it wasn't all joy and praise. If you go read those prophets, they were prophesying at this time during this return from exile. Haggai said this in Haggai chapter 2. Who is left among you who saw this house in its former glory? 27 · Application warning against nostalgia, using Uncle Rico from Napoleon Dynamite as an illustration

There's a warning here for us. Beware the temptation of glory days I don't know if you've seen the movie Napoleon Dynamite. Uncle Rico, he brags about how, you know, back in '82, he could throw a football a quarter mile. He bets Napoleon, you see those mountains out there? I bet I could throw this football over those mountains. And then he just thinks nostalgically about his high school days and he says, coach would have put me in in the 4th quarter, we would have won the state championship. I would have gone pro, be living in a mansion somewhere right now. In his mind, the good old days, the glory days are so much brighter than the present, what his life could have been. Now, certainly we can learn from the past and humble people don't look down on and despise past generations, but there is a dangerous, unhelpful kind of nostalgia about the glory days that actually prevents you from living in the moment. And participating in what God is doing today. If you think the 1950s or the 1730s or the 16th century or the 1st century church, that, that was the peak. That was the pinnacle of everything. That was as good as it gets. Then you're going to miss what God is doing today. God rebuked that older generation in Israel for thinking that way.

28 · Major theological claim establishing Christian optimism grounded in the resurrection

Rather, as Christians, I think this should be our mindset, that the best is yet to come. The best is yet to come. This is just basic Christian optimism. It's not self-help, positive thinking, prosperity gospel nonsense. It's Christian optimism that comes from the fact that our Lord and Savior rose from the dead. And he is alive today, ruling and reigning from the right hand of God the Father. If you believe God's word and you, you should, then you believe the promises of God's word. And you believe that the future is going to be better than the past or present. You believe that God right now is working all things together, the mess in your life. He's working all things together for your good. Romans 8:28. You believe that Jesus is building his church. Matthew 16. You believe that the earth will be filled with worshippers, Habakkuk 2:14. And faith is the assurance of things hoped for. It's the conviction of things not seen. If you've been reconciled to God through the death of Jesus Christ, the Son of God, then your future is an unending future of happy tomorrows. So don't despise the day of small things, small beginnings. That's God's ordinary way of working in the world. Oak trees grow from small acorns. Raging rivers grow from little trickles high up in the mountains. And the kingdom of heaven, Jesus said, it's like a tiny mustard seed that a sower sows and it grows until it becomes the biggest tree in the garden. Or little leaven that's worked in until the whole loaf is leavened. Matthew 13.

29 · Brief application connecting back to the exiles' example: avoid despising small beginnings by regularly, intentionally stopping to thank God for all things, especially small victories and evidences of grace

How do you avoid despising small beginnings? I think you do what these worshipers do. You regularly and you intentionally stop and you thank God. Thank God for all things and you thank God for small things, small victories and every evidence of grace.

30 · Conclusion synthesizing the sermon's three movements (priority, power, response of worship) into a concise answer to the opening question

Where do you start when your life is in ruins? You start with worship. You worship the Father through Christ the Son in the Spirit and then you take the next step of faith and you walk in the obedience of faith doing the next right thing and then You thank God and give Him all the glory every step of the way, no matter how small the progress is.

Let's pray. Father, You have given us Your very self and Your words and Your promises so that we might know You and be full of hope today. God, would You make out of our lives where our sin has made a mess, would You make monuments of Your grace and get glory in us? And would You help us to cling to Christ our Savior who died for our sin with unwavering hope and conviction and love and devotion? And would you cause the gospel of Jesus to function in our hearts and our lives, producing change that would bring great glory and honor to you. We pray this in Jesus' name. Amen.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through the pattern of restoration: from atonement and reconciliation with God, through worship that compels action, to the confident hope that God works through humble beginnings.

Monday Leviticus 16:30

The Day of Atonement in Leviticus shows Israel's deepest need: reconciliation with God through the covering of sin. The exiles in Ezra 3 grasped this urgently—they built the altar *before* the temple because they knew that without atonement, no building or work matters. We, too, begin our renewal not with self-improvement or circumstantial change, but with running to the cross where Jesus—our great High Priest—has already made the once-for-all atonement that secures God's favor forever.

Tuesday Leviticus 1

The burnt offering consumed entirely on the altar symbolized complete surrender and worship rising to God. The exiles offered these sacrifices (Ezra 3:3-4) not after rebuilding, but at the *beginning*, signaling that their work would flow from reconciled hearts. When we have been reconciled to God through Christ, our hands naturally move toward the work He calls us to in the world—worship becomes embodied in obedience, not trapped in sentiment.

Wednesday Psalm 100

This psalm calls us to enter God's gates with thanksgiving and gratitude—a inward posture. Yet the exiles showed us that such gratitude immediately became *construction*, *rebuilding*, *tangible work* in Jerusalem's ruins. Grateful worship is not complete until it flows into the kingdom work God has placed before us; the same hands that lift in praise must lift stones and lay foundations.

Thursday Zechariah 4:10

Zechariah's vision honors the 'day of small things'—the very modest foundation of the second temple that drew weeping from those who remembered the first. God speaks through Zechariah to assure that He *delights* in small beginnings because He sees them through to completion. When our lives lie in ruins and we feel capable only of humble, faltering steps toward renewal, we stand in the exact place where God works most powerfully.

Friday Romans 8:28

Paul's promise that God works *all things* together for good for those who love Him echoes through the exiles' return: captivity, ruin, and return all served God's purpose to humble His people and restore them to worship. Our own ruins—failures, sins, broken circumstances—are woven into the same sovereign pattern; God is renewing us through repentance, worship, and faithful work, just as He renewed Israel, and He will complete what He has begun.

    Ezra 3:1-13

    Leviticus 1
    → What changes when we grasp that Jesus is the propitiation God Himself provided?
  4. The sermon contrasted true piety with pietism—the difference between private devotion that naturally flows into public obedience versus private devotion that stays locked in the heart. Where are you tempted to create that false divide in your own life right now?
    → What would it look like this week for your worship to actually move 'out through your hands' into the world around you?
