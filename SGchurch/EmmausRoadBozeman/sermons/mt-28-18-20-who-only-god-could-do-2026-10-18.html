Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace God's eternal purpose to gather worshipers from every nation, discovering that the Great Commission belongs to the whole church, requires our joyful dependence on Christ's authority and presence, and calls each of us into purposeful partnership regardless of where we serve.

Monday Genesis 12:3

When God called Abram, He planted a seed that would grow across millennia: through one nation, all families of the earth would be blessed. This promise was not born on the Mount of Olives in Matthew 28—it was woven into God's redemptive design from the opening of His covenant history. The Great Commission is not innovation; it is the full flowering of what God declared in the dawn of His restoration work.

Tuesday Revelation 5:9

John's vision shows us the end: a great multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and tongue standing before the Lamb. But this future glory exists precisely because every tongue once stood in darkness, separated from God by sin. The diversity we see in Revelation is not a diversity of paths to God—it is a diversity united in one redemption, all ransomed by the blood of Christ alone.

Wednesday Philippians 1:3-5

Paul thanks the Philippian church for their *partnership* in the gospel from day one—not because they were all traveling apostles, but because ordinary believers in a local congregation owned the work of proclaiming Christ together. Partnership in gospel mission is the DNA of the church itself, woven into the corporate life of God's people, not confined to paid professionals or distant missionaries.

Thursday 1 Corinthians 3:5-7

We plant, we water, but God alone gives the growth—and this is not humbling news that diminishes our work; it is liberating news that removes the burden of outcome from our shoulders. Our faithfulness in whatever role we occupy (whether at home or overseas, in marketplace or church) is vital, yet the fruitfulness belongs entirely to the One who is building His church. This frees us to serve with joy rather than striving.

Friday Matthew 28:16-17

Even the disciples who stood before the risen Jesus experienced doubt (v. 17)—yet their hesitation did not disqualify them from receiving His commission. In our uncertainty, resistance, or fear as we engage in gospel partnership and mission, we meet the same Jesus who meets doubt with presence and authority. His absolute power and His nearness to us transform trembling faith into worship and obedience.

Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. What does Jesus mean when he claims 'all authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me' (Matthew 28:18), and how does this particular assertion set up everything he commands in the verses that follow?
    Matthew 28:18
    → How does knowing that Jesus possesses total authority change the way you approach obstacles or resistance when you're sharing your faith or serving in the church?
  2. The sermon emphasizes that the Great Commission was not a new mission but a continuation of God's purpose from the beginning. What does Genesis 12:3 reveal about God's heart for the nations, and how does that ancient promise connect to what Jesus is commanding his church to do in Matthew 28?
    Genesis 12:3
  3. Jesus gives this commission to 'all nations' (Matthew 28:19), yet the sermon notes there are over 3.6 billion unreached people. What does the enormity of this task reveal about our need, and what does it say about whether we can accomplish this through individual effort or isolated congregations?
    Matthew 28:19
    → When you think about this scale, what's your instinctive response—and what would it look like to move from that response toward joyful dependence on Christ rather than despair or self-sufficiency?
  4. The sermon claims the Great Commission was given 'to the whole church' rather than only to professional missionaries or apostles. How does that reshape your understanding of your own role in making disciples, whether you serve overseas, in your neighborhood, or within your local church?
    Matthew 28:19-20
  5. Jesus promises 'surely I am with you always, to the very end of the age' (Matthew 28:20). The sermon says this perpetual presence, combined with his total authority, means no situation in following Christ's call is outside his care and control. What specific circumstance in your life right now needs that truth?
    Matthew 28:20
    → How would worshiping Christ for his authority and presence reshape the way you approach that circumstance this week?
  6. The sermon presents the gospel as the power that moves us from despair over an impossible task to joyful partnership with other believers. In light of what Christ has accomplished through his death and resurrection, how does the gospel compel you to participate—however your circumstances allow—in building Christ's church alongside this family and churches worldwide?
    1 Corinthians 3:5-7
    → What is one concrete way you could express that participation this week—through prayer, service to your local church, or support of gospel efforts?
Sunday-evening family table

Jesus Is With Us in the Hard Things

For the parent

This prompt anchors in Jesus' promise of his presence in Matthew 28:20—the closing word of the Great Commission. It invites kids to name something hard or scary they face, then to grasp concretely that Jesus is present there too. Listen for how they understand Jesus' nearness, and gently affirm that his presence changes everything we do.

Jesus ended the Great Commission by saying, 'I am with you always, to the very end of the age.' What's something hard or a little scary that you're facing right now—maybe at school, or with a friend, or learning something new? How would it feel different if you really believed Jesus was right there with you in that thing?
Works for ages 7+; younger children may need a parent to help name a 'hard thing,' but they can absolutely grasp Jesus' presence
