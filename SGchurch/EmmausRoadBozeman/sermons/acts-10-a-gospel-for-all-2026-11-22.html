Theological claims· 7
  1. God has a plan for his grace in you to move beyond you to others, and he is already at work preparing others to hear and preparing you to speak. unit #4
  2. This good man still needed to be saved and to hear the gospel message about what Christ has done for him, and God had been at work in his life already. unit #12
  3. The angel's appearance is not prescriptive but signals that God is actively at work, moving, and acting to accomplish something in Cornelius's conversion. unit #13

  5. Peter had to come to terms with the fact that he was saved by faith in Christ and not by law-keeping, and that Gentiles are saved the same way. unit #20
  6. Peter needed a deeper understanding of the gospel to realize he was in no position to call anyone unclean, because his own standing before God was by grace alone, not by works. unit #22
  7. God prepares hearers, God prepares messengers, and when they come together, it is God who shows up and does the saving work by his Spirit. unit #27

5-day reading plan

This week we follow God's sovereign preparation across three dimensions—the heart He readies to hear, the messenger He transforms to speak, and the Spirit He sends to accomplish salvation—tracing how grace moves through us to others.

Monday Genesis 12:1

Abraham's call contains the entire logic of mission: God chose him not for his own sake alone, but so that through him all families of the earth would be blessed. This is the pattern of grace—God saves us into a purpose that extends beyond us. We are stepping into something God has already set in motion, participating in His ancient covenant to reach all nations through the gospel.

Tuesday Acts 2

Pentecost demonstrates this three-fold preparation in its fullest manifestation: the Spirit prepared the disciples to speak with boldness and clarity, the gathered crowd was positioned by God's providence to hear in their own languages, and when messenger and hearer converged, the Spirit fell and three thousand were saved. We see in this pattern what continues in every conversion—God's orchestration works from every angle simultaneously.

Wednesday Luke 7:36-50

The sinful woman came to Jesus, broken and seeking, because God's grace had already been moving in her heart—her repentance was itself a gift of His preparing work. Like Cornelius, she was a seeker whom God had already begun to draw toward the Savior. Her tears and worship show us that preparation often involves conviction, the Spirit's work softening our hearts long before we hear the full proclamation of Christ's finished work.

Thursday Mark 7

Jesus declared all foods clean, overturning the very distinctions that shaped Jewish identity and practice—a radical statement that purity comes from the heart, not from external observance. Peter's vision mirrors this teaching: nothing God has cleansed should be called unclean, whether food or person. The gospel must remake our deepest assumptions about who is in and who is out, reminding us that our own standing rests entirely on grace, not on the works of the law.

Friday Matthew 28

The Great Commission sends us into a world where the risen Jesus is already at work, already drawing people to Himself through the Holy Spirit—our task is not to accomplish salvation but to announce it and witness it. We go not bearing the weight of success on our shoulders, but participating in the Spirit's work of conviction and conversion. This reframes witness: we are not responsible for the outcome; we are simply faithful messengers joining what God is already doing in someone's heart.
