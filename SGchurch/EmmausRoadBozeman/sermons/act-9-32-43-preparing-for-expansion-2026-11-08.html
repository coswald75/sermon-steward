  4. The gospel's expansion to the Gentiles unveils God's heart for all nations, a truth present from Genesis but often obscured by Israel's particular calling. unit #6
  5. The expansion to the Gentiles pressed the church to confront the core question of whether salvation is by works or by grace through faith. unit #7
  6. The Gentile expansion clarified that covenant membership is by faith, not by birth or circumcision. unit #9
  7. Luke strategically returns narrative focus to Peter after Saul's conversion to position Peter as the inaugurator of Gentile expansion. unit #14

  10. Peter's public endorsement as apostle was necessary to prevent the formation of two separate churches (Jewish and Gentile). unit #19
  11. Peter had to inaugurate Gentile expansion despite Paul being the apostle to the Gentiles because the gospel's unity was at stake. unit #20
  12. Peter's apostolic authority among Jews made his endorsement of Gentile inclusion credible in Jerusalem in ways Paul's could not. unit #21
  13. Peter's apostolic endorsement in this passage contributes to establishing the authority of apostolic teaching that forms the New Testament. unit #22
  14. An apostle's authority is derived — he is a messenger serving at the pleasure of the one who sent him, not a source of authority in himself. unit #24

  16. Generosity is a visible evidence of the grace of God and the gospel at work in a person's life. unit #31

  18. The two miracles teach that the ministry of Jesus continues through the apostles by the power of the Spirit. unit #36
  19. Jesus uniquely secured salvation, but he enables others to continue his healing ministry — yet the glory remains with Jesus, not the apostles. unit #37
  20. The apostolic model of proclaiming Christ rather than self continues from the apostles to the present-day church. unit #38
  21. The apostolic office was unique and temporary, but the Spirit's empowering and the commission to make disciples continue with the whole church. unit #39
  22. The ministry inaugurated by Jesus and re-inaugurated through the apostles continues in and through the church today. unit #40
  23. The problem of evil — how to reconcile God's power and goodness with suffering — is a legitimate and widespread theological question. unit #43
  24. God's power and goodness are compatible with present suffering because we live in a fallen world under a redemptive plan not yet consummated. unit #44
  25. When God displays power in gospel expansion, he characteristically does so through compassionate healing of human suffering. unit #45
  26. Jesus' healing ministry established the pattern that when God brings the gospel, he displays his power as compassionate awareness of human suffering. unit #46
  27. The selective healing of Aeneas served to reveal God's power and compassion to the entire city, even though others remained unhealed. unit #48
  28. Death feels wrong because it is wrong — humans were created for eternal life, and death is an unnatural consequence of sin. unit #49
  29. Jesus' grief at Lazarus's tomb reveals that God acknowledges the wrongness of death even when he is about to overcome it. unit #50
  30. The two miracles symbolize the human condition: we are spiritually paralyzed (unable to walk in righteousness) and spiritually dead (under the reign of death). unit #51
  31. Christ came to live the perfect life of obedience we cannot live — he walked the path of righteousness in our place. unit #52
  32. Christ conquered death through his own death and resurrection, defeating our greatest enemy and opening the way to eternal life. unit #53
  33. The miracles of Aeneas and Dorcas were temporary signs pointing to the permanent resurrection life Christ provides. unit #54
  34. God's plan of expansion through Peter is working, resulting in people turning to the Lord in both Lydda and Joppa. unit #59
  35. The ultimate purpose (the 'forest') is God's plan to fill the earth with the knowledge of his glory as the waters cover the sea. unit #60

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we follow the arc of God's expanding gospel: from the foundational truth that the church continues Jesus' ministry, through the apostolic authentication that secured that continuity, to the practical grace-evidence that marks genuine Christian life, and finally to the vision that animates all our labor.

Monday Acts 1:8

Before Peter ever stepped into Lydda and Joppa, Jesus had declared the trajectory of His church: from Jerusalem to Judea to Samaria to the ends of the earth. The miracles we see this week are not random healings but the opening movements of that mandate being fulfilled. We are witnessing the Spirit empowering the gospel to move outward, and Peter's role positions him precisely where Jesus had always planned for the message to break into Gentile territory.

Tuesday Luke 5

When you read the paralyzed man lowered through the roof in Luke 5, and then encounter Aeneas in Acts 9, the echo is unmistakable: 'Arise.' The same word, the same power, the same compassion meeting human brokenness. Luke is showing us deliberately that Jesus' healing work does not end with His ascension — it continues through His apostles by the Spirit. We are not reading two separate stories but one unfolding drama of Christ's authority at work in His church.

Wednesday Genesis 12:1

God's promise to Abraham was always cosmic in scope: 'In you all the families of the earth will be blessed.' Yet Israel's election as a particular people could make that promise feel narrow, reserved for one nation. What unfolds in Acts 9 and beyond is the revelation that God's heart has never been tribal — He has always loved all peoples, and now the barrier between Jew and Gentile is coming down. The gospel that seemed to focus on one nation now reveals itself as the instrument of blessing for all humankind.

Thursday Galatians 2

Peter's role in inaugurating Gentile inclusion forced the early church to answer a question that threatened to splinter it: Is a Gentile saved by becoming Jewish first, or by faith alone? Galatians shows us the apostles wrestling with this fiercely, and Peter's own stumble at Antioch proves how real the pressure was. Yet what emerges is the clarity that salvation has always been by grace through faith — the gospel humbles all of us equally, whether circumcised or not, and grace alone is the foundation of our standing before God.

Friday Habakkuk 2:14

All the miracles, all the apostolic signs, all the gospel expansion — they serve one vast, glorious purpose: the knowledge of God's glory filling the earth. When we read of Aeneas and Dorcas rising, of whole cities turning to the Lord, we are seeing the opening bars of a symphony that will not end until Christ's glory covers all creation. Let that vision compel us this week: we are not merely solving local problems through our ministry, but participating in God's cosmic plan to be known and worshiped by all nations.
