Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. In Colossians 1:21, Paul describes the Colossians as people who were 'alienated and hostile in mind, doing evil deeds.' What does this description tell us about who these believers were before Christ, and why does Paul begin his argument about their standing before God by reminding them of this reality?
    Colossians 1:21
    → How does remembering our former condition as enemies of God shape the way we receive the verdict Paul announces in verse 22?
  2. Paul tells the Colossians that through Christ's death, they will be presented 'holy and blameless and above reproach' before God (verse 22). What is the difference between Christ presenting us this way and us trying to become this way through our own effort?
    Colossians 1:22
  3. The sermon emphasizes that the verdict of 'holy, blameless, and above reproach' is already settled for believers, even though we are still being sanctified. How does understanding that our standing before God is not dependent on our spiritual progress free us from a particular way of thinking about our relationship with Him?
    Romans 8:1
    → What false security or false anxiety might we fall into if we reversed this — if we made our final verdict depend on our sanctification rather than on Christ's work?
  4. According to the sermon, the Colossian church was being tempted to believe they needed 'something more' than the gospel they had received (see Colossians 2:4). What does Paul's insistence that they 'continue in the faith' (verse 23) suggest about how we are to respond to teachings that promise us a deeper spirituality or a more complete Christian life beyond what we already have in Christ?
    Colossians 1:23
  5. The sermon names a fallen condition focus: we tend to believe our standing with God is fragile and depends on our spiritual performance. Where do you see this belief showing up in your own prayers, confessions, or anxieties, and how does the gospel of Christ's finished work directly address that struggle?
  6. Paul calls the Colossians to be 'stable and steadfast, not shifting from the hope of the gospel' (verse 23). What does it look like this week to 'remain steadfast' in the gospel you have already received, rather than seeking something more or something different to secure your standing with God?
    Colossians 1:23
    → What would help you and others in our group stay planted in the confidence of Christ's work rather than drifting into anxiety about your spiritual performance?
Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the arc from our former enmity to our present reconciliation, then to our secure standing in Christ today, and finally to our steadfast perseverance as the means by which we possess what Christ has already won for us.

Monday Romans 5:10

Paul's assertion that we were 'reconciled to [God] through the death of his Son' while we were still 'enemies' shows us the radical reversal Christ accomplishes. We do not begin as distant observers hoping for acceptance; we begin as declared enemies whose enmity is fully absorbed and answered in Christ's death. This is not probation or probationary acceptance — it is the reinstatement of the full relational bond between parent and child.

Tuesday Romans 8:1

When Paul declares 'there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus,' he uses the present tense with deliberate force. The verdict is not pending; it is pronounced and standing in the courtroom of heaven right now. We need not wait until the last day to know our standing — every believer in Jesus possesses this declaration of 'no condemnation' as a settled, present reality that secures our soul today.

Wednesday Philippians 1:6

Paul's confidence that God 'will bring [his work in you] to completion until the day of Christ Jesus' assures us that our progressive transformation is not our responsibility alone to secure but God's committed work. The Holy Spirit's gradual work of making us actually holy in practice corresponds to the verdict of holiness already declared over us positionally. By the last day, our lived reality will perfectly align with our legal standing.

Thursday Hebrews 3:14

The call to 'hold firmly to the confidence we had at first' is not a command to achieve or earn a higher spiritual status, but to remain anchored in the gospel foundation we have already received in Christ. Our steadfastness is not the ground of our salvation but the means by which we secure and possess the salvation already purchased for us. We persevere not to become worthy but because we are already made worthy in Him.

Friday Colossians 2:4

Paul's warning against deceptive arguments designed to lead believers astray shows that the temptation to seek 'something more' than the gospel has always threatened the church. We are called to resist this subtle drift with steadfastness, remaining in the foundation that has already made us complete in Christ. Stability in gospel simplicity is not spiritual laziness — it is the posture of faith that secures all the riches we have been given.
