We need to unlock or unmask some problem within us that Christ hasn't fully addressed so that we can finally escape the suffering that we're enduring. Whatever the case may be, it's often that we are tempted to add something to Christ in order to reach the level that we want to get to.

31 · The pastor cross-references Colossians 1:16 and 1:13 to establish that Satan is a created being under Christ's authority, that all spiritual powers were created by Christ and for Christ, and that Christians have been transferred from darkness to Christ's kingdom, living under His rule rather than operating in enemy territory

Because he is a creature all the same. And as a creature, we can look at Colossians 1:16, for by him all things were created in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities— all things were created through him and for him. What are those powers? What are those enemies, those rulers and authorities? They are creatures. Christ is their maker. And Christians aren't his citizens. We have been transferred from the domain of darkness to the kingdom of the Son. We live in Christ's world and we live under Christ's rule. We're not living in the devil's world trying to run a secret operation. We live in Christ's world under Christ's rule.

32 · The pastor applies the doctrine of Christ's victory over the powers to daily Christian living, calling believers to go to work courageously, tell the truth without fear, raise children with optimism, pray and love without surrendering to darkness, because Christ rules the world even as the defeated enemy remains dangerous

That should put courage in us. When we— when you go to work tomorrow, you should go to work courageously. We could tell the truth without worrying if somebody who opposes our truth could somehow overthrow Jesus' kingdom. We can raise our kids with optimism and hope, teaching them to expect victory because Christ is ruling here and this is Christ's world. The enemy is defeated. Yes, he's dangerous. He can still kidnap. We need to be watchful. We need to discern his lies. But we don't have to tiptoe as if Satan somehow is in charge around here. We can pray. We can love other people without surrendering to the darkness because the King who canceled our debt and shamed the powers is Lord over this world.

33 · The pastor summarizes the sermon's application in three direct pastoral charges corresponding to the three movements of the text: those chasing breakthrough should look to the Savior they received, those burdened by guilt should bring it to the cross where debt was erased, and those living in fear should remember Christ's victory

So church, this morning, if you've been chasing a breakthrough, whether to break through your pain and suffering, to break through in your growth and spiritual maturity, I want to encourage you to look to the Savior you received. If guilt has pestered you and silenced you this week, bring it to the cross. Well, your debt has been erased. And if fear that somehow you live in Satan's world and that your enemies might win, look up and remember who won. We don't need more than Christ, we just need more of Him.

34 · The pastor closes in prayer, asking God to remind the congregation when they are tempted to look elsewhere for what only Christ supplies, to keep turning their eyes to the sufficient Savior, and to live courageously in the world Christ rules

Let's pray. Father, we thank you that in Christ we have complete fullness. God, as we go throughout this week, undoubtedly we will be tempted and we will fail to look at other things for what only Christ can supply. We'll put our hope and trust in other things. As we do, would you remind us of this passage, that in Him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily and that we are filled in Him. Keep turning our eyes to our sufficient Savior, that we would grow deep into Him, and as we do, we would live in the world that He rules and reigns over. With courage and confidence, speaking your truth, loving our neighbors, and suffering faithfully for your name. Through Jesus we pray, amen.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we deepen our grasp of Christ's all-sufficiency by tracing five theological claims across the cross-references: the fullness of grace in Him alone, the victory of the cross over sin's debt, the disarming of spiritual enemies, the Spirit's work in transformation, and our discernment of true teaching.

Monday John 1:16

John writes that from Christ's fullness we have all received grace upon grace—not a limited supply that runs out, but an inexhaustible fountain. The sermon's core claim rests on this reality: the problem with spiritual deception is not that Christ gave us inadequate grace, but that we've forgotten we're drawing from infinite supply. When we feel the pull toward philosophies that promise something beyond Christ, we're actually walking away from more, not toward it.

Tuesday Galatians 3:13

Paul declares the legal debt has been paid—Christ bore the curse our sin deserved. The sermon emphasized that at the cross, our record was nailed there, forgiven completely; we don't live under condemnation's weight. This passage shows us the mechanism of liberation: not a therapeutic technique or spiritual upgrade, but a substitutionary death that settled our account before a holy God. The deception Paul warns against in Colossians would have us believe we still owe something; Galatians assures us the bill is marked paid in full.

Wednesday Colossians 1:13

We have been rescued from the dominion of darkness and transferred into the kingdom of the Son—not relocated to a neutral zone, but placed under the reign of Christ where His authority is absolute and visible. The sermon's claim about spiritual warfare rests on this transfer: Satan deceives us into thinking we're still in his territory, still subject to his rule, but our citizenship has been relocated. We live in occupied enemy territory only insofar as Satan lies; the true reality is that we've been brought into Christ's kingdom where He rules openly and His victory is sure.

Thursday Ephesians 2:4-5

Paul reminds us that God's mercy and love made us alive in Christ while we were dead in our sins—resurrection is God's work, not our striving. The sermon's application about steadiness in the faith (Word, confession, worship) flows from this truth: spiritual growth isn't a solo climb up a mountain of techniques, but a deepening union with the One who raised us. Our part is to remain rooted in Him through ordinary, faithful practices; His part is to complete the work He began, transforming us from the inside out through His Spirit's power.

Friday 2 Corinthians 4:4

The god of this age blinds the minds of unbelievers so they cannot see the light of the gospel of Christ's glory—and Paul teaches us that deception operates by obscuring Christ's supremacy, not by denying His existence. When we hear teaching that treats Jesus as a doorway to deeper wisdom rather than the fullness of God, we're hearing the voice of that blinding power. Our defense is not theological cleverness but a clear-eyed return to the gospel: Does this teaching exalt Christ as Lord, or does it suggest we've outgrown Him? Does it call us to trust His Word, or to look elsewhere for answers? Ask these questions and the deception loses its power.

Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. In Colossians 2:6-7, Paul tells the believers to continue in Christ 'as you received him' and to be 'rooted and built up in him.' What does Paul mean by continuing 'as you received him,' and how does that shape what Christian growth should actually look like?
    Colossians 2:6-7
    → What are some of the ordinary, steady practices—the roots going deep—that Paul seems to have in mind as believers grow in Christ?
  2. Paul warns against being 'taken captive by philosophy and empty deceit' (2:8). What makes these false teachings particularly dangerous, and why would a believer struggling or suffering be vulnerable to them?
    Colossians 2:8
    → Can you think of a 'spiritual upgrade' or teaching beyond Christ that you've encountered—either personally or in the culture around us—that promises breakthrough or healing that Christ supposedly doesn't fully provide?
  3. According to Colossians 2:9-10, 'in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily, and you have been filled in him.' What does it mean that we are already 'filled' in Christ, and what does this claim actually require of us when we face real pain, confusion, or spiritual hunger?
    Colossians 2:9-10
  4. Paul describes our spiritual condition before Christ as being 'dead in trespasses' (2:13), and then speaks of being 'made alive together with him' and 'forgiven us all our trespasses' with the debt 'nailed to the cross' (2:12-14). How does understanding Christ's work as both our resurrection and our complete forgiveness reshape the way we approach our own spiritual struggles?
    Colossians 2:12-14
  5. In Colossians 2:15, Paul says Christ 'disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame, by triumphing over them in him.' How should this reality—that Satan is already defeated and disarmed—change the way we pray, resist temptation, or speak about spiritual warfare in our own lives?
    Colossians 2:15
    → Where do you find yourself still living as if the enemy has more power than Scripture says he does?
  6. The sermon's thesis is 'You don't need more than Christ; what you need is more of Him.' When you bring your deepest longings—for healing, breakthrough, relief, or answers—to Christ this week, what would it look like practically to trust that He is sufficient rather than looking for a spiritual solution beyond Him?
