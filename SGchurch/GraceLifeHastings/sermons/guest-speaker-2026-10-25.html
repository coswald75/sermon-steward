Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace Jesus's prayer for his church from the brokenness sin creates, through the positional and progressive unity he secured, to our calling as conduits of his glory — a visible witness that compels the world to believe.

Monday Genesis 3:12; Genesis 4

From Adam's blame-shift in the garden to Cain's murder of his brother, we see the cascading fracture of human relationship in the wake of sin. The pattern of relational destruction that began at humanity's fall now pervades every sphere—including our own churches—reminding us that surface solutions cannot mend what only reconciliation with God through Christ can heal.

Tuesday John 15:5; John 15:16

Jesus's declaration that apart from him we can do nothing establishes both our positional dependence and the progressive reality of bearing fruit as we abide in him. Our unity flows from our shared abiding in Christ—not from organizational effort or human alignment, but from the organic reality of branches drawing life from the same vine.

Wednesday Ephesians 4:11-16

Paul's vision of gifts flowing through each member to build up the whole body shows us that our personal growth in Christ is never private—it exists to strengthen the corporate body. When we grow in grace, we become more available to encourage, pray for, and serve one another, and corporate health rises as individual health deepens.

Thursday John 1:1; John 5:17

The interpenetrating communion of Father and Son in the Godhead—working together, indwelling one another, perfectly unified in purpose—is the very pattern and measure of the unity Jesus prays we would experience. We are invited into a fellowship that mirrors the triune God's own united life, a reality so staggering that it should reshape how we view our relationships in Christ.

Friday John 1:1; Romans 1:11-12

When Paul speaks of mutual encouragement through one another's faith, he captures what it means to be a conduit of Christ's glory—we do not dispense grace from above but share, from one recipient to another, the grace and truth we ourselves have received. Our visible unity becomes the world's clearest testimony that the Father sent the Son, because such love and sacrifice can come from no other source.

    Genesis 3:12, Genesis 4

  4. According to the sermon, church unity is both positional (already secured in Christ) and progressive (growing over time). How does understanding both dimensions change the way you approach relationships with other believers?
    → What would it look like for you to live from the reality of your positional unity this week while also pursuing progressive unity with someone in the church?
  5. The sermon emphasizes that individual sanctification serves the church's corporate unity, not the reverse. How does your personal abiding in Christ (or lack thereof) actually affect the spiritual health of the body around you?
    John 15:5
    → What specific unspoken encouragements, unprayed prayers, or unserved works might be missing from your church because of where you are spiritually?
  6. Jesus says the world will believe that the Father sent the Son when it sees the church's visible unity (John 17:23). What would it mean for our church to become a more compelling witness to Christ through the way we pursue unity with one another in the triune God?
    John 17:23
