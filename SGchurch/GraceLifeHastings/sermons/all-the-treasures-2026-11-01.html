  1. The completeness of the treasure in Christ does not eliminate the cost of ministry; rather, it defines the work—bringing people to Christ and maturing them in Him, a task that will cost us. unit #6

  3. Nothing is lacking in Christ's atoning sufferings for sin—that payment is complete and unrepeatable—but God has ordained a separate measure of afflictions for the church to endure, and Paul gladly takes his share. unit #9
  4. Christian suffering has no atoning power—not one drop—but it serves the distinct purpose of carrying the news of Christ's finished atonement to the world. unit #11

25 · Expounds the paradox of divine and human agency in Colossians 1:29: Paul toils with Christ's energy working powerfully in him

And for this I toil, struggling with all His energy that He powerfully works in me. We work hard for this, the kind of work that leaves us worn out at the end of the day. We struggle, we agonize, just like an athlete does on the field or a wrestler on the mat. We work for this. With all His energy that He powerfully works within us. His energy. Who's doing the work here? Well, Paul is, but so is Christ. Both work together. You know, we don't just sit around and say, well, we'll let go and let God do the work. We sit back and wait for each other to just be somehow transformed into maturity without any effort at all. Paul didn't do that. He worked. He toiled. But we also don't grit our teeth and suppose that everyone's maturity in this room depends on us. Paul didn't do that. He leaned on power that wasn't his.

26 · Agricultural analogy illustrating the cooperation of divine and human agency: farmers cannot make corn grow, but neither do they passively wait for God to plant—they work before sunrise because God works through their planting

And you farmers should be able to identify with this really well. I mean, think about planting season. My guess is none of you farmers think that you can make corn grow. You can't make it rain. You can't make a seed sprout. But also, no farmer— I suppose none of you sat back this past planting season and waited on God to plant it either. And you can't make it grow, but you don't sit back and wait for God to plant it. No, you get out there before the sun comes up. Why? Because God works through planting.

27 · Synthesizes the agricultural illustration into theological principle: work and dependence on God are not opposing categories but inseparable partners in the Christian life

So this work and dependence on God, they're not opposites. In fact, we can't have one without the other.

28 · Expounds Colossians 2:1-2, highlighting Paul's intercessory struggle for churches he's never visited, his prayer for their encouragement, and the organic metaphor of being 'knit together'—joints holding the body together

And Paul concludes toward the end by saying, I want you to know how great a struggle I have for you. Paul's struggling for people he's never met. While sitting in chains, he's praying for people he's never visited. And what's he praying? Verse 2, that their hearts may be encouraged, being knit together in love, to reach all the riches of full assurance of understanding and the knowledge of God's mystery, which is Christ. He wants them to be comforted, to be strengthened. He wants them steady. Error usually leaves people unsettled. No, the truth that Paul is proclaiming leaves people encouraged and knit together like a body being held together joint to joint, knit together in love to reach all the riches of full assurance. I mean, full assurance— notice that word full again. They're knit together to reach all the riches of full assurance.

29 · Makes a potentially controversial theological claim: full assurance is not achieved in isolation—God designed growth and understanding to happen corporately

Let me tell you something that may sound controversial or be difficult to hear. Full assurance doesn't come to people who try to go at it alone. It doesn't happen that way. God did not design it to happen that way. Full assurance does not happen to people who go at it alone. You grow, we grow into the riches of Christ together. And we understand Him better. In the fellowship of His people, better than we would ever do by ourselves,

30 · Returns to Colossians 2:3 with an extended metaphor: treasures hidden in Christ are not hidden from believers but hidden like gold in a mountain—inexhaustible, requiring lifelong excavation, but with a known location

because Christ is the one in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. The treasures are hidden in Christ. They're not hidden from you. They're hidden the way that gold is hidden in a mountain. You'll never run out. You can dig your whole life and never hit the bottom. But you don't need a secret map because you know exactly where they are. They're in him.

31 · Direct, concrete application of the corporate maturation principle: show up when the church meets, stay despite disappointment and awkwardness, forgive, pray for one another and for the wider body

So church, remember, you can't mature alone. So, so show up when the church meets and stay. Because knitting takes time. People are going to disappoint you. Things will get awkward. It would be easy to run away or to drift. But stay and let God do the work of knitting us together and maturing us, which means we're going to have to forgive. And we're going to have to pray. Pray for each other. Pray for people we've never met. Pray for other churches in town. Pray for Christians we'll only meet on the other side of heaven.

32 · Expounds Colossians 2:4 on 'plausible arguments' (error that sounds reasonable, deeper, more spiritual), then introduces the counterfeit-currency analogy: bank examiners study the real thing, not the fake, so they can recognize counterfeits immediately

And then finally, Paul tells us why he said all this. I say this in order that no one may delude you with plausible arguments. Plausible arguments, you know, an argument that sounds probable, but error almost never sounds crazy. It sounds reasonable. Sometimes plausible arguments can sound better than the truth because it sounds deeper or more spiritual or more exciting. I mean, if it sounded foolish, nobody would fall for it. So how do you stand against something that sounds so good, like plausible arguments? Well, you don't do it by being smarter than everybody who comes along. You stand by knowing the real thing. The more time you spend understanding this real treasure, the easier you're going to notice when somebody hands you a fake. You know, it's been said that those people who are tasked with identifying fake currency don't spend hardly any time observing counterfeit currency. They spend time examining the real thing. So that when the counterfeit arrives, they recognize it immediately. Dive deeper into Christ.

33 · Synthesizes the sermon's argument in closing: the treasure in Christ is complete, but the work of bringing people to it and growing them in it is unfinished

Church, by God's grace, here we are, still gathered around His Word and this table, and my encouragement is that we hold our place in the line, The people around us need us. And so we want to grow together in maturity, being knit together in love. All the treasures are in Him. Christ is among us and Christ is in us. Everything we need is in Him. Nothing is missing, nothing needs to be added. But there are people who haven't seen it yet, and some of us who haven't grown up into it yet, and some of them are sitting right next to us, or they're in our homes, or in our neighborhoods. The treasure is complete. The work of bringing people to it isn't.

34 · Closing pastoral prayer asking God to keep the church from looking beyond Christ, to make them willing to suffer for His body, to give strength for the work of maturation, to knit them together in love, and to feed them with Christ at the Lord's Table

Let's pray. Father, thank you that all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are in Christ and that you've given him to us. Please keep us from looking anywhere else and make us glad to suffer for his body. Give us strength for the work, and would you by grace knit us together as a church in love. And as we come to this table now, feed us with Christ. In his name we pray. Amen.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week, we trace Paul's vision of Christ's sufficiency through five cross-references, moving from the radical completeness of Christ's person, through the costly response He demands of His people, to the corporate maturity that only grows when we remain bound together in love.

Monday Colossians 2:9-10

Paul declares that in Christ 'all the fullness of the Deity lives in bodily form' and that we are 'complete in him.' This is the theological bedrock: Christ is not a gateway to higher knowledge but the inexhaustible repository of every treasure we need. There is nowhere else to look, no secret gnosis, no spiritual elite tier—only the complete sufficiency we already possess in Him.

Tuesday Acts 9:4

When Christ asks Saul, 'Why do you persecute me?', He reveals the mystical solidarity between His suffering body and Himself. Our trials for the gospel are not private ordeals but are reckoned as participation in His redemptive mission. This transforms suffering from meaningless pain into purposeful witness that carries the news of His forgiveness to a broken world.

Wednesday Colossians 3:16

Paul's command to 'let the message of Christ dwell among you richly as you teach and admonish one another' distributes the work of spiritual formation across the entire congregation. We grow deeper into Christ's treasures not through isolated study but through the collective voice of brothers and sisters who speak truth, correct gently, and pray for one another's advancement in Christ.

Thursday 1 John 3:1

John's declaration of our status—'See what great love the Father has lavished on us, that we should be called children of God'—stands independent of our feelings or circumstances. We are genuinely loved, genuinely indwelt, genuinely destined for glory with Christ, whether today we feel assured or discouraged. This objective reality is our anchor when emotions fluctuate.

Friday Psalm 68:6

The psalmist tells us that God 'sets the lonely in families'—He does not ordain isolation as the path to spiritual maturity. We are made for gathering, for being known, for growing in the presence of others who sharpen and strengthen us. When we resist the pull to journey alone and instead show up faithfully to the body, we access a depth of understanding of Christ that solitude can never provide.
