Theological claims· 13
  1. Allowing earthly things to consume our existence is not mere busyness or practicality—it is sin against the Lord that must be confessed. unit #4
  2. No charge can stand against believers because Christ is seated at God's right hand interceding for them, having completed the work of salvation. unit #7
  3. Being raised with Christ is a settled, accomplished reality that has already happened to every believer through faith in God's powerful work. unit #12
  4. Christ's sitting at God's right hand signifies not rest but reign—He rules over all affairs while His enemies are progressively being made His footstool with an outcome that is certain. unit #15
  5. Seeking and setting minds on things above should be the ordinary, ongoing default of Christian life, not an exceptional or one-time spiritual achievement. unit #19
  6. Setting one's mind on Christ actually enables proper love of people and proper work because it prevents making earthly things ultimate, freeing believers to engage them without resentment, bitterness, or idolatry. unit #25
  7. The Christian hope is not escape from the body but a raised body and renewed earth, which is why we set our minds on Christ's reign and rule. unit #30
  8. The commands to seek and set minds on things above are grounded in the settled reality that believers have died and their life is hidden with Christ in God. unit #39
  9. The believer's life is absolutely secure because it is kept with Christ in God, requiring any threat to penetrate both Christ and God to reach it. unit #43
  10. Life hidden with Christ in God is the ultimate spiritual reality; no deeper or higher place exists, so promises of special spiritual experiences beyond this are false. unit #47
  11. Believers will live eternally because their life is inseparably bound to Christ's, and He will never die again. unit #54
  12. Having Christ as one's life does not diminish personhood but rather provides ultimate security. unit #56
  13. Christ and believers are so united that He will not appear in glory without them—all His people will be revealed with Him despite their present weaknesses. unit #59

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through the settled realities of our union with Christ—from His cosmic reign to our hidden security—and discover how fixing our minds on these truths frees us to love and serve without fear.

Monday Romans 8:33-34

Paul's question "Who will bring any charge against those whom God has chosen?" echoes the sermon's declaration that our accuser has no standing in heaven. Christ's intercession at the Father's right hand is not a distant abstraction—it is active, present defense of our standing before God. As we begin this week, we rest in the fact that every sin confessed, every failure acknowledged, is already answered by the One seated in power on our behalf.

Tuesday Psalm 110:1

The ancient psalmist's vision—"The Lord says to my Lord, 'Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet'"—reveals that Christ's exaltation is not a reward He receives and relaxes into, but an active, ongoing sovereignty over all creation. His enemies are being progressively subdued, though we cannot yet see the final outcome. This reign is what gives us permission to set our minds on things above: Christ is not distant or passive, but actively ruling on behalf of His people.

Wednesday Ephesians 2:6

Paul declares that God has not only raised us up with Christ but seated us with Him in the heavenly realms—this is already done, not something we must earn or achieve. Our participation in Christ's resurrection and exaltation is as secure as His own. This is why the commands to seek and set our minds on things above are not calls to strain toward some distant spiritual achievement, but invitations to align our thoughts with a reality that is already ours in Christ.

Thursday 1 John 3:2

John reminds us that when Christ appears, we will see Him as He is, and we ourselves will be like Him—bodily, recognizable, raised and glorified. Our hidden life with Christ in God will one day be fully revealed, not as disembodied spirits floating in heaven, but as resurrected people on a renewed creation. This hope anchors our work today: we labor knowing that our bodies matter, our efforts on earth matter, because Christ's return means restoration, not obliteration.

Friday Colossians 2:20-23

Paul exposes the futility of human-made rules and earthly regulations that promise self-improvement or spiritual power—they have no value in checking the flesh. When we set our minds on Christ and His throne, we are liberated from the tyranny of making earthly pursuits and possessions our gods. Work becomes a joy, not a striving; relationships become gifts, not demands; possessions become tools, not treasures. We are free to engage the world fully because our life is hidden where the world cannot reach.
