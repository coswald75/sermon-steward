Theological claims· 4
  1. Paul's confidence in the gospel was not based on cultural approval but on the God who gave it, and Christians' confidence must rest on the same foundation. unit #8
  2. The gospel is not human speculation or one more religion—it is God's own good news for a lost world, originating from God himself. unit #30
  3. The gospel is not a suggestion or philosophy but news demanding life adjustment—just as 9/11, COVID-19, or election results force us to adjust our lives to new realities. unit #32
  4. Christians need not be embarrassed or apologize for the gospel's truth claims because we did not invent the gospel—God gave it as the perfect counsel of his will before time began. unit #33

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we deepen our grasp of the gospel's unshakeable origin—not in human invention but in God's eternal counsel—and discover how this foundation transforms our confidence, our witness, and our identity as Christ's own.

Monday Romans 16:26

Paul closes Romans by describing the gospel as 'the mystery hidden for long ages' but now 'disclosed and through the prophetic writings made known to all nations' (Romans 16:26). This echoes his opening claim: God alone authored this good news, unveiling it according to his perfect counsel before time began. We can proclaim with confidence because we are not peddling human philosophy but announcing God's own redemptive plan, now visible in Christ.

Tuesday Genesis 3:15

Genesis 3:15 contains the first promise of the gospel: the offspring of the woman will crush the serpent's head. This seed-promise, whispered in humanity's darkest hour, threads through every subsequent covenant and prophet, culminating in Jesus Christ our Lord. We see that God did not invent the gospel in the New Testament but wove it through Scripture's entire narrative, demonstrating his unwavering commitment to redemption across the ages.

Wednesday 2 Samuel 7

God's covenant with David promised an everlasting kingdom and an heir whose throne would endure forever (2 Samuel 7). In Romans 1:3, Paul announces that Jesus is precisely this promised Son of David. The resurrection—Christ's declaration in power as Son of God—vindicates God's ancient word and proves that his promises do not fail. Our confidence rests on a God who has kept covenant for millennia.

Thursday Revelation 7

John's vision shows a multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language standing before the throne, worshiping the Lamb (Revelation 7). This is the fulfillment of Romans 1:5—the obedience of faith among all nations for the sake of Christ's name. We are not isolated believers but part of a vast, multiethnic people whom God has called to himself, a reality that should stir our hearts to worship and compel our witness to unreached peoples.

Friday Isaiah 52:7

How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news, Isaiah proclaims (Isaiah 52:7). Paul echoes this image: we are messengers of gospel news, not inventors defending a hypothesis. The gospel is not a suggestion we nervously propose—it is news that transforms reality and calls for response. As we belong to Christ and are loved by God, we are set apart as his holy people precisely to announce this news with bold gratitude, not embarrassment.
