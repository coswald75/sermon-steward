Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how the gospel's power flows from God's character through Christ's work into our faith, calling us to rest entirely in Jesus rather than ourselves.

Monday 1 Corinthians 1

Paul writes that God's wisdom appears as foolishness to those who trust in human strength and eloquence (1 Cor. 1:25). We live in a culture that measures power by might, influence, and achievement—yet the cross, where Christ died in apparent weakness, is where God's greatest power is unleashed. When we embrace this paradox, we join Paul in refusing shame about a message the world despises.

Tuesday 2 Corinthians 5:21

Here Paul unveils the mechanics of our salvation: Christ became sin for us so that we might become God's righteousness in him. This is not moral transformation we achieve through effort—it is a righteous status God grants us through faith in what Christ accomplished. Our standing before God rests entirely on the finished work of the sinless Son, never on our spiritual performance.

Wednesday Habakkuk 2:4

The prophet declares that the righteous shall live by faith, and Paul anchors the gospel in this principle (Rom. 1:17). We begin our new life when we trust Christ; we continue it by the same faith. When sin strikes us, our instinct is to earn our way back through renewed effort—but faith calls us to run to Jesus' righteousness again, not to our own striving.

Thursday 1 Corinthians 1

In 1 Corinthians 1, Paul reminds the church that God called not many of the wise, powerful, or noble, but rather the weak and foolish of the world. The gospel erases every human hierarchy—rich and poor, slave and free, learned and simple all stand on level ground before the cross. This humbles us and fills us with gratitude that God's power to save knows no boundaries and shows no partiality.

Friday Habakkuk 2:4

Faith, not works, is how the righteous live—this is Paul's clarion call in Romans 1:17, and it cuts through the anxiety many of us carry about our standing with God. When you stumble, when your obedience falters, when you feel spiritually weak, remember: your righteousness before God is not your own achievement to defend or earn back. It is Christ's perfect work, received by faith, immovable and unchanging. Rest there.

Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. Paul opens Romans by declaring he is not ashamed of the gospel. What do you think would make someone ashamed of the gospel, and why might that temptation be especially strong in our current cultural moment?
    Romans 1:16
    → When have you felt pressure—from culture, friends, or even your own reasoning—to downplay or reframe your faith rather than speak it plainly?
  2. According to Romans 1:16, the gospel is described as 'the power of God for salvation.' What does it mean to call the gospel 'power,' and how is that different from calling it 'good advice' or 'an inspiring story'?
    Romans 1:16
  3. The sermon emphasizes that justification is not God overlooking our sin but God remaining righteous while declaring us righteous. Why is this distinction crucial, and what does it tell us about God's character?
    2 Corinthians 5:21
    → How does this differ from what the world or even some religious systems offer as a path to acceptance with God?
  4. Paul writes that in the gospel, righteousness is revealed 'from faith to faith' (Romans 1:17, echoing Habakkuk 2:4). What does it mean that the Christian life—from beginning to end—depends on faith in Christ rather than shifting to reliance on our own spiritual performance?
    Habakkuk 2:4
  5. The sermon names a subtle danger: measuring your standing before God by your spiritual performance, letting your assurance rise and fall with how well you're doing. When do you find yourself doing this, and what does the gospel say to that struggle?
    → What would change in your daily life if you truly believed your righteousness rests entirely on Christ, not on your effort?
  6. Since the gospel is God's power to save, and no one is excluded from its reach regardless of status or past, how should this reshape the way you pray for, speak to, and relate to non-believers in your life this week?
    Romans 1:16
    → Who is someone you've assumed is 'too far gone' for the gospel to reach, and what would it look like to trust God's power in their life?
