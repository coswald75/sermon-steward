Once again, Proverbs 27:19, as in water, face reflects reflects face, so the heart of man reflects a man. Your heart always reveals what you love most, and what you love most is always revealed in how you live. I really like this quote by John, the Puritan John Flavel. He said, the greatest difficulty in conversion is to win the heart to God, and the greatest difficulty after conversion is to keep the heart with God. So I hope, I hope that you are concerned about the condition of your heart and committed to guarding it and keeping it with God.

28 · Direct application to those troubled by the sermon's warnings: the solution is not self-reformation but gospel-centered heart alignment with God

And if you tremble at these words, then forget about trying to tidy up your life and get it in order yourself. Get your heart properly aligned with God through the gospel, right? It is our only hope.

29 · Explains the phrase 'the Jew first and also the Greek' in verses 9-10, showing that Jews will be judged first not because of special exemption but because receiving truth first results in first judgment, establishing the principle that greater knowledge brings greater responsibility

I want to highlight something, um, specific that, that is in verse 9 and 10. Verses 9 and 10 are essentially a repeat of verses 7 to 8. Okay, in verses 7 to 8, Paul presents a principle: good works lead to eternal life, evil works lead to wrath. And then verses 9 to 10 reverses the order and restates the same truth. But in both verses 9 and 10, Paul adds something. In both verses he says, the Jew first and also the Greek. So let's consider what this means. We've already said that God will judge everyone by their work, and when he does he will show no partiality. However, as I said before, the Jews, the Jews truly thought, they truly thought that they had special status with God because they were God's chosen people and they had the law and the prophets. But God says no, because I am a fair judge, an impartial judge, I will judge you, O Israel, first since you received the law first. Okay, God will judge Israel the same way he judges everyone else, by their works, but the sequence and order of judgment will begin with them. Being first to receive truth from God results in being first in judgment. Being first to receive truth from God results in being first in judgment. And here's the universal principle that applies to Paul's original audience and equally to us today: greater knowledge brings greater responsibility. Greater knowledge brings greater responsibility.

30 · Reinforces the principle that greater knowledge brings greater responsibility through James 3:1's warning that teachers face stricter judgment

We see this principle in James 3:1. Not many of you should become teachers, my brothers, for you know that we who teach will be judged with greater strictness.

31 · First application: accountability is proportional to knowledge received

And this verse will bring us to the first of a few applications before I close. The first application is this: you are responsible and will be held accountable for the knowledge and truth you possess from God's word. If you possess a deeper knowledge of scripture and Christian truth, then you will face a more exacting accountability. God will evaluate you based upon the knowledge and light you received. What have you done with the knowledge and light revealed to you? Have you lived it out? Or have you hidden it under a basket like it says in Matthew 5:15? In Matthew 5:16, Jesus tells us how we are to steward the light revealed to us. Matthew 5:16, Jesus says, in the same way, let your light shine before others so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father who is in heaven. So my hope and prayer is that out of your great love for Jesus and what he has done for you, that your greatest desire will be to live out and practice this verse.

32 · Second application: reject nominal Christianity

The second application: do not trust in a Christian identity that is not grounded in the full transformation of your life. Life transformation is the biblical expectation for the Christian. But let me make something very clear: we do not seek to transform so that we can be saved. Okay? We are transformed as a result of being saved. But after we are saved, after we belong to him, after our hearts have been changed, we continue to transform and conform our lives to God's word and imitate Christ's example. So, do not trust in a token or nominal Christianity that does not care about glorifying God and the Christlike transformation of our lives. So, my hope and prayer is that in the depths of your heart you desire to change and become more like Christ and to live out your identity in him.

33 · Third application: authentic salvation is visible through transformed living

A third application: know and appreciate this and frequently call it to mind. Your salvation is authenticated and shown to be genuine genuine by how you live. 2 Corinthians 5:17 says, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old is past, and behold, the new has come. Your actions, your behavior, your responses to life, your habits and priorities, they always reveal whether salvation, whether the gospel has truly transformed your heart. And the implication is, is that if you truly are a new creation, people should be able to see a transformed life. And the goal is that they see less of you, less of us, right, and more of Christ in us. Okay, we have a message for the world that is not just to be verbally proclaimed but is to also be demonstrated in how we live. However, if you call yourself a Christian and people cannot see Christlikeness in you at all, and instead they actually see unrighteousness and sinful behavior, they are likely to conclude that you are not a Christian. And if this describes you, I hope that you are concerned. Seek counsel from someone here at church. Let us help you with whatever's going on in your life.

34 · Evangelistic application directly addressing non-Christians, urging immediate repentance and faith to receive salvation and avoid eternal judgment

And if you are not a Christian, my prayer is that today you will humble yourself, repent of your sin, and receive the free gift of eternal life so that you can have a changed heart. This is the only way to avoid God's eternal wrath and judgment, and please do it today. Because tomorrow may be too late.

35 · Closes with a vision for the Christian community to live with Christ as supreme treasure, using the parable of the hidden treasure to illustrate joyful, wholehearted devotion that proves what the heart truly values

Now, for those who are Christ followers, my hope and prayer is that as a Christian community, that you are so impacted by the gospel and the love of Christ, that devotion to Christ and his kingdom day to day becomes your highest priority. That you seek to not just know Christ but to live like Christ. That you seek not just to know God's Word but to live it out. To not just know the gospel but to allow the gospel to influence and shape all you do. My hope and prayer is that the gospel takes root in you and and impacts you like the man Jesus spoke of in Matthew 13:44. Nothing— Matthew 13:44, Jesus gave this short parable. He said, the kingdom of heaven is like a treasure hidden in a field which a man found and covered up. Then in his joy, he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field. This man did not begrudgingly sell his belongings. No, no, no. This verse says that in his joy he went and sold all he had. Why? Why would he do such a thing? Why would he sell all of his possessions? I mean, what reckless behavior. I mean, has he lost his mind? Why would he do such a thing? Why? Well, I believe that Jesus gives the answer in Matthew 6:21. For where your heart is, for where your treasure is, there your heart will be also. This man's actions revealed his heart. This man's actions revealed the desire which ruled his heart. This man's actions authenticated his great love for this treasure. And what is this treasure for us, CLF? What is this treasure? It is Christ himself. May we go and do likewise.

6 questions for your group this week

  1. In Romans 2:6-11, Paul makes a striking claim: God will judge all people according to their works. What does this passage tell us about whose works will be judged and on what basis?
    Romans 2:6-11
    → Why do you think Paul emphasizes that this judgment applies 'to the Jew first and also to the Greek'—what assumption is he challenging?
  2. According to verses 7-8, there are two different kinds of people described by their patterns of action. What is the difference between those who 'by persistence in doing good' seek glory and honor, and those who 'do not obey the truth but give in to wickedness'?
    Romans 2:7-8

    → If someone says they believe the gospel but their habitual patterns show no real change, what might that suggest according to this passage?

    Proverbs 27:19
  5. The sermon surfaces a fallen condition focus: many of us are tempted to trust in nominal Christianity—a verbal profession without genuine transformation. If you've wrestled with this temptation yourself or seen it in others, what does the gospel offer as the only real solution?
    2 Corinthians 5:17
    → What would it look like practically this week to align your heart with God through the gospel rather than trying to reform yourself through effort?

    Matthew 6:21
    → Where are you most tempted right now to let worldly standards rather than God's Word rule your heart—and what would it look like to let Christ's love rule instead?
Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through the theological pillars of Romans 2:6-11: God judges by works because works reveal the heart's true ruler, and that heart's condition determines both our present life and our eternal destiny.

Monday Hebrews 9:27

Hebrews reminds us that one appointment awaits every person: judgment by the living God. This is not a doctrine meant to terrify us into compliance, but to awaken us to the gravity and reality of our accountability. As we face this truth together, the gospel's power becomes clear—only Christ's finished work shields us from condemnation.

Tuesday Proverbs 27:19

The ancient wisdom of Proverbs teaches what Romans 2 makes explicit: our lives are mirrors of our inner world. When we see persistent patterns of disobedience or self-centeredness in our behavior, we are seeing the heart's true condition exposed. The reverse is equally true—habitual righteousness reveals a heart transformed by grace.

Wednesday 2 Corinthians 5:17

In Christ, we become new creatures—not gradually reformed, but radically recreated by the Spirit's power. This transformation is not invisible; it surfaces inevitably in how we live, what we treasure, and what we pursue. The gospel doesn't leave our hearts unchanged; it installs a new ruler, and our works become the evidence of that divine renovation.

Thursday 2 Corinthians 5:14

Paul declares that Christ's love compels us—it is the governing power that reorients every motive and desire toward God's glory. When we grasp this love, self-centeredness loses its grip. We find ourselves gladly living for His renown, not because obligation demands it, but because the heart ruled by Christ naturally overflows in worship and service.

Friday 1 Corinthians 10:31

Even the ordinary—eating, drinking, the mundane tasks of daily life—belong to the arena of God's glory. This verse cuts through any false division between 'spiritual' and 'everyday'; everything we do either reflects Christ's lordship or the world's lie. As we move into the week ahead, we invite the Spirit to search our hearts: what does each decision, each conversation, each use of our time reveal about who truly rules us?
