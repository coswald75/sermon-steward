Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the arc of Romans' power: from its status as Scripture's fullest gospel statement, through its transformative effect on the soul, to its unifying mission that calls us beyond ourselves.

Monday Romans 1:17

Paul announces the theme of his letter here: "the gospel... is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes." This single verse contains the theological heartbeat that undergirds all sixteen chapters—the declaration that salvation comes through faith in Christ alone. As we begin this series, we are standing at the entrance to Scripture's greatest treasury, and Romans 1:17 is the key that unlocks it.

Tuesday Romans 3

In Romans 3, Paul establishes the common ground that divides the Rome church must stand upon: all are guilty before God, Jews and Gentiles alike. By taking his readers to the truth that every mouth is "stopped" and all are "accountable to God," Paul shows that unity cannot come through compromise on differences but only through shared conviction of sin and shared standing at the foot of the cross. This is the pastoral genius of the letter—it heals division not by avoiding hard doctrine but by going deeper into it.

Wednesday Romans 15

Romans 15 captures Paul's vision of the church mobilized together for gospel mission—the Gentiles ministering to the Jewish saints, the strong bearing with the weak, all working toward the spread of the gospel to the ends of the earth. When believers are united by a mission that exceeds their individual preferences and cultural attachments, their petty conflicts shrink before the magnitude of the task. We see here that unity is not achieved in isolation but in the corporate pursuit of something greater than ourselves.

Thursday Romans 6-8

These chapters move beyond abstract theology into the lived reality of the Christian life—freedom from sin's power, the indwelling Spirit's work, and the groaning hope of future glory. Paul does not simply explain the gospel; he shows how it reorients the entire trajectory of a believer's life, from bondage to liberation to Christlikeness. This is why Romans has sparked spiritual awakening and personal transformation throughout the church's history—it is a letter meant to change not just how we think but how we live.

Friday Romans 13:13-14

Paul concludes his doctrinal masterpiece not with abstract exhortation but with concrete calls to put off the works of darkness and put on Christ in our ordinary lives. The gospel's power reaches into the real, daily soil of where God has planted us—our relationships, our choices, our witness in the mundane. As we begin this series, we are reminded that Romans is not merely an intellectual treasure but a living word that transforms how we conduct ourselves in the very contexts where Christ has called us to shine.
