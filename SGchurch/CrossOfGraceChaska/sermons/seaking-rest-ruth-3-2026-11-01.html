Theological claims· 15
  1. God rewards those who by faith diligently seek him—believers should expect that he will act when they step out in faith, though they cannot presume how he will act. unit #3
  2. Biblical rest is security and satisfaction in Christ and in the purposes he has for me despite the nature of my circumstances. unit #6
  3. God sovereignly brings his people into seasons of restlessness and uncertainty not to be harsh but to reveal himself as their sole stability and navigate them through to a promised land. unit #7
  4. God puts his people in seasons of uncertainty so that they might turn to him and find rest for their souls in relationship with him rather than in resolved circumstances. unit #9
  5. One way to find rest in Christ during hard times is by taking courageous steps of faith. unit #11
  6. Ruth's words to Boaz constitute a bold, direct marriage proposal initiated by the woman—a courageous reversal of cultural norms. unit #21
  7. Ruth demonstrates progressive faith by trusting God with the gleaning (chapter 2) and now with the redeeming (chapter 3), stepping out by faith to see what God will do through his own laws. unit #23
  8. Ruth's approach to Boaz is not presumption but faithful response to the opportunity God has provided—courageously risking rejection while trusting God with the possibility of redemption. unit #24
  9. Godliness preserved Boaz and Ruth through a potentially destructive moment and positioned them for God's providential plan to unfold in unexpected ways. unit #34
  10. Godly character is not cold rule-keeping but the outworking of awareness and respect for the active, living presence of God. unit #35
  11. Believers can maintain godly character because Christ lives in them through union with his death and resurrection, producing godliness by grace and breaking sin's reign. unit #37
  12. Christ lives to break generational cycles of sin, making believers sons and daughters who redirect the generational flow through his power. unit #38
  13. God loves to bring his grace to bear on those who trust him, and maintaining godly character step by step can produce generations of kingdom impact. unit #40
  14. Christ, the God-man, does not cease working until the matters of our lives are settled and we are brought safely home to final rest. unit #45
  15. While waiting for God to complete his work, the personal question for every believer is: are you at rest with Christ? unit #49

She— it could be stated, what is your name, Ruth? In other words, are you Mrs. Boaz yet? Is the idea. So she's poking fun at this moment, like, what happened? What transpired? So verse 17, I mean, she's unloading probably up to 80 pounds of grain and saying, by the way, Naomi, Boaz doesn't want you to be empty. Remember, she had come back to Bethlehem stating, I am empty. I have nothing. And Boaz is saying, you will not be empty. Boaz will not let them remain empty-handed in all of this. He's committed to ensuring that they find rest, a place of security, a place of belonging.

44 · Expounds Ruth 3:18, emphasizing Naomi's instruction to wait and the reason for contentment: Boaz will not rest until the matter is settled

And with all of this Naomi says, She says, wait, my daughter, until you learn how the matter turns out, for the man will not rest but will settle the matter today. What a statement! It's like saying, Ruth, you have courageously risked it all. You have maintained your character and integrity through it all. Now be content to wait. How? How am I going to be content to wait? Well, knowing the man will not rest till the matter is settled.

45 · Applies Boaz's restless work to settle the matter typologically to Christ's work on behalf of believers

Church, let it be known there is a man, there is a God-man who has not ceased working for us until the matters of our lives are settled, till he has brought us safely home to a final place of rest. Let's just admit it, we folks, we are not, we are not God. The circumstances that he puts us in are well beyond us. They are circumstances, as we referenced last week, that only the cosmic stagehand can work out in his time and in his way for his glory and for our good. And so when we've done all that we can do When we've maintained our character through it all, what must we do but learn to contently wait, knowing that he, the God-man, will settle the matter?

46 · Provides practical instruction on contentment in waiting by quoting Philippians 4 at length

Philippians chapter 4 helps us think through it practically amidst hard, confusing times. Paul writes to the church in Philippi. He says, rejoice in the Lord always. Again I say, rejoice! Let your reasonableness, let your gentleness be known to everyone. Why? For the Lord is at hand. For the Lord lives. For the Lord is near. Therefore, don't be anxious about anything, but in everything, by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving, let your requests be known to God. And how will God meet you? And the peace of God, the rest of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.

47 · Distills Philippians 4 into concrete imperatives: worship, pray, trust God to bring rest and peace

To say it simply, worship your way to a place of rest in Christ. Pray your way to a place of contentment in Christ. And when you lay it all before Him, know this: He'll bring rest. He'll bring peace. He'll act to bring contentment to you and he'll guard your hearts and minds in Christ. Learn contentment in the waiting.

48 · Identifies the literary structure of Ruth 3's ending as a cliffhanger requiring waiting

Now, with all of this, at the end of chapter 3, there's a clear literary break. It's like we should see the curtains closing. Right? And we have to wait. What will chapter 4 bring? How will this all work out? Will God accomplish great things or will there continue to be great questions? How will this happen? And it's such a good place to stay, to once again, as it were, be suspended In the literary movement of the text. We're just suspended. I don't know what's next. But it's good to wait on it. To just— okay, this is what it is. Will God move? Will God act?

49 · Applies the literary suspension eschatologically and personally

Well, when we think of our own story, we do begin to recognize that our God has acted, he is act— he is acting, and one day he will finally complete the job, right? He will bring us safely home. But for now, the curtain closes and we are left to wait. Perhaps waiting with the question then more personally for us is, are you at rest? With the man Christ Jesus.

50 · Closing prayer applying the three main points to the congregation's lives, asking God to clarify courageous steps, enable godly character, and produce contentment in waiting

Let's pray. Lord, we thank you. You never leave us. You never forsake us. Even when in your sovereign plan you put us in situations where we don't even know what's up from down, what's left from right, But Lord, thank you in the midst of that, it's always your aim that you would root us deeply in yourself. You are always availing yourself again and again. And so, Lord Jesus, for my brothers and sisters, Holy Spirit, would you make it clear the courageous steps that need to be taken, whether in the doing or the undoing? Lord, would it be that your power and your presence would enable them to maintain godly character through it all? And Lord, as they wait upon you, would they find themselves— yeah, their hearts being guarded by you. Bring contentment to bear, Lord Jesus, we pray. Your mighty name we pray. Amen.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how God's people find rest in uncertainty through faithful courage, godly character, and trust in Christ our Redeemer who never ceases working on our behalf.

Monday Matthew 11

Jesus promises rest to those who come to him weary and burdened, declaring that his yoke is easy and his burden light (Matthew 11:28-30). This echoes the pattern established in Ruth: God invites us into active pursuit of him through faith, and in that pursuit—not in the absence of effort—we discover the deep, soul-level rest that circumstances alone cannot provide. Like Ruth gleaning and then approaching Boaz, we find rest not by passivity but by taking courageous steps of faith in response to God's revealed character and promises.

Tuesday Philippians 4

Paul writes from prison that the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard our hearts and minds in Christ Jesus (Philippians 4:7)—a promise made not to the comfortable but to those in genuine hardship and uncertainty. This rest is not the fruit of resolved circumstances but of a heart turned toward Christ in prayer and thanksgiving, discovering that his presence and purposes remain steady even when everything else feels unsettled. Ruth and Boaz model this: they enter the threshing floor in uncertainty yet maintain godly composure because their security rests not in what they can control but in God's faithfulness.

Wednesday Romans 6

Paul teaches that we are no longer slaves to sin because we have died with Christ and been raised to new life, and this union produces godliness not through external conformity but through the active reign of Christ in us (Romans 6:6-14). Boaz and Ruth's restraint at the threshing floor flows from this same reality: they are not merely following rules to avoid scandal, but they are living from an awareness that God himself is present and active in their lives. Their godliness becomes a platform upon which God's providence unfolds in unexpected and redemptive ways.

Thursday Ezekiel 16:8

God's covenant act toward the beloved is described as spreading his garment over her, a gesture of protection and permanent commitment (Ezekiel 16:8)—the very language Boaz uses when he promises to spread his garment over Ruth and redeem her (Ruth 3:9-13). Just as Boaz will not rest until he has settled the kinsman question and secured Ruth's future, Christ our Redeemer does not cease his work until we are fully redeemed and brought safely into our eternal home. We wait not in abandonment but under the faithful, relentless care of a Redeemer whose commitment to us never falters.

Friday Genesis 1-2

God's design for humanity from the beginning was work coupled with rest—the rhythm of image-bearing in a world under his perfect sovereignty (Genesis 1-2). When our world becomes uncertain and our circumstances unresolved, we are not experiencing a departure from God's design but an invitation to rediscover that our deepest rest has never been found in resolved circumstances but in relationship with God himself. Ruth's waiting season is not a punishment but a gracious navigation through which God reveals himself as her sole source of stability, leading her ultimately to rest, redemption, and a place in the lineage of Christ—a promised restoration far greater than anything she could have engineered herself.

Why Did Ruth Risk Everything?

For the parent

This prompt invites kids to think about Ruth's bold decision at the threshing floor—why she would take such a risky step. Listen for whether they see faith and obedience as connected, and use their answers to help them see that God rewards those who trust him enough to act.

Ruth had a safe life gleaning in the fields, but Naomi told her to go to Boaz at night and ask him to marry her. That was really risky—people might have misunderstood, and Boaz could have said no. Why do you think Ruth did it anyway? What was she trusting God to do?
