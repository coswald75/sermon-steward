Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how God's Word moves through the church via faithful preaching and noble receiving—from the gospel's power to transform, through the dual necessity of mind and heart, to our calling as both proclaimers and examiners of Scripture.

Monday John 1

John opens by declaring that the Word—Jesus Christ himself—is the creative and life-giving power of all things, the light that pierces darkness and gives life to all who believe (John 1:1-5, 12). This is the Word Paul proclaimed in Thessalonica and Berea: not mere doctrine, but the Person through whom God accomplishes salvation. As we gather Sunday by Sunday to hear God's Word preached, we encounter this same efficacious power at work, transforming hearts and awakening faith in the gospel.

Tuesday John 3:19-20

Jesus explains that people love darkness rather than light because their deeds are evil—so when the light comes, they respond with hostility, exactly as the jealous Jews of Thessalonica did (John 3:19-20). Their mob violence was not evidence that Paul preached falsehood; it was evidence that fallen hearts resist the truth that exposes their sin. The gospel does not upset a rightly ordered world; it restores order to a world corrupted by rebellion against God, and those who cling to darkness perceive this restoration as chaos.

Wednesday James 3:1

James warns that teachers of God's Word will be judged with greater strictness because their words carry weight in shaping the faith of others (James 3:1). Paul's reasoning in the synagogues was not mere human persuasion but faithful exposition of Scripture—he stood accountable not to the approval of crowds but to God's standard for those who handle His Word. The preacher's calling is both humbling and sacred: to stand aside so that God's powerful Word alone accomplishes its purposes in hearts.

Thursday 2 Peter 1

Peter insists that we have been given precious promises through Scripture, and these promises are not ambiguous or subjective—they come from the prophetic Word made more sure, calling us to diligence and virtue (2 Peter 1:3-4, 19-21). The Bereans exemplified this balance: they received the Word eagerly with open hearts yet examined the Scriptures daily with disciplined minds, never divorcing fervent belief from careful reasoning. Scripture is neither a text to be dissected coldly nor absorbed uncritically; it demands the whole person in submission to its truth.

Friday 2 Timothy 2

Paul charges Timothy to be diligent to present himself to God approved, handling accurately the word of truth, and to entrust God's Word to faithful people who will teach others (2 Timothy 2:2, 15). This is the congregation's noble calling: to receive each Sunday's proclamation not as judge but as disciple, examining the text to grow in understanding while submitting to its authority even when obedience costs us comfort or social standing. The worshipful dance between preacher and people reaches its fullness when we together commit ourselves to guard, treasure, and live out the enduring Word of God.
