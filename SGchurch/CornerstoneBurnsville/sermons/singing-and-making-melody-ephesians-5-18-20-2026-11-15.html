Ephesians 5:18-20 November 15, 2026 Pastor Rick Gamache
Thesis Worshipful singing is the church's Spirit-empowered, heart-engaged, gospel-grateful response to the glory of Christ—directed both to God and to one another—and therefore corporate singing is essential, not optional, for the gathered church.

Theological claims· 13
  1. Worship is our right response to the glory of God seen in Jesus Christ through the Holy Spirit, and the essence of that response is finding our deepest joy and satisfaction in Christ alone. unit #8
  2. God intends for his people to be a singing people because he created them to be a worshiping people, and singing is the expression of worship. unit #9
  3. The glory of Christ beheld by the Spirit produces affections so profound that mere talking is insufficient—the depth of feeling for Christ's glory demands songs. unit #10
  4. The Holy Spirit's main mission is to magnify Christ, so when we're filled with more of the Spirit, we behold more of Christ's glory, our hearts ignite with intense affection, and we sing. unit #14
  5. Authentic worship doesn't require white-hot emotions—desire for affection and repentance for lack of desire are both legitimate expressions of heartfelt worship. unit #18
  6. Songs both express emotion we already feel and trigger new emotion by helping us feel the wonder of the truths we sing, transforming singing from duty to delight. unit #19
  7. God expects us to use our bodies to glorify him and express what we feel about him through physical expressions like clapping, bowing, lifting hands, shouting, and dancing. unit #20
  8. We do not sing to enter God's presence—we sing as a joyful response to the fact that Jesus' blood has already made entrance into God's presence possible. unit #23
  9. Our singing is celebratory because Jesus has conquered sin, defeated hell, and secured unending joy in God's presence—a reality no power can silence. unit #24
  10. The corporate gathering exists for mutual strengthening and building up, kindling and stoking the fire of affection for God to equip believers for daily worship throughout the week. unit #26
  11. Our physical expressions of worship, particularly singing, help others see Christ's glory and fulfill their created purpose to worship God. unit #27
  12. Corporate singing is a form of Word ministry to one another, simultaneously praising God and encouraging the community of saints. unit #29
  13. Because Christ's perfect life is applied to our account and his death wiped away our guilt, giving us forgiveness and righteousness, we cannot help but sing songs of gratitude. unit #32

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how the Spirit's fullness produces Christ-exalting affection that spills over into joyful, corporate singing—both as our response to the gospel and as mutual ministry to one another.

Monday John 16:14

Jesus promises that the Spirit 'will glorify me, for he will take what is mine and declare it to you.' This is the Spirit's consuming work—not to draw attention to himself, but to unveil Christ's beauty and majesty to our hearts. When we experience the Spirit's filling, we are not swept up in ecstatic emotion for its own sake; we are beholding Christ in such radiance that affection becomes inescapable, and song becomes the natural overflow.

Tuesday Revelation 4:8-11

The heavenly host does not merely speak about God's worthiness; they sing it in unbroken chorus: 'Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God Almighty.' Speech reaches its limits when we encounter infinite glory—singing becomes the language adequate to the magnitude of what we behold. When we gather to sing on Sunday, we are joining that cosmic chorus, our voices becoming the fitting response to a glory that transcends mere words.

Wednesday Hebrews 10:19-22

Because Christ has opened 'a new and living way' through the curtain by his blood, we approach with 'a true heart' and 'full assurance of faith.' Our singing is not a means to access God's favor; it is the exuberant celebration of access already secured and freely given. This gospel foundation transforms singing from striving into gratitude, from duty into delight, because we sing as those who have already been welcomed into the Father's presence through Christ.

Thursday 1 Corinthians 14:26

Paul instructs the church that 'when you come together, each of you has a hymn, a lesson, a revelation...let all these be done for building up.' Our corporate singing is not a solo act of individual worship; it is mutual ministry, where our voices minister the gospel to one another's hearts. As we sing truth together, we become instruments in each other's transformation, stoking affection for Christ in those beside us and equipping the whole body for daily faithfulness.

Friday Revelation 7:9-10

John beholds a multitude from every nation and tongue standing before the throne, robed in white, crying out 'Salvation belongs to our God and to the Lamb!' Their song springs from the irreversible reality that they have been washed in blood and granted standing before the holy God. Our gratitude for Christ's finished work—his righteousness credited to us, his death removing our condemnation—is so immense that silence would be a betrayal of grace; we sing because we cannot remain silent in the presence of such unmerited mercy.
