Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through the unshakable foundation of Romans 8:28—God's eternal chain of foreknowledge, predestination, calling, justification, and glorification—and discover how the gospel frees us from fear, self-pity, and doubt to live with purpose and joy.

Monday Ephesians 1:4

Paul reminds us that before time itself, God chose us in Christ to be holy and blameless—not as an afterthought, but as the very foundation of His plan. This is the opening link in the unbreakable chain: God's choice of us is not reactive to our circumstances or deserving, but flows from His sovereign, eternal purpose. When we grasp that we were chosen before we were born, before we sinned, before we could do anything, we begin to trust that He will complete what He purposed.

Tuesday Romans 11:2

"God has not rejected His people whom He foreknew." The phrase "foreknew" carries the weight of personal intimacy: God did not merely foresee us as abstractions but knew us as His own. This is not distant omniscience but covenantal love—the same word Paul uses to describe how a husband loves his wife. Because God foreknew us with this personal, affectionate knowledge, the chain of salvation cannot break; His knowledge of us secures our belonging.

Wednesday Deuteronomy 7:7-8

Israel did not earn God's covenant love through superiority or worthiness; He chose them because He loved them and because of His promise. This same logic frames our salvation: we are foreknown and predestined not because of anything in us, but because of God's free, unmerited grace. The gospel humbles us as we grasp that our place in God's eternal plan rests entirely on His affection for us in Christ, not on our performance or deserving.

Thursday 1 Corinthians 1:23-24

To those outside the faith, the cross appears foolish and weak; but to those called by God, it is the very power and wisdom of God. This is the pivot point of the entire chain: through Christ's death and resurrection, all the links of foreknowledge, predestination, calling, justification, and glorification are secured and made operative. When we see the cross as God's supreme display of His for-us-ness, we understand why nothing in creation can separate us from His love—He gave His only Son.

Friday Ephesians 1:11

"In Him we have obtained an inheritance, having been predestined according to the purpose of Him who works all things according to the counsel of His will." The practical fruit of believing this is transformation: freed from anxiety about whether God is in control, we can fight the daily fight of faith to trust that even our hardships are being woven into His good purpose. This is the application that changes everything—we stop resisting suffering as meaningless and instead ask, "How is God using this to make me more like Christ?"
