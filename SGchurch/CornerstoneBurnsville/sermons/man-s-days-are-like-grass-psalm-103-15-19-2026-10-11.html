Theological claims· 13
  1. Because humans naturally focus on immediate realities and have only ever experienced this fallen world, we are prone to invest in worldly treasures and comforts despite not truly belonging to this world. unit #3
  2. God has instituted regular means of grace—weekly worship, daily Scripture, and Christian community—precisely because we need constant reminders that this world is not our home. unit #5
  3. We are not self-existent, we cannot control the circumstances of our lives, and we will not escape death. unit #9
  4. Death is an inescapable consequence of the curse resulting from sin, and while tomorrow is not promised, death certainly is. unit #15
  5. Returns to the mall metaphor and reinforces life's brevity with extended Spurgeon quotation using increasingly extreme analogies of fragility. unit #16
  6. Because God's love is eternal, your earthly life is insufficient to contain it; therefore, if you are in Christ, God intends you to experience his everlasting love everlastingly. unit #21
  7. The gospel's purpose is that we endure suffering for a brief moment and then enjoy Christ forever—Jesus died and rose to conquer death and secure this eternal life for us. unit #22
  8. Christians must remember death alongside God's eternal love—Christ's death has conquered, sanctified, and sweetened death so that though we die, death cannot truly harm us. unit #23
  9. Christ's death and resurrection have accomplished double imputation—our sin was placed on Christ and died with him, while his covenant-keeping obedience was credited to us—thereby securing eternal life and purchasing our sanctification. unit #24
  10. Because of Christ's death and resurrection, this brief earthly life exists to prepare us for eternal life where we will forever enjoy God's eternal love. unit #25
  11. In the scope of eternity, earthly life—including all its suffering—will be so infinitesimally brief that it will hardly register, while eternal enjoyment of God will constitute almost all of our existence. unit #30
  12. We live in the already-not-yet of God's kingdom—his throne is established and ruling now, though we await the day when his reign will be visibly and fully manifested. unit #33
  13. Because we are already citizens of God's eternal kingdom, investing in worldly wealth and comfort is foolish—we should live now as citizens of the kingdom we will inhabit forever. unit #35

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the arc from our created weakness and mortality, through God's eternal love that secures us in Christ, to the renewed perspective and holy ambition that should shape how we live now in light of eternity.

Monday Job 7

Job's anguished cry over the brevity and burden of human existence mirrors the psalmist's declaration that our days are like grass. Rather than despair at this reality, we must let it humble us: we are creatures utterly dependent on God's sustaining hand. When we grasp the smallness and fragility of our own life, we are freed from the delusion that we can secure ourselves through earthly striving.

Tuesday Psalm 102

The psalmist laments his fleeting days yet anchors himself to the Lord's enduring throne and everlasting compassion. This is the gospel's comfort: our mortality does not diminish God's covenantal faithfulness toward us. In Christ, we are loved not for a season but for eternity—a love so vast and deep that our brief earthly years become the mere opening movement of an endless symphony.

Wednesday John 3:16

God's love was enacted at the cross, where Christ bore the penalty our sin deserves and defeated death itself through his resurrection. This love is not abstract sentiment but concrete, redemptive action that rescues us from perishing and grants us everlasting life. When we stand in the light of this gift, the riches and accolades of this passing world shrink into proper insignificance.

Thursday Philippians 3:20-21

Our citizenship is already in heaven, and Christ is already our Savior and Lord; we await only the visible, glorious consummation of what is already true. This status transforms how we allocate our allegiance and resources in the present. We live as exiles and pilgrims, not as natives of this world, belonging to a kingdom whose king has already won the victory.

Friday Colossians 3:1-4

Seeking the things above is not escapism but the most productive posture for earthly living. When our minds are set on Christ and his kingdom, our daily work—whether in vocation, family, or church—takes on eternal weight and fruitfulness. We labor not for the applause of a dying age but for the approval of the one who sits enthroned at God's right hand, making every faithful deed eternal in its significance.
