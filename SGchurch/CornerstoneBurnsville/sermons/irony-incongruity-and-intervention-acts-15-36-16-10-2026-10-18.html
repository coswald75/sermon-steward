Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace God's sovereign hand through human disappointment, cultural wisdom, and closed doors, learning that his redirection of our plans is an act of love.

Monday Acts 9:27

Barnabas took the risk of bringing the once-persecuting Saul into the apostles' presence when no one else would trust him. Here we see that Barnabas's gift for seeing potential in broken people—the very character that later made him faithful to John Mark—was itself a gracious instrument in God's hands. His willingness to vouch for an imperfect believer became part of how the Lord advanced his kingdom.

Tuesday Colossians 4:10-11

Years after the sharp disagreement, John Mark—the young man at the center of the dispute—had become so effective in his apostolic work that Paul himself later requested his presence. The gospel's advance did not depend on Paul and Barnabas remaining together; God's purposes are larger than any single partnership. Both servants remained faithful, and both bore fruit in their respective labors.

Wednesday Galatians 5:2

Paul's stern warning here clarifies what was at stake in the Jerusalem Council: circumcision as a *requirement* severs Christ's sufficiency and entangles believers in the yoke of the law. Yet Paul's circumcision of Timothy days later was not contradiction but wisdom—he removed a *cultural obstacle* that would have prevented effective ministry among Jewish communities. The gospel itself is inherently offensive; unnecessary stumbling blocks are different from the scandal of the cross itself.

Thursday Proverbs 13:12

Hope deferred—the closed doors Paul and his team encountered as they traveled—does indeed make the heart sick. Yet what appeared as divine hindrance was divine redirection toward an unplanned continent and the opening of the gospel to the Western world. We cannot see from our present vantage what God sees from eternity; his "no" to our planned route was an immeasurable "yes" to purposes infinitely larger than we imagined.

Friday 2 Timothy 4:11

Paul's final request for John Mark—the young man he once rejected—stands as proof that God's interventions in our lives, however painful in the moment, serve his ultimate purposes and our ultimate good. What began as a wounding disagreement became, through years of faithfulness and divine providence, a reason for reconciliation and gratitude. Our task is to trust that every closed door closes only because our Father is opening a better one.

Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. What specific details does Luke include about the disagreement between Paul and Barnabas in Acts 15:36-41, and what does his refusal to assign blame tell us about how Scripture views conflict among faithful believers?
    Acts 15:36-41
    → How does this realistic portrayal of discord among apostles reshape the way you think about disagreements in our own church community?
  2. Paul circumcises Timothy in Acts 16:1-5 despite the Jerusalem Council's decision that circumcision is not required for salvation. How do you make sense of this apparent contradiction, and what does Paul's action suggest about the difference between a gospel issue and a wisdom issue?
    Acts 16:1-5; Galatians 5:2
    → Can you think of a current decision in your own life where distinguishing between 'gospel non-negotiable' and 'wisdom choice' would change how you approach it?
  3. The sermon identifies three movements in this passage: irony (the split), incongruity (the circumcision), and intervention (the Macedonian vision). Which of these most unsettles you, and why?
  4. Read Acts 16:6-10. What does it mean that the Spirit 'forbade' Paul and his companions from going to certain places, and how does God's redirection through closed doors demonstrate his sovereignty over gospel advance?
    Acts 16:6-10; Isaiah 46:10
    → When have you experienced a closed door in your own life that, looking back, you now recognize as God's redirection toward something better?
  5. The sermon emphasizes that God uses 'cracked and broken vessels' to accomplish his purposes. How does this truth address the temptation to despair when you become aware of your own sinfulness or weakness in ministry or witness?
    2 Corinthians 4:7; 2 Timothy 1:1-2
    → What specific weakness or failure in yourself have you been ashamed to use in service to others, and how might the gospel invite you to offer it differently?
  6. The sermon teaches that 'God's sovereign plans and timing are always better than ours because he has an eternal perspective.' When you face a situation where your plans have been disrupted or denied, what would it look like to actually trust this claim rather than merely believe it intellectually?
    Isaiah 46:10; Proverbs 13:12
Sunday-evening family table

When God Says No

For the parent

This prompt invites your family to reflect on a concrete moment from the sermon: Paul's plans kept getting blocked (closed doors, rejected paths) until God redirected him westward to Europe. Help your children see that God's 'no' answers are not punishments but redirections toward something better—a theme that brings deep comfort when plans fall apart.

In the sermon, Paul had a plan for his missionary trip, but God kept closing doors and saying no to what Paul wanted to do. Can you think of a time when your plans didn't work out the way you hoped? What happened instead? How might God have been redirecting you toward something better?
