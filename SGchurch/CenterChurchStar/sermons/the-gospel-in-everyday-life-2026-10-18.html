Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we explore how ordinary Christian life—devoted to one another, visible in public, and open to the broken—becomes God's means of advancing the Great Commission day by day.

Monday John 13:35

Jesus did not say the world would know His disciples by their doctrinal precision or their event attendance, but by love for one another. When we gather as a church community and genuinely live out the one anothers—bearing one another's burdens, forgiving one another, serving one another—we display a love that the world cannot manufacture on its own. This visible, embodied affection for each other becomes the most powerful argument for the gospel's truth.

Tuesday John 17

In His final prayer before the cross, Jesus did not ask for our comfort or convenience—He asked that we be one, so that the watching world might believe He was sent by the Father. Our unity is not a nice addition to the gospel; it is part of the gospel's verification to those outside the faith. When we choose sacrificial generosity with one another and refuse division, we are answering Jesus's deepest prayer and validating His mission to every observer.

Wednesday Colossians 4:5-6

Paul calls us to 'walk in wisdom toward outsiders,' not to wait for special missionary conferences or dramatic outreach events. The places we already occupy—our workplaces, neighborhoods, schools, and social circles—are our mission field. Our conduct in these ordinary settings, paired with readiness to speak of our hope when asked, fulfills the Great Commission where we already stand. We need not travel far; God has positioned us exactly where we are needed.

Thursday 1 Peter 2:12

Peter reminds us that our good deeds are not meant to be hidden; they are meant to shine publicly so that observers 'glorify God on the day He visits.' This means our generosity, our kindness, our sacrifice, and our hope must be observable to those who do not yet believe. The early church did not compartmentalize faith into Sunday gatherings; they lived it openly in the marketplace, in homes, and in the streets. We are called to the same transparent, gospel-soaked witness.

Friday 1 Peter 3:15

Being ready to give an answer assumes that our hope is visible enough that others notice it and ask. This readiness flows from the one anothers and public faithfulness of the earlier days this week—when we love visibly, live generously, and conduct ourselves with gospel wisdom, people naturally wonder why. Our preparedness to articulate the gospel follows from our preparedness to live it. The Great Commission advances not through programmatic events but through ordinary believers whose transformed lives prompt extraordinary conversations.

  5. The one another commands in Scripture are not disconnected from the Great Commission but are the means by which God is glorified, believers grow, and the gospel advances. unit #25
  6. Church division and stinginess put a stain on Jesus's name and hinder the gospel's display. unit #26
  7. Jesus prayed for Christian unity as the means by which the world would know he was sent by the Father, making observable love among Christians the final apologetic for the gospel. unit #27
  8. Modern Christians have more opportunities to connect than the early church, but technology is a tool to aid fellowship, not a replacement for embodied, face-to-face presence. unit #33

  11. The one anothers and the Great Commission are not either/or but both/and—the one anothers glorify God, bless believers, and serve as a powerful display of the gospel. unit #49
  12. The early church practiced the one anothers visibly, lived faith publicly, and remained open to anyone—fulfilling Jesus's teachings that the world would know his disciples by their love and glorify God by seeing good works. unit #51
  13. The community group and Sunday gathering are not merely for internal blessing but are part of the Great Commission, equipping believers to be outward-facing and welcoming others into the demonstration of the gospel. unit #55
