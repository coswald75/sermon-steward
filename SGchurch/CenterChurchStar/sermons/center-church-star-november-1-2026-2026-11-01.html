Theological claims· 15
  1. No one is innocent before God on the strength of their works, and those who know Scripture best are most at risk of believing they are the exception. unit #6
  2. False assurance of one's standing with God is the most terrifying spiritual condition because it prevents recognition of the need for rescue. unit #10
  3. Christian practices and disciplines are valuable blessings that yield real good, but they do not establish righteousness before God and only reveal their true value when the heart aligns with God himself. unit #14
  4. The significance of the Jewish people is exclusively grounded in God's sovereign choice, covenant faithfulness, and grace—not in any human merit or ethnic advantage—making their entire history a display of divine character. unit #16
  5. Israel's history is the unique, divinely ordained means by which humanity learns God's character—his faithfulness, promise-keeping, and grace are revealed through his dealings with this people despite their unfaithfulness. unit #17
  6. Israel's history functions both as a spectacle revealing God's grace and as a warning demonstrating that even recipients of extraordinary blessing can turn away from the giver. unit #18
  7. Romans is not merely a historical document about a first-century church but is primarily God's present speech through the risen Christ, making its message directly applicable to the contemporary congregation. unit #25
  8. True belief in the gospel is inseparable from ongoing recognition of need for the gospel—not as a past event but as present dependence on union with God. unit #29
  9. Paul's argument silences all human self-justification and establishes that the church shares the world's problem—universal sin requiring the exclusive solution of the gospel. unit #32
  10. The gospel is only good news to those who recognize their desperate need for rescue; without that recognition, it is merely information, and Scripture exists not as comfort but as God's instrument for bringing us to himself. unit #34
  11. Recognizing we will never be good enough produces clear vision of reality and enables us to stop self-rescue striving and rest in Christ's power to save. unit #36
  12. Present victory over temptation and sin's lies is secured by remembering both our sin and Christ's complete conquest of that sin at the cross, where the debt was fully paid. unit #37
  13. Salvation is not merely a past-tense event but an ongoing present reality continuously working in believers until the resurrection, requiring present-tense gospel application. unit #38
  14. Romans 1-3's difficult terrain exists not to address distant humanity's sin but to confront the church's own present ongoing need for the gospel, revealing a need greater than we realize and a salvation greater than any sin. unit #39
  15. God's work of salvation is guaranteed to continue from initial calling through final glorification, securing believers in his faithfulness rather than their own effort. unit #40

When we see that, when we are reminded, and when we hold closely to the salvation that is given to us, we are reminded where our help comes from. It is not as if we are pulled out of the pit, we sort of dust ourselves off, give a nod to Jesus, say, hey, thank you, and then go on about our away. That is not what this is about. We are a people who are called to consider our salvation by recalling what would have been if not for God. And in that understanding, we are driven to walk closely with the Savior forever. Not one day. Not when he returns. But now, in Starr, wherever you are, walking with the risen Savior now. And we do that knowing the wonderful joy of knowing that we will never be out of his reach. We will not fear being abandoned. But we will always, always watch our steps, horrified of the thought of being even one step too far removed from his embrace. Yet when we wander, wander, as we all know we do, we are given passages like this in Romans 3 and all that comes before it. We are given passage passages like this to remind us of the cold at the bottom of the pit where we once were. Like waking from a terrible dream only to be assured of things that no longer have a grasp on us.

31 · Jeff turns to Romans 3:19-20, the conclusion of the primary text, showing Paul's final move: the law's function is to silence all human defense and establish universal accountability

This all comes together when we consider the following 2 verses at the end of our passage. Now we know that whatever the law says, it speaks to those who are under the law, so that every mouth may be stopped and the whole world may be held accountable to God. For by works of the law no human being will be justified in his sight, since through the law comes the knowledge of sin.

32 · Jeff draws the application from Romans 3:19's 'every mouth stopped'—all human self-justification, blame-shifting, and excuse-making is silenced when confronted with sin

When we consider all these things in ourselves, we are helped to see what the issue is at the heart. So all our tendencies to evoke what we have done right, all of our tendency to evoke all that people have done wrong to us, and all of the disadvantages that we want to allude to that suggest why we are the way that we are, they're muted. It's like nothing. As the Scripture says, All mouths are stopped. When we are forced to see our sin, nothing else can be said. The secret of this passage is that our answer matches the world's. It is all of our problem. We must see our sin, and we must see the tragedy of life apart from God. The only solution, the only solution for us and for the world is the gospel that rescues us from that sin.

33 · Jeff preaches the gospel in compressed narrative form: incarnation, sinless life, substitutionary atonement, death, resurrection, victory

The gospel is that God the Son became a man. He became a man, and though as a man he was tempted in every way common to us, He did not sin. And for His people, though He had not sinned, He took on all of their sins, carrying that sin and all of that guilt, and He carried it to the cross, and He suffered the wrath His people deserved. And He died on their behalf. And having accomplished all He was determined to do, having paid for all of the sins of his people, he was raised from the dead. He was raised from the dead and proclaiming to all the world and to all of history that sin and death have been defeated. That's good news.

34 · Jeff returns to the sermon's central claim: the gospel cannot be believed as good news apart from believing one's need for it

If we don't see the truth of sin, the truth of our need, that is not good news. That's just news. You can't really believe the gospel if you don't really believe your need for the gospel. Nobody fears the pit that they're in. They don't if everyone around them is telling them, hey, you're going to be okay. Don't worry about it. You do you. This is normal. Nobody thinks they have a need when that's the case. And this is Paul's entire point in his argument thus far. We need to remember that if we have and know the Scripture, it is far more than a security blanket. It is a wonderful thing and it is comforting, but it is designed to do something particular, and that is to bring us to God. It is designed to bring us, to speak to us, whatever it takes for us to know Him.

35 · Jeff calls for repentance as the appropriate response to recognizing sin: turning from self-reliance to resting in Christ's completed work

And what we need is repentance, knowing our need to turn away from all others and to rest in the living God, to trust in what Jesus has done. For those who don't know the Scriptures, who aren't familiar with them, they also speak to us their ultimate accountability. Whether they know them or not, they will be measured against the word of the Lord. And most of all, they and we alike need to be reminded and even more made certain that this rescue cannot be done by anything we have the ability to do. Just as much as those around us who are content to believe they will figure it out, Or we've heard it all because we probably have said it all or thought it all, or that we're not that bad, or that we too— we need to be reminded that there will never be, ever, nobody, nobody will ever stand before the living God and hear Him say, you are good enough. That's a lie. Even the possibility of that lie needs to be purged from our minds and our hearts.

36 · Jeff identifies the liberation that comes from abandoning the lie of self-sufficiency: clear vision of reality produces rest rather than striving

And only when we release that lie, if only when we understand that we will never be good enough, Will we be able to see things as they really are? And seeing things clearly, this amazing thing happens. We stop trying to claw our way out and we just rest. We rest in Christ's living and powerful strength to rescue us.

37 · Jeff shifts to first-person testimony, modeling the application of gospel remembrance to ongoing sanctification

I am convinced, I am convinced that every vice I find myself ever entangled in, every temptation that lingers longer than it should, every lie that makes my mind feel captive is fully and completely defeated when I recall my sin and know that Jesus and Jesus alone has conquered and continues to conquer that sin in my life. In this, I recall the place where my sin was put to death, and I can go back like all of you to the one place where that happened, to the cross, knowing that That is where Jesus paid it all.

38 · Jeff confronts the error of viewing salvation as a past-tense event disconnected from present experience

We must not be a people who look at our relationship to the gospel, our rescue from sin, as merely this dot on a timeline sometime in the past. While it is something that happened, it's something that continues to actively work in the present and will continue to work in us until the resurrection. It is always taking place in us as the people of Christ.

39 · Jeff synthesizes the sermon's argument: Romans 1-3 addresses not distant others but the congregation's own present need for the gospel

The living God speaks to us this morning, not just to remind us of humanity's need— those over there— but as our present need for the gospel now, always, until Christ makes us new. This is all that has been secured and addressed in Christ's life, death, and resurrection. Romans 1 through 3 may be difficult terrain and slow going, but it speaks a word to us. Our need is likely much greater than we probably realize, but we can rest because where that road leads is to a salvation that is far greater than any sin we could ever realize.

40 · Jeff introduces external scripture (Philippians 1:6) as assurance supporting the claim that salvation is God's ongoing work from beginning to eschatological completion

As Paul so helpfully says to the Philippian church, I'm sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion on the day of Christ Jesus.

41 · Jeff returns to the opening road trip analogy, reinterpreting his initial impatience

When we started, I admitted to you all That I had become impatient, that I had started to be ready for things to move along with Romans, and I was getting tired and weary of how long it takes to get to the good stuff, so to speak. And that impatient kid in the back seat of my own heart was certainly pestering with that wonderful question all parents know too well: are we there yet? The payoff for taking a trip, no matter how you get there, is not just where you end up, though that is the target, but recognizing what it does take to get there. Whether it's enduring endless hours through the desert or the snowy mountains, we appreciate and love the destination when we understand what it takes to get there, when we know the road that it takes to bring us to that place.

42 · Jeff delivers the sermon's final synthesis: the gospel's beauty and worth-sharing quality only emerge from understanding the need it addresses and the depths of Christ's rescue

Brothers and sisters, friends, the gospel, the good news, is good. It is beautiful. But we cannot truly begin to understand what makes it good news, or even good news worth sharing, if we don't understand, if we don't believe our need for it. We need to understand the depths to which Christ reached down to rescue us. These places are difficult and they take time, but they're not meant for us to stay there. They're just the way we go to get where we do need to go and stay. They are a part of the way, and their role is to help us treasure even more the glories we are being brought to. And friends, I assure you, it is worth the wait.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we move through Paul's severest diagnosis—universal sinfulness—to grasp why the gospel alone can save us, and why those who know Scripture best often need this confrontation most.

Monday Romans 1

Paul opens his letter by establishing that God's wrath is revealed against all ungodliness—not because people lack information about God, but because they suppress the truth they already know. We who have grown up in the church and absorbed biblical knowledge are most susceptible to the lie that our familiarity with Scripture itself grants us standing before God, when in fact knowing God's Word without letting it confront our hearts is the deepest form of suppression.

Tuesday Romans 2:21-23

Paul's indictment of those who teach others but fail to teach themselves cuts at the heart of self-deception: we can be doctrinally sound, morally disciplined, and still fundamentally wrong about ourselves before God. This false assurance—the confidence that our knowledge and practice have made us acceptable—is not a small error but the very barrier that keeps us from fleeing to Christ for rescue.

Wednesday Romans 2:28-29

Circumcision was the covenant sign, the mark of God's people—yet Paul declares it worthless if the heart remains uncircumcised. Our church attendance, Bible reading, and moral uprightness are genuine goods, but they become destructive lies if we trust them to justify us. Only when we release confidence in our own righteousness can we discover that these practices become what they were always meant to be: expressions of a heart already loved and justified by grace.

Thursday Psalm 14

The Psalms that Paul quotes in Romans 3:10-18 are Israel's own voice condemning Israel—God's covenant people calling out their own depravity. This is not external judgment but internal conviction: even those chosen and blessed repeatedly by God's faithfulness still turn to foolishness and corruption. We stand in that same lineage and face the same capacity for self-deception, no matter how many times grace has reached us.

Friday Philippians 1:6

Paul's confidence that God will complete the work He began in us is not grounds for passivity but for present trust: we remain dependent on God's continuous faithfulness to finish what He started. This week's hard truths about our sinfulness and inability are not meant to drive us to despair but to anchor us in the only reliable foundation—not our perseverance, but His promise to perfect us until the day of Christ.
