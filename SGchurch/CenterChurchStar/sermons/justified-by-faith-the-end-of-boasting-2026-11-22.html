Theological claims· 10
  1. Romans 3:21-31 is designed to give the soul relief after the long indictment of sin, and we must slow down and let the reality of justification soak in before it normalizes. unit #6
  2. What used to be our ground for boasting—our knowledge and our deeds—is no longer relevant after justification because there is no room for it in light of what God has done in Jesus. unit #11
  3. After Christ's redemptive work, our boasting in ourselves—in what we know and do—has no legs to stand on; perceived advantages are baseless. unit #13
  4. Justified believers must remember that self-boasting has been excluded from the courtroom and has no place in the life of the redeemed. unit #17
  5. Believers stand only because of the redemption received from Jesus—they know they should not be standing at all, yet they are free. unit #19
  6. Faith is confidence that the gift is ours—not because we earned it, but because the one who gave it has guaranteed it. unit #22
  7. For those who have faith, the old boasting is excluded—only faith resides, and justification is by faith apart from works of the law. unit #23
  8. Your hearing, believing, and redemption are gifts you could never have obtained and would not even have known about apart from God's grace and his determination to make you his own. unit #27
  9. God has providentially preserved and delivered the gospel across history to bring it to you—this is a gift, and it is no fluke that you have heard and believed. unit #28
  10. To be justified means that our wrath was withheld and poured on Jesus, and in the cosmic town square, we are announced as justified because Christ held and killed our sin on the cross. unit #31

5-day reading plan

This week we walk through the radical shift that justification demands: from boasting in ourselves to resting entirely in Christ's finished work, understanding that our confidence, our faith, and even our hearing of the gospel are gifts we could never have earned.

Monday Romans 1:16-17

Paul declares that the gospel itself is the power of God for salvation—not our knowledge, our deeds, or our merit, but God's saving power revealed through faith. This is the foundation: we are justified not by what we bring to God, but by what He has already accomplished and now offers freely. When we grasp that the very power for our standing before God comes from outside us, our old boasting crumbles, and we stand amazed at a Savior we did not deserve.

Tuesday Romans 2:17-24

Here Paul exposes the false confidence of those who boast in the law, in their knowledge of God, and in their religious performance—only to be undone by their own failure to keep what they claim to teach. This passage reveals the bankruptcy of self-reliance: we cannot stand on what we know or what we do because we inevitably fail both. In the courtroom of justification, these credentials are not merely insufficient—they are irrelevant, cast aside by the One who alone can declare us righteous.

Wednesday Deuteronomy 6:4-5

The Shema demands total devotion—a law so perfect, so exacting, that it shows us the standard God upholds and the standard we cannot meet. Yet this very law, in Christ, becomes the measure of His justice: He satisfied every demand on our behalf, bearing the full weight of the law's curse so that His justice was upheld and we law-breakers are set free. We cannot boast in keeping what we could never keep; we can only marvel that the One who gave the law kept it perfectly for us.

Thursday Romans 3:27

Paul asks directly: on what basis is boasting excluded? Not by works, but by faith—by the principle of faith that acknowledges all standing comes from outside ourselves. This verse cuts to the heart: our old grounds for confidence were always an illusion, but now the illusion is exposed and definitively shut out. We are free precisely because we have nowhere to hide in self-righteousness, and that very freedom is our peace.

Friday Romans 3:23-26

Here the gospel reaches its climax: all have sinned and fall short, yet we are justified freely by His grace through the redemption in Christ Jesus, whose blood was the propitiation for our sins. We stand not because we deserved acquittal, but because Christ bore what we deserved so completely that God's justice was satisfied and we are declared righteous. This is the relief Paul has been driving toward—not pride, but the stunned gratitude of those snatched from the fire, knowing we should not be standing at all, yet forever free because Christ is our Justifier.
