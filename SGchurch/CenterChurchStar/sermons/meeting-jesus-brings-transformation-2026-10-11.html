Theological claims· 11
  1. Saul's immediate proclamation of Jesus is the direct result of receiving a new heart through tasting the all-satisfying grace of his Savior. unit #9
  2. Meeting Jesus produces internal renewal in which a heart of stone is replaced with a heart of flesh that beats for God's glory and the good of his people. unit #13
  3. When you're saved, your community is transformed—your former community may turn against you, but God gives you a new community, the church, through which he frequently meets your needs. unit #20
  4. Jesus delights to meet your needs through his people, but this requires meaningful connection to community—not consumerist attendance but deep, regular, shared life together as modeled in Acts. unit #21
  5. God's community does not operate according to natural human self-protection but according to a different logic of reconciliation and welcome. unit #25
  6. The church's welcome of former enemies is radically countercultural because it trusts that God's saving power is sufficient to transform anyone, even those who once sought to destroy the community. unit #27
  7. Gospel reconciliation begins when one person takes a step toward a former enemy, trusting in the transforming power of God. unit #28
  8. When we meet Jesus, our community is transformed—Jesus has the power to restore broken relationships and delights to meet our needs through his people. unit #31
  9. The church's peace amidst persecution is not dependent on circumstances but on their faith in God, grounded in their eyewitness testimony that if God can transform Saul, he is truly for his people and in control. unit #36
  10. The congregation hears the gospel and is part of the multiplying church today because God saved Saul and sent him to the Gentiles. unit #39
  11. The gospel transforms communities one soul at a time through the infectious spread of the light of Christ from believer to believer, and this process is always active even when we cannot see it. unit #40

So Saul's conversion at the road to Damascus and him receiving this persecution, being sent off, and then coming back We hear the gospel because of what God did in Saul. We see the church multiplying today by one soul at a time being added because God saved Saul. It's incredible.

40 · Universalizes the Saul pattern: every transformed believer becomes a link in the chain of gospel transmission

You see, when the gospel transforms one person, it leads to the conversion or the transformation of another. And another. And another. We might not end up writing Holy Scripture like Saul did. We're not going to, in fact. But we can know that each and every believer that becomes another light in this dark and broken and twisted world, the light of the gospel will continue to shine forth through each and every believer and will continue to bring soul after soul to saving faith in Jesus, including here in star. Communities are transformed not by mass, mass production events, but by one soul being saved at a time. The spread of the gospel is infectious. We may not always see it, but the gospel is always moving. It's always reaching more people, and it happens with one saved soul at a time.

41 · Synthesizes the sermon's three-part structure before moving into application, restating the thesis in summary form to prepare the congregation for the closing charge

Church, meeting Jesus brings transformation. It begins its work internally in the heart. It continues its work externally in redefining community. And it has the power to, at one person at a time, transform communities.

42 · First application segment: direct evangelistic appeal to unbelievers present

A few application points as we close. If you're here today and you're not a believer in Jesus, thanks for being here. Your willingness to sit through an hour and a half service is no small sacrifice. As we know, there are many things you could do on a Sunday morning. But if you're here, you're here for one reason or another. And you've heard that Jesus is on a rescue mission for your soul. He wants to bring souls into the family of God. Maybe in evaluating your own heart, you can see that you need transformation. If that's the case, I can promise you this. Jesus does not withhold his grace and transforming power from any who would humble themselves and confess their sin to him. If you don't understand what that looks like, I encourage you to talk to somebody who you know is a believer. Ask them what it's like to meet Jesus. If you don't, if you're feeling a prick in your heart, don't ignore it. Press in. Ask questions and know that Jesus is ready to answer those questions. Jesus is ready to meet with you.

43 · Second application segment: pastoral encouragement to believers struggling with ongoing sin

If you're a believer this morning and you see that your words reveal a heart of hidden sin or idolatry, be encouraged. God does not end his transforming work at salvation. He begins it. We are day by day being renewed by the gospel of grace through the power of the Holy Spirit. To become degree by degree more like Jesus. Saul himself, now if we would want to call him Paul, writes in 2 Corinthians 3:18, and we all with unveiled face beholding the glory of the Lord are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit. Confess your sin to Jesus. Confess your need for continued transformation in your heart. And invite your fellow believers into your time of need. Jesus will never reject you or leave you to change on your own.

44 · Third application segment: charge to the whole church to participate in God's transforming mission through evangelism and community care

And lastly, remember, Christian, that God can and will use you both to spread his gospel and to meet the needs of his people should you be obedient. Come to church with the expectation of meeting someone new and welcoming them into the fold. Invest time into communing with saints and learning of each other's needs. Be strengthened by the Spirit and share the transforming news of the gospel with your neighbors, with your coworkers, with your family members, with your friends. Church, there are souls that need to hear the transforming work of Jesus. And like Saul, you don't need to know everything about Jesus in order to do it. Because we have been equipped by the Spirit to go forth and share the good news. God is the one who will orchestrate the transformation. If you look at this entire section of Scripture, you see it's not Saul who transformed himself. It is God who transformed him. God will transform. God will do the work. But we get to participate by being obedient and participating in that mission.

45 · Doxological close lifting the congregation into worship by naming the joy of witnessing gospel transformation at all three levels

There is nothing more joyous than seeing the power of the gospel at work in transforming us, in transforming our fellow believers, and in transforming our communities.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how meeting Jesus produces transformation in three expanding circles—first in the heart, then in community relationships, and finally in the multiplication of God's people across all nations.

Monday Ezekiel 36:26

Ezekiel's promise of a new heart—tender, responsive, alive to God—found its fulfillment in Saul on the Damascus road. When we truly encounter Jesus, the Spirit replaces our hardened, self-centered hearts with hearts that naturally overflow in proclamation of Christ, just as Saul immediately declared Jesus in the synagogues. This transformation is not behavior modification; it is the pneumatic renewal of our very being, the spring from which all our words and actions flow.

Tuesday Luke 6:45

What comes out of the mouth reveals what fills the heart—and Saul's immediate proclamation that "Jesus is the Son of God" (Acts 9:20) demonstrates a heart newly captivated by Christ. Before Damascus, his words defended tradition and persecuted the church; after Damascus, his mouth becomes a herald of the gospel. We, too, are diagnosed by our speech: are our conversations consumed with self-protection and cultural comfort, or do we find ourselves speaking of Jesus' lordship and his transforming power?

Wednesday Acts 2:42-47

The Jerusalem church that initially feared Saul embodied the Acts 2 pattern: radical vulnerability, daily togetherness, and a willingness to share all things for one another's good. When Barnabas brought Saul to the apostles and vouched for his transformation, the community received him into this costly, intimate fellowship. Our participation in God's transforming work requires us to move beyond Sunday attendance into the shared life where we know one another's struggles, pray for one another's souls, and meet real needs in real time.

Thursday Acts 8:1-3

Saul had supervised the execution of Stephen and ravaged the church from house to house—yet the very community he hunted received him when Barnabas testified to his conversion. This welcome was not naive or sentimental; it was grounded in unwavering faith that if God could halt Saul's persecution mid-stride and remake his heart, then God's transforming power is utterly reliable and his saving grace knows no limits. When we welcome a former adversary or a prodigal back into community, we stake our hope on the same gospel power that resurrected Saul.

Friday Acts 9:1-19

Saul's encounter with the risen Jesus on the Damascus road was not merely for his own salvation; it was the beginning of a transformative mission that would spread the gospel to Gentiles and shape the entire direction of the church. As we consider our own meeting with Jesus, we must ask: how is God using our transformation to multiply His kingdom through us? The church's growth and peace in Acts 9:31 flowed directly from God's work in one man's heart. When we meet Jesus afresh and allow His transforming power to reshape us, we participate in the same multiplication that continues to this day.
