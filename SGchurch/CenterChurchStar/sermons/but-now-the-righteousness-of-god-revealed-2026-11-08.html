We did not know what we were learning as we were going through there. We thought what we were seeing is this is everything that God is going to hammer down. But what we found is this is everything that Jesus Christ would pay for with his life and his death. The righteousness revealed in the gospel and the good news is that God has shown himself to be all glorious. He is the one who at his own expense endured our sin, endures our sin, will endure our sin, our hatred, our venomous hearts. He revealed righteousness— his revealed righteousness shows that he yet remains just. He remains just, but he also frees those who receive his grace, the redemption given in Christ Jesus by faith.

35 · Palen expounds Romans 3:26—the entire scope of history exists to display God's righteousness as the one who is simultaneously just and justifier of those who have faith in Jesus

Then, in the way that only the Creator could do, he shows the splendor of his entire plan. What we see in verse 26, it was to show his righteousness. It was to show his righteousness at the present time so that he might be just and justifier. Just and justifier of the one. Who has faith in Jesus. The history of humankind, the history of all creation, though broken, corrupted by the fall, by the rebellion of those given the glory and image of God, was fashioned in a glorious display of God's righteousness, the display of the one who remains just yet also justifies those who have faith in Jesus.

36 · Palen asserts that the gospel is first and foremost a display of who God is—righteous in a way that holds justice and grace together simultaneously, punishing and liberating, upholding holiness and cleansing the wicked

As we come to the close of these 30 weeks, we need to consider that wonderful truth. For different reasons, we can believe that the gospel is good news because we can be saved by Jesus, and that's absolutely true. But the good news, first and foremost, is a showing. It is a display. It reveals to us and to all the cosmos who our God is, and he is righteous. That righteousness is more than just wrath. Far more than just wrath against rebellion. It is righteousness, justice, and grace somehow together at the same time. God is just, but he also justifies. He punishes and he liberates. He upholds his holiness and he cleanses the wicked, bringing them into that holiness.

37 · Palen notes that Romans 3:21-26 contains no requirement of human merit—only the persistent mention of faith as the means by which grace is received

You might have noticed that throughout the passage there is no mention of being good enough. Still, there's no message here that says we need to be good enough for God to justify. That has not changed. There has not been any mention of what it takes to earn being called righteous. Still, what you have seen though is this persistent mentioning of something we call faith. The relentless motion of grace to those who have faith, those who believe.

38 · Palen applies the passage by defining faith: believing that your only hope is Christ's blood leads to running from rebellion, experiencing freedom, and being restored to the image of God

Our call in this passage today is not to simply affirm that we believe these things to be true. That is certainly part of it, but we need to know that they are true of us. Do you require, do you need, is your only hope that Jesus' blood is a propitiation for your sins? That is what it means to believe, because when that happens, when you believe that your only hope is that his blood was spilled for our sin, you start to run away from all that rebels against When you know you need to be freed and you know that you have been freed by the blood of Christ, you are— it's like the shackles fall off and suddenly what you thought was always going to be a part of you is now shed. It's like a robe that falls off and you would otherwise feel naked, but you are actually— you are revealed to be As you were supposed to be all along, redeemed, glorifying to the Lord, the image of God, not rebellious, but free to enjoy and to walk with the living God in every way.

39 · Palen issues the central application question: do you see your need to be saved, and will you trust that Jesus suffered what you deserve?

I lost my spot. The question we have to answer is, do we see our need to be saved? Do we see our need to be saved? And will you trust the one who can save you? Will you trust that Jesus suffered what you deserve? Will you trust that Jesus suffered what you deserve? Have you been freed and justified in the court of God?

40 · Palen steps into direct pastoral address to those the Spirit is convicting, assuring them that wanting to be forgiven is the same as having it because it is a free gift secured by Christ

I trust also, I trust also that the Spirit of God, who knows all of our hearts perfectly, is probably pressing on some even now. If in hearing this good news you desire to be counted among those who are forgiven, who want your sins covered by the death of Christ, and you want to be declared innocent before God, I want you to know this scripture. Because it's yours. The most amazing thing is, is that everything in life, we can want it. There is nothing in life that you can want without any price. Getting it though is a different thing. Wanting and having is a different thing. What this tells us is that it's a gift. So wanting to be redeemed, wanting to be free from sin, wanting to be forgiven by the Lord God, wanting to be able to stand before Him clean and righteous and forgiven, that is the same thing as having it because Jesus Christ has secured it. It, and you have it as a gift. And this passage tells you it's yours.

41 · Palen issues concrete imperatives: trust in Jesus, believe He is your only hope, be certain you are forgiven, and live in the security that you have been purchased and cannot be lost

Trust. Trust in Jesus. Trust that his life and death have been exchanged for your own. Believe from your heart that he is your only hope in life and in death, and know, know Be certain that you are truly forgiven and live the rest of your life exactly like that. These promises are now yours and they can never be taken away. You cannot outmatch the blood of Jesus for your salvation. You cannot squirm your way out of the hand of the Father, and you cannot squirm your way out of the hand of the Son. You have been purchased by his blood, and you are his.

42 · Palen closes with a specific instruction for new believers: tell a Christian, or tell him, and know that the church exists to help them follow Jesus

If that describes you, and I hope it does, please tell someone you know is a Christian. And if you don't know anybody that's a Christian, come tell me. I really want to know. I want you to know that if you are trusting in Jesus for the first time, that this church exists to help you to follow Jesus for the rest of your life.

Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week, we trace God's righteousness revealed in Christ from the foundation of human inability through the sufficiency of Christ's propitiation, landing in the freedom and restoration the gospel alone secures.

Monday Romans 1:18

Paul begins his letter by unveiling God's wrath against all ungodliness—the terrifying reality of what we deserve by nature. This devastating portrait is not meant to crush us finally, but to prepare our hearts to receive the gospel's stunning reversal. Only when we grasp the weight of divine wrath can we comprehend the immeasurable grace that diverts that wrath to Christ and secures our peace.

Tuesday Romans 3:19-20

The law silences every mouth and holds the whole world accountable to God—it never justifies, only condemns. Paul has spent thirty weeks establishing this crushing reality: we cannot save ourselves through obedience. This is not despair; it is the necessary demolition of false hope that clears the ground for us to rest entirely on Christ's righteousness imputed to us.

Wednesday Romans 1:16-17

The gospel is God's power for salvation—not a moral philosophy or self-improvement plan, but the supernatural, efficacious display of God's saving activity. From Romans 1 onward, Paul unfolds why this power is necessary (human depravity) and how it operates (faith in Christ's substitutionary death). We are held by a power that humbles us and exalts Christ simultaneously.

Thursday Romans 3:25

Christ is presented as the place where God's wrath is satisfied—the mercy seat upon which His justice is satisfied and His mercy is shown. Every animal sacrifice in Israel pointed here: to the One whose blood would be shed not symbolically but truly, purchasing our redemption through His actual death. The gospel is not sentimental forgiveness but costly, substitutionary justice.

Friday Romans 1:16

We are not ashamed of the gospel because its power is directed toward our salvation—freedom from condemnation, rescue from the spiritual adversary, and restoration to the humanity we were made for. This freedom is not abstract; it transforms how we live, whom we trust, and what we pursue. As those purchased by Christ's blood, we live the rest of our lives as the redeemed and the freed.
