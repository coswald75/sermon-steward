0 · Pastor Jeff opens by directing the congregation to Psalm 127:1, establishing it as the sermon's focal text and noting its brevity

If you have your Bible this morning, we're going to be in Psalm 127, and it will be one of the shortest passages we probably ever do in this church. Maybe it will definitely be in the rankings for sure, but Psalm 127, and we are going to look at verse 1 this morning.

1 · Pastor Jeff tells a personal story about his father's construction work on a church building, emphasizing how witnessing the building process created a lasting connection between the structure and his father's craftsmanship

There is a highway, or there is a stretch of highway on Highway 50 east of Sacramento where I to this day can still hear my father's voice. Since I was about 10, whenever we would drive past this stretch of highway, my dad would point to a church that sat on, sits on the north side of the road and has this blue-colored steel roof. And he would point at it and he would start recounting, telling us stories about the time he was there working on it when it was being constructed. All right, there's some particular stories I recall where there were difficult portions that required them to have long meetings and nobody could quite figure out how to construct this particular piece of the building. Though the prints were set in front of them, it was peculiar, and they didn't have a lot of experience doing that piece. And then I remember him even discussing how these projects and his problem-solving mind, it would keep him up all night trying to think through it. You know, he'd go to sleep thinking about it, and he'd wake up thinking about it, and As I shared before, my father being in construction, he worked on numerous projects over his life, many projects over his life. This one though was special because this was the one that in God's grace I was able to see myself. I didn't just hear the stories, but I actually got to see it. It was one of the few that I got to see personally myself. And as a result of that, I would say it is nearly impossible now for me to not think of my dad, to not think of his craftsmanship and his work and his stories whenever I drive by.

2 · Pastor Jeff announces the church's first anniversary and frames the sermon as a recounting of God's work by returning to Psalm 127:1, a foundational passage for the church's planters, expressing hope that the congregation will develop the same deep connection to this text

As many of you have heard already this morning, this morning marks the 1-year anniversary of Center Church of Star, and I believe we are meant on such an occasion as this this, to recount all that God has done, to look at all that God has done and look at what he is yet to do still in this place and in this people. And we will be doing this in a very special way by revisiting a very particular passage that has held a place in the heart of these— of the people that have planted this church, that have helped build this church, that have worked to get this church to be where it is today. And no matter your familiarity with this verse, and no matter what special place it holds for you, I want you to understand that we have a ton of joy that you're here with us this morning. But I hope you too one day will hold this verse deeply in your own hearts. That you will be unable to go past it when you read the Scriptures and not think of what your heavenly Father has done and recount.

3 · Pastor Jeff transitions from the opening illustration to the exposition, expressing hope that future generations will recount God's work in building Center Church just as he recounts his father's construction work

As we can't recount with these helpful orienting words from the Scripture, I hope and pray that each of us who call Center Church home will press on and be encouraged by the work that we were all called to. And by doing so, maybe one day our children, the children of this church, will recount this glorious building project, recalling along with their friends and their parents that they had the privilege of seeing it built.

4 · Pastor Jeff reads Psalm 127:1 aloud and previews the sermon's three-part structure: the privilege of work, seeing, and enjoying

So turn with me to Psalm 127:1 as we look to consider the privilege of work, the privilege of seeing, and the privilege of enjoying. A Song of Ascents of Solomon. Unless the Lord builds the house, those who build it labor in vain. Unless the Lord watches the city, the watchman stays awake in vain.

5 · Pastor Jeff provides historical and liturgical context for Psalm 127:1, explaining Solomon's role as temple builder, the psalm's use as a Song of Ascents sung on pilgrimage, and its function as worship preparation

The superscription in our Bibles tells us this psalm is associated with Solomon, the man God would use to build the temple where his own presence would dwell. It is also a psalm of ascent, which means it was sung on the climb up to the temple that sat at the top. And pilgrims walking up the hill towards the temple would be out of breath singing these words together before they reached the temple courts. These words are meant to be said on the way by the people on their feet. As a kind of foreshadowing, this is a psalm that is designed to prepare our hearts for worship, to be reminded of those things that help us to know who we are and who God is, and so that knowing those things, we can properly respond to who God is. I encourage you, as we move this morning through this text, To keep in mind those things as you listen.

6 · Pastor Jeff analyzes the structure of Psalm 127:1, identifying its parallel construction, three parties involved (the Lord, the workers, and those who enjoy), and the paradox that God is the ultimate actor while human workers remain necessary

The psalmist also assures a project of sorts in this passage. This is conveyed in a parallel— 2 different things that are similar running next to each other. The first line refers to a building of a house and the second to the safeguarding of a city. And then the psalm points to 3 parties who are involved in these projects: the Lord, the workers, and those who go unmentioned, the ones who enjoy. In the first line, the Lord is shown as the primary actor. And notice, though, that in both examples, people work even though God is the one ultimately doing the work. Even though God builds the house and keeps watch over the city, builders and watchmen remain. Lastly, within the city and within the future house are those who will enjoy the safety and the work that was done.

7 · Pastor Jeff draws out the theological principle embedded in the psalm: God's sovereignty and human responsibility coexist—we are called both to trust and to act simultaneously

In God's wisdom and his creative nature, he reveals deep realities for us to consider and be shaped by. The most apparent is that while every task the Lord gives us requires action on our part, he ultimately does what is needed. This both reveals our responsibility and our dependence. He calls us to trust and to act simultaneously.

8 · Pastor Jeff surfaces and begins to refute the objection that if God does all the work, human labor is unnecessary, arguing that this view reduces work to mere necessity rather than privilege

The discomfort of this is that we often wonder, why do we need to work at all if God is going to be the one that does the heavy lifting? After all, it seems sort of like nonsense to think we need to do anything at all if God's going to get it done at the end of the day. We only need to trust Him. Maybe you've even been tempted. We just need to sit back and just trust Him and watch Him do what He does. Don't even need to worry about lifting a finger. The problem with this thinking— the problem with this thinking is that it restricts the idea of work to necessity alone. It takes the idea of work and says this is only something we do out of necessity. It's needed. That is, when we think this way, we carve out a meaning for work that limits it to being what needs to happen rather than what gets to happen.

9 · Pastor Jeff corrects a flat reading of the psalm by asserting that the point is not the finished product but the privilege and joy of working alongside God

If we read this psalm flatly, we think the point of this psalm is the finished house and the quiet and safe city. It isn't. The Spirit of God invites us to the privilege of work. Our passage reveals the joy of working, knowing that God is the only one doing the work, and that already ought to set our expectations to wonder, what is the joy of work?

10 · Pastor Jeff explains that while the work is physically real and demanding, knowing God is the ultimate builder transforms it from mere labor into a front-row seat to God's craftsmanship—the privilege of work is proximity to the Creator

You see, the workers have a real job to do. And their experience is hands-on. You're gonna get splinters when you're working. You're gonna smash your finger. Your back is gonna be sore. You're gonna be tired. But when they know, when they know who really keeps the enemies at bay, And when they know who really builds the house, their work is as much a front row seat to God's craftsmanship and his work as it is a practical participation. The privilege of work is that our vocation is shaped, our vocation is shaped not just by craft, but by the wonder and the amazement of working alongside the Creator. It is work because it does require real engagement and ownership, but it is a privilege because no other seat brings you as close to the splendor of seeing God work and protect. There is no closer seat than doing the work.

11 · Pastor Jeff applies the principle to both building and watching, arguing that while non-participants avoid the strain of work, they also miss the wonder of watching God overcome human limitations

This is the proven task of building. When you consider all that is required to erect a safe, long-lasting structure. Again, this is evident when we are tasked with keeping watch over the city, when the limits of our eyes restrict what we can see at night. We know what we're capable and not capable of. And for those of us who don't participate, sure, we spare— we are spared the strain, but we are also spared the wonder and the amazement of majesty on display When, though we cannot see, though we cannot be strong enough, we watch as God delivers. There's nothing like it.

12 · Pastor Jeff uses his childhood enjoyment of building toys and the cultural phenomenon of Bob Ross to illustrate the joy of watching a masterpiece take shape rather than simply viewing the finished product

I know not everyone gets excited about seeing things come together. This is just in my nature. I was the nerd that got excited about erector sets and K'Nex toys and Legos and all those kinds of things because of what could be made. But there is a privilege. There is a unique privilege and a joy of being invited to watch a masterpiece come together by a craftsman. Now, he's known for his quirky mannerisms and big hair, and young people, I'm sorry, you may miss this one, but Bob Ross became a part of all our culture by inviting his audience to watch the process of brushstrokes and happy trees becoming the most realistic displays of nature on canvas. Think about this. Nobody tuned in to him to watch him talk about his paintings, all complete and wrapped up and done. Why did we watch? What made it special was getting to watch a smear of paint turn into light on the water. And several blots of a brush form mountain peaks.

13 · Pastor Jeff synthesizes the Bob Ross illustration with the psalm's teaching, asserting that work becomes privilege when we witness God accomplishing the impossible—we are invited not to carry our fair share but to behold splendor taking shape

That is where our passage directs us. Our passage directs our hearts this morning not to carry our fair share, but to be a part of the privilege of seeing splendor take shape. The privilege of beholding what is not possible come into being. Work becomes a privilege when we see God do what only God can do. While the passage teaches us to remember who really does the work, it also invites us to go to work, all along knowing we get to go to work and watch as the master crafts what we could never. Every day in the hot sun and every night in the cold and darkness, watching the Creator masterfully direct the cosmos and deliver safety and raise the house.

14 · Pastor Jeff transitions to the third privilege—enjoyment—describing how those who live in the finished house and safe city experience the confidence and joy of security

Naturally, this brings us to the privilege of enjoyment. Those warmed by the walls of the house know the confidence they bring, the walls bring. Those who wake in the morning to safety where they live know the joy of threats kept They know the joy of new children being able to be brought and laid down peacefully, though it storms outside. They rejoice at the sanctuary of unthreatened community, walking and traveling from home to home without any worry or fear of shutting off the lights.

15 · Pastor Jeff argues that while passive enjoyment is real, it is incomplete—the deepest enjoyment belongs to those who have labored and witnessed God's work firsthand, because enjoyment overflows when paired with work

I ask you, though, As wonderful as that is, is that really the privilege to enjoy? Who do you imagine, who do you imagine is more intimate with the pleasure of a home than the one who has labored and watched the King of all raise its roof? Who has more confidence in the night than the one who stood on guard behind the safety of the Almighty? Yes, those who benefit from these things know the joy of safety and the roof over their head, sure, but do they really know it as they could? That is, do they have all that is made available to them? The privilege of enjoyment overflows when paired with work and seeing God work.

16 · Pastor Jeff returns to his father's construction work as an illustration, showing that those who build know the structure intimately in ways that passive users never can—the worker sees what disappears from the casual observer's view

There is just no way those who fill the buildings my Father built will ever know or experience them as him and his colleagues did. There are corners of that building, of that church that I mentioned, and there are corners of buildings that he built all over that no one will ever lay their eyes on again, but they knew them intimately. And that was one of the things about growing up with a dad that does construction is he could walk in the room and he could analyze it like that and he could see what was done and what wasn't done. And it is more than just a thing that you go into and are kept dry. It is something that took real work and craftsmanship. They know every cut, every angle, every dimension. What disappears in the view of those who simply walk in and walk out of buildings, the walker or the worker sees in their sleep. For the one who works with the Lord, the one who labors and watches him work, they will always know far more than those who just simply step in and step out.

17 · Pastor Jeff pivots from exposition to application, explicitly connecting the three privileges (work, seeing, enjoying) to Center Church's first year and the years ahead

As we look back over the last year of this little church and the years coming before, No, it is a building project and a watchtower, a watchpost and star. Psalm 127 speaks to us about this in 3 ways at least.

18 · Pastor Jeff calls the congregation to thank God for what He has already accomplished, clarifying that God's building project is not the church organization but the work of saving people and calling them together

First, it compels us to thank God for all he has already done. It drives us to say thank you. We must recognize that while some of us have given and done much, None of it could have happened without the hand of God. No matter what any individual or any family or whoever contributed what, nothing would have happened if it was not for the power and the direction and the will of God. And to leave that there alone would be a tragedy. We must not assume that the work of God that has been done was our coming here to plant a church. That is a part of it, certainly, but that is not the extent of it. You see, what God is doing in the raising of the house, in the setting of the watchtower, is God is working to save people, to call them and to move them here. Never forget the building project the living God has been working on in you and in me, in those who are his and whom he has brought together in this place. Know that every individual here, everyone who has worked to get us here, everyone who has simply enjoyed the fact that we are here, that the Lord has done this, is the direct result of God's masterful plan and craftsmanship. Every single bit of it. From where you live to hearing of this ministry to the facility in which we meet to the care of your children, all of it mastery at work and on display.

19 · Pastor Jeff clarifies the true nature of God's building work: not organizational formation but the calling of people to love one another sacrificially because of Christ's love for them—practical tasks matter only insofar as they serve this deeper work

Secondly, know that the building God is doing is not the formation of Center Church of Star as an organization. It is the calling of people together to love one another deeply, sacrificially, laying down their lives for one another because they deeply love a Savior who has sacrificially loved them and laid down his life for them. Many practical things need to be done. Bills need to be paid. Chairs need to be set up and torn down. Music needs to be played. Sermons need to be preached. Curtains need to be put away. Curtains need to go up. Trailers need to be moved. Trailers need to be stored. But those things matter only if God himself is calling the lost, raising the dead, and preserving the Saints.

20 · Pastor Jeff testifies to specific ways God has already been working at Center Church: saving the lost, delivering what was needed, sustaining the weak, restoring relationships, and bonding people together

Brothers and sisters, friends, I testify to you this morning, make no doubt about it, that while much work has been done, the Spirit of God has truly been doing a wonderful work here. Already, already we have had the privilege of seeing him save those lost in their sin. Already we have seen him deliver to us what we could not find. Already we have seen him sustain the weak, giving them strength to serve week after week. Already we have seen him restore relationships that seemed lost, brought together in, in only the way the Creator can. Already we have seen him bond and bring together those who otherwise had nothing in common. These and countless more have proven time and time again that our God is, is not was, is fervently working in STAR and in his people. Without a doubt.

21 · Pastor Jeff speaks directly as one who has witnessed God's work firsthand at Center Church, affirming that workers experience the truth of Jesus' promise 'I will build my church' in a uniquely intimate way

Let me make this very clear. Without a doubt, those of us who have been at work, we have been up close and personal seeing these things all happen. Those who work know these things more intimately than ever before. And those who are at work know in their core when they hear the words of the Lord Jesus tell to the Apostle Peter and the other boneheads around him, I will build my church. Those at work know what that looks like.

22 · Pastor Jeff invites those who have not yet participated in the work to join now, assuring them it is not too late to experience the privilege and joy of working with God

Lastly, know that we celebrate A year since the launch. But no, more importantly, the building project is not over. Though those doing the work can testify to all that Jesus has done and is doing, it is not too late for you too. Maybe you have not been doing the work. Maybe you're not sure what it means to do the work. But you can join in the work and experience the privilege and the joy of working, seeing, and enjoying the Master at work.

23 · Pastor Jeff challenges the congregation not to be content with passive attendance but to ask why they would not want to see God at work, praying that Jesus would draw them into the work so they can witness His masterful formation of the church

I know God is at work in you and in me, and it is my personal prayer that you and I would not be content with only knowing what is to come and go. Don't let your heart grow content with stepping in and stepping out. But ask yourself, if I don't want to see the Master at work, why? And watch Him fervently change your heart to desire to see Him do what He does. It is a privilege. I pray that Jesus, the builder of Center Church, would draw your affections and desires to join in the work. Again, not to carry your fair share, but to watch the Creator masterfully form who we will be. To watch him save the lost, to watch him transform relationships torn, to watch him go and develop his people into his image.

24 · Pastor Jeff urges the congregation to pray for increased desire to see God's power up close, testifying that witnessing God's transforming work surpasses even the majesty of natural wonders like the Grand Canyon

I encourage you to eagerly pray that your desire to see his power up close would increase. It is truly a privilege to behold him in all that he does. And as one who has seen him transform, save, and restore, I promise you there is nothing like it. Nothing compares. Having seen the peaks of the redwoods and the sequoias firsthand, standing next to them and feeling my size, nothing compares. Having stood on the edges of the Grand Canyon and perceiving its depths, and the 100 different ways it can kill me, I can tell you it doesn't even compare. These things are, as it has been said so well, these are merely drops in the bucket compared to the majesty of God's power at work in front of your own eyes. Nothing is more majestic than a man or a woman turning from selfishness Nothing will make your hair stand up more quickly than seeing people turn away from a lifetime of sin.

25 · Pastor Jeff issues a general exhortation to embrace all that God has given in witnessing His work through Center Church during its first year

As we celebrate our first year together, I want to encourage each of you to take up, take up all that God has given us in seeing him work in and through this church.

26 · Pastor Jeff introduces a second layer of meaning in Psalm 127:1 by explaining that 'house' refers not only to the temple but to the dynasty of David, connecting this to the psalm's continuation about children as heritage from the Lord

There is a second meaning in this passage. When the passage, oddly enough, of Solomon says, unless the Lord raises the house; its builders work in vain. That term house has a very special meaning relative to Solomon. That is because Solomon was both the one who would be building the temple, but when his father asked the Lord if he could build the temple, the Lord instead turned to him and said, You're not going to build my house, but I will build a house for you. And that house was the family of David. It is interesting that the rest of the psalm goes on to talk about the heritage of the Lord being what? Children. And children being a heritage from the Lord are like arrows in the quiver of a man. And how at the gates where all stand, he will not be put to shame by even his enemies.

27 · Pastor Jeff traces the Davidic covenant forward to Christ, arguing that the ultimate purpose of God building David's house was to bring the Savior into the world, and challenging the congregation to consider whether their own families serve this same ultimate purpose

You see, we can think of a lot of things that we want to see the Lord build. But the interesting thing is, is that the family that the Lord would build in the line of David would have one purpose, and it was not so that the line of David would be great. It would be so the world would get its Savior. Because it was through the line of David, the house of David, that we received the Lord Jesus Christ, the incarnate Son of God, who would come and would walk and would live and would lay down his life for you and for me. That was the purpose of the family of David. What other purpose would we want from our family than to bring the world the Savior it needs?

28 · Pastor Jeff transitions from exposition to specific application by calling the congregation to consider how their work—both in the church and in their families—can serve the purpose of bringing Christ to those who need Him

All that we do, all that we think, all that we lay our hands to, Whether it is the work of building this church or is the work of your own family being built, may it all be for the purpose of bringing the Savior to the people who need him. So I, as your friend, would call each of you in a very specific way to consider how this might be done in your life and in the life of your home.

29 · Pastor Jeff calls husbands and fathers to follow Jesus in a way that makes their families long to enjoy Him, urging them to be known for their pursuit of Christ and to teach by example that Christ, not comfort, sits at the center of the universe

Husbands and fathers, I invite you to follow the Lord Jesus in such a way that your family longs to enjoy him with you in every possible way. Be known in your home. Have a reputation among your children and your wife for your pursuit of Christ. Teach them not just by what you say, but by what you do. Show them what is worth your time and your efforts, and they will learn to value those things as well. Lead your family so they know Christ is the one who sits at the center of the universe and not their comfort. And more than anything, show him he is absolutely worth everything.

30 · Pastor Jeff calls wives and mothers to treasure Jesus above all so their families see Christ's splendor in their daily service, and to help their husbands by communicating that they desire to see Christ at work more than they desire convenience

Wives and mothers, treasure Jesus more than anything. Treasure Jesus more than anything so your husbands and your children see Christ's splendor in the diligence and service that you live by every day. Help your husbands, and we need your help. Help your husbands by telling them your desire to see Christ at work, both in and out of the home, outweighs your desire for convenience. We're going to assume you want things to be easy. We need your help to know You want Jesus more than the easy life. And if it doesn't exist in you, then help us by telling us and asking us to pray with you so that you can grow.

31 · Pastor Jeff addresses single men and women, affirming their value as Christ's prized possession and a gift to the church, calling them to give themselves to Christ and His people so their devotion shines as a witness

Single men and women, know that you are not an afterthought, but the prized possession of Christ and a gift to this church. Give yourself to him and his people as you are able. Shine so that your brothers and sisters see the city on the hill. Let your devotion to Christ, your union with the Creator, be your crown and his love a ring on your finger.

32 · Pastor Jeff calls children to ask their parents and other adults why they work and serve, and to remember their answers so that one day when they see the church building fully set up, they will recall the labor that made it possible

Children, learn to ask why. Look to mom, look to dad, look to grandma, look to grandpa, look to neighbors, friends, and ask them why. Ask them why they work. Ask them why they serve. Ask them why they're up early and down late. Ask them why we don't just come and go like the grocery store or the bank. And when they tell you, remember it. Because one day, one day you're gonna drive past a building or a place with this church's name on it, and the chairs will already be standing inside. And the screens will already be put up and the cables will already be laid. Remember that one day before, somebody had to do that.

33 · Pastor Jeff calls the whole congregation to deep fellowship, reminding them that the church is people, not equipment, and urging them to burrow into one another's lives and demonstrate their belonging to Christ through their love for each other

Brothers and sisters, most importantly, know you are not alone. You work with the Lord, but he has called you together. Don't forsake the fellowship, encouragement, and the care of and for one another. People are the church. We are the church. The church does not pack up on trailers. The church does not sit in storage. It is us. Do not hide in your homes, but burrow into each other's lives. Look for ways to help one another, to love one another, and testify how each of you belong to the Savior. And testify by your love for one another. Not just on Sundays.

34 · Pastor Jeff concludes by calling Center Church to be fixed on Jesus, to enjoy all that is available in Him together, and to pray that future generations will drive past the church and recall God's wonderful work in His people

Center Church, let us be, as we say, fixed on Jesus. His glory and power are a sight to behold, and when you see it, let us all together enjoy all that is available to us in him. Let our love for the Lord and his people never feel like work. And if it would so please him, let this congregation, this little church being built by Jesus, be a place our children, our friends, and even those we have not yet met Let it be a place they all will one day drive by and recall the wonderful work of God in this people.

Monday Matthew 16:18

When Jesus declares, 'I will build my church,' He reveals the exclusive agency behind all spiritual formation. We do not build the church through programs, planning, or programs alone—Christ Himself is the active builder, and the gates of hell cannot prevail against His work. As we labor at Center Church, we join what Christ is already doing, not the reverse.

Tuesday Psalm 127:1

The psalmist does not say, 'If the Lord does not build, sit idle'—he calls builders and watchers into the work. Our labor is real, urgent, and necessary to our obedience, yet its fruit depends entirely on God's hand. In this paradox, we discover the deep humility and joy of co-laboring: we work with all our might while clinging to the One whose power alone accomplishes what matters eternally.

Wednesday Matthew 16:18

Christ's promise to build His church defies human capability—no human strategy, no organizational skill, no charisma creates what only the risen Lord can create. When we witness a hardened heart converted, a fractured marriage reconciled, or a stranger welcomed into the family of God, we behold what only Christ does. This is the privilege that makes our work glorious: we labor knowing we partner with the One who accomplishes the impossible.

Thursday Psalm 127:1

The house God builds is not a structure of programs, budgets, or institutional mechanics—it is a people bound together by the love of Christ. Every conversation, every act of forgiveness, every sacrifice of time or comfort for another believer is the material of God's building. As we serve one another at Center Church, we are not maintaining an organization; we are participating in the formation of a bride, a body, a temple of the Holy Spirit.

Friday Matthew 16:18

Those who labor alongside Christ do not merely inherit a safe city or finished house—they taste the joy of witnessing transformation, of seeing God's hand move in real time, of knowing intimacy with His craftsmanship that no spectator can know. As we look back on Center Church's first year and forward to what Christ will build through us, may we embrace not ease, but the incomparable privilege of working, watching, and rejoicing in what God alone can do.
