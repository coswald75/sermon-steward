Theological claims· 9
  1. Opposition does not mean God is losing; the scattering in Acts 8 repurposes the biblical pattern of judgment-scattering into missional scattering for gospel advance. unit #11
  2. God sovereignly orchestrated persecution to send the gospel to Samaria because the church would never have voluntarily chosen that destination. unit #21
  3. An unchanged heart is marked not by stumbling in sin but by being content to remain in sin. unit #25
  4. Despite all obstacles, God's sovereign orchestration ensured the mission to Samaria succeeded. unit #26
  5. The Jerusalem church had to come to terms with a missionary God who loved Samaritans—a reality contradicting their assumption that God despised them as they did. unit #27
  6. The scattered believers were losing the god they thought they had—a god who serves their comfort—and discovering the true God whose mission absorbs their lives into bringing joy to others. unit #29
  7. The delayed Spirit-reception ensured the church would not divide into Jewish and Samaritan factions, demonstrating the gospel's power to unite groups with 1,000 years of animosity into one body. unit #31
  8. The inclusion of ethnic outsiders like Samaritans does not compromise the church, but the inclusion of unconverted professors destroys it. unit #35
  9. Obstacles to the mission test whether we trust God to overcome them or whether we will only trust a God who conforms to our plans. unit #40

And but he believes and he's baptized, but in time it comes to find out his heart was not right. Like his heart was not actually converted. He was a professing Christian but not an actual Christian. This is a challenge to the church. He became a member of the church but was not like a member of the church. His heart was still like his old heart, not a new one. Outward profession of faith, no inward change of heart. This is a recurring obstacle to the mission of the church when there's someone who says the right things but only to find out in time that their heart is not in line with their profession of faith. And it's a challenge and sometimes difficult to discern. I talk with people who know how to say the right things. There are times though in time when it comes out. Oh, they do not truly believe. They have not truly been converted. They do not have a new life in them. The gospel has not taken hold and actually transformed them from the inside. Now they've hung around church people enough to know exactly what to tell the pastor when he asks questions, but there they sit with an unchanged heart.

25 · Distinguishes between a believer struggling with sin and an unconverted person content in sin

Now we're not talking about somebody who's stumbling in a sin. We're talking about somebody who is just fine with staying in their sin. Ways that you recognize there is an unchanged heart, and Simon was one of those.

26 · Pivots from obstacles to success

Now, with these obstacles, the mission remained successful. So each one of these obstacles has another side of the coin. The gospel actually did go faithfully into Samaria. They would have not chosen to go there, They were forced to go there against their will. Nevertheless, the gospel is proclaimed and people hear the message and they believe it. God's hand is orchestrating events here and working out his own mission.

27 · Articulates the theological lesson: the church had to revise their theology of God

The church, whatever their own thoughts about Samaria and God, which was likely that God despised them, mixed-race, irreligious heretics, had to come to terms with this missionary God who determined to bring good news to all nations. God overrode their perceptions, both of him and the Samaritans, because this missionary God had a heart of Samaria, and he was going to orchestrate a plan to bring the gospel to them.

28 · Isolates Acts 8:8 ('joy in that city') as the theological climax revealing God's purpose

We have this amazing little summary statement: and there was joy in the city. That statement speaks to my heart and tells me something about God's plan, God's design God's heart. Think about the juxtaposition. You've got all these Christians fleeing Jerusalem, running for their lives, frantic, panicking, in danger, struggling, going into a community that their race has historically for 1,000 years despised. And the gospel comes through and the assessment, just in a short phrase, This is the result: joy in that city. Oh! Here comes God's plan shining through. This is what God was after. This is what God was doing. This is why you got scattered. This is why you lost your lease. This is why you got moved to a location you didn't want to go to. Why? So that there would be joy in that city.

29 · Returns to Kuyper's framework

I wonder if they were losing the god they had and beginning to discover the God that is. He wanted joy in that city, and they lost their homes and their safety and their livelihood in order for God to bring that about. Was Kuyper right? Oh, it wasn't about their immediate happiness and comfort. But it was overwhelmed by a bigger heart of God of sharing his good news and his love and his saving grace to a people that didn't have it, didn't know it, and he was willing for these people to pay a cost to get the message to the people that he was calling to himself.

30 · Addresses the converts-without-the-Spirit anomaly

Okay, what about these converts without the Spirit? What in the world are we going to do with this passage People are getting saved and they're not getting the Holy Spirit. Well, what ends up happening in the story is word gets back to Jerusalem, and so they send Peter and John. And while the situation seems so unusual, it seems clear that God was again orchestrating things with a purpose, because the connection with the apostles in Jerusalem was a very important connection. With this long history of division between Jerusalem and Samaria, with such a diversion of worship, with such animosity between them, having Peter and John make the connection in the gospel with them— this was an important part of the mission.

31 · Develops the unity-of-the-church argument

It's imperative that the church not be divided. Can you imagine had it not played out like this? And there was not this connection of the apostles from Jerusalem going to Samaria and making this connection, it's like when they laid their hands on them, then the Spirit was poured out on them. Had that not have happened, what was the risk of there being 2 churches? A Jewish church, a Samaritan church. Would not the divide had remained Would they not— of all the circumstances there, would they not have been a divided church? We'll remain us, we'll be the Samaritan church, you stay there, you be the Jerusalem church. The seal of the Spirit is poured out at the laying on of hands of the apostles from Jerusalem to establish the unity of the church. Do you see God's heart and hand in his mission of expansion? Going to nations, going to other peoples, even despised peoples. Nevertheless, what he's building is one church, one united people of God. This is the power of the gospel, that the power of the gospel is powerful enough to take 2 groups of people with a 1,000-year history of animosity and heal their hearts and reconcile their hearts and make them both and all part of one body. This is what Ephesians 4:4 tells us: There is one body and one Spirit, just as you were called to the one hope that belongs to your call— one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all, who is over all and through all and in all. What was important about this episode was to make that connection with the apostles in Jerusalem and this new church getting established in Samaria.

32 · Addresses the Simon obstacle's resolution

And we have the obstacle of Simon, and by God's grace, Simon was confronted and with his false profession and his heart that was not right with God. And he was challenged and called out and called to repentance. Now we know that he asked for prayer. We don't have really the story in full about whether his repentance was full, whether he was restored. We don't know what became of the man. All we know is, please pray for me. That what you're saying is not really going to come upon me. So we don't know how this played out, but the lesson is clear: an outward profession to Christ without an inward change of heart is not true Christianity.

33 · Direct pastoral warning to anyone sitting in the congregation with an outward profession but unchanged heart

If you are sitting here and if you said the right things and you made it through the membership interview and you said all the right things and I got all the right answers, but you are sitting there with an unchanged heart then you are not a true believer. And while we might unknowingly, or unbeknownst to us, count you as a member of the church, spiritually speaking, it is not a reality. You must be born again. You must be born again. The power of the gospel must have changed you. It must change you on the inside. This is— Christianity is not something you put on like a jacket. They are not words you just put in your mouth and repeat after me. It is a confession, a profession of a work of the Spirit in your heart, a renewed heart. And the challenge is you can fake it for a time, but the truth will out in time and it will become evident.

34 · Charges the church with the responsibility to confront false profession

The lesson is that the church needs to be as diligent as possible to say and call out, your heart's not right with God. This is not what we talked about. This is not our message. This is not what we agreed upon. We gathered together as a local congregation saying God has saved us by grace through Christ. That's who we are. He has changed our hearts. He's given us new life. He's forgiven us. He's washed us clean. That's why we are a local church. And trying to come in saying the right things but not having that be a reality in your heart heart is an obstacle to what God is doing, that we need to be careful and faithful to call out and call to repentance. If you said you were saved, but you're not saved, you need to get saved.

35 · Contrasts the two threats to the church's purity

It was not the inclusion of Samaria that would be the compromise to the church. Church, but the inclusion of the unconverted professing Christians certainly would be. Can you imagine if this was never addressed and all of a sudden we found out that as a local church, a third of the people, half the people, two-thirds of the people sitting in the room do not actually know the Lord. We would have to go down in history as the least effective, least inspiring, least appealing congregation that ever existed. Our hope, our success in the mission is in what the Spirit of God has done in your heart and in my heart.

36 · Signals the shift to concluding application

Okay, some closing thoughts, some application.

37 · Applies the Jerusalem church's faithfulness amid persecution to the congregation's current challenges

There's often trouble for the people of God. Question is, will we be faithful? Specifically, will we keep the gospel central? If we're being dispersed or if there's trouble disrupting our lives, if our little world gets upended, for this reason or for that, the question is, will we remain gospel-centered and faithful to proclaim it? Will we have the wherewithal and the wisdom to recognize that while we may have lots of troubles that we've got to address, solve— now we got to go driving around Bozeman and Belgrade and Four Corners looking for a building, we got to make phone calls, we got to occupy ourselves with all kinds of activities Okay, will we remain faithful? And our faithfulness will be measured by how gospel-centered we remain in all we say and do.

38 · Diagnostic questions probing the listener's faithfulness threshold

What kind of trouble does it take to cause us to be unfaithful? What kind of trouble needs to come into your life for you to conclude, I think I'll just keep my mouth shut? I think it would be better not to say anything in this situation. We've been reading about crowds opposing the church, influential and powerful people accusing leaders, leaders arrested, leaders thrown in prison, hypocrisy within the church. Now we read about a great persecution that broke out, men and women being hauled off to prison They had to leave their homes, but we see this did not change their message. This did not change who they were. Even if they had to leave, they left saying the same thing. Their confession was the same. Their testimony was the same.

39 · Imaginative reconstruction of a conversation between a scattered believer and a Samaritan neighbor

I could imagine that was opportunity for some conversations. So you just moved from Jerusalem and you're here in Samaria? You're looking to find a place to live in Samaria? I got to ask, what brought you here? It's a little unlikely. Why would you move from there to here? Well, let me tell you a story. Let me tell you something about a Savior who changed my life. Let me tell you about a Savior who came and and laid down his life and died. And when I heard him, when I heard of him, I was never the same. And this turned out to be a problem in Jerusalem, and that's why we're here. But what I know of Jesus and what he's done for me is so much greater than this little bit of trouble that we're having here. And so I'm glad that he brought me here, and I'm glad that you're my new neighbor. I'm glad that you're my new coworker, and I'm glad to tell you about what Christ has done for me.

40 · Reframes obstacles as a test of trust in God's sovereignty

There were many obstacles to the mission. The question that I wanna ask you is, will we trust God to overcome them? Are we in the right place? Are these the right people? Or are things going not according to our plan? Are we losing the God we had?

41 · Pastoral speculation about when the scattered believers recognized God's purposes

I don't know at what point the people that were relocating, that were being scattered, at what point they were aware and it dawned on them that God was actually carrying out a good plan. I don't know if they read Luke's manuscript and read that statement, and there was joy in the city. Maybe they just observed it. Maybe it was at that point when they realized, oh, these people are believing, a church is being formed, these people's lives are being changed. Maybe it's at that moment it dawned on them, oh, now I begin to see God's hand at work. Now I begin to see God's heart coming through all these troublesome events that we're in the mix of and swirling about in and trying to deal with.

I want to just land on that note, and I want to read you a little paragraph. This is Michael Green. He wrote kind of a classic book, Evangelism Through the Local Church. And maybe you're a little bit like me. When trouble comes, I say, God, why are you doing this to me? Why are you making life so hard? Why, why, why don't you just make things better? Why the trouble? And why the obstacles? And now what I want to hear God say is, well, Ron, I'm doing it for joy in that city. I'm doing it because I want my grace to go to them.

44 · Michael Green quotation on the joy of evangelism

Green writes this: one of the most widely credited and widely inaccurate myths is that Christianity is dull. It's nothing of the kind. Chapter 8 helps my heart to lift my eyes above the troubles of the day the obstacles that we're fighting up against and causes me to lift my eyes and see this missionary God who loves the world, who is working in and through his church and his people so that the grace of God can come and change their lives and that there can be joy in that city, joy in that life, joy in that household. That helps me because in the day-to-day stuff that I deal with, sometimes my eyesight gets lost and limited and it's small and it's down and it's inward. And Acts chapter 8 puts his hand underneath my chin, points my head upward, said, yes, it's not easy But can you see God at work? Can you see God's heart? Can you see what he wants to do? There are more people God will save. We need to be faithful even in the midst of trouble so that we will be the tools that God uses to see them come to know the saving grace of Jesus Christ. Friendship with Jesus, joy in the city.

Small-group discussion

6 questions for your group this week

  1. In Acts 8:1-4, Luke describes the church being scattered due to persecution, yet immediately tells us that 'those who were scattered went about preaching the word.' What does this sequence suggest about how the earliest believers understood the relationship between their displacement and God's purposes?
    Acts 8:4; Acts 1:8
    → How might the believers' willingness to preach while scattered differ from how we typically respond when circumstances disrupt our plans?
  2. The sermon emphasizes that the Jerusalem church would never have voluntarily chosen to take the gospel to Samaria. What does this historical animosity (rooted in 1 Kings 12 and 2 Kings 17) reveal about the limits of human mission planning, and what does God's decision to scatter the church into Samaria tell us about his purposes?
    Acts 8:1, 5; 1 Kings 12; 2 Kings 17:6
  3. When Peter and John arrived in Samaria and the Holy Spirit had not yet been given to the converts (Acts 8:14-17), this created an unusual and potentially dividing situation. Why do you think God delayed the Spirit's gift to the Samaritan believers, and what does this teach us about how the gospel overcomes centuries of ethnic division?
    Acts 8:14-17; Ephesians 4:4-6
    → How does the delayed Spirit-reception prevent the outcome that *would* have fractured the church?
  4. In Acts 8:9-24, Simon the magician 'believed' and was baptized, yet Peter discerned that his heart was not right with God. The sermon suggests that 'an unchanged heart is marked not by stumbling in sin but by being content to remain in sin.' What is the difference between a believer who struggles with temptation and a false professor, and how might we recognize this difference in our own lives and in those we shepherd?
    Acts 8:9-24; Romans 8:9
  5. The sermon states that scattered believers were 'losing the god they thought they had—a god who serves their comfort—and discovering the true God whose mission absorbs their lives.' When you face unwanted circumstances or displacement from comfort, what does this reveal about what kind of God you actually trust, versus the kind of God you wish to serve?
    Acts 8:4-8; Ephesians 1:13
    → How does the gospel of Christ's substitutionary work and resurrection reshape our willingness to surrender comfort for the sake of mission?
  6. The sermon concludes that 'obstacles to the mission test whether we trust God to overcome them or whether we will only trust a god who conforms to our plans.' As you think about your own life this week—your work, relationships, and circumstances—where might God be orchestrating a disruption to his missionary purposes, and what would faithfulness look like in that situation?
    → What does it mean practically to testify to God's grace in the midst of that trouble, rather than seeking primarily to escape it?
Daily readings · Monday–Friday

5-day reading plan

This week we trace how God's sovereign purposes transcend our comfort, scattering us into mission and transforming our understanding of who God truly is.

Monday Acts 1:8

Jesus promised His witnesses would carry the gospel to Judea and Samaria—not because the disciples wanted to, but because the Spirit would compel them. The scattering in Acts 8 was not the disruption of God's plan; it was the fulfillment of it. We are learning that God's purposes are far larger than our preferences, and He will use circumstances we never anticipated to accomplish His redemptive will.

Tuesday Ephesians 4:4-6

Paul's declaration of one body, one Spirit, one faith reminds us that the gospel does not merely add ethnic outsiders to the church—it fundamentally unites them as members of Christ's body. The Spirit's delayed gift to the Samaritans in Acts 8 protected this unity by preventing a fractured church from forming. Our common possession of the Spirit is the foundation that makes reconciliation possible where human hostility once seemed permanent.

Wednesday Romans 8:9

Paul's stark word—'if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he does not belong to him'—exposes the danger Simon the sorcerer faced. His desire to purchase the Spirit's power revealed that he loved power more than Christ; his heart had never truly submitted to the Lord. True faith is not measured by our occasional failures but by whether our trajectory is toward Christ or away from Him. The test is whether we are willing to relinquish control and embrace His Lordship.

Thursday 2 Kings 17:6

When Assyria scattered the northern kingdom centuries before, exile seemed like abandonment. Yet God's ancient pattern of judgment-exile prepared the way for Philip to preach Christ to a Samaritan remnant in Acts 8. What appeared to be punishment became the stage for redemption. We see that God's sovereignty over history—even the painful chapters—ultimately serves His purpose to bring His gospel to the nations.

Friday Psalm 16:11

The psalmist declares that in God's presence is fullness of joy—a joy deeper than comfort or security. The believers scattered from Jerusalem lost the life they planned but gained participation in God's redemptive mission, discovering a joy that cannot be shaken by circumstances. This is the radical reorientation the gospel demands: our lives are not primarily about our comfort with God added in, but about our absorption into His purposes, where we find a joy the world cannot give or take away.

When God Sends Us Where We Don't Want to Go

For the parent

This prompt invites your family to think about a time when circumstances—not our own choice—moved us somewhere unexpected. The goal is to help kids (and you) see that God's purposes are often bigger than our comfort, and that he can do good things through changes we never would have planned.

In the sermon, the early church got scattered from Jerusalem because of persecution—they didn't choose to leave, but God used it to bring the gospel to Samaria, a place the church didn't even like. Has God ever moved our family or put us somewhere we didn't expect? What good thing happened because of it—something we might have missed if everything had gone the way we planned?
Works for ages 7+; younger children can listen and share simple observations while parents guide the conversation toward God's purposes
