Theological claims· 12
  1. Christians never have to be powerless because we have the presence and power of the Holy Spirit, and without that power, our growth in Christ and gospel mission will fail. unit #5
  2. The promise of the Father is the Holy Spirit sent by Father and Son, and the Spirit's presence and power are to mark the New Testament church. unit #12
  3. The church today largely fails to present the gift of the Spirit as the supreme blessing of the gospel, despite Scripture setting forth the Spirit's bestowal as the climax of God's generosity. unit #13
  4. Sound doctrine and faithful ministry activity are insufficient—without the Spirit's presence and power, a church becomes dead, and people will leave to find churches marked by God's presence. unit #15
  5. Jesus is eager to fill us with His Spirit if we ask, and He promises to be with us always through the Spirit's indwelling and ongoing filling as we fulfill the Great Commission. unit #16
  6. God's presence among the church is vital to gospel witness because people leave dead churches and seek churches where God's presence and power are clearly active. unit #17
  7. People are drawn to churches where God's presence is clearly manifested, and God will mark Cross of Grace Church with His presence and power. unit #21
  8. Through the Spirit, the living God dwells in us, empowers us, and ensures we are never alone in the Christian life. unit #22
  9. The Spirit comforts God's people before multiplying them, and God wants to comfort Cross of Grace so He can bring fruitfulness and multiplication. unit #24
  10. The same Spirit who brought about the incarnation and empowered Jesus' ministry is the same Spirit who empowers believers today. unit #32
  11. The same Spirit who transformed and empowered the Ethiopian man to risk his life for Christ can empower Cross of Grace to be courageous witnesses in Chaska. unit #38
  12. God uses us most effectively in our weakest moments when we depend on the Spirit, not when we rely on our own giftedness or strength. unit #42

5-day reading plan

This week we trace the Spirit's centrality to the church's power, from the Father's promise through the disciples' empowerment to our own desperate dependence today.

Monday Joel 2:28-29, 32

Joel's ancient vision of the Spirit poured out on all flesh—servants and handmaids alike—sets the stage for what Jesus promises His disciples: not a private gift for an elite few, but the Father's promised empowerment for the entire covenant people. As we read these words, we recognize in them the very promise Jesus holds before the disciples in Luke 24:49, establishing that the Spirit's outpouring has been God's plan since the beginning.

Tuesday Acts 2:37-38

Peter's Pentecost proclamation shows the Spirit in action—convicting the conscience, moving the crowds to ask 'What shall we do?', and then empowering repentance and faith in thousands in a single day. This is not the work of human eloquence or programmatic skill, but the manifest power of the Spirit that transforms hearts and multiplies the church, demonstrating why we are powerless without Him.

Wednesday Luke 1:35

The Spirit who overshadowed Mary and brought forth the Son of God is the very Spirit whom Jesus breathes into His disciples and promises to send from the Father. This is no lesser spirit, no secondary gift—it is the same pneumatic power that made God incarnate and sustained Jesus' perfect ministry, now freely given to us for our witness and growth.

Thursday Acts 9:31

Luke's summary of the early church shows the pattern: churches that walk in the fear of the Lord and the comfort of the Holy Spirit are built up and multiplied in number. This verse reveals that human faithfulness alone—however doctrinally sound or programmatically active—cannot sustain or grow a congregation; only the manifest comfort and power of the Spirit brings multiplication and growth that endures.

Friday Psalm 67:1-2

The psalmist's prayer—'Let God be gracious to us and bless us, and make His face to shine upon us, that Your way may be known on earth'—reveals that the display of God's presence is inseparable from the advance of His gospel among the nations. As we intercede for Cross of Grace with these very words, we embrace the truth that our effectiveness in witness depends not on our cleverness or effort, but on the manifest face of God shining among us through the Spirit's presence and power.
